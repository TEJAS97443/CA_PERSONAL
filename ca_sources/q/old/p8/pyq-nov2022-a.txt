(ii)  Liquidity trap 
 
At a very high interest rate, say r*, the opportunity cost of holding money (in terms 
of foregone interest) is high and therefore, people will hold no money in speculative 
balances. When interest rates fall to very low levels, the expectation is that since 
the interest rate is very low it cannot go further lower and that in all possibility it will 
move upwards. In other words, investors would maintain cash savings rather than 
hold bonds. The speculative demand becomes perfectly elastic with respect to 
interest rate and the speculative money demand curve becomes parallel to the X 
axis. This situation is called a ‘Liquidity trap’.
