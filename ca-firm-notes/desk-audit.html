Unit 1 — Nature, Objective and Scope of Audit§

Margin note

Origin, and what an audit is§

The word is from the Latin audire, to hear: accounts were once read aloud to an official who heard and passed them. The listening became a documented examination; the logic did not change.

An audit is the independent examination of financial information of any entity, whether profit-oriented or not and irrespective of its size or legal form, carried out with a view to expressing an opinion on that information. Three words carry it: independent, examination, opinion. Management prepares the financial statements; the auditor opines on them.

An external audit engagement is one where the auditor is independent of the entity, appointed by or on behalf of the owners rather than by management, and reports to those owners. That separates it from internal audit, a function inside the entity reporting to management and those charged with governance.

The objective, in the Standard's own words (SA 200)§

SA 200 states the overall objectives of the independent auditor as: to obtain reasonable assurance about whether the financial statements as a whole are free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, thereby enabling the auditor to express an opinion on whether the financial statements are prepared, in all material respects, in accordance with an applicable financial reporting framework; and to report on the financial statements, and communicate as required by the Standards on Auditing, in accordance with the auditor's findings.

Reasonable assurance is a high level of assurance, obtained when the auditor has obtained sufficient appropriate audit evidence to reduce audit risk to an acceptably low level. It is not absolute assurance, and no synonym for it earns the mark.

SA 200 also carries the ideas the rest of the paper is built on: compliance with relevant ethical requirements; professional skepticism, a questioning mind alert to conditions that may indicate misstatement and a critical assessment of audit evidence; professional judgement; and conduct of the audit in accordance with all SAs relevant to it. Where an objective in a relevant SA cannot be achieved, the auditor evaluates whether that requires modification of the opinion or withdrawal.

The audit rests on a premise: that management, and where appropriate those charged with governance, are responsible for preparing the financial statements and for internal control, and will give the auditor the information and access the audit needs.

Verify: SA numbering and the wording of objectives move when a Standard is replaced. ICAI states that where new Standards on Auditing are enacted in place of existing ones, the corresponding provisions apply from a notified date. Confirm SA 200 against the Study Material for your attempt.

Remember this An audit gives reasonable assurance on whether the financial statements as a whole are free from material misstatement — nothing wider, nothing narrower.

It is not a certificate of correctness, not assurance about future viability, and not an opinion on the efficiency with which management has conducted the entity's affairs. State all three and there is little left to deduct.

Scope of audit§

Scope is the audit procedures the auditor judges necessary in the circumstances to achieve the objective, determined by the terms of the engagement, relevant legislation, and the pronouncements of the Institute. Two consequences are examined. Where law fixes the scope of a statutory audit, an agreement with the client cannot cut it down, and equally the auditor does not take on work outside that scope unless agreed. And the audit covers the financial statements as a whole, through selective testing, so the auditor looks for misstatements material individually or in the aggregate rather than for every misstatement.

Inherent limitations of an audit§

The most reliably examined topic in the unit. The auditor cannot obtain absolute assurance, and the reason is structural, not a failure of effort.

  1. The nature of financial reporting. Preparation involves management judgement and estimates about which a range of reasonable interpretations exists; some items carry irreducible estimation uncertainty.
  2. The nature of audit procedures. Management may not provide complete information, intentionally or otherwise. Fraud may involve sophisticated, carefully organised schemes, collusion and forgery. An audit is not an official investigation into alleged wrongdoing and the auditor has no legal powers of search. Audit evidence is therefore ordinarily persuasive rather than conclusive.
  3. Timeliness, and the balance of benefit against cost. Financial information loses value if it is late, which limits the time and cost that can be justified.
  4. Other matters. Fraud involving senior management or collusion; the existence and completeness of related party relationships and transactions; non-compliance with laws and regulations; and future events or conditions bearing on the entity's ability to continue as a going concern.

Add one supporting proposition: internal control has its own inherent limitations, so the auditor's reliance on it is never complete.

Exam trap Writing that the auditor "detects all frauds and errors"

Detection is not the objective; reasonable assurance that the financial statements as a whole are free from material misstatement, whether caused by fraud or error, is. Immaterial fraud, and fraud concealed by senior-level collusion, may not be found — saying so earns a mark rather than losing one.

Qualities of an auditor§

Technical competence in accounting, auditing and law is assumed. Examined beyond it: integrity, objectivity and independence; professional skepticism; professional judgement; knowledge of the client's business; tact with firmness; clear writing; methodical documentation; confidentiality. The fundamental principles and the threats to independence belong to the ethics unit of this desk.

Relationship of auditing with other disciplines§

Auditing borrows from everything and is subordinate to none. Accountancy — know how the number was made before judging it; auditing begins where accounting ends. Law — company law, contract, tax and sector regulation fix what the statements must contain and what must be reported. Economics — industry conditions and the concepts of value and income. Behavioural science — an audit is conducted on people, and judging what you are told is behavioural work. Statistics and mathematics — sampling and analytical procedures. Information technology — electronic records change both the controls and the approach. Financial management and production — ratios, funding structure and the processes generating transactions.

Unit 2 — Audit Strategy, Audit Planning and Audit Program§

Planning is examined as three nested things, and mixing them costs marks: the overall audit strategy (scope, timing and direction), the audit plan (the nature, timing and extent of procedures) and the audit program (the detailed list of procedures that instructs the team). SA 300 governs the first two.

The auditor's responsibility to plan§

The auditor shall plan the audit so that the engagement is performed in an effective manner. The requirement about who plans is asked and rarely answered: the engagement partner and other key members of the engagement team shall be involved in planning, including planning and participating in the discussion among engagement team members, because their experience and insight improve the effectiveness and efficiency of the planning process. Planning is not delegated wholesale to the assistant who fills in the program.

Planning is continual and iterative§

Say this in the Standard's terms. Planning is not a discrete phase at the start of the audit; it is a continual and iterative process that often begins shortly after, or in connection with, the completion of the previous audit and continues until the completion of the current engagement. As the audit progresses the auditor may need to revise the strategy and the plan — because the results of risk assessment or of further audit procedures differ from expectations, or the understanding of the entity changes. Strategy and plan feed each other: a change in the plan can force the strategy to be revised, and a revised strategy changes the plan.

Remember this Planning is a continual and iterative process, not a stage — it starts near the end of the previous audit and ends only when this one does.

Any answer that says planning "is completed before commencement of the audit" is contradicting SA 300. Say instead that a preliminary strategy and plan are established before substantive work begins and are revised as necessary as the audit progresses, with the revisions documented together with the reasons.

Preliminary engagement activities§

Before the audit begins, and at the beginning of the current engagement, the auditor shall undertake three preliminary engagement activities:

  1. Perform the procedures required regarding the continuance of the client relationship and the specific audit engagement.
  2. Evaluate compliance with relevant ethical requirements, including independence.
  3. Establish an understanding of the terms of the engagement — the Standard on agreeing engagement terms sits at the ethics unit of this desk.

Their purpose is to establish that the auditor has considered events or circumstances that may adversely affect the ability to plan and perform the audit; that the auditor can remain independent and able to perform the engagement; and that there is no misunderstanding with the client about the terms.

Client continuance is a judgement, not a formality. The matters considered include the integrity of the principal owners, key management and those charged with governance; whether the team is competent and has the capabilities, time and resources; whether the firm can comply with relevant ethical requirements; and significant matters arising in the current or previous engagement and their implications for continuing.

Elements of planning discussed with management§

The auditor may discuss elements of planning with management to facilitate the conduct of the engagement — for instance to coordinate some planned procedures with the work of the entity's personnel. Two limits are the examinable part: the overall audit strategy and the audit plan remain the auditor's responsibility, and care is needed so that discussion does not compromise effectiveness by making the procedures too predictable.

Establishing the overall audit strategy§

The strategy sets the scope, timing and direction of the audit and guides the development of the audit plan. In establishing it the auditor:

  • identifies the characteristics of the engagement that define its scope, such as the financial reporting framework and industry-specific reporting requirements;
  • ascertains the reporting objectives so as to plan the timing of the audit and the nature of the communications required;
  • considers the factors that, in the auditor's professional judgement, are significant in directing the engagement team's efforts;
  • considers the results of preliminary engagement activities and, where applicable, whether knowledge gained on other engagements performed by the engagement partner for the entity is relevant;
  • ascertains the nature, timing and extent of resources necessary to perform the engagement.

The last item is what the syllabus calls the strategy's assistance for the auditor. A completed strategy tells the auditor what resources to deploy on specific areas (experienced members on high-risk balances, an expert on complex matters, enough people to observe inventory counting at several locations), how much to allocate, when they are deployed (at an interim stage or at key cut-off dates), and how they are managed, directed, supervised and reviewed — briefing and debriefing meetings, partner and manager review, and whether an engagement quality control review is needed.

Developing the audit plan§

The audit plan is more detailed than the strategy and includes:

  • the nature, timing and extent of planned risk assessment procedures;
  • the nature, timing and extent of planned further audit procedures at the assertion level;
  • other planned audit procedures required so that the engagement complies with the SAs.

Planning of these procedures occurs over the course of the audit as the plan develops: the auditor may complete the planning of risk assessment procedures early, while the further audit procedures depend on the outcome of that risk assessment.

Documenting strategy and plan§

SA 300 requires documentation of the overall audit strategy, the audit plan, and any significant changes made to either during the audit together with the reasons. The record of significant changes is the item candidates forget, and it is what demonstrates that planning was iterative.

Verify: planning and documentation requirements sit in SA 300 and the documentation Standard. Where a Standard is replaced, ICAI applies the corresponding provisions from a notified date — confirm against the Study Material and Study Guidelines for your attempt.

The audit program§

An audit program is a detailed list of the audit procedures to be performed for each area, serving both as a set of instructions to the assistants and as a means of controlling and recording the proper execution of the work. Constructing one requires the auditor to consider the size and nature of the entity, its accounting system and existing internal control, the deadline and the availability and experience of staff. Nothing is standard: a program copied from another client or from last year without review is the failure the examiner is testing for.

Unit 3 — Risk Assessment and Internal Control§

SA 315 is how the auditor identifies and assesses the risks of material misstatement, SA 320 fixes the materiality against which "material" is measured, and SA 330 is the response.

Audit risk, decomposed§

Audit risk is the risk that the auditor expresses an inappropriate audit opinion when the financial statements are materially misstated. It is a function of the risks of material misstatement and detection risk.

ComponentDefinition — reproduce these words
Inherent riskThe susceptibility of an assertion to misstatement that could be material, before consideration of any related controls
Control riskThe risk that internal control will not prevent, or detect and correct, on a timely basis a misstatement that could be material
Detection riskThe risk that the auditor's procedures will not detect an existing misstatement that could be material

Inherent and control risk together are the risk of material misstatement; they exist independently of the audit, so the auditor assesses them but cannot change them. Detection risk is the only component he controls, by altering the nature, timing and extent of procedures — inversely, so the higher the assessed risk of material misstatement, the more persuasive the evidence required.

Sampling and non-sampling risk§

Sampling risk is the risk that a conclusion based on a sample differs from the conclusion had the whole population been tested. Concluding that controls are more effective, or a material misstatement absent, when the opposite is true damages audit effectiveness and can produce an inappropriate opinion; the converse damages efficiency. Non-sampling risk is the risk of an erroneous conclusion for any reason unrelated to sampling — an inappropriate procedure, misinterpreted evidence, an unnoticed misstatement.

Exam trap Saying non-sampling risk falls if the sample size rises

Only sampling risk responds to sample size. Non-sampling risk is reduced by proper planning, direction, supervision and review and by competent staff — never by selecting more items.

Materiality in planning and performing an audit (SA 320)§

Misstatements, including omissions, are material if they, individually or in the aggregate, could reasonably be expected to influence the economic decisions of users taken on the basis of the financial statements. Judgements are affected by the size or the nature of a misstatement or both, and rest on the common financial information needs of users as a group rather than any individual user. Materiality is applied in planning and performing the audit, and again in evaluating identified and uncorrected misstatements at the completion unit. The auditor's determination of materiality is a matter of professional judgement — a stated syllabus point, and why there is no formula.

Benchmarks. A percentage is often applied to a chosen benchmark: profit before tax, total revenue, gross profit, total expenses, total equity or net asset value. The choice turns on which elements users focus on, the entity's nature, life cycle and industry, its financing structure, and the relative volatility of the benchmark. Profit before tax from continuing operations is common; where volatile, gross profit or total revenue serves better.

Verify: the syllabus prescribes no percentage. Any figure applied to a benchmark comes from the firm's methodology and the Study Material's illustrations.

Where misstatements below overall materiality could still influence users' decisions on a particular class of transactions, account balance or disclosure, materiality is set for that item too — related party transactions, remuneration of management and those charged with governance, statutory disclosures.

Performance materiality is the amount, or amounts, set at less than materiality for the financial statements as a whole to reduce to an appropriately low level the probability that the aggregate of uncorrected and undetected misstatements exceeds that materiality — and, where applicable, at less than the level set for a particular class. Setting it is professional judgement, affected by the understanding of the entity and by misstatements found in previous audits.

Materiality and audit risk move inversely: lower materiality demands more extensive procedures. Materiality set at planning does not establish an amount below which uncorrected misstatements are always immaterial. The auditor revises it, and reconsiders performance materiality and the planned procedures, on learning something that would have changed the original determination — a decision to dispose of a major part of the business, or actual results differing significantly from the anticipated period-end results. Documenting materiality covers overall materiality, the levels for particular classes if any, performance materiality, and any revision.

Remember this Overall materiality, materiality for particular classes and performance materiality are three separate figures, and all of them plus any revision are documented.

The mark is lost by treating performance materiality as a synonym for materiality or as a percentage rule. It is a lower amount absorbing the aggregate of undetected and uncorrected misstatements.

Identifying and assessing the risk of material misstatement (SA 315)§

The objective is to identify and assess the risks of material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, at the financial statement level and the assertion level, through understanding the entity and its environment including internal control — the basis for responses under SA 330.

Risk assessment procedures are inquiries of management and others including the internal audit function, analytical procedures, and observation and inspection. Understanding the entity covers industry and other external factors including the applicable financial reporting framework; the nature of the entity — operations, ownership and governance, investments, structure and financing; its accounting policies; its objectives, strategies and related business risks; and the review of its financial performance.

Internal control is the process designed, implemented and maintained by those charged with governance, management and other personnel to provide reasonable assurance about achieving the entity's objectives regarding reliability of financial reporting, effectiveness and efficiency of operations, and compliance with applicable laws and regulations. Its five components are the control environment, the entity's risk assessment process, the information system and communication, control activities relevant to the audit, and monitoring of controls.

For controls relevant to the audit the auditor evaluates their design and whether they have been implemented, which needs procedures beyond inquiry — observation, inspection, walking a transaction through the system. Evaluation is recorded through a narrative record, an internal control questionnaire, a check list or a flow chart, and whether he then tests controls depends on whether he intends to rely on their operating effectiveness. Internal control has inherent limitations: human error, collusion, management override, cost against benefit, faulty judgement, and poor fit with non-routine transactions. Documenting the risks covers the engagement team discussion, the understanding obtained with its sources, and the assessed risks at both levels with the related controls.

Digital audit§

Key features of an automated environment: high volumes of routine transactions processed consistently, automated calculations, system-generated entries and reports, configurable logic, interfaces, and an electronic audit trail. An automated control either works for every transaction or fails for every one.

IT-related risks: systems that process data inaccurately or process inaccurate data; unauthorised access causing loss of data or improper change; privileged access permitting management override; unauthorised changes to master data or programs; failure to make necessary changes; inappropriate manual intervention.

Types of controls: by nature, manual, automated and IT-dependent manual; by purpose, preventive, detective and corrective; by level, general IT controls over access, change management, program development and IT operations, and application controls such as input validation, edit and limit checks, automated reconciliations and exception reports.

Internal Financial Controls as per regulatory requirements: the Companies Act, 2013 places responsibility on the board for internal financial controls and requires the auditor to report on their adequacy and operating effectiveness for the companies covered. Internal Financial Control against Internal Control over Financial Reporting is distinguished at the audit evidence unit.

Verify: that reporting requirement and its applicability come from the Companies Act, 2013, rules under it and ICAI guidance.

Audit approach: document the automated environment — infrastructure, applications, databases, interfaces and the systems in scope — using system notes, flow charts and questionnaires and an IT specialist where needed; identify the relevant controls; assess the IT risks; test the controls; report the findings. Testing methods are inquiry, observation, inspection and reperformance, inquiry alone never sufficing. Data analytics tests the whole population: completeness of extracted data, recomputation, journal entries with unusual users, dates or amounts, duplicate payments, ageing and exception reports. On findings, judge each deficiency's severity and its effect on planned substantive procedures, then communicate deficiencies to management and those charged with governance.

The auditor's responses to assessed risks (SA 330)§

The objective is to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence about the assessed risks of material misstatement by designing and implementing appropriate responses. Overall responses at the financial statement level: emphasising professional skepticism, assigning more experienced staff or specialists, more supervision, additional unpredictability in selecting procedures, and general changes to the nature, timing or extent of procedures.

Two rules govern further audit procedures at the assertion level. Tests of controls are performed when the risk assessment includes an expectation that controls are operating effectively — the auditor intending to rely on them — or when substantive procedures alone cannot provide sufficient appropriate audit evidence. Irrespective of the assessed risks, substantive procedures are performed for each material class of transactions, account balance and disclosure.

Unit 4 — Audit Evidence§

The phrase that carries the marks is sufficient appropriate audit evidence: "sufficient" is quantity, "appropriate" is quality, meaning relevance and reliability.

Audit evidence, sufficiency and appropriateness (SA 500)§

Audit evidence is the information used by the auditor in arriving at the conclusions on which the audit opinion is based, including the information in the accounting records underlying the financial statements and other information. The quantity needed is affected by the assessed risks of material misstatement and by the quality of what is obtained; volume never compensates for weakness.

Reliability rests on generalisations, each a mark in a "state the factors" question: independent external sources beat internally generated evidence, which is better where the related internal control is effective; evidence obtained directly by the auditor beats evidence obtained indirectly; documentary beats oral; originals beat photocopies; and evidence is more persuasive when items from different sources or of a different nature agree.

Relevance is the logical connection between the procedure and the assertion tested, which is why direction of testing decides everything. Tracing recorded sales to dispatch documents tests occurrence and says nothing about completeness; to test completeness you start outside the ledger, from dispatch documents into the sales record.

Remember this Sufficient is quantity, appropriate is quality — and appropriateness is relevance plus reliability.

The generalisations are rebuttable, not rules: a confirmation is unreliable if the auditor does not control the request. Write "ordinarily" and give the reason.

Assertions§

Assertions are the representations of management, explicit or otherwise, embodied in the financial statements, by which the auditor considers the types of potential misstatement.

Transactions and eventsOccurrence, completeness, accuracy, cut-off, classification

Sources, procedures, and the two kinds of testing§

Sources include the accounting records and supporting documents, minutes, third-party confirmations, information from a previous audit and an expert's work; the absence of information is itself evidence. The procedures for obtaining audit evidence are inspection, observation, external confirmation, recalculation, reperformance, analytical procedures and inquiry, and inquiry alone is almost never sufficient. A test of controls evaluates the operating effectiveness of controls at the assertion level; a substantive procedure is designed to detect material misstatements at the assertion level and comprises tests of details and substantive analytical procedures. On evaluation, inconsistent evidence or doubt about reliability obliges the auditor to modify or add procedures, and information produced by the entity must itself be tested for accuracy and completeness.

Exam trap Calling a walk-through or an inspection of a control document a "test of controls"

Evaluating whether a control is well designed and has been implemented is risk assessment. A test of controls tests whether it operated effectively throughout the period of intended reliance. Using either phrase for the other costs the mark.

Audit trail§

An audit trail lets a transaction be traced forward from source document through the books to the financial statements, and backward again. Manually it is the documents and cross-references; in an automated environment it is the system log — user, date and time, values before and after a change. Its absence is a control deficiency, because occurrence and completeness then cannot be tested.

Verify: rules under the Companies Act, 2013 require accounting software to carry an audit trail or edit log. Confirm the requirement and its applicability for your attempt.

Internal Financial Control and Internal Control over Financial Reporting§

Internal Financial Control is the wider concept: policies and procedures for the orderly and efficient conduct of business, adherence to company policies, safeguarding of assets, prevention and detection of frauds and errors, accuracy and completeness of the accounting records, and timely preparation of reliable financial information. Internal Control over Financial Reporting is the subset directed at reliability of financial reporting. The board carries the responsibility; the auditor reports with reference to financial statements for the companies covered.

Verify: that reporting requirement and its applicability come from the Companies Act, 2013, rules under it and ICAI guidance.

Audit sampling (SA 530)§

Audit sampling applies audit procedures to less than 100% of the items in a population of audit relevance such that all sampling units have a chance of selection, giving a reasonable basis for a conclusion about the whole population. Designing it means considering the purpose of the procedure and the characteristics of the population, and setting a sample size that reduces sampling risk to an acceptably low level. Both approaches are acceptable — statistical, using random selection and probability theory to measure sampling risk, and non-statistical. Sample size rises with the assessed risk and expected misstatement and falls as tolerable misstatement rises, tolerable misstatement being performance materiality applied to that procedure. Selection methods are random, systematic, monetary unit, haphazard and block selection; haphazard selection is inappropriate with statistical sampling and block selection ordinarily cannot be used. The nature and cause of each deviation and misstatement matters, not merely the amount.

External confirmations (SA 505)§

An external confirmation is audit evidence obtained as a direct written response to the auditor from a third party. A positive request asks the party to reply whether or not it agrees; a negative request asks for a reply only if it disagrees, and is less persuasive because silence is ambiguous.

Negative confirmations may be the sole substantive procedure only if all four conditions hold: the risk of material misstatement is assessed as low and evidence has been obtained about the operating effectiveness of relevant controls; the population is a large number of small, homogeneous balances; a very low exception rate is expected; and the auditor knows of no circumstances that would cause recipients to disregard the requests. The auditor maintains control over the requests, including that replies come directly to the auditor.

Management's refusal to allow the auditor to send a confirmation request has a four-step answer: inquire into the reasons and seek evidence of their validity and reasonableness; evaluate the implications for the assessed risks of material misstatement, including the risk of fraud; perform alternative procedures; and where the refusal is unreasonable or those procedures fail, communicate with those charged with governance and determine the implications for the opinion.

Opening balances in an initial audit engagement (SA 510)§

The objective is evidence about whether opening balances contain misstatements materially affecting the current period, and whether the accounting policies reflected in them have been consistently applied or changes properly accounted for and disclosed. Read the most recent financial statements and the predecessor auditor's report, check that closing balances were correctly brought forward or restated, and either review the predecessor's working papers or perform specific procedures. Reporting on opening balances is what candidates omit: unable to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence — qualified opinion or disclaimer; a material misstatement not properly accounted for or disclosed — qualified or adverse; policies not consistently applied — qualified or adverse; the prior period's opinion modified and the matter still relevant — modify this period's opinion.

Unit 6 — Audit Documentation§

This unit is short and it is scoring, because the answers are definitional and the examiner can ask them in three marks. SA 230 governs it.

Concept, and the three definitions§

Audit documentation is the record of the audit procedures performed, the relevant audit evidence obtained, and the conclusions the auditor reached. "Working papers" and "work papers" are the same thing.

An audit file is one or more folders or other storage media, in physical or electronic form, containing the records that comprise the audit documentation for a specific engagement.

An experienced auditor is an individual, whether internal or external to the firm, having practical audit experience and a reasonable understanding of audit processes, the Standards on Auditing and applicable legal and regulatory requirements, the business environment in which the entity operates, and the auditing and financial reporting issues relevant to the entity's industry. That definition matters because the whole test of adequacy is written in terms of this person.

Nature and purpose§

Documentation serves two purposes stated by the Standard: it provides evidence of the auditor's basis for a conclusion about the achievement of the overall objectives of the auditor, and evidence that the audit was planned and performed in accordance with the SAs and applicable legal and regulatory requirements.

Beyond those, it serves several additional purposes: assisting the engagement team to plan and perform the audit; assisting those responsible for supervision to direct, supervise and review the work; enabling the team to be accountable for its work; retaining a record of matters of continuing significance to future audits; enabling quality control reviews and inspections; and enabling external inspections required by law, regulation or otherwise.

Form, content and extent§

The test is a single sentence worth reproducing verbatim in substance: documentation must be sufficient to enable an experienced auditor, having no previous connection with the audit, to understand

  1. the nature, timing and extent of the audit procedures performed to comply with the SAs and applicable legal and regulatory requirements;
  2. the results of those procedures and the audit evidence obtained; and
  3. significant matters arising during the audit, the conclusions reached on them, and the significant professional judgements made in reaching those conclusions.

In documenting the nature, timing and extent of the procedures performed, the auditor records the identifying characteristics of the specific items or matters tested — which invoice numbers, which date range, which selection basis — together with who performed the work and when it was completed, and who reviewed it, when, and the extent of the review.

Remember this The test of adequacy is an experienced auditor with no previous connection to the audit, and the three "who and when" records are where the easy marks are.

Quote the test as the Standard frames it — nature, timing and extent of procedures; results and evidence obtained; significant matters, conclusions and significant professional judgements — then add identifying characteristics, preparer and date, reviewer and extent of review.

The extent varies with the size and complexity of the entity, the nature of the procedures, the identified risks of material misstatement, the significance of the evidence obtained, the nature and extent of exceptions identified, the need to record a conclusion not readily determinable from the work documented, and the audit methodology and tools used.

Two limits are examined. It is not necessary to include superseded drafts, notes reflecting incomplete or preliminary thinking, previous copies of documents corrected for typographical errors, or duplicates. And oral explanations by the auditor do not, on their own, represent adequate support for the work performed or the conclusions reached, although they may be used to explain or clarify information the documentation contains.

Where the auditor judges it necessary to depart from a relevant requirement of a SA, the file records how the alternative procedures achieve the aim of that requirement, and the reasons for the departure.

Exam trap Writing that documentation exists "to prove the auditor did the work"

That is half the purpose and it reads as defensive. The Standard's two purposes are evidence of the basis for the auditor's conclusion and evidence that the audit was planned and performed in accordance with the SAs and applicable requirements — and the practical test is what an experienced auditor with no previous connection to the audit could understand from the file. Add the three "who and when" items — identifying characteristics, preparer and date, reviewer and extent of review — because that is where the easy marks sit.

Completion memorandum§

A completion memorandum, also called an audit completion document, is a summary prepared by the auditor describing the significant matters identified during the audit and how each was addressed, or cross-referring to the supporting documentation that does so. It is not a substitute for the file: it makes an effective and efficient review and inspection possible, particularly on large and complex audits, and it helps the auditor consider the significant matters in the round when forming the opinion.

Assembly, ownership and custody§

The auditor assembles the final audit file on a timely basis after the date of the auditor's report. After assembly is complete, documentation is not deleted or discarded before the end of its retention period. If it becomes necessary to modify existing documentation or add new documentation after assembly, the auditor records the specific reasons for the change and when and by whom it was made and reviewed — and the same applies to procedures performed after the date of the report, where the file also records the circumstances encountered, the evidence obtained, the conclusions reached and their effect on the auditor's report.

Ownership and custody is a favourite short question, so state it in three parts. Unless law or regulation provides otherwise, audit documentation is the property of the auditor, not of the client. The auditor may, at his discretion, make portions of or extracts from the documentation available to the client, provided the disclosure does not undermine the validity of the work performed or, in the case of an audit, the independence of the auditor or of his personnel. And the auditor must adopt appropriate procedures for confidentiality, safe custody, integrity, accessibility and retrievability, and for retention.

Verify: the outer time limit for assembling the final audit file and the minimum retention period are set by the quality control Standard covered at the ethics unit of this desk, not by SA 230. Confirm both figures in the Study Material for your attempt rather than quoting a number from memory.

Going concern (SA 570)§

Management assesses; the auditor evaluates. His objectives are to conclude on the appropriateness of management's use of the going concern basis of accounting, whether a material uncertainty exists related to events or conditions that may cast significant doubt on the entity's ability to continue as a going concern, and to report on it.

Events or conditions that may cast doubt — group them; an ungrouped list reads as guessing.

  • Financial — net liability or net current liability position; fixed-term borrowings maturing with no realistic prospect of renewal; short-term borrowing funding long-term assets; withdrawal of creditor support; negative operating cash flows; adverse key ratios; inability to pay creditors or to comply with loan terms.
  • Operating — intention to liquidate or cease operations; loss of key management, of a major market, key customer, franchise, licence or principal supplier; labour difficulties; shortages of important supplies; a highly successful new competitor.
  • Other — non-compliance with capital or statutory requirements; pending proceedings producing claims the entity cannot satisfy; adverse changes in law; uninsured catastrophes.

Procedures once identified: ask management to make its assessment if it has not; evaluate its plans; where a cash flow forecast is significant, test the underlying data and support for its assumptions; consider facts since the assessment; take written representations on the plans and their feasibility.

Reporting follows the conclusion, and the vocabulary is the mark. Disclosure adequate — unmodified opinion plus a separate section headed Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern. Disclosure inadequate — qualified or adverse. Going concern basis inappropriate — adverse. Management unwilling to make or extend its assessment — you may be unable to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence.

Exam trap Calling the going concern section an "Emphasis of Matter paragraph"

An adequately disclosed material uncertainty requires a separate section headed "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern", not an Emphasis of Matter paragraph, and it sits with an unmodified opinion. Disclosed uncertainty is not by itself a reason to qualify.

Evaluating misstatements (SA 450)§

The auditor accumulates misstatements other than those clearly trivial, and considers whether the strategy and plan need revision, since a pattern of errors may mean the assessed risk was wrong. Misstatements go to the appropriate level of management with a request to correct them; on refusal he asks why. He then evaluates whether uncorrected misstatements are material individually or in aggregate, considering size, nature and circumstances of occurrence, plus the effect of uncorrected prior-period misstatements. Those go to governance, with a written representation requested on whether management believes their effect is immaterial. He documents the clearly-trivial threshold, the misstatements accumulated and his conclusion.

Written representations (SA 580)§

A written representation is a written statement by management to confirm certain matters or support other audit evidence. It is audit evidence, but never sufficient appropriate evidence on its own, and obtaining it does not reduce the other evidence required.

From whom may they be requested? From management — those with appropriate responsibilities for the statements and knowledge of the matters concerned, ordinarily the chief executive and chief financial officer; also, where appropriate, from those charged with governance, and from others with specialised knowledge of an assertion, such as in-house counsel.

Representations about management's responsibilities are mandatory: that it has fulfilled its responsibility for preparing the statements under the applicable framework, including where relevant fair presentation; that it has provided all relevant information and access agreed in the engagement terms (Unit 11's SA 210); and that all transactions are recorded and reflected. They are dated as near as practicable to, but not after, the date of the report. If the auditor concludes they are unreliable, or management withholds those about its responsibilities, he disclaims an opinion.

Communicating with governance (SA 260 and SA 265)§

Those charged with governance are the person or persons responsible for overseeing the strategic direction of the entity and its obligations of accountability, including overseeing the financial reporting process. SA 260 makes communication two-way and covers the auditor's responsibilities in relation to the audit, the planned scope and timing, and significant findings — qualitative aspects of accounting practices, difficulties encountered, matters discussed with management, representations requested, circumstances affecting the report — plus, for listed entities, a statement on independence.

SA 265 covers deficiencies in internal control — a control designed, implemented or operated so that it fails to prevent, or detect and correct, misstatements on a timely basis, or a necessary control that is missing. A significant deficiency is one that in the auditor's professional judgement merits the attention of those charged with governance; it goes to them in writing and on a timely basis, while other deficiencies worth management's attention go to management at an appropriate level. The writing describes the deficiencies and their potential effects, and adds context: the audit was to express an opinion, internal control was considered only so far as relevant to it, and the matters reported are limited to those found during the audit.

Unit 8 — Audit Report§

Forming an opinion (SA 700)§

The auditor forms an opinion on whether the financial statements are prepared, in all material respects, in accordance with the applicable financial reporting framework. He concludes whether reasonable assurance has been obtained that the statements as a whole are free from material misstatement: was sufficient appropriate evidence obtained, are uncorrected misstatements material individually or in aggregate (Unit 7's SA 450), and qualitatively — whether the accounting policies are disclosed and appropriate, the estimates reasonable and the disclosures adequate. Under a fair presentation framework he also asks whether the statements achieve fair presentation.

An unmodified opinion is that the statements give a true and fair view in accordance with the framework. Do not paraphrase that phrase.

Exam trap "Negative opinion", "clean report with remarks", "subject-to opinion"

Key audit matters (SA 701)§

Key audit matters are those matters that, in the auditor's professional judgement, were of most significance in the audit of the current period's statements. Selection narrows twice: from matters communicated with those charged with governance, to those needing significant auditor attention — higher assessed or significant risks, areas of significant management judgement including estimates with high estimation uncertainty, significant events or transactions — and from those, the matters of most significance. Each goes in a separate Key Audit Matters section, cross-referring to the disclosure and saying why it mattered most and how the audit addressed it. Required for listed entities' complete sets of general purpose financial statements, and where law or the auditor decides — and never a substitute for a modification, a required disclosure, or the going concern section.

Branch audit and joint audit§

Branch audit. Under the Companies Act, 2013 a branch may be audited by the company's auditor or by another person qualified for appointment as the company's auditor. The branch auditor reports on the branch accounts to the company's auditor, who deals with that report as he considers necessary and remains responsible for his own opinion on the statements.

Joint audit. More than one auditor is appointed for the same entity. Work is divided by identifiable units, areas, assets, income or periods, and the division is documented. Each joint auditor is responsible for the work allotted to him; all are jointly and severally responsible for work not divided, decisions taken jointly, requirements applicable to all, and the statements complying with disclosure requirements. A joint auditor may rely on the others' work without reviewing it, and where he disagrees he issues his own report.

Reporting under the Companies Act, 2013, including CARO§

The company auditor's report has two layers: the SA 700 opinion on truth and fairness, and the statutory reporting the Act requires, placed under other reporting responsibilities and kept out of the opinion paragraph — statements on such matters as information and explanations obtained, proper books of account and branch returns, compliance with accounting standards, adverse observations on financial transactions, director disqualification, internal financial controls where that reporting applies, and other prescribed matters.

CARO — the Companies (Auditor's Report) Order — is issued by the Central Government under the Act and requires the auditor of specified classes of companies to report on additional matters. Three things to hold: it is a reporting requirement, not an opinion; it is given as an annexure referred to from the report; and where an answer is unfavourable or qualified the report must state the reasons. Its matters run broadly to property, plant and equipment and title deeds; inventory and working-capital facilities; investments, loans, guarantees and deposits; statutory dues and default in repayment; end use of funds raised; fraud and whistle-blower complaints; related-party and managerial matters; and the ability to meet liabilities.

Government audit§

Government audit is conducted by the C&AG, a constitutional authority independent of the executive. Its content is examined as a list of audits within the audit:

  • Audit against provision of funds — expenditure is covered by a sanctioned grant or appropriation and does not exceed it.
  • Audit of sanctions — expenditure is sanctioned by a competent authority.
  • Audit against rules and orders (regularity or legality audit) — expenditure conforms to the statute, rules and orders.
  • Propriety audit — spending is not more than the occasion demands, no authority sanctions expenditure benefiting itself, and public money is not spent for a particular person unless justified.
  • Performance or efficiency-cum-performance audit — the three Es: economy, efficiency and effectiveness against stated objectives.
  • Audit of receipts and of stores and stock — assessment and collection of revenue follow the law; stores are procured, priced and accounted for properly.

For a government company the C&AG appoints the auditor, may direct the manner of audit, comment upon or supplement his report, and order a supplementary or test audit.

Local bodies§

Municipalities and panchayats spend money raised by taxes, user charges and grants, so the audit is about authority and application, not profit. Check that expenditure has proper sanction and is on the purpose for which the grant was made; that grants are used within their conditions and utilisation certificates are supported; that budget provisions are not exceeded; that taxes, rents and fees are fully assessed, collected and deposited; and that tenders, contracts and stores follow the prescribed procedure.

Not-for-profit organisations, trusts and societies§

Start with the governing instrument and read the objects, because application of funds outside the objects is the point of the audit. Then verify that income is applied to the objects; that corpus and restricted funds are kept distinct from income and used only as the donor directed; donations, grants, subscriptions and legacies against counterfoils, correspondence and bank credits; compliance with grant conditions and utilisation certificates; investments held in the entity's own name within the restrictions of the instrument and statute; and the extra reporting the governing statute and the tax exemption require.

Partnership firms§

There is no general statutory audit of a firm; the audit exists because the partners agreed to it, so the partnership deed and the engagement letter fix the scope. Read the deed for the profit-sharing ratio, capital, interest on capital and drawings, salary or commission to partners, borrowing powers and each partner's authority, then verify that the accounts follow it — the division of profit above all, and the adjustments for admission, retirement or death including goodwill. The value of the audit to the firm is also examinable: settlement of accounts between partners, valuation of goodwill, deterrence of fraud, and credibility with lenders and tax authorities.

Limited liability partnerships and co-operative societies§

An LLP is a body corporate with perpetual succession whose partners have limited liability. Its audit is required only where the prescribed limits of turnover or contribution are crossed, so ask first whether an audit is required at all. The LLP agreement plays the part the partnership deed plays: verify contributions actually brought in, that borrowings and drawings are authorised, the filings made with the Registrar, and the statement of account and solvency signed by the designated partners.

A co-operative society is audited under the co-operative societies law and its own bye-laws, by an auditor drawn from the panel the Registrar maintains. The special features are what get asked:

  • Overdue debts are classified by age, and their effect on profit and on bad-debt provisions considered; overdue interest is excluded from income to the extent the law or bye-laws require.
  • Restrictions apply to a member's shareholding, to loans to non-members and to borrowings, and transactions are tested against them.
  • Appropriation of profit is regulated: a prescribed part goes to the reserve fund before distribution, with prescribed contributions to education or charitable purposes.
  • The auditor reports on the accounts, gives the society an audit classification under the Registrar's scheme, and makes a special report to the Registrar on fraud, personal profiteering by office bearers, mismanagement, or loans given contrary to the rules.

Verify: the audit thresholds for LLPs, and the appointment, reserve-fund appropriation, classification and special-report requirements for co-operative societies and public trusts, are statutory and vary between the central and State laws. Take them from the Study Material and Study Guidelines for your attempt.

Educational institutions, hotels, clubs and hospitals§

These four come as "state the special points in the audit of ...". Tie every income figure to a physical record.

  • Educational institution — fees register against the students on roll, with arrears, remissions and free-studentships authorised by the appropriate authority; admission fees and caution money treated as the institution's rules require; government grants and their utilisation; hostel and mess accounts; scholarships received and disbursed; salaries against the sanctioned strength, with provident fund deducted and deposited; investment of endowments; capital expenditure sanctioned by the governing body.
  • Hotel — occupancy and guest registers reconciled with room-sale summaries and serially numbered bills; tariff and discount authorisation; food and beverage stock control, wastage and pilferage tested through gross-margin analysis; banquet contracts; casual labour records; foreign-currency receipts; and controls over linen, crockery and cutlery.
  • Club — the members' register and categories; entrance fees treated consistently with the club's rules; subscriptions accrued, with arrears and the consequences the rules attach; bar and kitchen stocks and margins; borrowing and asset purchases authorised by the managing committee.
  • Hospital — patient registers for in-patients, out-patients and operations reconciled with bills; rates and any free or concessional treatment authorised under the rules; income from theatre, laboratory, pharmacy and ambulance; consultants' share arrangements; purchase, issue, expiry and custody of medicines, especially controlled drugs; and donations, grants and legacies restricted to their stated purpose.
Remember this Governing document, then who appoints, then the entity-specific verifications, then the extra report. Those four, in that order, are the answer structure the examiner is marking.

Unit 10 — Audit of Banks§

The accounting system in banks§

A bank's financial statements are drawn up in the formats the banking law prescribes, with the prescribed schedules, and are governed by the regulator's directions as well as the applicable accounting framework. Four features shape the audit.

  • Volume and homogeneity. Millions of small transactions of a few standard types, so testing rests on controls and analytics, not on vouching entries.
  • Dispersion. Business originates at branches; the statements are a consolidation of branch returns, which must be reconciled and controlled.
  • Automation. A centralised core banking system captures transactions at the branch and posts the general ledger automatically, computing interest, charges and asset classification. The audit is therefore IT-dependent: test the configuration, the masters and the exception reports.
  • Regulation. Extensive returns to the regulator, and reporting duties beyond the opinion on truth and fairness.

Audit approach§

The bank as a whole is audited by statutory central auditors, with branch auditors for branches selected on a risk basis; a branch auditor reports to the central auditors and proposes adjustments through a memorandum of changes rather than altering the branch accounts himself. The bank also runs concurrent audit, internal inspection and revenue audit; the auditor evaluates that work under Unit 4's SA 610 rather than substituting it for his own procedures.

The approach runs: understand the entity and its IT environment; evaluate the design and operating effectiveness of automated and manual controls, including access and change controls; test the system-generated reports before relying on them; select branches and balances by assessed risk; apply analytical procedures to yields on advances and cost of deposits; verify the consolidation of branch returns and the inter-branch and inter-bank reconciliations; and close with year-end procedures, a subsequent-events review under Unit 7's SA 560 and written representations under SA 580.

Verify: the appointment mechanism for central and branch auditors, the branch-selection norms, and the additional reporting a bank auditor must make — including the long form audit report and the regulator's prescribed certificates — are set by statute and by the regulator and change frequently. Take the current position from the Study Material and Study Guidelines for your attempt.

Audit of revenue items§

Bank income is mostly interest, and the recurring exam theme is income leakage — income the bank was entitled to but never recorded.

  • Interest on advances accrues, except that income on a non-performing asset is recognised only on realisation. Test the rate masters and manual overrides, re-compute interest on a sample, check penal interest actually applied, and verify that interest credited on accounts which turned non-performing has been reversed or not recognised.
  • Interest and dividend on investments — verify against each security's terms, the accrual at the balance sheet date, and that income on non-performing investments follows the same realisation logic.
  • Commission, exchange and brokerage, largely recognised on realisation — verify against the bank's schedule of charges (locker rent, processing fees, guarantee and letter-of-credit commission spread over the instrument's life, remittance charges) and test for charges that should have been levied.
  • Discount on bills is apportioned over the tenor; unexpired discount belongs to the next period.
  • Interest expended on deposits and borrowings — test the rate masters, interest on overdue and matured deposits, and the year-end accrual.
  • Recoveries in non-performing accounts are appropriated between principal and interest per the bank's stated policy, applied consistently.

Advances and non-performing assets§

Advances are the largest asset and the largest source of judgement. The objectives are existence, valuation, recoverability and disclosure, and the procedures follow the loan's lifecycle: sanction within the delegated powers of the authority that gave it; documentation complete and the charge on security created and registered; drawing power computed from current stock and receivable statements; securities insured and valued at the prescribed intervals; the account operated with regular servicing and no persistent excess over limit; a credit file holding the borrower's latest financial statements and inspection reports; and, in consortium accounts, the bank's share established. Balances are confirmed and the year-end position agreed with the returns filed with the regulator.

Classification rests on one idea: an advance performs while it generates income for the bank, and becomes a non-performing asset when interest or principal remains overdue beyond the prescribed period. The test differs by facility — overdue instalments for term loans, an "out of order" test for cash credit and overdraft, a separate test for bills purchased and discounted, and a crop-season measure for agricultural advances. Non-performing assets are graded in ascending order of the age of default and the shortfall in security: sub-standard, doubtful and loss assets.

Provisioning follows classification: a general provision on standard assets; progressively higher provisions as an asset ages from sub-standard through the doubtful categories, computed separately on the secured and unsecured portions; and full provision or write-off for a loss asset. Classification is borrower-wise, not facility-wise.

The auditor's contribution is scepticism about the bank's own classification: fresh loans that service old ones, temporary credits just before the year end, renewals without fresh appraisal, restructuring that only re-dates the default, and stale security values.

Verify: the number of days of overdue that make an asset non-performing, the doubtful sub-categories and their ageing, every provisioning percentage, and the treatment of restructured and security-shortfall cases are prudential norms fixed by the regulator and revised often. Never quote a percentage or a day-count from memory — take them from the Study Material and Study Guidelines for your attempt.

Remember this Bank audit is a controls-and-classification audit, not a vouching audit. Income recognition follows classification, and classification follows the borrower.

Interest on a non-performing asset is recognised only on realisation, so misclassifying one advance misstates both the asset and the income. That link is what makes advances the highest-risk area in a bank audit and the most examined.

Exam trap Quoting provisioning percentages or overdue day-counts in an answer

Prudential norms move, and a wrong percentage converts a good answer into a wrong one. Write the logic — standard against non-performing, sub-standard to doubtful to loss by age of default and security shortfall, provision rising with age and computed separately on the unsecured portion — and say that the rates are those prescribed by the regulator. The second trap in the same answer is treating classification as facility-wise when it is borrower-wise.

Unit 11 — Ethics and Terms of Audit Engagements§

Ethics, and why it is a state of mind§

Ethics is the body of principles about right conduct, owed by a profession beyond the client who pays. The phrase to reproduce is that ethics is a state of mind — an attitude, not a checklist: a member behaves ethically because of what he is, not because a rule caught him.

Why it is needed. A user cannot verify the work, so he relies on the auditor's character; self-regulation lasts only while the public accepts that members discipline themselves.

A rules-based approach sets detailed prohibitions and bright lines: certain and enforceable, but it cannot foresee everything and invites compliance in the letter that defeats the spirit. A principles-based approach states principles and requires the member to identify, evaluate and address threats: it reaches the unanticipated, but demands judgement. The profession's Code is principles-based. Keep "legal" and "ethical" apart — an act may be lawful and still fall below the professional standard.

The five fundamental principles§

  • Integrity — be straightforward and honest in professional relationships.
  • Objectivity — do not let bias, conflict of interest or undue influence override judgement.
  • Professional competence and due care — attain and maintain the skill the work requires, and act diligently under applicable professional standards.
  • Confidentiality — do not disclose information acquired professionally without proper and specific authority, unless there is a legal or professional right or duty to disclose, and never use it for advantage.
  • Professional behaviour — comply with relevant laws and avoid conduct discrediting the profession.

Independence, threats and safeguards§

Independence has two halves. Independence of mind permits an opinion without influences that compromise professional judgement. Independence in appearance is the avoidance of facts and circumstances so significant that a reasonable and informed third party would likely conclude that objectivity or scepticism was compromised.

The five threats are self-interest, self-review, advocacy, familiarity and intimidation; the safeguards come in two classes:

  • From the profession, legislation or regulation — entry requirements of education, training and experience; continuing professional development; monitoring and disciplinary procedures.
  • In the work environment — firm-wide: leadership stressing compliance, quality control and documented independence policies, rotation of senior personnel, a disciplinary mechanism. Engagement-specific: another accountant to review the work, rotating the partner, and discussing the matter with governance.

Where a threat cannot be reduced to an acceptable level, the engagement is refused.

Professional scepticism — a questioning mind, alertness to conditions indicating possible misstatement from error or fraud, and critical assessment of evidence — is objectivity at work, required by SA 200 throughout.

Terms of audit engagements (SA 210)§

The preconditions for an audit, established before acceptance:

  1. an acceptable financial reporting framework, used by management to prepare the statements; and
  2. that management and, where appropriate, those charged with governance agree to the premise on which an audit is conducted — responsibility for preparing the statements under that framework, including where relevant fair presentation; for such internal control as enables statements free from material misstatement whether from fraud or error; and for giving the auditor all relevant information, anything further he requests, and unrestricted access to the people from whom he needs evidence.

If they are absent, he discusses the matter with management and does not accept unless law requires it — the same following where a limitation on scope imposed before acceptance would lead to a disclaimer.

The agreed terms go into an audit engagement letter or other written agreement covering the objective and scope of the audit, the auditor's and management's responsibilities, the applicable reporting framework, and the expected form and content of his reports, with a statement that circumstances may make a report differ from it.

On a recurring audit he assesses whether the terms need revision and whether to remind the entity of the existing ones. A fresh letter is indicated by any sign that the entity misunderstands the objective and scope of the audit, revised or special terms, a change of senior management or ownership, a significant change in the business, or a change in law or the framework.

Asked to change the terms to a review or a related service, he does not agree without reasonable justification; if he cannot agree and may not continue, he withdraws where the law allows and considers whether to report the circumstances.

Quality control (SQC 1 and SA 220)§

SQC 1 works at the level of the firm: a system of quality control giving reasonable assurance that the firm and its personnel comply with professional standards and legal requirements and that reports issued are appropriate. Its elements are leadership responsibilities for quality, ethical requirements, acceptance and continuance of clients and engagements, human resources, engagement performance and monitoring.

It also sets the engagement-documentation rules SA 230 leaves to it: the final audit file is assembled on a timely basis after the report date, nothing is deleted thereafter, and it is retained for the prescribed minimum period.

Verify: the assembly time limit and the minimum retention period are prescribed figures — take both from the Study Material for your attempt.

SA 220 carries that to the individual audit: the engagement partner is responsible for overall quality, the team's ethical compliance including independence, acceptance and continuance, and engagement performance — direction, supervision and review, consultation, an engagement quality control review where required, and differences of opinion.

Remember this Independence is tested twice — in mind and in appearance. Name the threat, name the safeguard, state the conclusion.

"The auditor should be independent", without naming the threat the facts create, loses the mark.

Exam trap Treating the engagement letter as a formality, or independence as a question of fact only

SA 210 makes the letter the record of an agreement, and the preconditions must exist before it is signed — no letter cures an unacceptable framework or a refusal of the premise. And independence in appearance fails on how a reasonable third party would see it, however independent the auditor is in fact.

  • Familiarity — long association, or a close family member employed there.
  • Intimidation — threatened dismissal, replacement or litigation, or pressure to cut the work.

Where safeguards cannot reduce a threat to an acceptable level, the auditor declines or discontinues the engagement — threats without that consequence answer half the question.

The opinion decision, cold. Misstatement, material but not pervasive — qualified; misstatement, pervasive — adverse; no evidence, material but not pervasive — qualified; no evidence, pervasive — disclaimer. A disclosed material uncertainty on going concern is an unmodified opinion with a Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern section, never an Emphasis of Matter paragraph. "Negative opinion" does not exist.

Ethics, cold. Principles: integrity, objectivity, professional competence and due care, confidentiality, professional behaviour. Threats: self-interest, self-review, advocacy, familiarity, intimidation. Independence in mind and in appearance; safeguards from the profession and the work environment; no safeguard, no engagement.
