## Unit 5 — Audit of Items of Financial Statements

ICAI states that the list of items in this unit is **illustrative only** — so the examiner can name any
line item, and memorising procedures item by item is the wrong preparation. Learn the **approach**, then
apply it to whatever line the question names.

### The repeatable approach

```arch
   1. WHAT IS THE ITEM?        nature, how it arises, framework requirement
                 |
   2. WHICH ASSERTIONS ARE AT RISK?     and in which direction
                 |
   3. WHAT PROCESS PRODUCED THE NUMBER? controls -> rely or not
                 |
   4. CHOOSE THE MIX            tests of controls + tests of details
                                + substantive analytical procedures
                 |
   5. DISCLOSURE + CONCLUDE     presentation per the framework, then document
```

Step 2 is where the marks are, because the **direction of risk** decides the procedure:

| Type of item | Usually misstated by | Assertion that drives the work |
|---|---|---|
| Income and assets | **Over**statement | Occurrence, existence, valuation, rights |
| Expenses and liabilities | **Under**statement | **Completeness**, cut-off |

A completeness risk cannot be tested from inside the ledger: vouching recorded entries confirms only that
what is recorded is genuine, so to find what is missing you work from outside — dispatch documents, goods
received notes, post-year-end payments, third-party statements.

Step 4's toolkit, worth naming in any answer: **vouch** to the source document; **confirm** with the third
party; **physically verify**; **recompute**; test **cut-off** either side of the year end; review
**subsequent** transactions; check **presentation and disclosure** against the applicable framework.

> **Verify:** what each line item must disclose — ageing, related party amounts, revaluation, dues to
> micro and small enterprises — comes from the applicable financial reporting framework and the statutory
> format, both of which change. Confirm the disclosure requirements for your attempt.

:::key Answer any "how will you audit X" question in the order assertion → procedure → disclosure, never as an unstructured list of checks.
Two lines of assertions at risk before the procedures turns a list into an audit answer, because it
shows *why* each procedure is done. Close with disclosure — presentation is an assertion too.
:::

### Income

| Item | Assertion at risk and the procedures |
|---|---|
| Sale of products and services | Occurrence and **cut-off**. Test controls over dispatch and invoicing; agree the register with the ledger and the returns filed; vouch invoices to dispatch documents; test cut-off across the year end; examine post-year-end credit notes; analytical procedures on margins and price per unit; check the recognition policy |
| Interest income | Accuracy. Recompute on the principal outstanding at the agreed rate; agree counterparty statements and tax withholding certificates; check accrual for the unexpired period |
| Rental income | Completeness. Read the agreements for rate, period and escalation; recompute from a rent roll; trace receipts; check vacant periods |
| Dividend income | Occurrence and cut-off. Verify the holdings statement and bank credit or dividend advice; check recognition when the right to receive is established |
| Net gain or loss on sale of investments | Accuracy and classification. Agree contract notes; recompute against carrying amount; verify the presentation the framework requires |

### Expenses

| Item | Assertion at risk and the procedures |
|---|---|
| Purchases | **Completeness** and cut-off. Test three-way matching; search for unrecorded liabilities; test goods received but not invoiced; analytical review of consumption |
| Employee benefits expense | Completeness and accuracy. Reconcile payroll with headcount and statutory returns; recompute a sample; verify statutory dues; check the valuation supporting long-term benefits |
| Depreciation | Accuracy. Verify method and useful lives against the policy and statute; recompute on additions and disposals for the part of the year held |
| Interest expense | Completeness. Recompute on each borrowing from the agreement; agree lender statements; check accrual of unpaid interest; verify any permitted capitalisation |
| Power and fuel | Accuracy. Agree meter readings and supplier bills; test units consumed against production |
| Rent | Occurrence. Read the agreement for rate and escalation; check approvals and any related party element |
| Repairs to building; repairs to machinery | **Classification** — capital against revenue. Scrutinise large items for anything enhancing capacity or extending useful life, and check it was capitalised |
| Insurance | Cut-off. Verify the policy period, recompute the prepaid portion, check the assets covered |
| Taxes (rates and duties) | Completeness. Verify demands, challans and returns; check whether disputed amounts are provided or disclosed |
| Travelling expenses | Occurrence. Test against policy and approvals; look for a personal element and for directors' travel disclosure |
| Miscellaneous expenses | Classification. Scrutinise for large or unusual items and anything belonging under a named head; this ledger is where misclassification hides |

### Equity and liabilities

| Item | Assertion at risk and the procedures |
|---|---|
| Share capital | Rights and disclosure. Verify authorised capital in the constitutional documents; check resolutions, allotment records and statutory registers; verify issued, subscribed and paid-up capital and its movement |
| Reserves and surplus | Disclosure. Prepare a movement schedule; verify appropriations and any restriction on utilisation |
| Long-term borrowings | Completeness and classification. Read the agreements; confirm balances with lenders; verify security and charge; recompute the current and non-current split; check covenants |
| Trade payables | **Completeness**. Search for unrecorded liabilities; obtain and reconcile supplier statements; review post-year-end payments; examine debit and long-outstanding balances; verify ageing disclosure |
| Provisions | Valuation. Verify the basis of estimation and its reasonableness; test the movement; consider management bias; check adequacy against subsequent events |
| Short-term borrowings and other current liabilities | Completeness. Obtain bank confirmations; agree stock statements and drawing power; verify statutory dues, customer advances and unpaid expenses |

### Assets

| Item | Assertion at risk and the procedures |
|---|---|
| Land and buildings | Existence, rights and valuation. Inspect title deeds and the name they stand in; verify any charge; physically verify; check the basis of any revaluation |
| Plant and equipment; furniture and fixtures; office equipment | Existence and valuation. Verify additions to invoices and installation records and the date of capitalisation; test the physical verification programme and discrepancies; verify disposals and the gain or loss |
| Vehicles | Rights. Check the registration certificate is in the entity's name; verify insurance and hypothecation |
| Goodwill | Existence. Verify that it arose from **acquisition** — internally generated goodwill is not recognised — and test impairment as the framework requires |
| Brand and trademarks; computer software | Valuation. Verify registration or licence, the capitalisation criteria, the amortisation charge and impairment |
| Loans and advances | **Recoverability**. Read the agreements; obtain balance confirmations; test interest accrual; assess recoverability and any statutory restriction; check related party loans |
| Trade receivables | Existence and valuation. Send **external confirmations**; test post-year-end receipts; review ageing and later credit notes; evaluate the provision for doubtful debts |
| Inventories | Existence and valuation. Attend the count and perform test counts; test cut-off of receipts and dispatches; verify valuation at the lower of cost and net realisable value; identify slow-moving and obsolete stock; confirm third-party stock |
| Cash and cash equivalents | Existence. Count cash on hand; obtain bank confirmations and examine reconciliations for long-outstanding cheques; verify deposit maturities and restricted balances |
| Other current assets | Recoverability. Examine the nature of each item, its support, and whether it will be realised or expensed |

### Contingent liabilities

A contingent liability is disclosed rather than provided, so the risk is **completeness of
disclosure** and the audit is a search rather than a verification. Inquire of management and read the
minutes of the board and of those charged with governance; review legal correspondence and the legal
expense ledger; examine the registers of guarantees and letters of credit and the bank confirmations
revealing them; examine statutory demands and appeals; consider events after the year end; and obtain
written representations. Then check the classification the framework requires — provide, disclose or
treat as remote — and the adequacy of the note.

:::trap Provisioning what should be disclosed, or disclosing what should be provided
The distinction is the probability and measurability of the outflow, not the size of the amount. A
probable obligation that can be reliably estimated is a **provision**; a possible obligation, or one
that cannot be reliably measured, is a **contingent liability** in the notes; a remote one needs
neither. The auditor's role is to evaluate management's classification, not to certify the outcome of
the dispute.
:::

:::think You are asked to audit an item the Study Material never mentions. What are your first two sentences?
Name the assertions at risk and the direction of the risk. Everything else follows from them, and the
examiner cannot mark you down for a procedure that is logically tied to a correctly identified
assertion.
:::

:::incident The repair that built a mezzanine
@symptom: A manufacturing client's "repairs to building" ledger is ₹64 lakh against ₹11 lakh last
year. Performance materiality is ₹18 lakh.
@signal: Three invoices account for ₹41 lakh — two from a civil contractor for "structural work and
flooring", one from a fabricator for steel joists. The plant manager mentions that the stores area now
has an upper level. There is no addition to buildings in the fixed asset register and depreciation is
unchanged.
@ask: What is the misstatement, which assertions are affected, and what do you do?
@diagnosis: This is **classification** between capital and revenue expenditure. Expenditure creating a
new structure, or enhancing capacity or extending useful life, is capital; only expenditure keeping the
asset in its existing condition is a repair. Charging ₹41 lakh to revenue understates buildings and
overstates expenses, so the assertions affected are **classification and accuracy** of the expense,
**existence and valuation** of property, plant and equipment, and the accuracy of depreciation.

The procedures: read the work order and bill of quantities; inspect the mezzanine; obtain the drawings or
completion certificate; check whether capital expenditure approval was taken; scrutinise the rest of the
ledger for items of the same character; recompute depreciation from the date the asset was ready for use.
Then ask management to capitalise it and correct depreciation; on refusal the misstatement is accumulated
with others and evaluated for its effect on the opinion at the completion and reporting units, and
communicated to those charged with governance. Note what surfaced it — a **substantive analytical
procedure** on that head, not the vouching of small bills.
:::

:::attempt How would you audit "sale of products and services" and "trade payables"? Answer both in the assertion-first format.
For **sale of products and services** open with occurrence and cut-off as the assertions at risk and
overstatement as the direction, then run the procedures in transaction order — controls over order,
dispatch, invoicing and credit approval; agreement of the register with the ledger and the returns filed;
vouching invoices to dispatch documents; cut-off testing across the year end; post-year-end credit notes,
where sales pushed into this period surface; substantive analytical procedures on gross margin and average
realisation; related party sales; the recognition policy and disclosure.

For **trade payables** open with completeness, understatement as the direction, and the reason the
procedures differ: an omitted liability cannot be found by examining recorded ones. Then the search for
unrecorded liabilities through post-year-end invoices and payments and goods received but not invoiced;
supplier statements reconciled; debit and long-outstanding balances explained; purchase cut-off; related
party amounts; the ageing and classification disclosures.
:::
