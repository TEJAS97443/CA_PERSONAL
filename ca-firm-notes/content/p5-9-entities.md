## Unit 9 — Special Features of Audit of Different Type of Entities

Every entity here is audited under the same Standards on Auditing. Four things change, and every answer
runs in this order: the **governing document**, who **appoints**, the **entity-specific verifications**,
and the **extra report** the statute demands beyond the opinion.

| Entity | Governing document | Appointed by | Extra report |
|---|---|---|---|
| Government company | Companies Act, 2013 and government rules | Comptroller and Auditor General of India (C&AG) | C&AG comments on or supplements it |
| Local body | State municipal or panchayat law | As that law provides | To the prescribed authority |
| Trust or society | Trust deed, or memorandum, rules and bye-laws | Trustees or the managing committee | As the trust or societies law requires |
| Not-for-profit organisation | Governing instrument and tax-exemption conditions | Governing body | Grant utilisation certificates |
| Partnership firm | Partnership deed and engagement letter | Agreement of the partners | None beyond the partners |
| Limited liability partnership | LLP agreement and the LLP law | Designated partners | Statement of account and solvency |
| Co-operative society | Co-operative societies law and bye-laws | From the Registrar's panel | Special report to the Registrar; audit classification |

### Government audit

Government audit is conducted by the **C&AG**, a constitutional authority independent of the executive.
Its content is examined as a list of audits within the audit:

- **Audit against provision of funds** — expenditure is covered by a sanctioned grant or
  appropriation and does not exceed it.
- **Audit of sanctions** — expenditure is sanctioned by a competent authority.
- **Audit against rules and orders** (regularity or legality audit) — expenditure conforms to the
  statute, rules and orders.
- **Propriety audit** — spending is not more than the occasion demands, no authority sanctions expenditure
  benefiting itself, and public money is not spent for a particular person unless justified.
- **Performance or efficiency-cum-performance audit** — the three Es: **economy, efficiency and
  effectiveness** against stated objectives.
- **Audit of receipts** and of **stores and stock** — assessment and collection of revenue follow the
  law; stores are procured, priced and accounted for properly.

For a **government company** the C&AG appoints the auditor, may direct the manner of audit, comment upon or
supplement his report, and order a supplementary or test audit.

### Local bodies

Municipalities and panchayats spend money raised by taxes, user charges and grants, so the audit is about
authority and application, not profit. Check that expenditure has proper sanction and is on the purpose for
which the grant was made; that grants are used within their conditions and utilisation certificates are
supported; that budget provisions are not exceeded; that taxes, rents and fees are fully assessed, collected
and deposited; and that tenders, contracts and stores follow the prescribed procedure.

### Not-for-profit organisations, trusts and societies

Start with the governing instrument and read the **objects**, because application of funds outside the objects
is the point of the audit. Then verify that income is applied to the objects; that **corpus and restricted
funds are kept distinct from income** and used only as the donor directed; donations, grants, subscriptions
and legacies against counterfoils, correspondence and bank credits; compliance with grant conditions and
utilisation certificates; investments held in the entity's own name within the restrictions of the instrument
and statute; and the extra reporting the governing statute and the tax exemption require.

### Partnership firms

There is no general statutory audit of a firm; the audit exists because the partners agreed to it, so the
**partnership deed and the engagement letter fix the scope**. Read the deed for the profit-sharing ratio,
capital, interest on capital and drawings, salary or commission to partners, borrowing powers and each
partner's authority, then verify that the accounts follow it — the division of profit above all, and the
adjustments for admission, retirement or death including goodwill. The value of the audit to the firm is also
examinable: settlement of accounts between partners, valuation of goodwill, deterrence of fraud, and
credibility with lenders and tax authorities.

### Limited liability partnerships and co-operative societies

An **LLP** is a body corporate with perpetual succession whose partners have limited liability. Its audit is
required only where the prescribed limits of turnover or contribution are crossed, so ask first whether an
audit is required at all. The **LLP agreement** plays the part the partnership deed plays: verify
contributions actually brought in, that borrowings and drawings are authorised, the filings made with the
Registrar, and the statement of account and solvency signed by the designated partners.

A **co-operative society** is audited under the co-operative societies law and its own bye-laws, by
an auditor drawn from the panel the Registrar maintains. The special features are what get asked:

- **Overdue debts** are classified by age, and their effect on profit and on bad-debt provisions considered;
  **overdue interest** is excluded from income to the extent the law or bye-laws require.
- **Restrictions** apply to a member's shareholding, to loans to non-members and to borrowings, and
  transactions are tested against them.
- **Appropriation of profit** is regulated: a prescribed part goes to the reserve fund before
  distribution, with prescribed contributions to education or charitable purposes.
- The auditor reports on the accounts, gives the society an **audit classification** under the
  Registrar's scheme, and makes a **special report to the Registrar** on fraud, personal profiteering
  by office bearers, mismanagement, or loans given contrary to the rules.

> **Verify:** the audit thresholds for LLPs, and the appointment, reserve-fund appropriation,
> classification and special-report requirements for co-operative societies and public trusts, are
> statutory and vary between the central and State laws. Take them from the Study Material and
> Study Guidelines for your attempt.

### Educational institutions, hotels, clubs and hospitals

These four come as "state the special points in the audit of ...". Tie every income figure to a physical
record.

- **Educational institution** — fees register against the students on roll, with arrears, remissions and
  free-studentships authorised by the appropriate authority; admission fees and caution money treated as the
  institution's rules require; government grants and their utilisation; hostel and mess accounts;
  scholarships received and disbursed; salaries against the sanctioned strength, with provident fund
  deducted and deposited; investment of endowments; capital expenditure sanctioned by the governing body.
- **Hotel** — occupancy and guest registers reconciled with room-sale summaries and serially numbered bills;
  tariff and discount authorisation; food and beverage stock control, wastage and pilferage tested through
  gross-margin analysis; banquet contracts; casual labour records; foreign-currency receipts; and controls
  over linen, crockery and cutlery.
- **Club** — the members' register and categories; entrance fees treated consistently with the club's rules;
  subscriptions accrued, with arrears and the consequences the rules attach; bar and kitchen stocks and
  margins; borrowing and asset purchases authorised by the managing committee.
- **Hospital** — patient registers for in-patients, out-patients and operations reconciled with bills; rates
  and any free or concessional treatment authorised under the rules; income from theatre, laboratory,
  pharmacy and ambulance; consultants' share arrangements; purchase, issue, expiry and custody of medicines,
  especially controlled drugs; and donations, grants and legacies restricted to their stated purpose.

:::key Governing document, then who appoints, then the entity-specific verifications, then the extra report. Those four, in that order, are the answer structure the examiner is marking.
A partnership audit that never mentions the deed, a trust audit silent on the objects, or a co-operative
audit silent on overdue debts and the Registrar's report loses marks however long it is.
:::

:::trap Writing "the auditor is appointed by the members" for every entity
Appointment differs, and the examiner uses it to separate prepared candidates. A government company's auditor
is appointed by the **C&AG**; a co-operative society's from the **Registrar's panel**; a firm's by
**agreement of the partners**; an LLP's by the **designated partners**; a trust's by the **trustees**.
Equally, propriety and performance audit belong to government audit — do not import "the three Es" into a
hotel answer.
:::

:::think Why is propriety audit peculiar to government audit rather than a company audit?
Because a company auditor reports on truth and fairness, not on whether spending was wise. Propriety audit
asks whether public money was spent with due regard to economy and the public interest — a question only the
custodian of public funds is entitled to press.
:::

:::attempt A charitable trust running a school received a ₹40 lakh grant for a new laboratory block and a large anonymous donation "towards the corpus". State the audit points.
Deal with the two receipts separately, because their character differs.

**The grant** is a restricted receipt. Read the sanction letter for its conditions, verify the receipt against
the bank credit, check that expenditure charged to it is on the laboratory block and within the sanction,
confirm that unspent amounts are carried as a liability or restricted fund rather than taken to income, verify
the utilisation certificate, and check the disclosure of the grant and the asset it funded.

**The corpus donation** is not income to be spent. Verify the donor's instruction or the trustees'
resolution recording it; confirm it is credited to the corpus and not to the income and expenditure
account, invested in the modes the instrument and statute permit, and that only the income from it is
applied to the objects. For an anonymous donation, check what the governing statute and the
tax-exemption conditions require by way of donor records.

Across both, confirm application towards the stated **objects**, examine the trustees' minutes for authority,
and consider the extra reporting the State public trust law requires. The mark usually missed is the **corpus
and income** distinction.
:::
