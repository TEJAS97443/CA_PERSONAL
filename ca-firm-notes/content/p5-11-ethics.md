## Unit 11 — Ethics and Terms of Audit Engagements

### Ethics, and why it is a state of mind

Ethics is the body of principles about right conduct, owed by a profession beyond the client who pays. The
phrase to reproduce is that **ethics is a state of mind** — an attitude, not a checklist: a member behaves
ethically because of what he is, not because a rule caught him.

**Why it is needed.** A user cannot verify the work, so he relies on the auditor's character; self-regulation
lasts only while the public accepts that members discipline themselves.

A **rules-based** approach sets detailed prohibitions and bright lines: certain and enforceable, but it cannot
foresee everything and invites compliance in the letter that defeats the spirit. A **principles-based**
approach states principles and requires the member to identify, evaluate and address threats: it reaches the
unanticipated, but demands judgement. The profession's Code is principles-based. Keep "legal" and "ethical"
apart — an act may be lawful and still fall below the professional standard.

### The five fundamental principles

- **Integrity** — be straightforward and honest in professional relationships.
- **Objectivity** — do not let bias, conflict of interest or undue influence override judgement.
- **Professional competence and due care** — attain and maintain the skill the work requires, and act
  diligently under applicable professional standards.
- **Confidentiality** — do not disclose information acquired professionally without proper and specific
  authority, unless there is a legal or professional right or duty to disclose, and never use it for advantage.
- **Professional behaviour** — comply with relevant laws and avoid conduct discrediting the profession.

### Independence, threats and safeguards

Independence has two halves. **Independence of mind** permits an opinion without influences that compromise
professional judgement. **Independence in appearance** is the avoidance of facts and circumstances so significant that a reasonable and
informed third party would likely conclude that objectivity or scepticism was compromised.

The five **threats** are self-interest, self-review, advocacy, familiarity and intimidation; the **safeguards**
come in two classes:

- **From the profession, legislation or regulation** — entry requirements of education, training and experience;
  continuing professional development; monitoring and disciplinary procedures.
- **In the work environment** — firm-wide: leadership stressing compliance, quality control and documented
  independence policies, rotation of senior personnel, a disciplinary mechanism. Engagement-specific: another
  accountant to review the work, rotating the partner, and discussing the matter with governance.

Where a threat cannot be reduced to an acceptable level, the engagement is refused.

**Professional scepticism** — a questioning mind, alertness to conditions indicating possible misstatement from
error or fraud, and critical assessment of evidence — is objectivity at work, required by SA 200 throughout.

### Terms of audit engagements (SA 210)

The **preconditions for an audit**, established before acceptance:

1. an **acceptable financial reporting framework**, used by management to prepare the statements; and
2. that management and, where appropriate, those charged with governance **agree to the premise** on which an
   audit is conducted — responsibility for preparing the statements under that framework, including where
   relevant fair presentation; for such internal control as enables statements free from material misstatement
   whether from fraud or error; and for giving the auditor all relevant information, anything further he
   requests, and unrestricted access to the people from whom he needs evidence.

If they are absent, he discusses the matter with management and does not accept unless law requires it — the
same following where a limitation on scope imposed **before acceptance** would lead to a disclaimer.

The agreed terms go into an **audit engagement letter** or other written agreement covering the objective and
scope of the audit, the auditor's and management's responsibilities, the applicable reporting framework, and
the expected form and content of his reports, with a statement that circumstances may make a report differ
from it.

On a **recurring audit** he assesses whether the terms need revision and whether to remind the entity of the
existing ones. A fresh letter is indicated by any sign that the entity misunderstands the objective and scope
of the audit, revised or special terms, a change of senior management or ownership, a significant change in the
business, or a change in law or the framework.

Asked to **change the terms** to a review or a related service, he does not agree without reasonable
justification; if he cannot agree and may not continue, he withdraws where the law allows and considers whether
to report the circumstances.

### Quality control (SQC 1 and SA 220)

**SQC 1** works at the level of the **firm**: a system of quality control giving reasonable assurance that the
firm and its personnel comply with professional standards and legal requirements and that reports issued are
appropriate. Its elements are leadership responsibilities for quality, ethical
requirements, acceptance and continuance of clients and engagements, human resources, engagement performance
and monitoring.

It also sets the engagement-documentation rules SA 230 leaves to it: the **final audit file** is assembled on a
timely basis after the report date, nothing is deleted thereafter, and it is **retained** for the prescribed
minimum period.

> **Verify:** the assembly time limit and the minimum retention period are prescribed figures — take both from
> the Study Material for your attempt.

**SA 220** carries that to the **individual audit**: the engagement partner is responsible for overall quality,
the team's ethical compliance including independence, acceptance and continuance, and engagement performance —
direction, supervision and review, consultation, an engagement quality control review where required, and
differences of opinion.

:::key Independence is tested twice — in mind and in appearance. Name the threat, name the safeguard, state the conclusion.
"The auditor should be independent", without naming the threat the facts create, loses the mark.
:::

:::trap Treating the engagement letter as a formality, or independence as a question of fact only
SA 210 makes the letter the record of an **agreement**, and the preconditions must exist before it is signed —
no letter cures an unacceptable framework or a refusal of the premise. And independence **in appearance**
fails on how a reasonable third party would see it, however independent the auditor is in fact.
:::

:::think Why is "no rule was broken" not an answer to an ethics question?
Because ethics is a state of mind: the threat must still be identified and addressed even where no rule names
it.
:::

:::ladder Your firm has audited a client for eleven years, the engagement partner's daughter has just joined it as a finance executive, and the client offers the firm a systems-implementation assignment. Advise.
@weak: The firm should resign because eleven years is too long and a relative works there.
@good: Three threats arise — familiarity from the long association and the daughter's employment, and
self-review from implementing a system the firm would later audit. Rotate the engagement partner and refuse the
systems work if self-review cannot be reduced.
@great: Take the facts one at a time, naming threat and safeguard. **Long association** creates a **familiarity** threat, and self-interest if fees are concentrated: rotate the
engagement partner and senior personnel. **The daughter in a finance role** creates a **familiarity** threat
and, where she can influence the subject matter, self-interest; if she can influence the records, removing the
partner from the engagement — not disclosing the relationship — is the safeguard.

**The systems implementation** creates a **self-review** threat, since the firm would audit the output of a
system it designed. If no safeguard reduces that to an acceptable level for an audit client, **decline the
assignment**.
:::

:::attempt State the five threats to auditor independence with one example each, and say what the auditor does when no safeguard is sufficient.
- **Self-interest** — a financial interest in the client, or dependence on its fees.
- **Self-review** — auditing figures the firm prepared, or a system it built.
- **Advocacy** — promoting the client's shares or acting for it in a dispute.
- **Familiarity** — long association, or a close family member employed there.
- **Intimidation** — threatened dismissal, replacement or litigation, or pressure to cut the work.

Where safeguards cannot reduce a threat to an acceptable level, the auditor **declines or discontinues** the
engagement — threats without that consequence answer half the question.
:::

**The opinion decision, cold.** Misstatement, material but not pervasive — qualified; misstatement, pervasive —
adverse; no evidence, material but not pervasive — qualified; no evidence, pervasive — disclaimer. A disclosed
material uncertainty on going concern is an **unmodified** opinion with a Material Uncertainty Related to Going
Concern section, never an Emphasis of Matter paragraph. "Negative opinion" does not exist.

**Ethics, cold.** Principles: integrity, objectivity, professional competence and due care, confidentiality,
professional behaviour. Threats: self-interest, self-review, advocacy, familiarity, intimidation. Independence
in mind and in appearance; safeguards from the profession and the work environment; no safeguard, no
engagement.

**Entity-specific points.** Governing document, appointment, verifications, extra report — the deed for a firm;
objects and the corpus-income distinction for trusts; overdue debts and the Registrar's report for
co-operatives; propriety and the three Es for government audit; and, for banks, classification, income on
non-performing assets only on realisation, and evergreening.

**Verify before the exam.** CARO's reporting matters and the companies covered; statutory reporting under the
Companies Act, 2013; bank provisioning percentages and overdue day-counts; the file-assembly and retention
figures; LLP audit thresholds and co-operative reserve-fund rules; the Code of Ethics; and the inclusions and
exclusions in the **Study Guidelines for your attempt** — these notes do not replace them.
