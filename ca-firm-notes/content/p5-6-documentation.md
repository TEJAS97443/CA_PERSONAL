## Unit 6 — Audit Documentation

This unit is short and it is scoring, because the answers are definitional and the examiner can ask
them in three marks. SA 230 governs it.

### Concept, and the three definitions

**Audit documentation** is the record of the audit procedures performed, the relevant audit evidence
obtained, and the conclusions the auditor reached. "Working papers" and "work papers" are the same
thing.

An **audit file** is one or more folders or other storage media, in physical or electronic form,
containing the records that comprise the audit documentation for a specific engagement.

An **experienced auditor** is an individual, whether internal or external to the firm, having
practical audit experience and a reasonable understanding of audit processes, the Standards on
Auditing and applicable legal and regulatory requirements, the business environment in which the
entity operates, and the auditing and financial reporting issues relevant to the entity's industry.
That definition matters because the whole test of adequacy is written in terms of this person.

### Nature and purpose

Documentation serves two purposes stated by the Standard: it provides **evidence of the auditor's
basis for a conclusion** about the achievement of the overall objectives of the auditor, and
**evidence that the audit was planned and performed in accordance with the SAs** and applicable legal
and regulatory requirements.

Beyond those, it serves several additional purposes: assisting the engagement team to plan and perform
the audit; assisting those responsible for supervision to **direct, supervise and review** the work;
enabling the team to be **accountable** for its work; retaining a record of matters of **continuing
significance** to future audits; enabling **quality control reviews and inspections**; and enabling
external inspections required by law, regulation or otherwise.

### Form, content and extent

The test is a single sentence worth reproducing verbatim in substance: documentation must be
sufficient to enable an **experienced auditor, having no previous connection with the audit**, to
understand

1. the **nature, timing and extent** of the audit procedures performed to comply with the SAs and
   applicable legal and regulatory requirements;
2. the **results** of those procedures and the **audit evidence obtained**; and
3. **significant matters** arising during the audit, the **conclusions** reached on them, and the
   **significant professional judgements** made in reaching those conclusions.

In documenting the nature, timing and extent of the procedures performed, the auditor records the
**identifying characteristics** of the specific items or matters tested — which invoice numbers, which
date range, which selection basis — together with **who performed the work and when it was completed**,
and **who reviewed it, when, and the extent of the review**.

:::key The test of adequacy is an experienced auditor with no previous connection to the audit, and the three "who and when" records are where the easy marks are.
Quote the test as the Standard frames it — nature, timing and extent of procedures; results and evidence
obtained; significant matters, conclusions and significant professional judgements — then add identifying
characteristics, preparer and date, reviewer and extent of review.
:::

The **extent** varies with the size and complexity of the entity, the nature of the procedures, the
identified risks of material misstatement, the significance of the evidence obtained, the nature and
extent of exceptions identified, the need to record a conclusion not readily determinable from the
work documented, and the audit methodology and tools used.

Two limits are examined. It is **not necessary** to include superseded drafts, notes reflecting
incomplete or preliminary thinking, previous copies of documents corrected for typographical errors,
or duplicates. And **oral explanations by the auditor do not, on their own, represent adequate
support** for the work performed or the conclusions reached, although they may be used to explain or
clarify information the documentation contains.

Where the auditor judges it necessary to **depart** from a relevant requirement of a SA, the file
records how the alternative procedures achieve the aim of that requirement, and the **reasons** for
the departure.

:::trap Writing that documentation exists "to prove the auditor did the work"
That is half the purpose and it reads as defensive. The Standard's two purposes are evidence of the
**basis for the auditor's conclusion** and evidence that the audit was **planned and performed in
accordance with the SAs** and applicable requirements — and the practical test is what an experienced
auditor with no previous connection to the audit could understand from the file. Add the three "who
and when" items — identifying characteristics, preparer and date, reviewer and extent of review —
because that is where the easy marks sit.
:::

### Completion memorandum

A **completion memorandum**, also called an audit completion document, is a summary prepared by the
auditor describing the **significant matters** identified during the audit and how each was addressed,
or cross-referring to the supporting documentation that does so. It is not a substitute for the file:
it makes an effective and efficient **review and inspection** possible, particularly on large and
complex audits, and it helps the auditor consider the significant matters in the round when forming
the opinion.

### Assembly, ownership and custody

The auditor **assembles the final audit file on a timely basis after the date of the auditor's
report**. After assembly is complete, documentation is **not deleted or discarded** before the end of
its retention period. If it becomes necessary to modify existing documentation or add new
documentation after assembly, the auditor records the **specific reasons** for the change and **when
and by whom** it was made and reviewed — and the same applies to procedures performed after the date
of the report, where the file also records the circumstances encountered, the evidence obtained, the
conclusions reached and their effect on the auditor's report.

**Ownership and custody** is a favourite short question, so state it in three parts. Unless law or
regulation provides otherwise, **audit documentation is the property of the auditor**, not of the
client. The auditor **may, at his discretion, make portions of or extracts from the documentation
available to the client**, provided the disclosure does not undermine the validity of the work
performed or, in the case of an audit, the independence of the auditor or of his personnel. And the
auditor must adopt appropriate procedures for **confidentiality, safe custody, integrity,
accessibility and retrievability**, and for retention.

:::attempt State the purposes of audit documentation and the test for its adequacy, and explain what is recorded about the items tested.
Give the two purposes stated by SA 230 first — evidence of the auditor's basis for a conclusion about
the achievement of the overall objectives, and evidence that the audit was planned and performed in
accordance with the SAs and applicable legal and regulatory requirements — then add the additional
purposes: planning and performing the audit, direction, supervision and review, accountability of the
team, a record of matters of continuing significance to future audits, quality control reviews and

For the test, use the Standard's own construction: documentation sufficient to enable an experienced
auditor with no previous connection with the audit to understand the nature, timing and extent of the
procedures performed, their results and the evidence obtained, and the significant matters,
conclusions and significant professional judgements. Define "experienced auditor" if the question
gives you room, because it is a defined term.

On the items tested, three records earn the marks: the **identifying characteristics** of the items or
matters tested, **who performed the work and the date it was completed**, and **who reviewed it, the
date and the extent of the review**. Finish with the point most answers miss — oral explanations alone
are not adequate support, though they may clarify what the documentation contains.
:::
