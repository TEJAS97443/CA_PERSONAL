## Unit 10 — Audit of Banks

### The accounting system in banks

A bank's financial statements are drawn up in the formats the banking law prescribes, with the
prescribed schedules, and are governed by the regulator's directions as well as the applicable
accounting framework. Four features shape the audit.

- **Volume and homogeneity.** Millions of small transactions of a few standard types, so testing rests
  on controls and analytics, not on vouching entries.
- **Dispersion.** Business originates at branches; the statements are a **consolidation of branch
  returns**, which must be reconciled and controlled.
- **Automation.** A centralised core banking system captures transactions at the branch and posts the
  general ledger automatically, computing interest, charges and asset classification. The audit is
  therefore **IT-dependent**: test the configuration, the masters and the exception reports.
- **Regulation.** Extensive returns to the regulator, and reporting duties beyond the opinion on truth
  and fairness.

### Audit approach

The bank as a whole is audited by **statutory central auditors**, with **branch auditors** for branches
selected on a risk basis; a branch auditor reports to the central auditors and proposes adjustments
through a **memorandum of changes** rather than altering the branch accounts himself. The bank also runs
**concurrent audit**, internal inspection and revenue audit; the auditor evaluates that work under Unit
4's SA 610 rather than substituting it for his own procedures.

The approach runs: understand the entity and its IT environment; evaluate the design and operating
effectiveness of automated and manual controls, including access and change controls; test the
system-generated reports **before** relying on them; select branches and balances by assessed risk;
apply analytical procedures to yields on advances and cost of deposits; verify the consolidation of
branch returns and the inter-branch and inter-bank reconciliations; and close with year-end procedures,
a subsequent-events review under Unit 7's SA 560 and written representations under SA 580.

> **Verify:** the appointment mechanism for central and branch auditors, the branch-selection norms,
> and the additional reporting a bank auditor must make — including the long form audit report and
> the regulator's prescribed certificates — are set by statute and by the regulator and change
> frequently. Take the current position from the Study Material and Study Guidelines for your
> attempt.

### Audit of revenue items

Bank income is mostly interest, and the recurring exam theme is **income leakage** — income the bank
was entitled to but never recorded.

- **Interest on advances** accrues, except that income on a non-performing asset is recognised only
  **on realisation**. Test the rate masters and manual overrides, re-compute interest on a sample, check
  penal interest actually applied, and verify that interest credited on accounts which turned
  non-performing has been **reversed or not recognised**.
- **Interest and dividend on investments** — verify against each security's terms, the accrual at the
  balance sheet date, and that income on non-performing investments follows the same realisation logic.
- **Commission, exchange and brokerage**, largely recognised on realisation — verify against the bank's
  schedule of charges (locker rent, processing fees, guarantee and letter-of-credit commission spread
  over the instrument's life, remittance charges) and test for charges that should have been levied.
- **Discount on bills** is apportioned over the tenor; unexpired discount belongs to the next period.
- **Interest expended** on deposits and borrowings — test the rate masters, interest on overdue and
  matured deposits, and the year-end accrual.
- **Recoveries in non-performing accounts** are appropriated between principal and interest per the
  bank's stated policy, applied consistently.

### Advances and non-performing assets

Advances are the largest asset and the largest source of judgement. The objectives are **existence,
valuation, recoverability and disclosure**, and the procedures follow the loan's lifecycle: sanction
within the delegated powers of the authority that gave it; documentation complete and the charge on
security created and registered; drawing power computed from current stock and receivable statements;
securities insured and valued at the prescribed intervals; the account operated with regular servicing
and no persistent excess over limit; a credit file holding the borrower's latest financial statements
and inspection reports; and, in consortium accounts, the bank's share established. Balances are
confirmed and the year-end position agreed with the returns filed with the regulator.

**Classification** rests on one idea: an advance performs while it generates income for the bank, and
becomes a **non-performing asset** when interest or principal remains overdue beyond the prescribed
period. The test differs by facility — overdue instalments for term loans, an "out of order" test for
cash credit and overdraft, a separate test for bills purchased and discounted, and a crop-season measure
for agricultural advances. Non-performing assets are graded in ascending order of the age of default and
the shortfall in security: **sub-standard**, **doubtful** and **loss** assets.

**Provisioning** follows classification: a general provision on standard assets; progressively higher
provisions as an asset ages from sub-standard through the doubtful categories, computed separately on
the secured and unsecured portions; and full provision or write-off for a loss asset. Classification is
**borrower-wise, not facility-wise**.

The auditor's contribution is scepticism about the bank's own classification: fresh loans that service
old ones, temporary credits just before the year end, renewals without fresh appraisal, restructuring
that only re-dates the default, and stale security values.

> **Verify:** the number of days of overdue that make an asset non-performing, the doubtful
> sub-categories and their ageing, every provisioning percentage, and the treatment of restructured
> and security-shortfall cases are prudential norms fixed by the regulator and revised often. Never
> quote a percentage or a day-count from memory — take them from the Study Material and Study
> Guidelines for your attempt.

:::key Bank audit is a controls-and-classification audit, not a vouching audit. Income recognition follows classification, and classification follows the borrower.
Interest on a non-performing asset is recognised only on realisation, so misclassifying one advance
misstates both the asset and the income. That link is what makes advances the highest-risk area in a
bank audit and the most examined.
:::

:::trap Quoting provisioning percentages or overdue day-counts in an answer
Prudential norms move, and a wrong percentage converts a good answer into a wrong one. Write the
**logic** — standard against non-performing, sub-standard to doubtful to loss by age of default and
security shortfall, provision rising with age and computed separately on the unsecured portion — and
say that the rates are those prescribed by the regulator. The second trap in the same answer is
treating classification as facility-wise when it is borrower-wise.
:::

:::think Why does an auditor test the bank's exception reports before relying on them?
Because in a core banking environment the report *is* the audit evidence. If the query behind it is
wrong or its parameters have been changed, a clean report proves nothing — so its completeness and
accuracy have to be established first, like any other system-generated information.
:::

:::attempt A cash credit account stayed within its limit all year, but the stock statements stopped in October and interest was serviced from a fresh temporary overdraft sanctioned in March. Set out your audit response.
Do not stop at "the account is within limit" — that is the comfort the arrangement was built to create.

**Facts to establish.** When the last stock and receivable statement was received and what drawing power
it supported; whether drawings after October exceeded that drawing power; who sanctioned the March
overdraft, under what powers and against what security and appraisal; and whether the credits servicing
interest came from the borrower's own receipts or from that overdraft.

**Assessment.** Servicing interest out of a fresh facility from the same bank is **evergreening** — the
account looks regular only because the bank funded its own interest. Without current stock statements
drawing power was never established, so the conduct of the account is not evidence of health. Read the
credit file, the borrower's latest financial statements and inspection reports, and look for temporary
credits reversed soon after the year end.

**Conclusion and reporting.** Evaluate whether the account should be non-performing despite its apparent
regularity, and whether the classification, the provision and the treatment of interest already credited
are correct. Put the finding in the memorandum of changes so it reaches the consolidated position, report
the control weakness over drawing-power monitoring under Unit 7's SA 265, and consider the effect on your
opinion if the bank will not reclassify and the amount is material.
:::
