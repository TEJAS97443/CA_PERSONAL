## Unit 7 — Completion and Review

Six Standards close an audit, in five loops: SA 560, SA 570, SA 450, SA 580, and SA 260 with SA 265.

### Subsequent events (SA 560)

**Subsequent events** are events between the date of the financial statements and the date of the
auditor's report, and facts becoming known to him after that date. The obligation differs by window;
one paragraph covering all three loses half the marks.

| Situation | Duty to search | If the fact matters |
|---|---|---|
| Between the date of the statements and the date of the report | **Active duty** — procedures to identify every event needing adjustment or disclosure | Ask management to adjust or disclose; on refusal, modify the opinion under SA 705 |
| After the date of the report, before the statements are issued | **No obligation** to perform procedures | Discuss with management and governance; if amended, perform procedures on the amendment, extend them to the new approval date and give a new or amended report, or dual-date it. If not amended, the response turns on whether the report has reached the entity — see the closing gate |
| After the statements have been issued | **No obligation** to perform procedures | As above; if amended, also review how management informed earlier recipients, and report with an Emphasis of Matter or Other Matter paragraph referring to the note on the amendment. If management will not act, notify governance that you will seek to prevent future reliance |

**Procedures in the first window:** inquire of management; understand how it identifies such events; read post-year-end minutes of owners, governance and the board and its committees; read the
latest subsequent interim financial statements; obtain written representations that all such events
are adjusted or disclosed.

### Going concern (SA 570)

Management assesses; the auditor evaluates. His **objectives** are to conclude on the
appropriateness of management's use of the going concern basis of accounting, whether a **material
uncertainty** exists related to events or conditions that may cast significant doubt on the entity's
ability to continue as a going concern, and to report on it.

**Events or conditions that may cast doubt** — group them; an ungrouped list reads as guessing.

- **Financial** — net liability or net current liability position; fixed-term borrowings maturing
  with no realistic prospect of renewal; short-term borrowing funding long-term assets; withdrawal of
  creditor support; negative operating cash flows; adverse key ratios; inability to pay creditors or
  to comply with loan terms.
- **Operating** — intention to liquidate or cease operations; loss of key management, of a major
  market, key customer, franchise, licence or principal supplier; labour difficulties; shortages of
  important supplies; a highly successful new competitor.
- **Other** — non-compliance with capital or statutory requirements; pending proceedings producing
  claims the entity cannot satisfy; adverse changes in law; uninsured catastrophes.

**Procedures once identified:** ask management to make its assessment if it has not; evaluate its plans;
where a cash flow forecast is significant, test the underlying data and support for its assumptions;
consider facts since the assessment; take written representations on the plans and their feasibility.

Reporting follows the conclusion, and the vocabulary is the mark. Disclosure **adequate** — unmodified
opinion plus a separate section headed **Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern**. Disclosure **inadequate** — qualified or adverse. Going concern basis
**inappropriate** — adverse. Management **unwilling to make or extend its assessment** — you may be
unable to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence.

:::trap Calling the going concern section an "Emphasis of Matter paragraph"
An adequately disclosed material uncertainty requires a **separate section headed "Material
Uncertainty Related to Going Concern"**, not an Emphasis of Matter paragraph, and it sits with an
**unmodified** opinion. Disclosed uncertainty is not by itself a reason to qualify.
:::

:::incident The order book that vanished in April
@symptom: An auto-components company. At the year end current liabilities exceed current assets. In
April its largest customer, over half of revenue, terminated supply. A term loan matures in four
months; the bank has not committed to renewal.
@signal: Note 32 describes both facts, says management is negotiating with two prospective customers
and with the bank, and calls the outcome uncertain. Its forecast assumes both succeed.
@ask: Identify the conditions, state your further procedures, and say what your report contains if
you accept management's basis.
@diagnosis: The conditions stack: net current liability position, borrowings maturing without
realistic prospect of renewal, loss of a key customer. It is also a first-window SA 560 event, so you
had an active duty to find it.

Procedures: test the replacement plan for substance rather than intention — signed orders, capacity
to serve them; trace the forecast to the records and challenge its assumptions; get the bank's
correspondence; read post-year-end minutes; take written representations on the plans.

Report: Note 32 adequate — **unmodified opinion** with a **Material Uncertainty Related to Going
Concern** section cross-referring to it. Note 32 silent or misleading — **qualified or adverse**, on
pervasiveness. Plans with no substance at all — the basis is inappropriate and the opinion
**adverse**.
:::

### Evaluating misstatements (SA 450)

The auditor accumulates misstatements other than those **clearly trivial**, and considers whether
the strategy and plan need revision, since a pattern of errors may mean the assessed risk was wrong.
Misstatements go to the appropriate level of management with a request to correct them; on refusal he
asks why. He then evaluates whether **uncorrected** misstatements are material
individually or in aggregate, considering size, nature and circumstances of occurrence, plus the
effect of uncorrected prior-period misstatements. Those go to governance, with a written
representation requested on whether management believes their effect is immaterial. He documents the
clearly-trivial threshold, the misstatements accumulated and his conclusion.

### Written representations (SA 580)

A **written representation** is a written statement by management to confirm certain matters or
support other audit evidence. It is audit evidence, but never sufficient appropriate evidence on its
own, and obtaining it does not reduce the other evidence required.

**From whom may they be requested?** From management — those with appropriate responsibilities for
the statements and knowledge of the matters concerned, ordinarily the chief executive and chief
financial officer; also, where appropriate, from those charged with governance, and from others with
specialised knowledge of an assertion, such as in-house counsel.

**Representations about management's responsibilities** are mandatory: that it has fulfilled its
responsibility for preparing the statements under the applicable framework, including where relevant
fair presentation; that it has provided all relevant information and access agreed in the engagement
terms (Unit 11's SA 210); and that all transactions are recorded and reflected. They are dated as
near as practicable to, but not after, the date of the report. If the auditor concludes they are
unreliable, or management withholds those about its responsibilities, he **disclaims an opinion**.

### Communicating with governance (SA 260 and SA 265)

**Those charged with governance** are the person or persons responsible for overseeing the strategic
direction of the entity and its obligations of accountability, including overseeing the financial
reporting process. SA 260 makes communication two-way and covers the auditor's responsibilities in
relation to the audit, the planned scope and timing, and significant findings — qualitative aspects
of accounting practices, difficulties encountered, matters discussed with management, representations
requested, circumstances affecting the report — plus, for listed entities, a statement on
independence.

SA 265 covers **deficiencies in internal control** — a control designed, implemented or operated so
that it fails to prevent, or detect and correct, misstatements on a timely basis, or a necessary
control that is missing. A **significant deficiency** is one that in the auditor's professional
judgement merits the attention of those charged with governance; it goes to them **in writing** and on
a timely basis, while other deficiencies worth management's attention go to management at an
appropriate level. The writing describes the deficiencies and their potential effects, and adds
context: the audit was to express an opinion, internal control was considered only so far as relevant
to it, and the matters reported are limited to those found during the audit.

:::key Completion is where evidence becomes an opinion — six Standards closing five loops the auditor cannot sign over.
SA 560 closes the timeline, SA 570 the assumption under the framework, SA 450 the arithmetic of the errors
found, SA 580 management's acknowledgement of its responsibilities, and SA 260 with SA 265 the line to
governance. A problem set "just before signing" tests one of those.
:::

Say first that you had **no obligation to perform audit procedures** after the date of your report —
this is the second SA 560 situation — so the only question is what you do now. Then discuss with

- **Management amends.** Perform procedures on the amendment; extend subsequent-event procedures to

- **Management does not amend.** Modify the opinion under SA 705 if the report is not yet with the
