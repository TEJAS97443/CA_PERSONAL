## Unit 3 — Risk Assessment and Internal Control

SA 315 is how the auditor **identifies and assesses** the risks of material misstatement, SA 320 fixes
the **materiality** against which "material" is measured, and SA 330 is the **response**.

### Audit risk, decomposed

**Audit risk** is the risk that the auditor expresses an inappropriate audit opinion when the financial
statements are materially misstated. It is a function of the **risks of material misstatement** and
**detection risk**.

```board
                          AUDIT RISK
        +---------------------+---------------------+
  RISK OF MATERIAL MISSTATEMENT              DETECTION RISK
  (the entity's risk; exists                (the auditor's own risk;
   before and without the audit)             controlled by the audit)
   +----+----+                              +-------+-------+
INHERENT  CONTROL                       SAMPLING       NON-SAMPLING
  RISK      RISK                          RISK             RISK
```

| Component | Definition — reproduce these words |
|---|---|
| **Inherent risk** | The susceptibility of an assertion to misstatement that could be material, **before consideration of any related controls** |
| **Control risk** | The risk that internal control will not **prevent, or detect and correct, on a timely basis** a misstatement that could be material |
| **Detection risk** | The risk that the auditor's procedures will not detect an existing misstatement that could be material |

Inherent and control risk together are the **risk of material misstatement**; they exist independently of
the audit, so the auditor assesses them but cannot change them. Detection risk is the only component he
controls, by altering the **nature, timing and extent** of procedures — inversely, so the higher the
assessed risk of material misstatement, the more persuasive the evidence required.

### Sampling and non-sampling risk

**Sampling risk** is the risk that a conclusion based on a sample differs from the conclusion had the
whole population been tested. Concluding that controls are more effective, or a material misstatement
absent, when the opposite is true damages **audit effectiveness** and can produce an inappropriate
opinion; the converse damages **efficiency**. **Non-sampling risk** is the risk of an erroneous
conclusion for any reason unrelated to sampling — an inappropriate procedure, misinterpreted evidence, an
unnoticed misstatement.

:::trap Saying non-sampling risk falls if the sample size rises
Only sampling risk responds to sample size. Non-sampling risk is reduced by proper **planning, direction,
supervision and review** and by competent staff — never by selecting more items.
:::

### Materiality in planning and performing an audit (SA 320)

Misstatements, including omissions, are **material** if they, individually or in the aggregate, could
reasonably be expected to influence the economic decisions of users taken on the basis of the financial
statements. Judgements are affected by the **size or the nature** of a misstatement or both, and rest on
the **common financial information needs of users as a group** rather than any individual user.
Materiality is applied in **planning and performing** the audit, and again in **evaluating** identified
and uncorrected misstatements at the completion unit. **The auditor's determination of materiality is a
matter of professional judgement** — a stated syllabus point, and why there is no formula.

**Benchmarks.** A percentage is often applied to a chosen benchmark: profit before tax, total revenue,
gross profit, total expenses, total equity or net asset value. The choice turns on which elements users
focus on, the entity's nature, life cycle and industry, its financing structure, and the **relative
volatility** of the benchmark. Profit before tax from continuing operations is common; where volatile,
gross profit or total revenue serves better.

> **Verify:** the syllabus prescribes no percentage. Any figure applied to a benchmark comes from the
> firm's methodology and the Study Material's illustrations.

Where misstatements **below** overall materiality could still influence users' decisions on a particular
class of transactions, account balance or disclosure, materiality is set for that item too — related
party transactions, remuneration of management and those charged with governance, statutory disclosures.

**Performance materiality** is the amount, or amounts, set at **less than materiality for the financial
statements as a whole** to reduce to an appropriately low level the probability that the aggregate of
**uncorrected and undetected** misstatements exceeds that materiality — and, where applicable, at less
than the level set for a particular class. Setting it is professional judgement, affected by the
understanding of the entity and by misstatements found in previous audits.

**Materiality and audit risk** move inversely: lower materiality demands more extensive procedures.
Materiality set at planning does **not** establish an amount below which uncorrected misstatements are
always immaterial. The auditor **revises** it, and reconsiders performance materiality and the planned
procedures, on learning something that would have changed the original determination — a decision to
dispose of a major part of the business, or **actual results differing significantly from the anticipated
period-end results**. **Documenting materiality** covers overall materiality, the levels for particular
classes if any, performance materiality, and any revision.

:::key Overall materiality, materiality for particular classes and performance materiality are three separate figures, and all of them plus any revision are documented.
The mark is lost by treating performance materiality as a synonym for materiality or as a percentage
rule. It is a **lower** amount absorbing the aggregate of undetected and uncorrected misstatements.
:::

### Identifying and assessing the risk of material misstatement (SA 315)

The objective is to identify and assess the risks of material misstatement, whether due to fraud or
error, at the **financial statement level** and the **assertion level**, through understanding the entity
and its environment including internal control — the basis for responses under SA 330.

**Risk assessment procedures** are inquiries of management and others including the internal audit
function, analytical procedures, and observation and inspection. **Understanding the entity** covers
industry and other external factors including the applicable financial reporting framework; the nature of
the entity — operations, ownership and governance, investments, structure and financing; its accounting
policies; its objectives, strategies and related business risks; and the review of its financial
performance.

**Internal control** is the process designed, implemented and maintained by those charged with
governance, management and other personnel to provide reasonable assurance about achieving the entity's
objectives regarding **reliability of financial reporting, effectiveness and efficiency of operations,
and compliance with applicable laws and regulations**. Its five components are the **control
environment**, the entity's **risk assessment process**, the **information system** and communication,
**control activities** relevant to the audit, and **monitoring of controls**.

For controls relevant to the audit the auditor evaluates their **design** and whether they have been
**implemented**, which needs procedures **beyond inquiry** — observation, inspection, walking a
transaction through the system. Evaluation is recorded through a narrative record, an internal control
questionnaire, a check list or a flow chart, and whether he then **tests controls** depends on whether he
intends to rely on their operating effectiveness. Internal control has **inherent limitations**: human
error, collusion, management override, cost against benefit, faulty judgement, and poor fit with
non-routine transactions. **Documenting the risks** covers the engagement team discussion, the
understanding obtained with its sources, and the assessed risks at both levels with the related controls.

### Digital audit

**Key features of an automated environment**: high volumes of routine transactions processed
consistently, automated calculations, system-generated entries and reports, configurable logic,
interfaces, and an electronic **audit trail**. An automated control either works for every transaction or
fails for every one.

**IT-related risks**: systems that process data inaccurately or process inaccurate data; unauthorised
access causing loss of data or improper change; privileged access permitting management override;
unauthorised changes to master data or programs; failure to make necessary changes; inappropriate manual
intervention.

**Types of controls**: by nature, **manual, automated and IT-dependent manual**; by purpose,
**preventive, detective and corrective**; by level, **general IT controls** over access, change
management, program development and IT operations, and **application controls** such as input validation,
edit and limit checks, automated reconciliations and exception reports.

**Internal Financial Controls as per regulatory requirements**: the Companies Act, 2013 places
responsibility on the board for internal financial controls and requires the auditor to report on their
adequacy and operating effectiveness for the companies covered. Internal Financial Control against
Internal Control over Financial Reporting is distinguished at the audit evidence unit.

> **Verify:** that reporting requirement and its applicability come from the Companies Act, 2013, rules
> under it and ICAI guidance.

**Audit approach**: document the automated environment — infrastructure, applications, databases,
interfaces and the systems in scope — using system notes, flow charts and questionnaires and an IT
specialist where needed; identify the relevant controls; assess the IT risks; test the controls; report
the findings. **Testing methods** are inquiry, observation, inspection and reperformance, inquiry alone
never sufficing. **Data analytics** tests the whole population: completeness of extracted data,
recomputation, journal entries with unusual users, dates or amounts, duplicate payments, ageing and
exception reports. On **findings**, judge each deficiency's severity and its effect on planned
substantive procedures, then communicate deficiencies to management and **those charged with
governance**.

### The auditor's responses to assessed risks (SA 330)

The objective is to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence about the assessed risks of material
misstatement by designing and implementing appropriate responses. **Overall responses at the financial
statement level**: emphasising professional skepticism, assigning more experienced staff or specialists,
more supervision, additional **unpredictability** in selecting procedures, and general changes to the
nature, timing or extent of procedures.

Two rules govern **further audit procedures at the assertion level**. **Tests of controls** are performed
when the risk assessment includes an expectation that controls are **operating effectively** — the
auditor intending to rely on them — or when substantive procedures alone cannot provide sufficient
appropriate audit evidence. **Irrespective of the assessed risks**, substantive procedures are performed
for each **material** class of transactions, account balance and disclosure.
