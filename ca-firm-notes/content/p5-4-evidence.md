## Unit 4 — Audit Evidence

The phrase that carries the marks is **sufficient appropriate audit evidence**: "sufficient" is
quantity, "appropriate" is quality, meaning **relevance and reliability**.

### Audit evidence, sufficiency and appropriateness (SA 500)

**Audit evidence** is the information used by the auditor in arriving at the conclusions on which the
audit opinion is based, including the information in the accounting records underlying the financial
statements and other information. The quantity needed is affected by the assessed **risks of material
misstatement** and by the **quality** of what is obtained; volume never compensates for weakness.

**Reliability** rests on generalisations, each a mark in a "state the factors" question: **independent
external sources** beat internally generated evidence, which is better where the related **internal
control is effective**; evidence obtained **directly by the auditor** beats evidence obtained
indirectly; **documentary** beats oral; **originals** beat photocopies; and evidence is more persuasive
when items from **different sources or of a different nature** agree.

**Relevance** is the logical connection between the procedure and the **assertion** tested, which is why
direction of testing decides everything. Tracing recorded sales to dispatch documents tests
**occurrence** and says nothing about **completeness**; to test completeness you start outside the
ledger, from dispatch documents into the sales record.

:::key Sufficient is quantity, appropriate is quality — and appropriateness is relevance plus reliability.
The generalisations are rebuttable, not rules: a confirmation is unreliable if the auditor does not
control the request. Write "ordinarily" and give the reason.
:::

### Assertions

**Assertions** are the representations of management, explicit or otherwise, embodied in the financial
statements, by which the auditor considers the types of potential misstatement.

| Group | Assertions |
|---|---|
| Transactions and events | Occurrence, completeness, accuracy, cut-off, classification |
| Balances at the period end | Existence, rights and obligations, completeness, valuation and allocation |
| Presentation and disclosure | Occurrence and rights and obligations, completeness, classification and understandability, accuracy and valuation |

> **Verify:** the revised risk-assessment Standard regroups these and folds disclosures into the
> transaction and balance categories. Use your attempt's grouping; never mix two versions.

### Sources, procedures, and the two kinds of testing

Sources include the accounting records and supporting documents, minutes, third-party confirmations,
information from a previous audit and an expert's work; the **absence** of information is itself
evidence. The **procedures for obtaining audit evidence** are inspection, observation, external
confirmation, recalculation, reperformance, analytical procedures and inquiry, and inquiry alone is
almost never sufficient. A **test of controls** evaluates the **operating effectiveness** of controls at
the assertion level; a **substantive procedure** is designed to **detect material misstatements** at the
assertion level and comprises **tests of details** and **substantive analytical procedures**. On
**evaluation**, inconsistent evidence or doubt about reliability obliges the auditor to modify or add
procedures, and information **produced by the entity** must itself be tested for accuracy and
completeness.

:::trap Calling a walk-through or an inspection of a control document a "test of controls"
Evaluating whether a control is well **designed** and has been **implemented** is risk assessment. A test
of controls tests whether it **operated effectively throughout the period of intended reliance**. Using
either phrase for the other costs the mark.
:::

### Audit trail

An **audit trail** lets a transaction be traced forward from source document through the books to the
financial statements, and backward again. Manually it is the documents and cross-references; in an
automated environment it is the system log — user, date and time, values before and after a change. Its
absence is a control deficiency, because occurrence and completeness then cannot be tested.

> **Verify:** rules under the Companies Act, 2013 require accounting software to carry an audit trail or
> edit log. Confirm the requirement and its applicability for your attempt.

### Internal Financial Control and Internal Control over Financial Reporting

**Internal Financial Control** is the wider concept: policies and procedures for the orderly and
efficient conduct of business, adherence to company policies, safeguarding of assets, prevention and
detection of frauds and errors, accuracy and completeness of the accounting records, and timely
preparation of reliable financial information. **Internal Control over Financial Reporting** is the
**subset** directed at reliability of financial reporting. The board carries the responsibility; the
auditor reports **with reference to financial statements** for the companies covered.

> **Verify:** that reporting requirement and its applicability come from the Companies Act, 2013, rules
> under it and ICAI guidance.

### Audit sampling (SA 530)

**Audit sampling** applies audit procedures to less than 100% of the items in a population of audit
relevance such that **all sampling units have a chance of selection**, giving a reasonable basis for a
conclusion about the whole population. **Designing** it means considering the purpose of the procedure
and the characteristics of the population, and setting a **sample size** that reduces sampling risk to an
acceptably low level. Both **approaches** are acceptable — **statistical**, using random selection and
probability theory to measure sampling risk, and **non-statistical**. Sample size rises with the assessed
risk and expected misstatement and falls as tolerable misstatement rises, tolerable misstatement being
**performance materiality** applied to that procedure. **Selection methods** are random, systematic,
monetary unit, haphazard and block selection; haphazard selection is inappropriate with statistical
sampling and **block selection ordinarily cannot be used**. The **nature and cause** of each deviation
and misstatement matters, not merely the amount.

### External confirmations (SA 505)

An **external confirmation** is audit evidence obtained as a **direct written response to the auditor
from a third party**. A **positive** request asks the party to reply whether or not it agrees; a
**negative** request asks for a reply **only if it disagrees**, and is **less persuasive** because silence
is ambiguous.

**Negative confirmations** may be the *sole* substantive procedure only if **all four** conditions hold:
the risk of material misstatement is assessed as low and evidence has been obtained about the operating
effectiveness of relevant controls; the population is a large number of small, homogeneous balances; a
**very low exception rate** is expected; and the auditor knows of no circumstances that would cause
recipients to disregard the requests. The auditor **maintains control** over the requests, including that
replies come **directly to the auditor**.

**Management's refusal to allow the auditor to send a confirmation request** has a four-step answer:
inquire into the **reasons** and seek evidence of their validity and reasonableness; evaluate the
implications for the assessed risks of material misstatement, **including the risk of fraud**; perform
**alternative procedures**; and where the refusal is unreasonable or those procedures fail, communicate
with **those charged with governance** and determine the implications for the opinion.

### Opening balances in an initial audit engagement (SA 510)

The objective is evidence about whether **opening balances** contain misstatements materially affecting
the current period, and whether the **accounting policies** reflected in them have been consistently
applied or changes properly accounted for and disclosed. Read the most recent financial statements and
the predecessor auditor's report, check that closing balances were correctly **brought forward** or
restated, and either **review the predecessor's working papers** or perform specific procedures.
**Reporting on opening balances** is what candidates omit: unable to obtain sufficient appropriate
evidence — **qualified opinion or disclaimer**; a material misstatement not properly accounted for or
disclosed — **qualified or adverse**; policies not consistently applied — **qualified or adverse**; the
prior period's opinion modified and the matter still relevant — **modify this period's opinion**.

### Four more Standards this unit carries

| Standard | What to be able to state |
|---|---|
| **SA 610** Using the work of internal auditors | The **internal audit function** performs assurance and consulting activities to evaluate and improve the effectiveness of governance, risk management and internal control. The external auditor has **sole responsibility for the audit opinion**, undiminished by using its work, which may be used only if its **organisational status** supports **objectivity**, it has adequate **competence**, and it applies a **systematic and disciplined approach**. Extent depends on the specific work, the assessed risks and the degree of **subjectivity**, with **reperformance of some of the work used** |
| **SA 501** Specific considerations for selected items | **Inventory**: attend the physical counting unless impracticable, evaluating instructions, observing, inspecting and performing **test counts**, then test the final records against the counts; failing attendance use **alternative procedures**, and failing those modify the opinion; third-party inventory needs confirmation or inspection. **Litigation and claims**: inquire of management and in-house counsel, read minutes, correspondence with external legal counsel and legal expense accounts, and seek **direct communication** with that counsel where a risk exists. **Segment information**: understand management's methods, evaluate them against the framework, and perform analytical procedures |
| **SA 550** Related parties | A **related party** is one so defined by the applicable framework or, where the framework has minimal requirements, a person or entity with **control or significant influence**, one under the reporting entity's control or influence, or one under **common control**. Risk is higher because relationships are complex, systems may not identify them and transactions may **not be on normal market terms**. Inquire about identities, relationships and the purpose of transactions, and about the **controls** over identifying, disclosing and authorising them and significant transactions **outside the normal course of business**; stay **alert** while inspecting records and minutes |
| **SA 520** Analytical procedures | Evaluations of financial information through analysis of plausible relationships among financial and non-financial data, including **investigation** of fluctuations inconsistent with other information. Used as risk assessment procedures, as **substantive analytical procedures**, and near the end of the audit for the overall conclusion. Designing one means judging suitability for the assertions, evaluating the **reliability of the data** behind the expectation, making the expectation **sufficiently precise** to identify a material misstatement, and fixing the acceptable **difference**. Investigating results means inquiring of management **and** obtaining evidence relevant to those responses |

:::think Why is management's explanation not, by itself, the answer to an unexpected variance?
Because SA 520 requires audit evidence **relevant to management's responses**, not a record of the
response. Inquiry alone is not sufficient appropriate audit evidence.
:::

:::ladder The auditor plans to rely on the client's internal audit function for testing purchase controls.
@weak: He can rely on the internal auditor's report, since internal audit has tested those controls.
@good: He must first evaluate the function's objectivity through its organisational status and policies,
its competence, and its systematic and disciplined approach; if satisfied he may use the work and must
perform procedures on it.
@great: The three criteria as **conditions precedent** — fail one and the work cannot be used at all —
then the extent, weighing the specific work, the assessed risks and the degree of subjectivity, so the
more judgement and the higher the risk the more he does himself. Add **reperformance of some of the work
used**, then the sentence earning the last mark: the external auditor has **sole responsibility for the
audit opinion**.
:::
