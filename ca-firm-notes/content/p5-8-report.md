## Unit 8 — Audit Report

### Forming an opinion (SA 700)

The auditor forms an opinion on whether the financial statements are prepared, in all material
respects, in accordance with the applicable financial reporting framework. He concludes whether
**reasonable assurance** has been obtained that the statements as a whole are free from material
misstatement: was sufficient appropriate evidence obtained, are uncorrected misstatements material
individually or in aggregate (Unit 7's SA 450), and qualitatively — whether the accounting policies are
disclosed and appropriate, the estimates reasonable and the disclosures adequate. Under a fair presentation
framework he also asks whether the statements **achieve fair presentation**.

An unmodified opinion is that the statements give a **true and fair view** in accordance with the
framework. Do not paraphrase that phrase.

### Basic elements of the report

```text
INDEPENDENT AUDITOR'S REPORT
  Addressee (e.g. To the Members of ... Limited)

  Opinion                       - entity audited, each statement by its title, the notes
                                  including significant accounting policies, period covered,
                                  and the opinion itself
  Basis for Opinion             - audit conducted in accordance with SAs; cross-reference to
                                  the auditor's responsibilities section; independence and
                                  other ethical responsibilities; evidence sufficient and
                                  appropriate
  Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern      (where applicable - SA 570)
  Key Audit Matters             (where applicable - SA 701)
  Other Information             (where applicable)
  Responsibilities of Management and Those Charged with Governance for the
    Financial Statements        - preparation, internal control, going concern assessment,
                                  oversight of the financial reporting process
  Auditor's Responsibilities for the Audit of the Financial Statements
                                - reasonable assurance is high but not absolute; materiality;
                                  professional judgement and scepticism; risks; internal
                                  control; estimates; going concern; presentation;
                                  communication with those charged with governance
  Other Reporting Responsibilities   (statutory reporting, kept out of the opinion)
  Signature of the auditor / Place of signature / Date of the report
```

The **date** cannot precede the date on which sufficient appropriate evidence was obtained, including that
the statements were approved by those with recognised authority.

### Which opinion? The decision that carries the marks

Modify when either the statements as a whole **are not free from material misstatement**, or the auditor is
**unable to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence**. How far the effect reaches decides which
modification.

**Pervasive** effects are those that, in the auditor's judgement, are not confined to specific elements,
accounts or items; or, if confined, represent a substantial proportion of the statements; or, for
disclosures, are fundamental to understanding them.

| Nature of the matter | Material but **not pervasive** | Material **and pervasive** |
|---|---|---|
| Financial statements are materially misstated | **Qualified opinion** | **Adverse opinion** |
| Inability to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence | **Qualified opinion** | **Disclaimer of opinion** |

The opinion section is then headed "Qualified Opinion", "Adverse Opinion" or "Disclaimer of Opinion", and the
basis section becomes "Basis for Qualified Opinion" and so on, describing the matter and quantifying its
effects where practicable. On a **disclaimer** he states that he does not express an opinion, cuts his
responsibilities description back, and gives no Key Audit Matters section unless law requires it. The
expected modification and its wording go to those charged with governance.

A **management-imposed scope limitation** is challenged first: ask for removal, tell those charged with
governance, try alternatives — then qualify, or if pervasive withdraw where legally permitted, or disclaim.

:::trap "Negative opinion", "clean report with remarks", "subject-to opinion"
None exists. The four names are **unmodified**, **qualified**, **adverse** and **disclaimer of opinion**;
the umbrella for the last three is **modified opinion**. Right reasoning under an invented label scores
nothing.
:::

### Key audit matters (SA 701)

**Key audit matters** are those matters that, in the auditor's professional judgement, were of most
significance in the audit of the current period's statements. Selection narrows twice: from matters
communicated with those charged with governance, to those needing significant auditor attention — higher
assessed or significant risks, areas of significant management judgement including estimates with high
estimation uncertainty, significant events or transactions — and from those, the matters of
most significance. Each goes in a separate **Key Audit Matters** section, cross-referring to the disclosure
and saying why it mattered most and how the audit addressed it. Required for listed entities' complete sets
of general purpose financial statements, and where law or the auditor decides — and never a substitute for a
modification, a required disclosure, or the going concern section.

### Emphasis of Matter and Other Matter paragraphs (SA 706)

| | Emphasis of Matter | Other Matter |
|---|---|---|
| Refers to | A matter **appropriately presented or disclosed** in the statements | A matter **not** presented or disclosed in them |
| Used when | It is fundamental to users' understanding of the statements | It is relevant to understanding the audit, the auditor's responsibilities or his report |

Both sit in a separate section under an appropriate heading. An Emphasis of Matter paragraph is used only
where the matter is shown free of material misstatement and is not a key audit matter, and it states that
the opinion is **not modified** in respect of it.

### Comparative information (SA 710)

**Comparative information** is the amounts and disclosures for prior periods. Its form decides what the
opinion covers.

| | Corresponding figures | Comparative financial statements |
|---|---|---|
| Status | An **integral part** of the current statements, read only in relation to the current figures | Included **for comparison** and, if audited, referred to in the opinion |
| Opinion refers to | The **current period only** | **Each period** presented |

For **corresponding figures** the opinion does not refer to them, with three exceptions: an unresolved
prior-period modification (modify in respect of the current or the corresponding figures as the case may
be); a material misstatement in prior statements that carried an unmodified opinion, where the
corresponding figures are not restated (modify in respect of the corresponding figures); and an unaudited
prior period (an Other Matter paragraph says so, which does not excuse evidence on opening balances under
SA 510). For **comparative financial statements**, a prior-period opinion that now differs is explained in
an Other Matter paragraph, as is an audit of that period by another auditor.

### Branch audit and joint audit

**Branch audit.** Under the Companies Act, 2013 a branch may be audited by the company's auditor or by
another person qualified for appointment as the company's auditor. The branch auditor reports on the branch
accounts to the company's auditor, who deals with that report as he considers necessary and remains
responsible for his own opinion on the statements.

**Joint audit.** More than one auditor is appointed for the same entity. Work is divided by identifiable
units, areas, assets, income or periods, and the division is documented. Each joint auditor is responsible
for the work allotted to him; all are **jointly and severally** responsible for work not divided, decisions
taken jointly, requirements applicable to all, and the statements complying with disclosure requirements. A
joint auditor may rely on the others' work without reviewing it, and where he disagrees he issues his **own
report**.

### Reporting under the Companies Act, 2013, including CARO

The company auditor's report has two layers: the SA 700 opinion on truth and fairness, and the **statutory
reporting** the Act requires, placed under other reporting responsibilities and kept out of the opinion
paragraph — statements on such matters as information and explanations obtained,
proper books of account and branch returns, compliance with accounting standards, adverse observations on
financial transactions, director disqualification, internal financial controls where that reporting applies,
and other prescribed matters.

**CARO** — the Companies (Auditor's Report) Order — is issued by the Central Government under the Act and
requires the auditor of specified classes of companies to report on additional matters. Three things to
hold: it is a **reporting requirement, not an opinion**; it is given as an annexure referred to from the
report; and where an answer is unfavourable or qualified the report must state the **reasons**. Its matters
run broadly to property, plant and equipment and title deeds; inventory and
working-capital facilities; investments, loans, guarantees and deposits; statutory dues and default in
repayment; end use of funds raised; fraud and whistle-blower complaints; related-party and managerial
matters; and the ability to meet liabilities.

:::ladder A material investment in an unlisted subsidiary cannot be verified because the subsidiary's records are unavailable. State what the auditor does and the opinion.
@weak: The auditor cannot check the investment, so he gives a negative opinion and says so.
@good: This is an inability to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence, not a misstatement. The auditor
performs alternative procedures; if he still cannot obtain evidence and the effect is material but not
pervasive, he expresses a qualified opinion, describing the matter in the Basis for Qualified Opinion
section.
@great: Classify first: an **inability to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence**, so the choice
lies between a **qualified opinion** and a **disclaimer of opinion** — never adverse. Attempt alternative
procedures: audited statements of the subsidiary, valuation evidence, confirmations. Then judge
pervasiveness. One material investment among many items is material but **not pervasive**: express a
**qualified opinion**, say the statements give a true and fair view **except for** the possible effects of
the matter, and in "Basis for Qualified Opinion" describe the investment and the possible effect if
quantifiable. Were that investment most of the balance sheet the effect would be pervasive and a
**disclaimer** would follow.
