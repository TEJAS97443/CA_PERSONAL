## In plain words

### Three windows after 31 March

Tapri Tea Co. Ltd's year ends on 31 March 2026. Iyer & Associates will sign their report on 28 May 2026, and the annual report goes out to shareholders on 26 June 2026. Bunty thinks the audit froze on 31 March.

> **Bunty:** Ma'am, the balance sheet is as at 31 March. Why are we reading May's board minutes?
> **Lakshmi:** Because on 18 April, Kadak Distributors went insolvent owing Tapri ₹3.20 crore. Was that money good on 31 March?
> **Bunty:** ...Probably not.
> **Lakshmi:** Then 31 March was wrong, and April told us.

**Subsequent events** are events occurring between the date of the financial statements and the date of the auditor's report, and facts that become known to the auditor after the date of the auditor's report.

They come in two kinds. Some give evidence about conditions that **already existed** on 31 March: Kadak was already sinking when the year ended; April only revealed it. Those change the numbers. Others reflect conditions that **arose later**: a fire at the samosa factory on 6 May did not exist on 31 March, so the 31 March figures stay, but readers may need to be told.

The auditor's duty changes with the calendar. Until the report is signed, the auditor must go looking for such events. After signing, the auditor no longer has to look, but if a fact turns up that would have changed the report, the auditor must act on it. And if the accounts have already gone out to the world, acting can mean making sure everyone who received them is told, or stopping people from relying on the report.

::: analogy The blood test
A blood sample is drawn on 31 March and the report is printed on 28 May. If the lab learns in April that the sample already showed an infection, the report is corrected: the condition existed on the day of the test. If the patient breaks a leg on 6 May, the blood report stays the same, though the doctor's letter mentions it. And if the report has already been posted when the lab finds the error, the lab phones everyone who got a copy.
:::

::: key
Dates decide duties. Before the report date, the auditor must search for subsequent events; after it, the auditor need not search, but must act on any fact that would have changed the report.
:::

## How it works

### 1. Two types of events

Financial statements may be affected by certain events that occur after the date of the financial statements. Financial reporting frameworks ordinarily identify two types of events (SM 7.4–7.5):

| Type of event | Examples |
|---|---|
| (a) Evidence of conditions that **existed** at the date of the financial statements | Insolvency of a major debtor before the report date; a legal claim, already provided for, settled out of court at a reduced amount |
| (b) Evidence of conditions that **arose after** the date of the financial statements | Issue of new share capital; planned merger; destruction of substantial inventories by fire before the report date |

The Study Material's examples already point to the treatment: the insolvency gives evidence on "the recoverability of the money due from debtor as on date of the financial statements", and the settlement "evidence on adjustment in provision amount" (SM 7.4–7.5). ICAI's answers then treat type (a) as requiring **adjustment** of the financial statements (the Study Material's answer to Test Your Understanding 1, SM 7.57) and type (b) as leaving the amounts unchanged but requiring **disclosure** (RTP May 2024 Q16), where it is of such importance that non-disclosure would affect users' ability to make proper evaluations or decisions (MTP January 2026 Series I Q6(c)).

### 2. The objectives

The objectives of the auditor are to (SM 7.5):

- (a) obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence about whether events occurring between the date of the financial statements and the date of the auditor's report that require adjustment of, or disclosure in, the financial statements are appropriately reflected in those financial statements; and
- (b) respond appropriately to facts that become known to the auditor after the date of the auditor's report, that, had they been known to the auditor at that date, may have caused the auditor to amend the auditor's report.

### 3. Window 1: up to the date of the auditor's report

The auditor shall perform audit procedures designed to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence that ==all events occurring between the date of the financial statements and the date of the auditor's report== that require adjustment of, or disclosure in, the financial statements have been identified. The auditor is not, however, expected to perform additional audit procedures on matters to which previously applied audit procedures have provided satisfactory conclusions (SM 7.6).

The procedures shall cover the period from the date of the financial statements to the date of the auditor's report, or as near as practicable thereto. The auditor shall take into account the risk assessment in determining their nature and extent, which shall include (SM 7.6):

- (a) obtaining an understanding of any procedures management has established to ensure that subsequent events are identified;
- (b) inquiring of management and, where appropriate, those charged with governance as to whether any subsequent events have occurred which might affect the financial statements;
- (c) reading minutes, if any, of the meetings of the entity's owners, management and those charged with governance, that have been held after the date of the financial statements, and inquiring about matters discussed at any such meetings for which minutes are not yet available;
- (d) reading the entity's latest subsequent interim financial statements, if any.

Such information may also be obtained from accounting records pertaining to the period after the date of the financial statements, reading the entity's latest available budgets, etc. When the procedures identify events that require adjustment or disclosure, the auditor shall determine whether each such event is appropriately reflected in the financial statements (SM 7.6).

**Written representation.** The auditor shall request management and, where appropriate, those charged with governance, to provide a written representation in accordance with [[aud.complete.sa580|SA 580]] that all events occurring subsequent to the date of the financial statements and for which the applicable financial reporting framework requires adjustment or disclosure have been adjusted or disclosed (SM 7.7).

::: definition Date the financial statements are issued
The date that the auditor's report and audited financial statements are made available to third parties. It generally depends on the regulatory environment of the entity; in some circumstances it may be the date they are filed with a regulatory authority. Since audited financial statements cannot be issued without an auditor's report, the date of issue must be at or later than the date of the auditor's report, and at or later than the date the auditor's report is provided to the entity (SM 7.7).
:::

### 4. Window 2: after the report, before the statements are issued

The auditor has no obligation to perform any audit procedures regarding the financial statements after the date of the auditor's report. However, when, after the date of the auditor's report but before the date the financial statements are issued, a fact becomes known to the auditor that, had it been known at the date of the auditor's report, may have caused the auditor to amend the report, the auditor shall (SM 7.7):

- (a) discuss the matter with management and, where appropriate, those charged with governance;
- (b) determine whether the financial statements need amendment and, if so,
- (c) inquire how management intends to address the matter in the financial statements.

**If management amends the financial statements**, the auditor shall (SM 7.7–7.8):

- (a) carry out the audit procedures necessary in the circumstances on the amendment;
- (b) extend the audit procedures on subsequent events to the date of the new auditor's report, and provide a new auditor's report on the amended financial statements. The new auditor's report shall not be dated earlier than the date of approval of the amended financial statements.

**Amendment restricted to the subsequent event.** When law, regulation or the financial reporting framework does not prohibit management from restricting the amendment to the effects of the subsequent event, and those approving the financial statements are not prohibited from restricting their approval to that amendment, the auditor is permitted to restrict the procedures on subsequent events to that amendment. The auditor shall then either (SM 7.8):

- (a) amend the auditor's report to include an additional date restricted to that amendment, indicating that the procedures on subsequent events are restricted solely to the amendment described in the relevant note; or
- (b) provide a new or amended report with an Emphasis of Matter or Other Matter paragraph conveying that the procedures on subsequent events are restricted solely to that amendment.

Where law, regulation or the framework does not require management to issue amended financial statements, the auditor need not provide an amended or new report (SM 7.8).

**If management does not amend** when the auditor believes amendment is needed (SM 7.8–7.9):

- (a) if the auditor's report has **not yet been provided** to the entity, the auditor shall modify the opinion as required by [[aud.report.sa705|SA 705]] and then provide the report;
- (b) if the report **has already been provided**, the auditor shall notify management and, unless all of those charged with governance are involved in managing the entity, those charged with governance, not to issue the financial statements to third parties before the necessary amendments have been made. If they are nevertheless issued without the amendments, the auditor shall take appropriate action to seek to prevent reliance on the auditor's report.

### 5. Window 3: after the statements are issued

After the financial statements have been issued, the auditor has no obligation to perform any audit procedures regarding them. However, when a fact becomes known that, had it been known at the date of the auditor's report, may have caused the auditor to amend the report, the auditor shall (SM 7.9):

- (a) discuss the matter with management and, where appropriate, those charged with governance;
- (b) determine whether the financial statements need amendment and, if so,
- (c) inquire how management intends to address the matter in the financial statements.

**If management amends the financial statements**, the auditor shall (SM 7.9–7.10):

- (a) carry out the audit procedures necessary in the circumstances on the amendment;
- (b) review the steps taken by management to ensure that anyone in receipt of the previously issued financial statements together with the auditor's report thereon is informed of the situation;
- (c) unless the amendment may lawfully be restricted to the subsequent event: extend the audit procedures to the date of the new auditor's report, date the new report no earlier than the date of approval of the amended financial statements, and provide a new auditor's report on the amended financial statements;
- (d) where the amendment may be so restricted: amend the auditor's report, or provide a new report, as in Window 2.

The new or amended report shall include an **Emphasis of Matter paragraph or Other Matter paragraph** referring to a note to the financial statements that more extensively discusses the reason for the amendment of the previously issued financial statements, and to the earlier report provided by the auditor (SM 7.10).

**If management does not inform the recipients and does not amend**, the auditor shall notify management and, unless all of those charged with governance are involved in managing the entity, those charged with governance, that the auditor will seek to prevent future reliance on the auditor's report. If, despite such notification, they do not take these necessary steps, the auditor shall take appropriate action to seek to prevent reliance on the auditor's report (SM 7.10).

### Dating rules

- The original report is dated no earlier than the date on which the auditor has obtained sufficient appropriate audit evidence, including evidence that the statements have been prepared and that those with recognised authority have taken responsibility for them (SM 8.22).
- A new report on amended financial statements is dated **no earlier than the date of approval of the amended financial statements** (SM 7.8, 7.9).
- A restricted amendment gets an **additional date** in the report, restricted to that amendment (SM 7.8).

## Worked examples

::::: example Bunty's April–May list
Iyer & Associates are finalising the audit of Tapri Tea Co. Ltd for 2025-26; the report is to be signed on 28 May 2026. Overall materiality is ₹1.50 crore. Reading the April and May board minutes and the April management accounts, and inquiring of Dev Malhotra, the CFO, Bunty lists:

1. On 24 April 2026, Tapri sold its entire stock of the discontinued "Elaichi Gold" premix, carried at its cost of ₹3.40 crore at 31 March, to a bulk buyer for ₹1.90 crore, incurring selling costs of ₹12 lakh. The premix had stopped selling in January 2026.
2. On 8 May 2026, cut-off testing found that goods invoiced and recorded as sales of ₹1.15 crore on 31 March 2026 were dispatched only on 3 April; they had been counted in the closing inventory.
3. Gujarat Kirana Mart, a distributor, owed Tapri ₹2.80 crore at 31 March, all within its 30-day credit period, and paid ₹1.10 crore of it on the due dates in April. On 9 May 2026 a cyclone destroyed its only warehouse and its uninsured stock; it filed for insolvency on 22 May and expects to pay nothing to unsecured creditors.
4. On 15 May 2026, the board approved an agreement to acquire all the shares of Kulhad Express Pvt Ltd for ₹18 crore.

Classify each event, state what the financial statements should show, and state the auditor's responsibilities.

:::: answer Model answer
**Relevant provision.** As per SA 560, the auditor shall perform audit procedures designed to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence that all events occurring between the date of the financial statements and the date of the auditor's report that require adjustment of, or disclosure in, the financial statements have been identified, and shall determine whether each such event is appropriately reflected. Financial reporting frameworks ordinarily identify two types of events: those that provide evidence of conditions that existed at the date of the financial statements, and those that provide evidence of conditions that arose after that date.

**Working note**

| Event | Amount (₹) | Type | Treatment |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 1. Premix: 3,40,00,000 − (1,90,00,000 − 12,00,000) | 1,62,00,000 | Existed at 31 March | Adjust: write down to net realisable value |
| 2. Sales recorded before dispatch | 1,15,00,000 | Existed at 31 March | Adjust: reverse the sale and the receivable |
| 3. Gujarat Kirana: 2,80,00,000 − 1,10,00,000 | 1,70,00,000 | Arose after | Disclose; no adjustment |
| 4. Acquisition of Kulhad Express | 18,00,00,000 | Arose after | Disclose |

**Analysis.**

1. The April sale gives evidence of the net realisable value of the premix at 31 March: it had stopped selling in January, so the fall in value existed at the year-end. The inventory is written down from its cost of ₹3.40 crore to ₹1.78 crore (₹1.90 crore less selling costs of ₹0.12 crore), a write-down of ₹1.62 crore, which exceeds materiality. The financial statements require adjustment.
2. The cut-off error existed at 31 March: the goods were still Tapri's inventory on that date. Sales and trade receivables are overstated by ₹1.15 crore, and so is profit, since the goods are also in closing inventory. It is below materiality on its own but must be corrected or accumulated as a misstatement, and with item 1 it makes ₹2.77 crore. Bunty's finding also calls for extending cut-off testing, as it may not be the only such error.
3. An insolvency soon after the year-end ordinarily gives evidence that the debt was doubtful at the year-end, so the auditor must test the facts, not the label. Here the distributor paid within its credit terms, including ₹1.10 crore in April, and its failure was caused by the cyclone of 9 May. The receivable was recoverable at 31 March; the condition arose after that date. No adjustment is made to the 31 March amount, but the loss of ₹1.70 crore still owed, above materiality, should be disclosed. Had the evidence shown overdue amounts or financial difficulty at 31 March, the event would require adjustment.
4. The acquisition agreement arose after the year-end and is disclosed, not adjusted.

**Auditor's responsibilities.** The auditor's procedures should cover the period up to 28 May 2026 or as near as practicable, and include understanding management's procedures for identifying subsequent events, inquiring of management and those charged with governance, reading minutes of meetings held after 31 March (and inquiring about meetings whose minutes are not yet available) and reading the latest interim financial statements. The auditor should obtain evidence for each conclusion above (the sale invoice and selling costs; dispatch records; the distributor's payment record and insolvency filing) and request a written representation that all events requiring adjustment or disclosure have been adjusted or disclosed.

**Conclusion.** Events 1 and 2 require adjustment; events 3 and 4 require disclosure. If management does not adjust for them, the uncorrected misstatements (₹1.62 crore for the premix alone, ₹2.77 crore in all) are material and would require a modified opinion under SA 705; so would a failure to disclose the ₹1.70 crore loss.

::::: example The judgment nobody mentioned
Iyer & Associates signed and handed over their unmodified report on Tapri's 2025-26 financial statements on 28 May 2026; the annual report is to be issued on 26 June 2026. On 9 June, Lakshmi reads in a newspaper that on 25 May 2026 the Commercial Court decreed damages of ₹5.60 crore against Tapri in a trademark suit filed in 2024 by Masala Chaiwala LLP. Tapri's financial statements show the suit only as a contingent liability. Tapri's legal team received a copy of the decree on 26 May; on 28 May, Kesar and Dev signed a written representation that all events after the date of the financial statements requiring adjustment or disclosure had been adjusted or disclosed. Kesar now refuses to amend: "The report is signed. Sab adjust ho jayega next year."

(i) Advise Iyer & Associates.

(ii) What does the 28 May representation mean for the audit?

(iii) What if the board instead amends the financial statements only for the judgment, as the law permits?

**(i) Relevant provision.** As per SA 560, the auditor has no obligation to perform audit procedures after the date of the auditor's report. However, when, after that date but before the date the financial statements are issued, a fact becomes known that, had it been known at the date of the report, may have caused the auditor to amend it, the auditor shall discuss the matter with management and, where appropriate, those charged with governance, determine whether the financial statements need amendment and, if so, inquire how management intends to address the matter. If management does not amend and the report has already been provided to the entity, the auditor shall notify management and, unless all of those charged with governance are involved in managing the entity, those charged with governance, not to issue the financial statements to third parties before the necessary amendments; if they are nevertheless issued without them, the auditor shall take appropriate action to seek to prevent reliance on the report.

**Facts and analysis.** The judgment existed on 25 May, before the report date, but became known to the auditor only on 9 June, before the issue date: Window 2. The decree relates to an infringement before 31 March, so it gives evidence of a condition that existed at the year-end; ₹5.60 crore is well above materiality of ₹1.50 crore. Had it been known, the report would have been amended. The financial statements need amendment; management refuses; the report has been provided.

**Conclusion.** Iyer & Associates should notify Kesar and Tapri's board (whose members are not all in management) **not to issue** the financial statements to third parties before the necessary amendment. If Tapri issues them anyway, the firm should take appropriate action to seek to prevent reliance on its report.

**(ii)** The decree was in management's hands two days before it represented that all subsequent events requiring adjustment or disclosure had been adjusted or disclosed, so the written representation is inconsistent with other audit evidence. The representation never replaced the procedures: although written representations provide necessary audit evidence, they do not provide sufficient appropriate audit evidence on their own about any of the matters with which they deal (SM 7.29). As per SA 580, if written representations are inconsistent with other audit evidence, the auditor shall perform audit procedures to attempt to resolve the matter; if it remains unresolved, the auditor shall reconsider the assessment of the competence, integrity, ethical values or diligence of management, and determine the effect that this may have on the reliability of representations and audit evidence in general. If she concludes that the written representations are not reliable, Lakshmi shall take appropriate actions, including determining the possible effect on the opinion in any new report in accordance with SA 705, having regard to the requirement of a disclaimer of opinion (SM 7.37). The other representations in the 28 May letter, and the evidence that rests on management's word, have to be re-evaluated in that light.

**(iii)** If the amendment is lawfully restricted to the judgment, the auditor carries out the procedures necessary on the amendment and may restrict the subsequent-events procedures to it, and then either amends the report to include an **additional date** restricted to that amendment, or provides a new or amended report with an **Emphasis of Matter or Other Matter paragraph** conveying that the procedures on subsequent events are restricted solely to the amendment described in the relevant note. Without such a restriction, the procedures are extended to the date of a new report, dated no earlier than the approval of the amended financial statements.

::::: example Three pieces of news for CA Meera Joshi
CA Meera Joshi audited Chai Junction Distributors, a partnership firm whose three partners all run the business, for 2024-25. She set materiality at ₹20 lakh. The financial statements, with her unmodified report, were given to the firm's bank on 15 July 2025. In September 2025 she learns that:

(a) on 20 August 2025, a fire at the firm's Ujjain godown destroyed uninsured stock costing ₹40 lakh;

(b) the closing stock of ₹2.30 crore at 31 March 2025 included ₹62 lakh of goods held on consignment for Tapri, a fact that existed when she signed her report; and

(c) on 2 September 2025, the partners decided to convert the firm into an LLP.

Advise CA Meera Joshi on her responsibilities under SA 560 for each matter. For (b), state also what she must do if the partners will neither amend the financial statements nor tell the bank.

:::: answer Model answer
**Relevant provision.** As per SA 560, after the financial statements have been issued, the auditor has no obligation to perform any audit procedures regarding them. However, when, after they have been issued, a fact becomes known to the auditor that, had it been known at the date of the auditor's report, may have caused the auditor to amend the report, the auditor shall discuss the matter with management and, where appropriate, those charged with governance, determine whether the financial statements need amendment and, if so, inquire how management intends to address the matter.

**(a) and (c): no duty under SA 560.** The fire and the decision to convert occurred after the date of her report and after the statements were issued. They are not facts that existed at the date of the report, so they could not have caused her to amend it. SA 560 places no responsibility on her for the 2024-25 statements; both belong to 2025-26 and will be dealt with in that year's financial statements and audit.

**(b): a fact that existed.** Inventory is overstated by ₹62 lakh, 27% of the stock shown and more than three times materiality of ₹20 lakh. Had she known it, she would have amended her report, so SA 560's post-issue requirements apply.

## Under the hood

### SA 560 inside other Standards

| Where | Link to SA 560 |
|---|---|
| SA 700 (SM 8.22) | The report's date tells users that events up to that date have been considered; later events fall under SA 560 |
| SA 706 (SM 8.49) | A significant subsequent event between the year-end and the report date is an example of a matter for an Emphasis of Matter paragraph |
| SA 580 (SM 7.36) | The representation letter: all subsequent events requiring adjustment or disclosure have been adjusted or disclosed |
| SA 570 (SM 7.20) | Subsequent-events procedures identify events that mitigate or affect going concern |
| SA 710 (SM 8.59) | A possible misstatement in comparatives the auditor audited last year triggers SA 560's requirements |

See [[aud.report.sa706|SA 706]], [[aud.complete.sa570|SA 570]] and [[aud.report.sa710|SA 710]].

### Why the representation does not replace the procedures

Even after inquiry, reading minutes and reading the books after the year-end, the auditor may not come to know of every subsequent event; the written representation is the acknowledgment from management that all events requiring adjustment or disclosure have been dealt with (SM 7.58, TYU 2). It is obtained in addition to the procedures, never in place of them: written representations do not by themselves provide sufficient appropriate audit evidence (SM 7.54).

ICAI's suggested answers first fix the window ("after the date of the auditor's report but before the date the financial statements are issued"), state that there is no obligation to perform procedures after the report date, then list the steps in order: discuss, determine, inquire; if amended, procedures, (review of steps to inform recipients), new report and its date; if not, the SA 705 modification or the notification, and finally "appropriate action to seek to prevent reliance on the auditor's report".
:::

::: trick Discuss, Determine, Inquire: "DDI" opens Windows 2 and 3
Both post-report windows start with the same three verbs in this order. Condition: only when a fact becomes known that, had it been known at the report date, may have caused the auditor to amend the report; there is no duty to go looking.
:::

## Answer frame

### A case on a subsequent event

1. **Fix the window** from the dates: event or fact before the report date (Window 1), after the report but before issue (Window 2), or after issue (Window 3).
2. **Relevant provision** for that window, in SA 560's words.
3. **Facts**: type of event (existed at the year-end, or arose after), amount against materiality, management's response, whether the report has been provided.
4. **Analysis**: adjust or disclose; which branch of the window applies.
5. **Conclusion**: the steps in order, ending with the opinion, the new report's date and paragraph, or the action to prevent reliance.

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: Bunty's minutes that stop on 31 March
Bunty reads every board minute up to the year-end and none after. **Fix:** SA 560 requires reading minutes of meetings held **after** the date of the financial statements, and inquiring about meetings whose minutes are not yet available (ICAI's January 2025 MCQ 4).
:::

::: mistake Adjusting for the fire
Writing down 31 March inventory for a May fire. **Fix:** the fire is a condition that arose after the year-end: disclose it if significant; adjust only for events that give evidence of conditions existing at the year-end.
:::

::: mistake "Once signed, the auditor has no duty"
**Fix:** there is no duty to perform procedures after the report date, but a duty to respond to facts that become known and would have changed the report, in both Window 2 and Window 3.
:::

::: mistake Keeping the old date on the new report
**Fix:** a new report on amended financial statements is dated no earlier than the date of approval of the amended statements, after extending the subsequent-events procedures to that date (or carries an additional date restricted to a lawful restricted amendment).
:::

## Practice

::: quiz Check yourself
Q: Case scenario. Iyer & Associates are auditing Tapri for 2025-26; the report will be signed on 28 May 2026. On 2 May, a court upholds a customer's claim, pending since 2024 and not provided for, at ₹2.10 crore. Materiality is ₹1.50 crore. Which of the following is correct?
- [ ] It arose after 31 March, so it only needs disclosure
- [x] It gives evidence of a condition that existed at 31 March: the financial statements should be adjusted
- [ ] The auditor has no responsibility, since it happened after the balance sheet date
- [ ] Mention it in an Other Matter paragraph instead of adjusting
> The claim relates to events before the year-end; the judgment is evidence of the obligation existing at 31 March, above materiality: adjust. A later disclosure or an Other Matter paragraph does not replace an adjustment.

Q: After the report date but before the financial statements are issued, the auditor learns a fact that requires amendment. Management refuses, and the auditor's report has **not yet** been provided to the entity. The auditor shall:
- [x] Modify the opinion as required by SA 705 and then provide the report
- [ ] Notify management not to issue the financial statements to third parties
- [ ] Provide the unmodified report and add an Emphasis of Matter paragraph
- [ ] Withdraw from the engagement without reporting
> Not yet provided: modify and then provide. Notification not to issue applies when the report has already been provided.

Q: Case scenario. Tapri's board met on 12 May and 26 May 2026. When Iyer & Associates are ready to sign on 28 May, Bunty has read the 12 May minutes, but the minutes of the 26 May meeting are still being drafted. What does SA 560 require for the 26 May meeting?
- [ ] Nothing: SA 560 requires reading only the minutes that are available at the report date
- [ ] Postponing the report until the 26 May minutes are approved and read
- [ ] A written representation that the meeting discussed nothing relevant, in place of any other step
- [x] Inquiry about the matters discussed at the 26 May meeting
> SA 560 requires reading minutes of meetings held after the date of the financial statements and inquiring about matters discussed at any such meetings for which minutes are not yet available (SM 7.6). The report need not wait for the minutes, and a written representation is obtained in addition to the procedures, not in place of them.

Q: A new auditor's report on amended financial statements in Window 2 (no restriction of the amendment) shall be dated:
- [ ] The same date as the original report
- [ ] The date the fact became known to the auditor
- [x] No earlier than the date of approval of the amended financial statements
- [ ] The date the financial statements are issued to third parties
> The auditor extends the procedures to the new report's date, which cannot precede approval of the amended statements.

Q: After the financial statements have been issued, management amends them for a fact the auditor discovered. Select all that SA 560 requires of the auditor.
- [x] Carry out the audit procedures necessary on the amendment
- [x] Review the steps taken by management to ensure that anyone in receipt of the previously issued statements and report is informed
- [x] Include an Emphasis of Matter or Other Matter paragraph referring to the note explaining the amendment and to the earlier report
- [ ] Re-perform the entire audit of the year from the beginning
> Those three are the post-issue requirements, with the extension of procedures to the new report's date. A complete re-audit is not required.
:::

## Recap

- Subsequent events: events between the date of the financial statements and the date of the auditor's report, and facts known to the auditor after the report date.
- Two types: conditions that existed at the year-end (insolvent debtor, claim settled for less: adjust) and conditions that arose after (fire, share issue, merger: disclose).
- Objectives: evidence that events up to the report date are appropriately reflected; respond to facts learnt after the report date that may have changed it.
- Window 1 procedures: understand management's procedures; inquire; read minutes of meetings after the year-end; read latest interim statements; written representation.
- Date the financial statements are issued: when the report and statements are made available to third parties; at or after the report date and the date the report is provided to the entity.
- Windows 2 and 3 open with discuss, determine, inquire. Amended: procedures on it; new report dated no earlier than approval (or an additional date for a restricted amendment).
- Window 2, not amended: report not provided, modify (SA 705); provided, notify not to issue; issued anyway, act to prevent reliance.
- Window 3: review steps to inform recipients; Emphasis or Other Matter paragraph; if no steps, notify that the auditor will seek to prevent future reliance, then act. (The lab phones everyone who got a copy.)

## Flashcards

Q:: SA 560: what are subsequent events?
A:: Events occurring between the date of the financial statements and the date of the auditor's report, and facts that become known to the auditor after the date of the auditor's report.

Q:: SA 560: the two types of subsequent events?
A:: (a) Those that provide evidence of conditions that existed at the date of the financial statements; (b) those that provide evidence of conditions that arose after that date.

Q:: SA 560: two examples of events evidencing conditions that existed at the year-end?
A:: Insolvency of a major debtor between the year-end and the report date; settlement of a legal claim, already provided for, out of court at a reduced amount.

Q:: SA 560: three examples of events evidencing conditions that arose after the year-end?
A:: Issue of new share capital; planned merger; destruction of substantial inventories by fire between the year-end and the report date.

Q:: SA 560: what are the objectives of the auditor?
A:: (a) To obtain sufficient appropriate evidence that events up to the report date requiring adjustment or disclosure are appropriately reflected; (b) to respond appropriately to facts known after the report date that, had they been known then, may have caused the auditor to amend the report.

Q:: SA 560: the four procedures for events up to the report date?
A:: Understand management's procedures to identify subsequent events; inquire of management and TCWG; read minutes of meetings held after the year-end (and inquire about those not yet minuted); read the latest subsequent interim financial statements.

Q:: SA 560: meaning of "date the financial statements are issued"?
A:: The date the auditor's report and audited financial statements are made available to third parties; it must be at or after the date of the auditor's report and the date the report is provided to the entity.

Q:: SA 560: the first three steps when a fact becomes known after the report date?
A:: Discuss the matter with management and, where appropriate, TCWG; determine whether the financial statements need amendment; if so, inquire how management intends to address it.

Q:: SA 560: management will not amend; the report has already been provided. What does the auditor do?
A:: Notify management and (unless all TCWG manage the entity) TCWG not to issue the statements to third parties before amendment; if issued anyway, take appropriate action to seek to prevent reliance on the report.

Q:: SA 560: how is a new report on amended financial statements dated?
A:: No earlier than the date of approval of the amended financial statements, with subsequent-events procedures extended to that date.

Q:: SA 560: what must a new report on amended, previously issued financial statements include?
A:: An Emphasis of Matter or Other Matter paragraph referring to the note explaining the reason for the amendment and to the earlier report provided by the auditor.

Q:: SA 560: why obtain a written representation on subsequent events after performing procedures?
A:: The procedures may not reveal every subsequent event; the representation is management's acknowledgment that all events requiring adjustment or disclosure have been adjusted or disclosed.
