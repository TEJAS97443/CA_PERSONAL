## In plain words

### Three things that do not sit still

It is 31 March 2026 and Tapri Tea Co. Ltd's year is ending in three different ways at once. At the Dewas warehouse, forklifts are moving premix cartons in and out. At the samosa factory, half-made samosas sit in trays at four stages of folding. And on Lakshmi's desk is a court notice: BrewBucks has sued Tapri for ₹45 crore over its new "BrewBhai" cold coffee.

> **Bunty:** Ma'am, the stock sheet says ₹38 crore, the legal file says "no material litigation", and the segment note is neatly typed. I have ticked all three.
> **Lakshmi:** Did you see the stock? Did you ask the lawyers? Do you know how the segments were split?
> **Bunty:** *(quietly)* I saw the spreadsheet, Ma'am.

Most items in the accounts are audited with the ordinary tools of SA 500. SA 501 picks out three that need **specific considerations**, because the ordinary tools fail on them:

- **Inventory**: a spreadsheet cannot prove that cartons exist or are not rotten. So the auditor attends the physical count, watches it, inspects the stock and counts some of it.
- **Litigation and claims**: management may simply not mention the lawsuit. So the auditor searches for cases and, where there is a risk, writes directly to the company's external lawyers.
- **Segment information** (figures split by product line or region): the split can be done in ways that flatter one business. So the auditor understands and tests the method used.

Each item has its own question. For inventory: does it **exist**, and in what **condition**? For litigation: is the list **complete**? For segments: is the information **presented and disclosed** as the framework requires?

::: analogy The annual health check-up
A routine check-up covers most things with a stethoscope and a few questions. But the eyes get a chart on the wall, the heart an ECG and the blood a lab report, because asking "how are your eyes?" proves nothing. SA 501 is the auditor's specialist tests for three organs: the count for inventory, the lawyer's letter for litigation, the method check for segments.
:::

::: key
SA 501's objective is sufficient appropriate audit evidence about the ==existence and condition of inventory==, the completeness of litigation and claims, and the presentation and disclosure of segment information. Each has its own procedure. For inventory and litigation, the Standard itself says what happens when even alternative procedures fail: the opinion is modified under SA 705.
:::

## How it works

SA 501, "Audit Evidence: Specific Considerations for Selected Items", deals with specific considerations by the auditor in obtaining sufficient appropriate audit evidence with respect to certain aspects of inventory, litigation and claims involving the entity, and segment information in an audit of financial statements (SM 4.68).

### 1. The objective

The objective of the auditor is to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence regarding the (SM 4.68):

- (A) existence and condition of inventory;
- (B) completeness of litigation and claims involving the entity; and
- (C) presentation and disclosure of segment information in accordance with the applicable financial reporting framework.

### 2. Inventory: attend the count

When inventory is **material** to the financial statements, the auditor shall obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence regarding its existence and condition by (SM 4.68–4.69):

1. **attendance at physical inventory counting**, unless impracticable, to:
   - (i) evaluate management's instructions and procedures for recording and controlling the results of the entity's physical inventory counting;
   - (ii) observe the performance of management's count procedures;
   - (iii) inspect the inventory; and
   - (iv) perform test counts; and
2. **performing audit procedures over the entity's final inventory records** to determine whether they accurately reflect actual inventory count results.

Attendance at physical inventory counting involves (SM 4.69): (a) inspecting the inventory to ascertain its existence and evaluate its condition, and performing test counts; (b) observing compliance with management's instructions and the performance of procedures for recording and controlling the results of the count; and (c) obtaining audit evidence as to the reliability of management's count procedures. These may serve as tests of controls or substantive procedures, depending on the auditor's risk assessment, planned approach and the specific procedures carried out.

### 3. Planning the attendance

*Learn this list in order: ICAI asks it as "which matters are missing" (SM 4.69–4.70).* Matters relevant in planning attendance at physical inventory counting include, for example:

- (a) the nature of inventory;
- (b) stages of completion of work in progress;
- (c) the risks of material misstatement related to inventory;
- (d) the nature of the internal control related to inventory;
- (e) whether adequate procedures are expected to be established and proper instructions issued for physical inventory counting;
- (f) the timing of physical inventory counting;
- (g) whether the entity maintains a perpetual inventory system;
- (h) the locations at which inventory is held, including the materiality of the inventory and the risks of material misstatement at different locations, in deciding at which locations attendance is appropriate; and
- (i) whether the assistance of an auditor's expert is needed to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence.

### 4. When the count does not happen on the day, or at all

**Count at a date other than the date of the financial statements.** In addition to the procedures above, the auditor shall perform audit procedures to obtain evidence about whether **changes in inventory between the count date and the date of the financial statements** are properly recorded. Relevant matters: whether the perpetual inventory records are properly adjusted; the reliability of those records; and the reasons for significant differences between the physical count and the perpetual records (SM 4.70).

**Unable to attend due to unforeseen circumstances.** The auditor shall **make or observe some physical counts on an alternative date**, and perform audit procedures on **intervening transactions** (SM 4.70).

**Attendance impracticable.** The auditor shall perform **alternative audit procedures** to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence regarding existence and condition. If that is not possible, the auditor shall **modify the opinion** in accordance with SA 705 (SM 4.70–4.71).

- Impracticability may arise from the nature and location of the inventory, for example where it is held in a location that may pose threats to the safety of the auditor (SM 4.71).
- ==General inconvenience to the auditor is not sufficient== to support a decision that attendance is impracticable. As SA 200 explains, the matter of difficulty, time or cost involved is not in itself a valid basis for omitting an audit procedure for which there is no alternative, or for being satisfied with audit evidence that is less than persuasive (SM 4.71).
- An alternative procedure may be, for example, inspecting documentation of the subsequent sale of specific inventory items acquired or purchased before the count (SM 4.71). Where alternatives cannot provide the evidence, SA 705 requires a modified opinion because of the scope limitation ([[aud.report.sa705|SA 705]]).

### 5. Inventory held by a third party

When inventory under the custody and control of a third party is material, the auditor shall obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence regarding its existence and condition by performing **one or both** of (SM 4.71):

- (a) requesting **confirmation from the third party** as to the quantities and condition of inventory held on behalf of the entity;
- (b) performing **inspection or other audit procedures** appropriate in the circumstances.

Other audit procedures may include, for example (SM 4.72): inspecting documentation regarding inventory held by third parties, such as warehouse receipts; requesting confirmation from other parties when inventory has been pledged as collateral; attending, or arranging for another auditor to attend, the third party's physical counting, if practicable; obtaining another auditor's report, or a service auditor's report, on the adequacy of the third party's internal control for ensuring that inventory is properly counted and adequately safeguarded.

### 6. Litigation and claims: find them

The auditor shall design and perform audit procedures to **identify** litigation and claims involving the entity which may give rise to a risk of material misstatement, including (SM 4.72–4.73):

- (a) **inquiry of management** and, where applicable, others within the entity, including **in-house legal counsel**;
- (b) **reviewing minutes** of meetings of those charged with governance and **correspondence** between the entity and its external legal counsel; and
- (c) **reviewing legal expense accounts**.

Other procedures may include using information from the risk assessment procedures performed to understand the entity and its environment. Evidence obtained to identify litigation may also bear on its valuation or measurement; SA 540 deals with litigation and claims requiring accounting estimates (SM 4.73).

### 7. Litigation and claims: ask the lawyers

If the auditor assesses a risk of material misstatement regarding litigation or claims that have been identified, or when audit procedures performed indicate that other material litigation or claims may exist, the auditor shall, in addition to the procedures required by other SAs, **seek direct communication with the entity's external legal counsel**. The auditor shall do so through a **letter of inquiry** requesting the external legal counsel to communicate directly with the auditor (SM 4.73).

- If law, regulation or the respective legal professional body **prohibits** the external legal counsel from communicating directly with the auditor, the auditor shall perform **alternative audit procedures** (SM 4.74).
- If it is considered unlikely that the external legal counsel will respond appropriately to a **letter of general inquiry** (for example, if the counsel's professional body prohibits response to such a letter), the auditor may seek direct communication through a **letter of specific inquiry** (SM 4.74).

::: definition Letter of specific inquiry
A letter of specific inquiry includes (SM 4.74):

- (a) a list of litigation and claims;
- (b) where available, management's assessment of the outcome of each of the identified litigation and claims and its estimate of the financial implications, including costs involved; and
- (c) a request that the entity's external legal counsel confirm the reasonableness of management's assessments and provide the auditor with further information if the list is considered by the external legal counsel to be incomplete or incorrect.
:::

**Meeting the lawyers.** The auditor may also judge it necessary to meet the external legal counsel to discuss the likely outcome, for example where (i) the matter is a significant risk; (ii) the matter is complex; or (iii) there is disagreement between management and the external legal counsel. Such meetings ordinarily require management's permission and are held with a representative of management in attendance (SM 4.74).

**When the door is shut.** If (a) management refuses permission to communicate or meet with the external legal counsel, or the counsel refuses to respond appropriately to the letter of inquiry, or is prohibited from responding; **and** (b) the auditor is unable to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence by performing alternative audit procedures, the auditor shall **modify the opinion** in accordance with SA 705 (SM 4.74–4.75).

### 8. Segment information

::: definition Segment information
Information about different types of products and services of an enterprise and its operations in different geographical areas (SM 4.76).
:::

The auditor shall obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence regarding the presentation and disclosure of segment information in accordance with the applicable financial reporting framework by (SM 4.76):

- (a) **obtaining an understanding of the methods used by management** in determining segment information, and (i) evaluating whether such methods are likely to result in disclosure in accordance with the framework; and (ii) where appropriate, testing the application of such methods; and
- (b) performing **analytical procedures or other audit procedures** appropriate in the circumstances.

The auditor's responsibility for segment information is **in relation to the financial statements taken as a whole**. So the auditor is not required to perform the procedures that would be necessary to express an opinion on the segment information presented on a standalone basis (SM 4.76).

Matters that may be relevant in understanding management's methods include, for example (SM 4.76–4.77): (1) sales, transfers and charges between segments, and elimination of inter-segment amounts; (2) comparisons with budgets and other expected results, for example operating profits as a percentage of sales; (3) the allocation of assets and costs among segments; (4) consistency with prior periods, and the adequacy of the disclosures with respect to inconsistencies.

## Worked examples

:::: example Where should the team stand on 31 March?
For the 31 March 2026 count at Tapri Tea Co. Ltd, overall materiality is ₹1.50 crore and inventories in the draft balance sheet total ₹38 crore, held as follows:

| Location | Inventory (₹ crore) |
|---|---|
| Dewas warehouse (premix, kulhads) | 14.60 |
| Siliguri warehouse (tea) | 9.80 |
| Indore samosa factory (WIP and finished) | 5.10 |
| Frosty Chain Logistics, Pune: third-party cold store (frozen samosas) | 4.20 |
| 40-city cafés | 4.30 |
| **Total** | **38.00** |

All locations count on 31 March. Further facts:

- Dewas holds ₹2.10 crore of old elaichi premix that has not sold since December 2025.
- The Siliguri tea lies in 18 grades, whose quality and condition are judged by tasting.
- Samosa work in progress at Indore passes through four stages: dough, filling, frying and packing.
- Each café holds about ₹10.75 lakh of stock, counted by its own manager using written instructions from head office. Perpetual inventory records are kept at the warehouses and the factory, not at the cafés.

Required:

(i) Applying the matters that SA 501 treats as relevant in planning attendance at physical inventory counting, advise Iyer & Associates at which locations to attend the count and what to plan for each.

(ii) How should Iyer & Associates obtain evidence about the frozen samosas held by Frosty Chain?

::: answer Model answer
**(i) Relevant provision.** As per SA 501, matters relevant in planning attendance at physical inventory counting include the nature of inventory; stages of completion of work in progress; the risks of material misstatement related to inventory; the nature of the internal control related to inventory; whether adequate procedures are expected to be established and proper instructions issued for physical inventory counting; the timing of counting; whether the entity maintains a perpetual inventory system; the locations at which inventory is held, including the materiality of the inventory and the risks of material misstatement at different locations, in deciding at which locations attendance is appropriate; and whether the assistance of an auditor's expert is needed (SM 4.69–4.70).

**Analysis by location.**

| Location (₹ crore) | Share | What matters | Plan |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Dewas (14.60) | 38.4% | Largest; obsolete premix | Attend; inspect condition of slow-moving premix |
| Siliguri (9.80) | 25.8% | Grades judged by tasting | Attend; consider an auditor's expert |
| Indore (5.10) | 13.4% | Four stages of WIP | Attend; see how stage is recorded |
| Cafés (4.30) | 11.3% | 40 small sites, no perpetual records | Attend a sample chosen for risk |
| Frosty Chain (4.20) | 11.1% | Third-party custody | See (ii) |

Each café's ₹10.75 lakh is about 7% of materiality, but together the cafés hold ₹4.30 crore, nearly three times materiality, counted by managers without perpetual records to compare with. Iyer & Associates should evaluate the head-office count instructions before 31 March, attend a sample of cafés selected for risk (for example, the largest and those with past count differences) and perform test counts there. For every location they should understand the internal control over inventory and obtain management's count instructions in advance, to evaluate whether adequate procedures have been established.

**(ii) Frosty Chain.** As per SA 501, when inventory under the custody and control of a third party is material, the auditor shall obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence regarding its existence and condition by one or both of: requesting confirmation from the third party as to the quantities and condition of inventory held on behalf of the entity; and performing inspection or other appropriate procedures. ₹4.20 crore is well above overall materiality of ₹1.50 crore, so it is material: Iyer & Associates should request Frosty Chain's confirmation of quantities and condition and, as appropriate, inspect warehouse receipts, attend (or arrange another auditor to attend) Frosty Chain's count if practicable, or obtain a service auditor's report on Frosty Chain's controls over counting and safeguarding.
:::

:::: example Bunty's three count-day dramas
State, with reasons, what the auditor should do in each case.

(a) Tapri's Siliguri warehouse was to be counted on 31 March 2026, but a landslide closed the only road and the team could reach it only on 6 April. The 6 April count found 1,92,400 kg of tea. From 1 April up to the 6 April count, receipts were 18,600 kg and dispatches 27,300 kg. The perpetual records show 2,03,500 kg at 31 March; average cost is about ₹480 per kg.

(b) Bunty wants to skip the Dewas count because the date clashes with three other clients' counts. "We will rely on the stock register, Ma'am. It is just inconvenient."

(c) Konkan Salt Works Ltd, another client, holds raw salt of ₹2.60 crore (9% of its total assets) at a port jetty where a gas leak has made the area unsafe; the authorities bar outsiders until July, after the report is due. Materiality is ₹80 lakh. No subsequent sale or other documents can be matched to the specific stock.

::: answer Model answer
**(a) Unforeseen circumstances.** As per SA 501, if the auditor is unable to attend physical inventory counting due to unforeseen circumstances, the auditor shall make or observe some physical counts on an alternative date and perform audit procedures on intervening transactions. The team should observe the 6 April count, test the receipts and dispatches between 1 and 6 April, and roll the count back to 31 March:

| Working | Quantity (kg) |
|---|---|
| Count on 6 April | 1,92,400 |
| Less: receipts, 1–6 April | (18,600) |
| Add: dispatches, 1–6 April | 27,300 |
| **Derived quantity at 31 March** | **2,01,100** |
| Perpetual records at 31 March | 2,03,500 |
| **Difference** | **2,400** |

The records exceed the derived quantity by 2,400 kg, about ₹11,52,000 at ₹480 per kg. The auditor should investigate the reasons for the difference and ensure the records are adjusted.

**(b) Inconvenience.** As per SA 501, the matter of general inconvenience to the auditor is not sufficient to support a decision that attendance is impracticable; as explained in SA 200, difficulty, time or cost is not in itself a valid basis for omitting a procedure for which there is no alternative. Bunty's plan is not acceptable: the Dewas warehouse holds ₹14.60 crore of inventory, and the team must be scheduled to attend.

**(c) Impracticable, no alternatives.** As per SA 501, if attendance is impracticable (for example, a location posing threats to the auditor's safety), the auditor shall perform alternative audit procedures; if it is not possible to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence by alternative procedures, the auditor shall modify the opinion in accordance with SA 705. The possible effect, ₹2.60 crore, exceeds materiality of ₹80 lakh, but is confined to one item at 9% of total assets, so it is material but not pervasive: a **qualified opinion** ("except for the possible effects"), with the reasons in the Basis for Qualified Opinion section.

:::: example The fee note from Mumbai
Tapri's list of litigation and claims for 2025-26 shows two consumer cases of ₹6 lakh each. Overall materiality is ₹1.50 crore. Reviewing the legal expense account, Bunty finds fees of ₹28 lakh paid to Kapadia & Co., Advocates, Mumbai, with the narration "BrewBhai: defence strategy". The January 2026 board minutes record the in-house counsel's warning that BrewBucks "is expected to file a suit for about ₹45 crore over the BrewBhai brand". Dev says: "Nothing has been filed. Our lawyers say we will win. No need to bother them."

Required: Advise Lakshmi on (i) what these findings mean and what SA 501 requires her to do; (ii) whether she should also meet Kapadia & Co.; and (iii) what she must do if Kapadia & Co. reply that their professional body prohibits them from communicating directly with auditors.

::: answer Model answer
**(i) The findings.** As per SA 501, the auditor shall design and perform audit procedures to identify litigation and claims which may give rise to a risk of material misstatement, including inquiry of management and others within the entity, including in-house legal counsel; reviewing minutes of meetings of those charged with governance and correspondence between the entity and its external legal counsel; and reviewing legal expense accounts (SM 4.73). Two of those procedures, the legal expense account and the board minutes, point to a claim of about ₹45 crore that management's list leaves out. When audit procedures indicate that other material litigation or claims may exist, the auditor shall seek direct communication with the entity's external legal counsel, through a letter of inquiry requesting the counsel to communicate directly with the auditor (SM 4.73). Dev's view that "nothing has been filed" does not remove that requirement: Lakshmi should also inspect BrewBucks' notices and Tapri's correspondence with Kapadia & Co., and consider whether the matter needs provision or disclosure.

**(ii) A meeting.** The auditor may judge it necessary to meet the external legal counsel, for example where the matter is a significant risk or is complex, or where management and the external legal counsel disagree (SM 4.74). A threatened claim of ₹45 crore, thirty times materiality, over a brand Tapri still sells, may well be a significant risk, so a meeting may be needed. Such meetings ordinarily require management's permission and are held with a representative of management in attendance.

**(iii) Prohibition.** If law, regulation or the respective legal professional body prohibits the entity's external legal counsel from communicating directly with the auditor, the auditor shall perform alternative audit procedures (SM 4.74): for example, inspecting the notices and the counsel's correspondence and fee notes, obtaining the in-house counsel's assessment and reading later board minutes. If the counsel is prohibited from responding and Lakshmi cannot obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence by alternative procedures, she shall modify the opinion in accordance with SA 705 (SM 4.74–4.75).
:::

Tapri reports three segments: packaged premix, cafés, and food manufacturing. The premix segment sells to the cafés at internal transfer prices, and head-office costs are allocated to all three. Bunty plans to "audit each segment separately and give it its own opinion". Guide him on the auditor's responsibility and the procedures.

## Under the hood

### Where SA 501 meets its neighbours

- **Assertions.** Attending the count proves existence and condition, not ownership or valuation; those need other evidence (purchase documents, net realisable value). See [[aud.evid.assertions|assertions and audit procedures]].
- **Internal audit.** Observation of inventory counts is among the internal audit work SA 610 allows the auditor to use ([[aud.evid.sa610|SA 610]]); the evaluation SA 610 requires comes first.
- **Confirmations.** A third party's confirmation of inventory is an external confirmation; SA 505 governs how it is requested and evaluated ([[aud.evid.sa505|SA 505]]).
- **The rest of inventory.** Cut-off, valuation and the other substantive procedures on inventories are in [[aud.items.current|audit of inventories, receivables, cash and loans]].
- **Reporting litigation.** In January 2025 Q4(c), ICAI's answer combined SA 501 (direct communication with the legal counsel), SA 706 (reporting an uncertainty relating to the future outcome of exceptional litigation) and section 143(3) (whether the company has disclosed the impact of pending litigations on its financial position). See [[aud.report.sa706|SA 706]].

### The Study Material's own cases

- Case Study 1 (SM 4.115–4.117): the correct count procedure is to inspect existence *and* condition at all locations, observe the count and perform test counts (not "count every item", not "condition is for experts"); for inventory with third parties, confirmation or inspection, either or both.
- Test Your Understanding 7 (SM 4.75, 4.140): procedures suggesting that not all material litigation has been disclosed warrant direct communication with the external lawyer.

::: trick Unforeseen versus impracticable

## Answer frame

### A case question on SA 501

1. **Relevant provision**: "As per SA 501, …", the requirement for the item in the facts, in the Standard's words.
2. **Facts of the case**: which item, whether it is material, what happened (count missed, impracticable, held by a third party; a suit not disclosed; a refusal).
3. **Analysis**: which branch of SA 501 applies, and whether alternative procedures can still give the evidence.
4. **Conclusion**: the procedure to perform, or the SA 705 consequence when evidence cannot be obtained.

### Three answers to "Management refuses access to the lawyers. What should the auditor do?"

::: cols
**Loses marks**

"Qualify the report."

Skips the alternative procedures and says nothing about which opinion.
---
**Passes**

"Perform alternative procedures; if no evidence, modify the opinion under SA 705."

The rule, without the facts or the letter of inquiry.
---
**Full marks**

When direct communication is required, the letter of inquiry (and the specific letter's three contents if asked), the refusal rule with both conditions, alternative procedures, and SA 705 as the consequence.

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: "count every carton"
Writing that the auditor counts all the inventory. **Fix:** the auditor attends management's count: evaluates instructions, observes, inspects (existence and condition) and performs **test** counts, then tests the final records.
:::

::: mistake Too busy to attend
Calling attendance "impracticable" because of other engagements, travel or cost. **Fix:** general inconvenience is not sufficient; difficulty, time or cost is not a valid basis to omit a procedure with no alternative.
:::

::: mistake The representation that replaces the count
Accepting a written representation, or adding an Emphasis of Matter paragraph, when the count could not be attended and alternatives failed. **Fix:** that is a scope limitation; modify the opinion under SA 705.
:::

::: mistake A standalone opinion on segments
**Fix:** the responsibility is in relation to the financial statements taken as a whole; no procedures for a standalone segment opinion are required.
:::

Q: Case scenario. Cotton bales of Godavari Textiles Ltd worth ₹6 crore (material) lie in a warehouse run by Krishna Warehousing, which has issued warehouse receipts for them. The bales are pledged to Indore Co-operative Bank as collateral for a cash credit loan. Which of the following meets SA 501?
- [x] Requesting Krishna Warehousing's confirmation of the quantity and condition of the bales, inspecting the warehouse receipts and requesting confirmation from the bank that holds the pledge

- [ ] Obtaining a written representation from management on the quantity and value of the bales

The stock statement is the company's own document, a representation is not enough on its own, and the custodian confirms quantity and condition, not value.

## Recap

- SA 501 objective: evidence on existence and condition of inventory, completeness of litigation and claims, and presentation and disclosure of segment information.
- Material inventory: attend the count (evaluate instructions, observe, inspect, test count) unless impracticable, and test the final inventory records.
- Count on another date: test changes to the year end. Unforeseen absence: counts on an alternative date plus intervening transactions. Impracticable: alternative procedures, else modify under SA 705.
- General inconvenience is not impracticability; difficulty, time or cost is not in itself a valid basis to omit a procedure for which there is no alternative.
- Third-party inventory: confirmation of quantities and condition, inspection or other procedures, or both.
- Litigation: inquiries (including in-house counsel), minutes and lawyer correspondence, legal expense accounts; on a risk, a letter of inquiry to external counsel, specific if a general one will not be answered.
- Refusal of access to counsel plus failed alternatives: modify under SA 705.
- Segments: understand and test management's methods, plus analytical procedures; the responsibility is for the financial statements as a whole. (Bunty's spreadsheet is not a warehouse.)

## Flashcards

Q:: SA 501: what is the objective of the auditor?
A:: To obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence regarding the existence and condition of inventory; the completeness of litigation and claims involving the entity; and the presentation and disclosure of segment information in accordance with the applicable financial reporting framework.

Q:: SA 501: what four things does attendance at physical inventory counting aim to do?
A:: Evaluate management's instructions and procedures for recording and controlling the count; observe the performance of management's count procedures; inspect the inventory; perform test counts.

Q:: SA 501: list the matters relevant in planning attendance at physical inventory counting.
A:: Nature of inventory; stages of completion of WIP; risks of material misstatement; nature of internal control; whether adequate procedures and proper count instructions are expected; timing of the count; whether a perpetual inventory system is maintained; locations (materiality and risks at each); whether an auditor's expert is needed.

Q:: SA 501: the auditor cannot attend the count due to unforeseen circumstances. What then?
A:: Make or observe some physical counts on an alternative date, and perform audit procedures on intervening transactions.

Q:: SA 501: attendance at the count is impracticable. What then?
A:: Perform alternative audit procedures for existence and condition; if that is not possible, modify the opinion in accordance with SA 705.

Q:: SA 501: material inventory is held by a third party. What procedures?
A:: One or both of: request confirmation from the third party of quantities and condition held on the entity's behalf; perform inspection or other appropriate audit procedures.

Q:: SA 501: what procedures identify litigation and claims?
A:: Inquiry of management and others, including in-house legal counsel; reviewing minutes of TCWG meetings and correspondence with external legal counsel; reviewing legal expense accounts.

Q:: SA 501: when must the auditor seek direct communication with external legal counsel?
A:: When the auditor assesses a risk of material misstatement regarding identified litigation or claims, or procedures indicate other material litigation or claims may exist.

Q:: SA 501: what does a letter of specific inquiry include?
A:: A list of litigation and claims; where available, management's assessment of each outcome and estimate of financial implications, including costs; a request that counsel confirm the reasonableness of those assessments and say if the list is incomplete or incorrect.

Q:: SA 501: when shall the auditor modify the opinion regarding litigation?
A:: When management refuses permission to communicate or meet with external legal counsel, or counsel refuses or is prohibited from responding, and the auditor cannot obtain sufficient appropriate evidence by alternative procedures.

Q:: SA 501: what is the auditor's responsibility for segment information?
A:: It is in relation to the financial statements taken as a whole; the auditor need not perform procedures needed for an opinion on segment information on a standalone basis.
