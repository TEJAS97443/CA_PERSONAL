## In plain words

### "Your team has seen every voucher, Lakshmi-ji"

It is 28 May 2026. The audit of Tapri Tea Co. Ltd is finished, the board is approving the accounts, and Lakshmi slides a two-page letter across the table, typed on Tapri's letterhead and waiting for a signature.

> **Kesar:** Lakshmi-ji, your team has seen every voucher. Why should I sign a letter also?
> **Lakshmi:** Because no voucher can show me the vouchers you did not give us, Kesar-ji.
> **Bunty:** Ma'am, once he signs, can we skip the stock count?
> **Lakshmi:** No, Bunty. A signature is evidence. It is not proof.

A **written representation** is that letter: management's written statement to the auditor, confirming certain matters or supporting other evidence. It counts as audit evidence, some of which the auditor cannot get any other way: only management can say that it gave the auditor everything and recorded every transaction. But it is never enough on its own: a written promise still sits beside vouchers, counts and confirmations.

Two representations are compulsory: that management has prepared the financial statements as its responsibility requires, and that it has given the auditor all information and recorded all transactions. If management refuses them, or the auditor comes to doubt management's integrity so much that they cannot be believed, the audit cannot end in an opinion: the auditor **disclaims**. The letter is dated as close as possible to the report, never after it, and covers every period the report covers.

::: analogy The consent form before surgery
The hospital has your X-rays and blood reports, yet the surgeon still asks you to sign a form saying you have disclosed every medicine you take and every allergy you have. No test can reveal what you did not mention. The form does not replace the X-ray. You sign it on the morning of the operation, not a month earlier, because things change. And if you refuse to sign, there is no operation.
:::

::: key
Written representations are necessary audit evidence but never sufficient on their own. The two about management's responsibilities (preparing the financial statements; providing all information and recording all transactions) are compulsory: if they are not provided, or are unreliable because of doubt about management's integrity, the auditor disclaims an opinion.
:::

## How it works

### 1. What a written representation is

::: definition Written representation
A written statement by management provided to the auditor to confirm certain matters or to support other audit evidence. Written representations in this context do not include financial statements, the assertions therein, or supporting books and records (SM 7.28).
:::

SA 580, "Written Representations", deals with the auditor's responsibility to obtain written representations from management and, where appropriate, those charged with governance (SM 7.29).

### 2. Why they are audit evidence, and why they are not enough

Audit evidence is all the information the auditor uses in arriving at the conclusions on which the opinion is based ([[aud.evid.sa500|SA 500]]). So (SM 7.29):

- Written representations are **necessary information** that the auditor requires in connection with the audit; so, ==similar to responses to inquiries, written representations are audit evidence==.
- They are an important source of audit evidence. If management modifies or does not provide the requested representations, it may alert the auditor to the possibility that one or more significant issues may exist.
- A request for **written**, rather than oral, representations in many cases prompts management to consider the matters more rigorously, enhancing the quality of the representations.
- But although they provide necessary audit evidence, they **do not provide sufficient appropriate audit evidence on their own** about any of the matters with which they deal.
- And reliable written representations do not affect the nature or extent of other audit evidence the auditor obtains about management's responsibilities or about specific assertions.

### 3. The objectives

The objectives of the auditor are (SM 7.29–7.30):

- (a) to obtain written representations from management and, where appropriate, those charged with governance that they believe that they have fulfilled their responsibility for the preparation of the financial statements and for the completeness of the information provided to the auditor;
- (b) to support other audit evidence relevant to the financial statements or specific assertions in them by means of written representations, if determined necessary by the auditor or required by other SAs; and
- (c) to respond appropriately to written representations provided, or if those requested are not provided.

**From whom.** The auditor shall request written representations from management with appropriate responsibilities for the financial statements and knowledge of the matters concerned (SM 7.30).

### 4. The two representations about management's responsibilities

**(I) Preparation of the financial statements.** The auditor shall request management to provide a written representation that it has fulfilled its responsibility for the preparation of the financial statements in accordance with the applicable financial reporting framework, including where relevant their fair presentation, as set out in the terms of the audit engagement (SM 7.30).

**(II) Information provided and completeness of transactions.** The auditor shall request management to provide a written representation that (SM 7.31):

- (a) it has provided the auditor with all relevant information and access as agreed in the terms of the audit engagement; and
- (b) all transactions have been recorded and are reflected in the financial statements.

**Management may ask its own experts first.** Management may make inquiries of others who participate in preparing the financial statements, including people with specialised knowledge: an **actuary** responsible for actuarially determined measurements, **staff engineers** with knowledge of environmental liability measurements, and **internal counsel** for provisions for legal claims (SM 7.31).

**"To the best of our knowledge and belief."** Management may include qualifying language to that effect. It is reasonable for the auditor to accept such wording ==if the auditor is satisfied that the representations are being made by those with appropriate responsibilities and knowledge== of the matters included. To reinforce informed representations, the auditor may ask management to confirm in them that it has made such inquiries as it considered appropriate (SM 7.31).

**Why these two are necessary.** Other audit evidence that management has fulfilled these responsibilities is not sufficient without management's confirmation that it believes it has done so, because the auditor cannot judge solely on other evidence whether management has prepared the financial statements and provided information on the basis of the agreed acknowledgement of its responsibilities. For example, the auditor could not conclude that management has provided all relevant information agreed in the terms of the engagement without asking, and receiving confirmation, that it has (SM 7.32).

**Reconfirming the acknowledgement.** These representations draw on the responsibilities management acknowledged in the terms of the engagement ([[aud.ethics.sa210|SA 210]]). The auditor may also ask management to reconfirm its acknowledgement and understanding of those responsibilities. This is particularly appropriate when (SM 7.32):

- those who signed the terms of the audit engagement on behalf of the entity no longer have the relevant responsibilities;
- the terms of the audit engagement were prepared in a previous year;
- there is any indication that management misunderstands those responsibilities; or
- changes in circumstances make it appropriate to do so.

Management's responsibilities shall be described in these representations in the manner in which they are described in the terms of the audit engagement (SM 7.32).

### 5. Other written representations

**Required by other SAs.** Other SAs require the auditor to request written representations (in ICAI's illustrative letter: SA 450, SA 550 and SA 560). If, in addition, the auditor determines that one or more are necessary to support other audit evidence, the auditor shall request them (SM 7.33).

**About the financial statements.** Such representations supplement, but do not form part of, the representation on management's responsibilities. They may include (SM 7.33):

- whether the selection and application of accounting policies are appropriate; and
- whether matters such as the following, where relevant under the framework, have been recognised, measured, presented or disclosed in accordance with it:
  - plans or intentions that may affect the carrying value or classification of assets and liabilities;
  - liabilities, both actual and contingent;
  - title to, or control over, assets, the liens or encumbrances on assets, and assets pledged as collateral; and
  - aspects of laws, regulations and contractual agreements that may affect the financial statements, including non-compliance.

**About information provided.** The auditor may ask for a representation that management has communicated to the auditor all deficiencies in internal control of which it is aware (SM 7.33).

**About specific assertions.** When obtaining evidence about, or evaluating, management's **judgments and intentions**, the auditor may consider (SM 7.34):

- the entity's past history in carrying out its stated intentions;
- the entity's reasons for choosing a particular course of action;
- the entity's ability to pursue a specific course of action; and
- the existence or lack of any other information obtained during the audit that may be inconsistent with management's judgment or intent.

The auditor may also request representations about specific assertions, to support an understanding of management's judgment or intent, or of the completeness of an assertion. For example, if management's intent is important to the valuation basis for investments, sufficient appropriate evidence may not be obtainable without a written representation about its intentions; even then, the representation does not on its own provide sufficient appropriate evidence for that assertion (SM 7.34).

### 6. Date, period and form

**Date.** The date of the written representations shall be ==as near as practicable to, but not after, the date of the auditor's report==. Because they are necessary audit evidence, the opinion cannot be expressed, and the report cannot be dated, before the date of the written representations. And because the auditor is concerned with events up to the date of the report that may require adjustment or disclosure, they are dated as near to it as practicable (SM 7.34).

**Period.** They shall be for all financial statements and period(s) referred to in the auditor's report, because management needs to reaffirm that its earlier representations for prior periods remain appropriate. Where current management was not present during all the periods, and says it cannot represent for them, that does not diminish its responsibilities for the financial statements as a whole: the requirement to request representations covering the whole of the relevant period(s) still applies (SM 7.34–7.35).

**Form.** The written representations shall be in the form of a **representation letter addressed to the auditor**. If law or regulation requires management to make written public statements about its responsibilities, and the auditor determines that they provide some or all of the required representations, those matters need not be repeated in the letter (SM 7.35).

### 7. When representations are doubtful, or refused

**Doubt about reliability.** If the auditor has concerns about the competence, integrity, ethical values or diligence of management, or about its commitment to or enforcement of these, the auditor shall determine the effect of those concerns on the reliability of representations and audit evidence in general. If representations are **inconsistent with other audit evidence**, the auditor shall perform audit procedures to attempt to resolve the matter; if it remains unresolved, the auditor shall reconsider the assessment of management's competence, integrity, ethical values or diligence and determine the effect on reliability. If the auditor concludes the representations are not reliable, the auditor shall take appropriate actions, including determining the possible effect on the opinion under SA 705, having regard to the requirement of disclaimer (SM 7.37).

**Representations not provided.** If management does not provide one or more of the requested representations, the auditor shall (SM 7.38):

- (a) discuss the matter with management;
- (b) re-evaluate the integrity of management and evaluate the effect that this may have on the reliability of representations and audit evidence in general; and
- (c) take appropriate actions, including determining the possible effect on the opinion under SA 705, having regard to the requirement of disclaimer.

**When the disclaimer is required.** The auditor shall disclaim an opinion in accordance with [[aud.report.sa705|SA 705]] if (SM 7.38):

- (a) the auditor concludes that there is sufficient doubt about the integrity of management such that the representations about management's responsibilities (preparing the financial statements; information provided and completeness of transactions) are not reliable; or
- (b) management does not provide those representations.

## Formats and formulas

### ICAI's illustrative representation letter (SM 7.35–7.37)

*Reproduce the headings and the bullets in this order: January 2026 asked for the "Financial Statements" part for 5 marks.*

*On the letterhead of the entity*

To Iyer & Associates, Chartered Accountants. Date: 28th May, 2026.

Dear Sir,

This representation letter is provided in connection with your audit of the financial statements of Tapri Tea Co. Ltd for the year ended March 31, 2026 for the purpose of expressing an opinion as to whether the financial statements give a true and fair view in accordance with the applicable accounting standards in India.

We confirm that (to the best of our knowledge and belief, having made such inquiries as we considered necessary for the purpose of appropriately informing ourselves):

**Financial Statements**

- We have fulfilled our responsibilities, as set out in the terms of the audit engagement dated [date], for the preparation of the financial statements in accordance with financial reporting Standards, in particular, the financial statements give a true and fair view in accordance with the applicable accounting standards in India.
- Significant assumptions used by us in making accounting estimates, including those measured at fair value, are reasonable.
- Related party relationships and transactions have been appropriately accounted for and disclosed in accordance with the requirements of applicable accounting standards in India. (SA 550)
- All events subsequent to the date of the financial statements and for which applicable accounting standards in India require adjustment or disclosure have been adjusted or disclosed. (SA 560)
- The effects of uncorrected misstatements are immaterial, both individually and in the aggregate, to the financial statements as a whole. A list of the uncorrected misstatements is attached to the representation letter. (SA 450)

**Information provided**

- We have provided you with:
  - Access to all information of which we are aware that is relevant to the preparation of the financial statements such as records, documentation and other matters;
  - Additional information that you have requested from us for the purpose of the audit; and
  - Unrestricted access to persons within the entity from whom you determined it necessary to obtain audit evidence.
- All transactions have been recorded in the accounting records and are reflected in the financial statements.
- We have disclosed to you the results of our assessment of the risk that the financial statements may be materially misstated as a result of fraud.
- We have disclosed to you all information in relation to fraud or suspected fraud that we are aware of and that affects the entity and involves:
  - Management;
  - Employees who have significant roles in internal control; or
  - Others where the fraud could have a material effect on the financial statements.
- We have disclosed to you all information in relation to allegations of fraud, or suspected fraud, affecting the entity's financial statements communicated by employees, former employees, analysts, regulators or others.
- We have disclosed to you all known instances of non-compliance or suspected non-compliance with laws and regulations whose effects should be considered when preparing financial statements.
- We have disclosed to you the identity of the entity's related parties and all the related party relationships and transactions of which we are aware. (SA 550)

Chief Financial Officer

::: tip
SA 580's own two representations are the first bullet under "Financial Statements" and the first two under "Information provided". The rest supports other evidence.
:::

## Worked examples

:::: example Dev edits the letter
Iyer & Associates will sign the auditor's report on Tapri's 2025-26 financial statements on 28 May 2026. On the morning of 28 May, Dev Malhotra, the CFO, returns the draft representation letter signed and dated that day, with two edits:

(i) The first representation now reads: "The financial statements have been prepared by the accounts department and audited by Iyer & Associates." Dev's note: "Shorter, and it says who did what."

(ii) He has added: "The balance of ₹2.60 crore due from Sagar Hotels Pvt Ltd, outstanding for 210 days, is fully recoverable." His note: "So your team can stop chasing the hotel for a confirmation and a payment plan."

Required: Comment on Dev's edits and advise Lakshmi under SA 580.

::: answer Model answer
**(i) The responsibilities representation.** As per SA 580, the auditor shall request management to provide a written representation that it has fulfilled its responsibility for the preparation of the financial statements in accordance with the applicable financial reporting framework, including, where relevant, their fair presentation, as set out in the terms of the audit engagement; management's responsibilities shall be described in the manner in which they are described in the terms of the audit engagement (SM 7.30, 7.32). Dev's sentence does neither: it says who prepared the statements and, by naming the auditors, suggests that the responsibility is shared with them. That is an indication that management misunderstands its responsibilities, a circumstance in which it is particularly appropriate to ask management to reconfirm its acknowledgement and understanding of those responsibilities in the written representations (SM 7.32). Lakshmi should restore the required wording. If management does not provide it, she shall discuss the matter with management, re-evaluate management's integrity and the effect on the reliability of representations and audit evidence in general, and, this being a representation about management's responsibilities, disclaim an opinion in accordance with SA 705 (SM 7.38).

**(ii) The receivable.** The auditor may request other written representations to support other audit evidence (SM 7.33), but although written representations provide necessary audit evidence, they do not provide sufficient appropriate audit evidence on their own about any of the matters with which they deal (SM 7.29). A balance outstanding for 210 days needs evidence of its recoverability: the team should continue its procedures, such as the confirmation, receipts after the year-end, correspondence with the hotel and its payment plan. If that evidence is inconsistent with the representation, Lakshmi shall perform audit procedures to attempt to resolve the matter and, if it remains unresolved, reconsider her assessment of management's competence, integrity, ethical values or diligence and its effect on the reliability of representations and audit evidence in general (SM 7.37). Any shortfall in the allowance for the balance is a misstatement to be evaluated.

**Conclusion.** The first edit must be reversed, failing which a disclaimer of opinion follows. The second may stay as a supporting representation, but it does not end the team's work on the Sagar Hotels balance.
:::

:::: example Farah inherits a mistake
Iyer & Associates audit Brahmaputra Leaf Ltd, a tea estate supplying Tapri, for 2025-26. Its managing director resigned on 30 September 2025, and Ms. Farah Khan took charge on 1 October. Two matters arise at the end of the audit:

(a) Green-leaf purchases of ₹1.40 crore made in 2024-25 were left out of that year's books. They are recorded in 2025-26 and separately disclosed as a prior period item in the statement of profit and loss. Farah signs the representation letter for the whole of 2025-26 but deletes the specific representation on this item: "That was the old MD's mistake. I will not sign for it."

(b) Bonds of ₹6.00 crore are shown as long-term investments; their market value at 31 March 2026 is ₹5.10 crore. Farah readily signs a representation that the company will hold them to maturity in 2031. The records show that the company sold bonds of this kind before maturity in each of the last three years to pay plucking-season wages, and its cash flow forecast shows a shortfall of ₹2.00 crore in July 2026.

Required: Advise the auditor on (a) and (b) under SA 580.

::: answer Model answer
**(a) The prior period item.** As per SA 580, the auditor shall request written representations for all periods referred to in the auditor's opinion, and, as SA 710 requires, shall also obtain a specific written representation regarding any prior period item that is separately disclosed in the current year's statement of profit and loss (SM 8.59). Current management may assert that it was not in place when the matter arose, but this fact does not diminish its responsibilities for the financial statements as a whole, and the requirement to request the representations from it still applies (SM 7.35). The representation is requested from Farah, who may make the inquiries she needs to give it. If she does not provide it, the auditor shall discuss the matter with management; re-evaluate the integrity of management and evaluate the effect that this may have on the reliability of representations and audit evidence in general; and take appropriate actions, including determining the possible effect on the opinion in the auditor's report in accordance with SA 705, having regard to the requirement of disclaimer of opinion (SM 7.38).

**(b) The bonds.** Their classification as long-term investments rests on management's intention. When evaluating judgments and intentions, the auditor may consider the entity's past history in carrying out its stated intentions, its reasons for choosing a particular course of action, its ability to pursue that course, and any other information obtained during the audit that may be inconsistent with management's judgment or intent (SM 7.34). Here the past history (sales before maturity in each of the last three years) and the ability (a shortfall of ₹2.00 crore in July 2026) both point the other way. Where management's intent is important to the valuation basis for investments, a written representation about that intention may be necessary, but it does not provide sufficient appropriate audit evidence on its own for that assertion (SM 7.34). As it is inconsistent with other audit evidence, the auditor shall perform audit procedures to attempt to resolve the matter, for example by asking how the July shortfall will be funded and reading the board minutes. If the matter remains unresolved, the auditor shall reconsider the assessment of management's competence, integrity, ethical values or diligence and determine the effect on the reliability of representations and audit evidence in general (SM 7.37).

**Conclusion.** (a) The specific representation is required from current management; if it is not provided, the auditor proceeds as above. (b) The signature does not settle the bonds: if the intention is not supported, neither is their classification as long-term nor the valuation basis that rests on it, with market value ₹0.90 crore (₹6.00 crore − ₹5.10 crore) below cost. That is a possible misstatement to be evaluated under SA 450 and, if material, SA 705.
:::

:::: example "Read our annual report instead"
Iyer & Associates will sign their report on Tapri's 2025-26 financial statements on 28 May 2026. Two weeks earlier, Dev tells Lakshmi that Tapri will not sign a representation letter this year: "Our annual report contains a statement by the directors, required by law, that they are responsible for preparing the financial statements and that the statements give a true and fair view. It is public and signed, so it is stronger than your private letter. Anything else, Kesar will confirm orally at the closing meeting."

Required: Advise Lakshmi under SA 580.

::: answer Model answer
**Relevant provision.** As per SA 580, the written representations shall be in the form of a representation letter addressed to the auditor. If law or regulation requires management to make written public statements about its responsibilities, and the auditor determines that such statements provide some or all of the representations required regarding management's responsibilities, the relevant matters covered by such statements need not be included in the representation letter (SM 7.35). The written representations shall be dated as near as practicable to, but not after, the date of the auditor's report (SM 7.34).

**Facts and analysis.**

1. **The directors' statement.** Lakshmi must determine whether it provides any of the required representations. At most it covers the representation on the preparation of the financial statements, and only if it matches management's responsibilities as described in the terms of the audit engagement (SM 7.32); the matters it covers may then be left out of the letter.
2. **What it does not cover.** It says nothing of the second required representation: that management has provided the auditor with all relevant information and access as agreed in the terms of the audit engagement, and that all transactions have been recorded and are reflected in the financial statements (SM 7.31). Nor does it give the representations other SAs require, such as those on subsequent events (SA 560), uncorrected misstatements (SA 450) and related parties (SA 550) (SM 7.36). These must be in a letter addressed to Iyer & Associates.
3. **Kesar's oral confirmation.** Responses to inquiries are audit evidence, but SA 580 asks for representations in writing, and a request for written, rather than oral, representations may prompt management to consider the matters more rigorously (SM 7.29). An oral confirmation is not a written representation.

**Conclusion.** Lakshmi should request a representation letter addressed to the firm and dated as near as practicable to, but not after, 28 May 2026, leaving out only the matters she determines the directors' statement covers. If Tapri still does not provide the representations about management's responsibilities, she shall discuss the matter with management, re-evaluate management's integrity, and disclaim an opinion in accordance with SA 705 (SM 7.38).
:::

### A letter is not a substitute for the work

ICAI's Test Your Understanding in the ethics chapter describes auditors who relied only on a management representation letter for tax matters under appeal and performed no other procedures: a lack of **professional skepticism** (SM 11.14, 11.43; see [[aud.nature.sa200|SA 200]]). The representation is one brick; the audit is the wall.

::: exam How the marks are earned
ICAI's suggested answers quote the requirement nearly word for word ("as near as practicable to, but not after", "does not diminish such persons' responsibilities") and apply it to the facts in a sentence or two. In change-of-management questions, the date and its reason, the period and its reason, and the conclusion on management's stand each earn marks. On a continued refusal, MTP May 2026 Series II Q6(a) ends "the auditor should consider appropriate modification in the audit report"; for the representations on management's responsibilities, SA 580 itself names that modification: a disclaimer of opinion (SM 7.38).
:::

::: trick Two must-haves, one must-not
The **two must-haves** are the responsibilities representations: preparation, and information plus completeness. Refused or unreliable for integrity reasons: **disclaimer**, never "qualified". The **one must-not** is the date: never after the report. Condition: other representations that are refused lead to SA 705's judgment on the opinion, not automatically to a disclaimer.
:::

## Answer frame

### A case question on written representations

1. **Relevant provision**: "As per SA 580, …", the requirement the facts turn on (date, period, form, qualifying language, the two required representations, reliability, refusal, disclaimer).
2. **Facts of the case**: who refused or wrote what, and on what date.
3. **Analysis**: is it one of the two responsibilities representations? Is the date within the rule? Does a change of management change anything (it does not)? Is there an inconsistency to resolve?
4. **Conclusion**: what management must provide, what the auditor must do (discuss, re-evaluate integrity), and the opinion if it is not provided (disclaimer, for the responsibilities representations).

### "Explain the date of and period covered by written representations"

| Point | What earns the mark |
|---|---|
| Date | As near as practicable to, but not after, the date of the auditor's report |
| Why | Necessary evidence: the opinion cannot be expressed, nor the report dated, before it; and events up to the report date matter |
| Period | All financial statements and period(s) referred to in the report |
| Why | Management reaffirms that earlier representations for prior periods remain appropriate |
| New management | Not being present does not diminish its responsibilities for the financial statements as a whole; the requirement still applies |

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: the qualified opinion for a missing letter
Management refuses the representation that it has fulfilled its responsibility for preparing the financial statements, and Bunty writes "qualified opinion, disagreement with management". **Fix:** for the two responsibilities representations, SA 580 requires a **disclaimer of opinion** in accordance with SA 705. ICAI's May 2026 MCQ 4, on a refused representation about receivables, lists Bunty's answer as the action not to take even there.
:::

::: mistake "Management has confirmed it, so no more work"
Treating a written representation as sufficient evidence. **Fix:** written representations are necessary audit evidence but do not provide sufficient appropriate audit evidence on their own; reliable ones do not reduce other evidence the auditor obtains.
:::

::: mistake "We were not here then"
Accepting representations from new management for only part of the year. **Fix:** representations cover all periods referred to in the report; a change of management does not diminish the responsibilities of current management for the financial statements as a whole.
:::

## Practice

::: quiz Check yourself
Q: Case scenario. CA Meera signed and dated her auditor's report on Malwa Biscuits Ltd on 12 August 2026, having been told the representation letter "is on its way". The directors signed the letter the next day and dated it 13 August 2026. Which of the following is correct?
- [ ] It is acceptable: a gap of one day is immaterial
- [ ] It is acceptable, since the letter is addressed to the auditor and covers all periods in the report
- [x] It is not acceptable: written representations shall not be dated after the auditor's report, because the opinion cannot be expressed, and the report cannot be dated, before the date of the written representations
- [ ] It is not acceptable: the letter should be dated at least 15 days before the report
> SA 580: as near as practicable to, but not after, the date of the auditor's report. Here the opinion was expressed before necessary audit evidence existed. No "15 days" rule exists; an early letter would also miss events up to the report date.

Q: Which of the following representations is required by SA 580 itself, so that its refusal leads to a disclaimer of opinion?
- [ ] Significant assumptions used in accounting estimates are reasonable
- [x] All transactions have been recorded and are reflected in the financial statements
- [ ] Management intends to hold the investments to maturity
- [ ] All deficiencies in internal control known to management have been communicated
> The two required representations are on preparation of the financial statements, and on information provided and completeness of transactions. The others support other evidence.

Q: Which of the following statements about written representations is correct? Select all that apply.
- [x] They are audit evidence, similar to responses to inquiries
- [x] A request for written rather than oral representations may prompt management to consider the matters more rigorously
- [ ] Reliable written representations reduce the other audit evidence the auditor needs
- [ ] They include the financial statements and the supporting books of account
> Reliable representations do not reduce other evidence, and the definition excludes financial statements, assertions and books.

Q: Case scenario. Shree Spices Ltd's representation letter opens "to the best of our knowledge and belief". Because the CFO is travelling, it is signed by the office administrator, who had no part in preparing the financial statements and does not know the matters represented. Which is correct?
- [ ] Accept it: qualifying language is always acceptable
- [ ] Accept it: any officer of the company may sign the representations
- [x] Do not accept it as it stands: the qualifying wording is acceptable only if the auditor is satisfied that the representations are made by those with appropriate responsibilities and knowledge of the matters, so request them from such management
- [ ] Reject it for the words "to the best of our knowledge and belief", whoever signs it
> The words are not the problem; the signatory is. SA 580 requires representations from management with appropriate responsibilities for the financial statements and knowledge of the matters concerned, and accepts "to the best of our knowledge and belief" on that condition.

Q: Indus Pipes Ltd's engagement letter was signed in 2023 by its then CFO, who has since left; a new CFO took charge in 2025. What may the auditor do about management's responsibilities when obtaining the written representations for 2025-26?
- [ ] Nothing more: the 2023 letter binds the company until the auditor changes
- [x] Ask management to reconfirm, in the written representations, its acknowledgement and understanding of those responsibilities
- [ ] Ask the former CFO to sign the 2025-26 letter, since he signed the terms
- [ ] Obtain a fresh engagement letter instead, which makes the written representations unnecessary
> Reconfirmation is particularly appropriate when those who signed the terms no longer have the relevant responsibilities, or the terms were prepared in a previous year: both apply here. Representations come from current management, and nothing replaces the two required ones.

## Recap

- Written representation: a written statement by management to the auditor to confirm certain matters or support other evidence; not the financial statements, assertions or books.
- Necessary audit evidence, like responses to inquiries; never sufficient on its own; reliable ones do not reduce other evidence.
- Objectives: obtain the responsibilities representations, support other evidence, respond appropriately to representations given or refused.
- The two required representations: responsibility for preparing the financial statements fulfilled; all information and access provided and all transactions recorded.
- "To the best of our knowledge and belief" is acceptable if made by those with appropriate responsibilities and knowledge.
- Date as near as practicable to, but not after, the report date; cover all periods in the report, even after a change of management; form: a letter addressed to the auditor.
- Inconsistent: resolve, then reconsider integrity. Refused: discuss, re-evaluate integrity, SA 705.
- Disclaimer when the responsibilities representations are refused, or unreliable because of sufficient doubt about management's integrity. (No signature, no surgery.)

## Flashcards

Q:: SA 580: what is a written representation?
A:: A written statement by management provided to the auditor to confirm certain matters or to support other audit evidence. It does not include financial statements, the assertions therein, or supporting books and records.

Q:: SA 580: are written representations sufficient appropriate audit evidence?
A:: No. They are necessary audit evidence, but they do not provide sufficient appropriate audit evidence on their own about any of the matters with which they deal.

Q:: SA 580: what are the objectives of the auditor?
A:: (a) Obtain representations that management has fulfilled its responsibility for preparing the financial statements and for the completeness of information provided; (b) support other audit evidence where necessary or required by other SAs; (c) respond appropriately to representations provided or not provided.

Q:: SA 580: the two required representations about management's responsibilities?
A:: (I) It has fulfilled its responsibility for the preparation of the financial statements per the framework, as set out in the terms of engagement; (II) it has provided all relevant information and access as agreed, and all transactions have been recorded and are reflected in the financial statements.

Q:: SA 580: when may the auditor accept "to the best of our knowledge and belief"?
A:: When the auditor is satisfied that the representations are being made by those with appropriate responsibilities and knowledge of the matters included.

Q:: SA 580: when is it particularly appropriate to ask management to reconfirm its acknowledgement of responsibilities?
A:: When the signatories of the engagement terms no longer have the responsibilities; the terms were prepared in a previous year; management seems to misunderstand them; or changes in circumstances make it appropriate.

Q:: SA 580: what four things may the auditor consider when evaluating management's judgments and intentions?
A:: Past history in carrying out stated intentions; reasons for the course of action; ability to pursue it; other information inconsistent with the judgment or intent.

Q:: SA 580: what is the date of the written representations?
A:: As near as practicable to, but not after, the date of the auditor's report on the financial statements.

Q:: SA 580: which periods must written representations cover?
A:: All financial statements and period(s) referred to in the auditor's report, even if current management was not present during all of them.

Q:: SA 580: what is the form of written representations?
A:: A representation letter addressed to the auditor (matters already in written public statements required by law need not be repeated).

Q:: SA 580: what must the auditor do if requested representations are not provided?
A:: Discuss the matter with management; re-evaluate management's integrity and its effect on the reliability of representations and evidence in general; take appropriate actions, including the possible effect on the opinion under SA 705, having regard to the disclaimer requirement.

Q:: SA 580: when shall the auditor disclaim an opinion?
A:: When there is sufficient doubt about management's integrity such that the representations about its responsibilities are not reliable, or when management does not provide those representations.
