## In plain words

### Three kinds of money in Tapri's balance sheet

Tapri, listed since 2024, raised more money this year for its cold-chain fleet. Kesar has ideas about spending it.

> **Kesar:** ₹60 crore over the face value, Lakshmi-ji. Premium! Our own money, and I have ideas for every rupee.
> **Lakshmi:** The Companies Act keeps a short list for that money, Kesar-ji. Each idea will have to find its name on it.
> **Bunty:** And an idea that is not on the list, Ma'am?
> **Lakshmi:** Stays an idea.

This side of the balance sheet holds three kinds of money: **share capital** (what the owners put in), **reserves and surplus** (profits kept back, plus items such as securities premium) and **borrowings** (money from banks, debenture holders and depositors that must be repaid).

Each is audited for a different fear. Share capital and reserves are hedged in by the Companies Act, 2013, so the auditor checks **legality and authority**: authorised, within the authorised capital, at a lawful price, and used only as the law permits. Borrowings are liabilities, so the fear is **omission**: a loan left out, a covenant broken, next year's instalment shown as long-term.

::: analogy The dhaba, the gullak and the sahukar
A dhaba owner has three tins of money: what the family put in (capital), the gullak of savings from good seasons (reserves), and the sahukar's loan (borrowings). An honest accountant checks that the family's money was put in as agreed, that the gullak is opened only for what the family allowed, and above all that every sahukar's loan is in the book with its interest and conditions, because a forgotten sahukar always comes back.
:::

::: key
For share capital and reserves the auditor's question is "was it authorised and lawful?" (resolutions, Memorandum, sections 52 to 54, 66 and 68). For borrowings it is "is every claim recorded, and correctly classified?" (board minutes, loan agreements, confirmations from lenders, covenants).
:::

## How it works

### 1. The assertions for these captions

Balance sheet captions carry the assertions of existence, completeness, cut-off, valuation, rights and obligations, and presentation and disclosure (SM 5.8–5.10; see [[aud.evid.assertions|assertions]]). The Study Material arranges the procedures below under existence, completeness, valuation and disclosure.

### 2. Share capital: the core procedures

Applications and allotment need careful attention: each party must have performed its part of the contract in time, and underwriting contracts are verified (SM 5.11–5.12). To establish **existence, completeness and valuation** of share capital (SM 5.12–5.13):

1. Tally the period-end share capital (authorised, issued and paid up) to the previous year's audited financial statements.
2. If there is **no change**, obtain a written confirmation or representation from the **Company Secretary** that the capital structure did not change.
3. If there is a change, verify that paid-up capital is **within the authorised capital**, examining the Memorandum of Association (MOA).
4. Obtain certified copies of the board and shareholders' **resolutions** authorising the increase or decrease in authorised or paid-up capital.
5. For a **fresh issue**, check compliance with the Companies Act, 2013 on return of allotment, minimum subscription, minimum application money, a separate bank account, and underwriting commission (section 40).
6. Check that **no shares were issued at a discount** (section 53).
7. Check whether shares were issued for cash or for **consideration other than cash** (to promoters for services, underwriters for commission).
8. Check compliance with SEBI regulations and guidelines.
9. Verify the forms filed with the MCA (SH-7, notice of alteration of share capital; PAS-3, return of allotment) and the RBI (FC-GPR, for FDI by a non-resident), agreeing the number of securities and issue price.
10. On an increase, verify that the fee and stamp duty payable to the MCA were accurately calculated.

**Shares issued at a premium (section 52).** When shares are issued at a premium, for cash or otherwise, the premium is transferred to a **securities premium account**; the Act prescribes no restriction or condition on issuing at a premium. The provisions on reduction of share capital apply as if the securities premium account were paid-up share capital (SM 5.13–5.14). The account may be applied:

- (a) towards the issue of unissued shares to members as fully paid **bonus shares**;
- (b) in writing off the **preliminary expenses** of the company;
- (c) in writing off the **expenses of, or the commission paid or discount allowed on, any issue of shares or debentures**;
- (d) in providing for the **premium payable on redemption** of redeemable preference shares or debentures;
- (e) for the **purchase of its own shares** or other securities under section 68 (buy-back).

A class of companies that may be prescribed, whose financial statements comply with the accounting standards prescribed for that class under section 133, may apply it only (a) in paying up unissued equity shares as fully paid bonus shares; (b) in writing off the expenses of, or commission paid or discount allowed on, any issue of equity shares; or (c) for buy-back under section 68 (SM 5.14–5.15). The auditor verifies (i) that the premium has been transferred to the securities premium account and (ii) that ==any application of it is only for the purposes mentioned== (SM 5.15).

**Shares issued at a discount (section 53)** (SM 5.15–5.16):

- (1) A company shall not issue shares at a discount, except sweat equity shares under section 54.
- (2) Any share issued at a discounted price shall be **void**.
- (2A) Notwithstanding (1) and (2), a company may issue shares at a discount to its **creditors** when its debt is converted into shares under a statutory resolution plan or debt restructuring scheme in accordance with RBI guidelines, directions or regulations under the RBI Act, 1934 or the Banking (Regulation) Act, 1949.
- (3) On default, the company and every officer in default are liable to a penalty which may extend to the amount raised at a discount or **five lakh rupees, whichever is less**, and the company must **refund all monies received with interest at twelve per cent per annum** from the date of issue.

The auditor checks the movement in share capital, and for every issue reads the minutes of directors' and shareholders' meetings authorising it and the issue price, to verify that no shares were issued at a discount, and whether any discounted issue to creditors falls within (2A) (SM 5.16).

**Sweat equity shares (section 54).** "Sweat equity shares" mean equity shares issued by the company to **employees or directors** at a **discount** or for **consideration other than cash**, for providing know-how or making available rights in the nature of intellectual property rights or value additions, by whatever name called. The auditor verifies that they are **of a class of shares already issued**, and that:

- (a) the issue is authorised by a **special resolution**;
- (b) the resolution specifies the number of shares, the current market price, the consideration, if any, and the class or classes of directors or employees to whom they are issued;
- (c) omitted;
- (d) if listed, they are issued as per SEBI regulations, and if not, as per the rules prescribed.

The holders rank **pari passu** with other equity shareholders (SM 5.16–5.17).

**Reduction of capital (section 66) and buy-back (section 68).** Accumulated losses, assets of doubtful value or surplus capital may require a reduction. The auditor examines compliance with section 66: a **special resolution** at a properly convened meeting, authority in the Articles, no default on deposits, and the **Tribunal's order** filed with the Registrar (all eleven checks under "Under the hood"). Section 66 does not apply to a buy-back, which must comply with section 68 (SM 5.18–5.20).

### 4. Reserves and surplus

**Reserves** are the amounts appropriated out of profits that are not intended to meet any liability, contingency, commitment or diminution in the value of assets known to exist at the balance sheet date. They are a vital source of internal finance (SM 5.24).

A capital reserve can generally be used for writing down fictitious assets or losses or (subject to the Articles) for issuing bonus shares if it is **realised**; but securities premium and the capital redemption reserve can be used only for the purposes in sections 52 and 55 respectively (SM 5.24–5.25).

The procedures for **existence, completeness and valuation** (SM 5.25–5.26):

- Trace and tally the opening balance to the previous year's audited financial statements.
- Trace the movement in the profit and loss balance to the surplus or deficit as per the statement of profit and loss, and in the statement of changes in equity.
- Verify the **board's resolution recommending** the dividend and the **shareholders' resolution declaring** it. Under AS 4 (Revised) or Ind AS 10, dividends proposed or declared after the balance sheet date are **not a liability** at that date, but are disclosed.
- For securities premium, obtain the board's resolution for the issue above nominal value, and see that any utilisation is only for section 52 purposes.

### 5. Borrowings

**Liabilities** are the financial obligations of an enterprise other than owners' funds; verifying them is as important as verifying assets, since an omitted, understated or overstated liability means the statements are not true and fair. **Borrowings** are monies from external sources such as bank loans, debentures and public fixed deposits (SM 5.27).

**Existence: all borrowings represent valid claims by banks or other third parties** (SM 5.28):

1. Review **board minutes** for approval of new lending agreements and bond issues; significant debt commitments must be approved by the board.
2. Agree the details recorded (interest rate, nature, repayment terms) to the **loan agreement**; verify that borrowing limits imposed by agreements are not exceeded.
3. Obtain independent **balance confirmations** (SA 505) for all borrowings from the lenders.
4. Agree leases and hire purchase creditors to the underlying contracts.
5. For **debentures**, examine the **trust deed** for terms and dates of redemption, borrowing restrictions and compliance with covenants.
6. When debt is retired, ensure a **discharge** is received on the assets securing it.
7. Obtain a **written representation** that all recorded liabilities are valid claims by the lenders.

**Completeness: all borrowings are accounted for on a timely basis** (SM 5.28–5.30): obtain a schedule of short-term and long-term borrowings (opening, new debt and renewals, repaid, closing); look for additional debt in board minutes, significant contracts, lenders' confirmations and subsequent cash disbursements; trace closing balances to the ledger; review **transactions after the period end** for unrecorded liabilities (a fresh loan near the year end). The **direct confirmations** ask for all relevant information (interest rates, due dates, collateral and security), non-replies get reminders, and differences are reconciled and test-checked (see [[aud.evid.sa505|SA 505]]).

**Valuation: the liability is recorded at the correct amount** (SM 5.30–5.32):

- Accounting policies and methods for recording debt are appropriate and consistently applied; agree loan balances and payables to the loan agreement.
- Recompute interest and any discount or premium on redemption, and the amortisation of premium or discount.
- For foreign currency loans, check the closing exchange rate and recompute the restatement (AS 11).
- Read the loan and debt agreements: test compliance with **loan covenants**; where a provision has not been complied with, determine whether the debt should be classified as **current**; if the lender has waived enforcement, **obtain confirmation of the waiver from the lender**.
- Examine due dates for proper classification between long-term and short-term, and verify any instalments of long-term loans falling due within twelve months that are disclosed.
- Examine restrictive covenants and default provisions and ensure their disclosure.
- Examine the documents evidencing **charges**, and compliance with the statute on creation and registration of charges, and their disclosure.
- If the value of the security falls below the loan outstanding, examine whether the loan is classified as secured only to the extent of the market value of the security.
- Examine hire purchase agreements for the amounts outstanding and the security.
- Review borrowings from **related parties** for compliance with AS 18 or Ind AS 24.
- Verify that liabilities for bills discounted, bills negotiated and cheques discounted are correctly reflected and disclosed.
- Verify that the amount borrowed is within the **borrowing powers** in the Articles and Memorandum, and that the restrictions on the board's powers in **section 180** are not contravened; check compliance with sections 185 and 186.
- Examine the **purpose** of the borrowing and that it is not used against the interest of the company.
- For deposits accepted, examine compliance with the directives of the RBI or other appropriate authority.

## Formats and formulas

*These lists are what "state the disclosure requirements of Schedule III" questions ask for. Reproduce them in this order.*

### Share capital (Schedule III, Part I; SM 5.20–5.22)

For each class of share capital (different classes of preference shares treated separately):

- (a) the number and amount of shares authorised;
- (b) the number of shares issued, subscribed and fully paid, and subscribed but not fully paid;
- (c) par value per share;
- (d) a reconciliation of the number of shares outstanding at the beginning and at the end of the reporting period;
- (e) the rights, preferences and restrictions attaching to each class, including restrictions on the distribution of dividends and the repayment of capital;
- (f) shares held by the holding company or ultimate holding company, including shares held by their subsidiaries or associates, in aggregate;
- (g) shares held by each shareholder holding **more than 5 per cent** shares, specifying the number;
- (h) shares reserved for issue under options and contracts or commitments for the sale of shares or disinvestment, with terms and amounts;
- (i) for the five years immediately preceding the balance sheet date: (A) shares allotted as fully paid-up under contracts without payment in cash; (B) shares allotted as fully paid bonus shares; (C) shares bought back, each by aggregate number and class;
- (j) terms of securities convertible into equity or preference shares, with the earliest date of conversion, in descending order from the farthest date;
- (k) calls unpaid (showing the aggregate value of calls unpaid by directors and officers);
- (l) forfeited shares (amount originally paid-up);
- (m) **shareholding of promoters**:

*Shares held by promoters at the end of the year:*

| S. No. | Promoter name | No. of shares | % of total shares | % change in year |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | **Total** | | | |

Details are given separately for each class; the percentage change is on the number at the beginning of the year (or at the date of a first issue during the year). If an issue for a specific purpose is not fully used at the balance sheet date, a note shows how the unutilised amount is used or invested.

### Reserves and surplus (Schedule III, Part I; SM 5.26–5.27)

- (i) Classified as: (a) capital reserves; (b) capital redemption reserve; (c) securities premium; (d) debenture redemption reserve; (e) revaluation reserve; (f) share options outstanding account; (g) other reserves (nature, purpose and amount of each); (h) surplus, that is, the balance in the statement of profit and loss showing allocations and appropriations such as dividend, bonus shares and transfers to and from reserves (additions and deductions since the last balance sheet shown under each head).
- (ii) A reserve specifically represented by earmarked investments is termed a "**fund**".
- (iii) A debit balance in the statement of profit and loss is shown as a **negative figure under "Surplus"**; the balance of reserves and surplus, after adjusting it, is shown under "Reserves and Surplus" even if the result is negative.

### Borrowings (Schedule III, Part I; SM 5.32–5.34)

| Long-term borrowings | Short-term borrowings |
|---|---|
| (a) Bonds or debentures | (a) Loans repayable on demand: from banks; from other parties |
| (b) Term loans: from banks; from other parties | (b) Loans and advances from related parties |
| (c) Deferred payment liabilities | (c) Deposits |
| (d) Deposits | (d) Other loans and advances (specify nature) |
| (e) Loans and advances from related parties | |
| (f) Long-term maturities of finance lease obligations | |
| (g) Other loans and advances (specify nature) | |

For both: **secured and unsecured**, with the nature of security; the aggregate of loans **guaranteed by directors or others** under each head; the period and amount of **default** in repaying loans and interest. Long-term only: bonds or debentures (rate, redemption or conversion particulars) in descending order of maturity from the farthest date (for instalments, the first instalment's date); redeemed bonds or debentures that can be reissued; terms of repayment of term loans. Short-term only: **current maturities of long-term borrowings** separately. Other long-term liabilities: trade payables and others. Bank borrowings not used for their specific purpose: where they were used.

**Additional regulatory information** (SM 5.35–5.37): (1) for borrowings on the security of current assets, whether the **quarterly returns or statements** of current assets filed with the banks agree with the books, and if not, a summary of the reconciliation and reasons for material discrepancies; (2) if the company is a declared **wilful defaulter**, the date of declaration and details of defaults; (3) charges or satisfaction **yet to be registered with the Registrar** beyond the statutory period, with details and reasons; (4) **utilisation of borrowed funds and share premium** passed through intermediaries to ultimate beneficiaries (or received from funding parties): dates, amounts, details, and a declaration of compliance with FEMA, 1999 and the Companies Act and of no violation of the Prevention of Money-Laundering Act, 2002.

## Worked examples

::::: example Tapri's cold-chain issue
Tapri's authorised capital is ₹50 crore in equity shares of ₹10 each; 4,50,00,000 shares were issued and fully paid at 1 April 2025. In 2025-26 Tapri made a preferential issue of 40,00,000 equity shares of ₹10 each at ₹160 per share, fully paid, with issue expenses of ₹1.50 crore. Kesar proposes to use the securities premium to:

(i) write off the issue expenses;

(ii) pay the final dividend of ₹18 crore;

(iii) issue fully paid bonus shares in the ratio 1:10 on the enlarged capital;

(iv) provide for the premium of ₹2 crore payable on redemption of its debentures in 2027.

Required:

(a) Compute the share capital and securities premium arising on the issue.

(b) State the procedures Iyer & Associates should apply to the issue.

(c) Comment on each proposal.

:::: answer Model answer
**(a) Computation**

| Particulars | Shares | ₹ crore |
|---|---|---|
| Capital before the issue (₹10 each) | 4,50,00,000 | 45.00 |
| Preferential issue (₹10 each) | 40,00,000 | 4.00 |
| **Paid-up capital after the issue** | **4,90,00,000** | **49.00** |
| Securities premium: 40,00,000 × (₹160 − ₹10) | | 60.00 |

Paid-up capital of ₹49 crore is within the authorised capital of ₹50 crore.

**(b) Procedures.** Verify paid-up capital against the authorised capital in the MOA; obtain certified copies of the resolutions authorising the issue and its price; check Companies Act compliances (return of allotment, minimum application money, separate bank account) and SEBI regulations; check it is not at a discount; verify Form PAS-3 for the number of shares and price; verify the fee and stamp duty; and see that ₹60 crore is transferred to the securities premium account (section 52).

**(c) The proposals.** As per section 52, securities premium may be applied only for bonus shares, preliminary expenses, expenses of or commission or discount on an issue of shares or debentures, premium on redemption of preference shares or debentures, and buy-back (a prescribed class: equity bonus shares, expenses of an equity issue, buy-back).

1. **Issue expenses of ₹1.50 crore**: permitted (on both lists).
2. **Dividend of ₹18 crore**: **not permitted**; it is not a section 52 purpose.
3. **Bonus 1:10**: a permitted use, but 49,00,000 bonus shares (₹4.90 crore) take paid-up capital to ₹53.90 crore, above the authorised ₹50 crore. Authorised capital must first be increased by altering the capital clause of the MOA (an ordinary resolution, with notice to the Registrar in Form SH-7); the auditor verifies this.
4. **Premium on redemption of debentures, ₹2 crore**: **permitted** (use (d) of section 52); it is not on the prescribed class's shorter list, so the auditor confirms that Tapri is not of that class before accepting it.

After (i), (iii) and (iv), securities premium is ₹60.00 − ₹1.50 − ₹4.90 − ₹2.00 = ₹51.60 crore.

:::: example Sharbat's two discounts
Kesar's cousin runs Sharbat Ventures Ltd, an unlisted public company whose only shares so far are equity shares of ₹100 each with equal voting rights. In 2025-26 it made two issues below face value:

(i) Under a debt restructuring scheme drawn up by its lenders in accordance with the directions of the Reserve Bank of India, ₹6 crore owed to Pragati Bank was converted into 8,00,000 equity shares of ₹100 each, an issue price of ₹75 per share.

(ii) On 1 October 2025 it allotted 40,000 "Class B" equity shares of ₹100 each, carrying differential voting rights, a class it had never issued before, at ₹60 per share paid in cash, to its chief technology officer, a director, for making available the rights in its recipe-ordering app. A special resolution states the number of shares, the current market price, the consideration and the class of director.

Required: Advise the statutory auditor on both issues, computing the amounts involved for any issue that is not valid.

::: answer Model answer
**Relevant provisions.** As per section 53, a company shall not issue shares at a discount except sweat equity shares under section 54; any share issued at a discounted price is void. Notwithstanding this, a company may issue shares at a discount to its creditors when its debt is converted into shares under a statutory resolution plan or debt restructuring scheme in accordance with RBI guidelines, directions or regulations. On default, the company and every officer in default are liable to a penalty up to the amount raised through the issue at a discount or ₹5 lakh, whichever is less, and the company must refund all monies received with interest at 12% per annum from the date of issue. As per section 54, sweat equity shares are equity shares issued to employees or directors at a discount or for consideration other than cash, for know-how, intellectual property rights or value additions; the auditor verifies that they are **of a class of shares already issued**, that a special resolution with the prescribed particulars authorises them, and (if unlisted) that the prescribed rules are followed (SM 5.15–5.17).

**(i) Conversion of Pragati Bank's debt.** Pragati Bank is a creditor, and its debt of ₹6 crore (8,00,000 × ₹75) is converted under a debt restructuring scheme in accordance with RBI directions: the issue falls within the exception for creditors and is **valid**, although at a discount of ₹25 per share (₹2 crore in all). The auditor verifies the scheme and its basis in the RBI directions, reads the minutes of the directors' and shareholders' meetings authorising the issue and its price, agrees the number of shares and price to Form PAS-3, and checks that the debt is no longer shown as a borrowing.

**(ii) Class B shares to the director.** The recipient, the consideration and the resolution meet section 54, but the Class B shares are not of a class already issued, so the issue is **not** sweat equity. Nor is it a conversion of debt for a creditor. Shares issued at a discount outside these two exceptions are **void**:

- monies to be refunded: 40,000 × ₹60 = ₹24,00,000, with interest at 12% per annum from 1 October 2025: ₹24,00,000 × 12% × 6/12 = ₹1,44,000 to 31 March 2026;
- penalty on the company and every officer in default: up to the lesser of ₹24,00,000 and ₹5,00,000, that is, ₹5,00,000.

The auditor should see that the ₹24,00,000 is shown as a liability to be refunded with interest, not as share capital, and consider the reporting consequences if it is not.
:::

::::: example Narmada Bank's letters
Tapri's borrowings at 31 March 2026, as per the books:

(i) a term loan of ₹120 crore from Narmada Bank, secured by hypothecation of the cold-chain vehicles, repayable in 24 equal quarterly instalments of ₹5 crore from 30 June 2026; interest accrued but not due ₹1.80 crore;

(ii) a cash credit of ₹35 crore from Narmada Bank, repayable on demand and secured by hypothecation of stocks and book debts; the stock statement filed with the bank for the quarter ended 31 March 2026 shows inventories of ₹46 crore, against ₹38 crore in the books;

(iii) a foreign currency term loan of USD 20,00,000 from Lakeshore Bank, recorded at ₹83.20 per USD; the closing rate is ₹86.40 per USD;

(iv) the term loan agreement requires debt-equity not above 1.5; at 31 March 2026 it is 1.62, and Dev says the bank has orally agreed not to act.

Required:

(a) How should these be classified and disclosed?

(b) What should the auditor do about the covenant, and to confirm that the borrowings exist and are complete?

:::: answer Model answer
**(a) Classification and disclosure**

1. **Term loan**: the instalments due on 30 June, 30 September and 31 December 2026 and 31 March 2027 (₹20 crore) are current maturities, shown separately under short-term borrowings; ₹100 crore is a long-term term loan from a bank, secured, with the nature of security and terms of repayment stated. Interest accrued but not due goes to other current liabilities.
2. **Cash credit**: short-term borrowings, "loans repayable on demand from banks", secured, with the nature of security. Being a borrowing on the security of current assets, the additional regulatory information must state whether the quarterly statements filed with the bank agree with the books; here they do not (₹46 crore against ₹38 crore, a difference of ₹8 crore), so a summary of the reconciliation and the reasons for the material discrepancy must be disclosed. The auditor obtains that reconciliation and verifies the reasons: a statement that overstates inventory may also have let Tapri draw more from the bank than its security supports.
3. **Foreign currency loan**: check the closing rate and recompute the restatement as per AS 11: USD 20,00,000 × ₹86.40 = ₹17.28 crore against ₹16.64 crore recorded, ₹64 lakh more.

**(b) The auditor's work**

1. **Covenant**: the entity is not in compliance. An oral assurance is not evidence: obtain **confirmation of the waiver from the lender**; without it, determine whether the term loan should be classified as current, and ensure the covenant and default are disclosed.
2. **Existence**: board minutes, loan agreements (rates, terms, limits), independent balance confirmations from both banks under SA 505 (asking for interest rates, due dates, collateral and security), and a written representation that recorded liabilities are valid claims.
3. **Completeness**: the schedule of borrowings (opening, new, repaid, closing) traced to the ledger; unrecorded debt sought in minutes, contracts, confirmations and post-year-end transactions.
4. **Compliance**: charges registered; borrowing powers and section 180; use for the stated purpose.

## Under the hood

### Reduction of capital: the eleven checks (SM 5.18–5.19)

1. The meeting for the special resolution was properly convened and the proposal circularised in advance to all shareholders.
2. The Articles authorise reduction of capital.
3. No default in repaying deposits or interest on them (otherwise no reduction).
4. The Tribunal's order confirming the reduction; the order and minutes registered and filed with the Registrar.
5. The Registrar's certificate on the reduction.
6. Vouch the entries reducing capital and writing down assets with the resolution and documents; presentation as per Schedule III, Part I.
7. Revaluation of assets properly disclosed in the balance sheet.
8. For reduction of unpaid capital, cancellation of lost capital or repayment of excess capital: the adjustments in the register of members, and old certificates altered or replaced and cancelled.
9. "And reduced" added to the name in the balance sheet, if the Tribunal's order requires.
10. All terms and conditions imposed by the Tribunal complied with.
11. The Memorandum suitably altered.

### Small facts that win MCQs

- **Increase in authorised capital** needs alteration of the capital clause of the MOA by an **ordinary resolution** (SM 5.124).
- The **capital redemption reserve** is not a free reserve: no dividends from it, only fully paid bonus shares (SM 5.124).
- **Dividends** are recommended by the board and declared by the shareholders at the AGM (ordinary resolution); shareholders can reduce, not increase, the amount (SM 5.125).
- A **cash credit** against stocks and debtors, guaranteed by directors: secured short-term "loans repayable on demand" from banks, with the guaranteed aggregate disclosed (SM 5.136; see [[fm.sources.short|short-term finance, Paper 6A]]).
- **Borrowing from banks** (SM 5.125–5.126) adds to the procedures above: reconcile the loan or overdraft with the pass book and a certificate from the bank; check the charge in the register of charges; only the board of directors can authorise a company's borrowing.

::: trick "Bonus, Preliminary, Expenses, Redemption, Buy-back"
Section 52's five uses in order: **B**onus shares, **P**reliminary expenses, **E**xpenses/commission/discount on issue, **R**edemption premium, **B**uy-back. A prescribed class keeps only the first B (equity bonus), E (equity issues) and the last B. Condition: dividends and losses are never on the list.

## Answer frame

### A section-based case (premium, discount, sweat equity)

1. **Relevant provision**: the section, in the Study Material's words (the list of uses; "void"; the penalty and refund; the conditions).
2. **Facts of the case**: price, recipients, resolution, amounts.
3. **Analysis**: is the use or issue within the section? Compute premium, refund, interest, penalty where asked.
4. **Conclusion**: valid or not, and what the auditor verifies or reports.

### "What procedures to verify borrowings?"

Answer by assertion, as the Study Material does: existence, completeness, valuation, then the Schedule III disclosure.

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: the dividend paid from premium
"Premium is the company's own money, so it can pay dividends." **Fix:** securities premium is applied only for the section 52 purposes; dividends and losses are not among them.
:::

::: mistake "Discount is allowed if the shareholders agree"
**Fix:** shares at a discount are void, whatever the shareholders resolve, except sweat equity shares and the RBI-scheme conversion of debt into shares for creditors.
:::

::: mistake The whole term loan as long-term
**Fix:** instalments falling due within twelve months are current maturities, shown separately under short-term borrowings; a covenant breach without the lender's written waiver may make the loan current.
:::

## Practice

::: quiz Check yourself
Q: Case scenario. Malwa Bakers Ltd, which is not of the prescribed class, has a securities premium of ₹3 crore. Its board proposes four uses of it. Which ONE does section 52 permit?
- [ ] Paying an interim dividend of ₹1 crore to equity shareholders
- [x] Writing off ₹40 lakh of discount allowed on its issue of debentures
- [ ] Transferring ₹2 crore to general reserve "to strengthen the balance sheet"
- [ ] Writing off the loss of ₹90 lakh on the sale of its old bakery
> Section 52's uses are bonus shares, preliminary expenses, the expenses of, commission paid or discount allowed on any issue of shares or debentures, premium on redemption, and buy-back. Discount on debentures is the third. Dividends, transfers to free reserves and losses are not on the list.

Q: Sharbat Ventures Ltd raised ₹3,60,000 through an issue of shares at a discount that is void under section 53. What is the maximum penalty (₹) on the company under section 53?
= 360000
> The penalty may extend to the amount raised through the issue at a discount or ₹5 lakh, whichever is less: the lesser of ₹3,60,000 and ₹5,00,000 is ₹3,60,000. Writing "₹5 lakh" for every case is the usual slip. (The company must also refund the monies with interest at 12% per annum.)

Q: Case scenario. Tapri buys back 10,00,000 of its shares. Bunty starts looking for the Tribunal's order confirming the reduction of capital and the special resolution under section 66. Which of the following is correct?
- [ ] Bunty is right: every fall in share capital needs the Tribunal's order under section 66
- [ ] Bunty is right, but only the Registrar's certificate under section 66 is needed for a buy-back
- [x] Section 66 does not apply to a buy-back of its own securities under section 68; the auditor checks compliance with section 68 instead
- [ ] A buy-back is valid only if the Articles authorise a reduction of capital under section 66
> Section 66(6) exempts a buy-back under section 68 from the reduction provisions, so the auditor ensures compliance with the specific requirements of section 68 (SM 5.19–5.20).

Q: Case scenario. Kulhad Crafts Ltd has a term loan of ₹8 crore from a bank, secured by a charge on its kiln shed. Floods damaged the shed, and a registered valuer puts its market value at ₹5 crore at 31 March 2026; the bank holds no other security. How should the auditor expect the loan to be shown?
- [ ] As a secured loan of ₹8 crore, since the charge is registered
- [ ] As an unsecured loan of ₹8 crore, since the security is impaired
- [ ] As a secured loan of ₹8 crore, with a note that the security has fallen in value
- [x] As secured to the extent of ₹5 crore, the market value of the security, and unsecured for the remaining ₹3 crore
> Where the value of the security falls below the loan outstanding, the auditor examines whether the loan is classified as secured only to the extent of the market value of the security (SM 5.31).

Q: A term loan of ₹60 crore is repayable in 12 equal half-yearly instalments, the first due 30 September 2026. How much (₹ crore) is shown as current maturities of long-term borrowings at 31 March 2026?
= 10
> Each instalment is ₹60 crore ÷ 12 = ₹5 crore. Due within 12 months: 30 September 2026 and 31 March 2027, two instalments = ₹10 crore.

## Recap

- Share capital is tallied to last year's audited statements, with the Company Secretary's written confirmation when nothing changed; after a change: within authorised capital (MOA), resolutions, fresh-issue compliances, no discount, cash or non-cash consideration, SEBI, MCA forms (SH-7, PAS-3), fees and stamp duty.
- Section 52: premium goes to a securities premium account and is used only for bonus shares, preliminary expenses, issue expenses or commission or discount, redemption premium and buy-back; a prescribed class keeps only equity bonus, equity issue expenses and buy-back.
- Section 53: shares at a discount are void, except sweat equity and RBI-scheme debt conversion for creditors; penalty up to the amount raised or ₹5 lakh, whichever is less; refund with 12% interest.
- Section 54: sweat equity to employees or directors, at a discount or for non-cash consideration; special resolution with particulars; class already issued; pari passu. Section 66 reduction: special resolution and Tribunal's order.
- Reserves: revenue reserves distributable; capital reserves not free (premium: section 52; CRR: section 55); a debit balance of profit and loss is negative "Surplus"; dividends proposed after the year end are disclosed, not a liability.
- Borrowings: existence (minutes, agreements, confirmations, trust deed, discharge, representation); completeness (schedule, later transactions); valuation (interest, closing rate, covenants and written waivers, current maturities, charges, section 180). (The sahukar always comes back.)

## Flashcards

Q:: Share capital: what does the auditor do when there has been no change during the year?
A:: Tally authorised, issued and paid-up capital to the previous year's audited financial statements and obtain a written confirmation or representation from the Company Secretary that the capital structure did not change.

Q:: Section 52: list the purposes for which securities premium may be applied.
A:: (a) Fully paid bonus shares; (b) writing off preliminary expenses; (c) writing off expenses of, or commission paid or discount allowed on, any issue of shares or debentures; (d) premium payable on redemption of redeemable preference shares or debentures; (e) buy-back under section 68.

Q:: Section 52: what two things does the auditor verify?
A:: That the premium received has been transferred to a securities premium account, and that any application of it is only for the purposes permitted.

Q:: Section 53: what are the consequences of issuing shares at a discount in contravention?
A:: The shares are void; the company and every officer in default are liable to a penalty up to the amount raised at a discount or ₹5 lakh, whichever is less; the company must refund all monies received with interest at 12% per annum from the date of issue.

Q:: Section 53: when may shares be issued at a discount?
A:: Sweat equity shares under section 54; and to creditors when debt is converted into shares under a statutory resolution plan or debt restructuring scheme in accordance with RBI guidelines.

Q:: Section 54: what are sweat equity shares?
A:: Equity shares issued to employees or directors at a discount or for consideration other than cash, for providing know-how or making available intellectual property rights or value additions.

Q:: Section 54: what conditions does the auditor verify for sweat equity?
A:: Of a class already issued; authorised by a special resolution; the resolution specifies the number, current market price, consideration and class of directors or employees; listed: as per SEBI regulations, unlisted: as per prescribed rules.

Q:: What is a reserve, as per the Study Material?
A:: Amounts appropriated out of profits that are not intended to meet any liability, contingency, commitment or diminution in value of assets known to exist at the balance sheet date.

Q:: Schedule III: how is a debit balance of the statement of profit and loss shown?
A:: As a negative figure under the head "Surplus"; reserves and surplus, after adjusting it, is shown even if the result is negative.

Q:: Borrowings: what if the entity breaches a loan covenant?
A:: Determine whether the debt should be classified as current; if the lender has waived enforcement, obtain confirmation of the waiver from the lender; ensure disclosure of restrictive covenants and default.

Q:: Schedule III: what is disclosed for borrowings secured on current assets (additional regulatory information)?
A:: Whether the quarterly returns or statements of current assets filed with banks or financial institutions agree with the books, and if not, a summary of the reconciliation and reasons for material discrepancies.

Q:: How is a cash credit against stocks and debtors, guaranteed by directors, shown?
A:: Under short-term borrowings as secured "loans repayable on demand" from banks, with the nature of security, and the aggregate amount of loans guaranteed by directors disclosed.
