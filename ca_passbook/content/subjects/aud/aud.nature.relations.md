## In plain words

### Bunty's longest day

Bunty thinks an audit is a pile of vouchers and a red pen. So Lakshmi gives him one day at Tapri Tea Co. Ltd with a different job every hour.

> **Bunty:** Ma'am, the GST notices, the store manager, the delivery app's servers and the samosa line? I came to audit the accounts.
> **Lakshmi:** All of those end up in the accounts, Bunty. You cannot check a number if you do not understand where it came from.
> **Kesar:** *(passing by)* Take him to the samosa line first. Nobody understands my business on an empty stomach.

By evening Bunty has used accounting (to read the statements), law (to see whether the GST demand needs a provision or a note), people skills (the store manager answered every question except the one about the cash drawer), statistics (to pick 60 delivery invoices out of 48,000 so the sample says something about all of them), mathematics (to recheck the count of 12,400 kulhads in the warehouse), economics (milk prices rose all year, so why did Tapri's margins not fall?), financial management (the working capital ratios), data processing (the app's records are the books now) and production knowledge (how many samosas a kilo of potatoes really makes).

That is what ICAI means when it calls auditing **interdisciplinary**: it draws from many subjects and uses them. ICAI singles out one of them: the financial statements are the result of the accounting process, so a sound knowledge of accounting principles is a fundamental requirement.

::: analogy The judge at the cooking contest
A good judge knows recipes, the food-safety rules and the price of tomatoes this month. She notices which contestant is nervous about the fridge, tastes one spoon from each pot instead of the whole pot, reads the oven's digital log and knows how a kitchen line runs. She is judging the dish, but she needs all of that to judge it well.
:::

::: key
Auditing draws from accountancy, law, behavioural science, statistics, economics and financial management (and, in ICAI's chart, data processing and production). Each gives the auditor a tool; a sound knowledge of accounting principles is the one requirement ICAI calls fundamental.
:::

### 2. What each discipline gives the auditor

The Study Material explains the links in two places: a paragraph (SM 1.6–1.7) and a one-line statement for each discipline (SM 1.7–1.8). Learn the one-liners; use the paragraph to explain them.

1. **Auditing and Accounting.** ==Auditing reviews the financial statements which are nothing but a result of the overall accounting process.== Since the audit of financial statements is concerned with financial information, a sound knowledge of accounting principles is a fundamental requirement for an auditor to conduct the audit and express an opinion (SM 1.6–1.7). At Tapri: judging whether the elaichi premix stock is valued correctly needs the accounting rules for inventory first.
2. **Auditing and Law.** An auditor should have a good knowledge of business laws affecting the entity. Good knowledge of business laws and various taxation laws helps the auditor to understand the financial statements in a better way, in accordance with applicable laws (SM 1.6–1.7). At Tapri: whether a GST demand is a liability, a provision or a note starts as a question of law.
3. **Auditing and Economics.** The auditor is expected to be familiar with the overall economic environment of the client: knowledge of economics helps him understand the economic environment in which the specific business is operating (SM 1.7). At Tapri: a year of rising milk prices makes unchanged margins a question, not a comfort.
4. **Auditing and Behavioural Science.** Knowledge of human behaviour is essential for an auditor to effectively discharge his duties. During the audit, the auditor has to interact with a lot of persons, seeking information and making inquiries, which can be done only with knowledge of human behaviour (SM 1.7–1.8). At Tapri: the store manager who answers every question but one.
5. **Auditing and Statistics & Mathematics.** The auditor is expected to have knowledge of **statistical sampling** for meaningful conclusions and of **mathematics for verification of inventories**. It is not possible for an auditor to check each and every transaction, so auditors use statistical methods to draw samples in a scientific manner (SM 1.7–1.8; [[aud.evid.sa530|SA 530 audit sampling]]). At Tapri: 60 invoices chosen from 48,000 so that the result speaks for all of them.
6. **Auditing and Data Processing.** EDP auditing is developing as a discipline in itself (SM 1.8). At Tapri: orders, payments and refunds live in the delivery app before they reach the ledger ([[aud.risk.it|internal control in an IT environment]]).
7. **Auditing and Financial Management.** The auditor is expected to have knowledge of financial techniques such as working capital management, funds flow, ratio analysis and capital budgeting. He needs them for applying some of the audit procedures and carrying out the audit effectively; knowledge of financial markets, part of the study of financial management, is also expected of a professional auditor (SM 1.7–1.8). At Tapri: ratios that move against the trend point the audit at an area ([[aud.evid.sa520|analytical procedures]]; [[fm.ratio.liquidity|ratio analysis in Paper 6A]]).
8. **Auditing and Production.** A good auditor is one who understands the client and his business functions, such as production, cost system and marketing (SM 1.8). At Tapri: knowing how many samosas a kilo of potatoes yields tells you whether the wastage figure is believable ([[cma.intro.relations|cost and financial accounting in Paper 4]]).

## Worked examples

:::: example The dairy that needed four kinds of knowledge
Sahyadri Dairy Producer Co. Ltd buys milk from 14,000 farmers at 220 village collection centres and turns it into ghee, paneer and flavoured milk. During the 2025-26 audit, the auditors notice:

(1) The company has received a GST show-cause notice disputing the tax rate it applied to flavoured milk.

(2) Milk procurement prices rose sharply after a drought, while competing dairies cut their selling prices; yet the company's gross margin is the same as last year.

(3) The quantity of ghee obtained from each 100 litres of milk is reported higher than in any earlier year.

(4) Unsold paneer at the year end is carried at cost, although its shelf life is a few days.

For each observation, identify the discipline other than auditing on which the auditor must draw, and explain, with reference to the case, why the auditor is expected to have a working knowledge of it.

::: answer Model answer
Auditing is interdisciplinary in nature: it draws from diverse subjects and makes use of them. In the given case, the matters noticed cannot be evaluated through mere verification of the accounting records.

**(1) GST show-cause notice: Auditing and Law.** An auditor should have a good knowledge of business laws affecting the entity; knowledge of business laws and taxation laws helps him understand the financial statements in accordance with applicable laws. The notice can be evaluated, for its effect on liabilities and disclosures, only with an understanding of the tax law involved.

**(2) Unchanged margin in a drought year: Auditing and Economics.** The auditor is expected to be familiar with the overall economic environment of the client. Rising procurement prices after a drought and price cuts by competitors would ordinarily squeeze margins; an unchanged gross margin in that environment needs explanation, and the auditor should design his procedures in that light.

**(3) Record ghee yield: Auditing and Production.** A good auditor understands the client and his business functions, such as production, cost system and marketing. Knowledge of how ghee is produced from milk lets him judge whether a record yield is plausible or whether output, and hence inventory and profit, may be overstated.

**(4) Paneer carried at cost: Auditing and Accounting.** Auditing reviews the financial statements, which are the result of the overall accounting process, so a sound knowledge of accounting principles is a fundamental requirement. Whether paneer with a shelf life of a few days can be carried at cost is an accounting question about the valuation of inventories.

Hence, to form an opinion in this case, the auditor needs working knowledge of law, economics, production and accounting in addition to auditing.
:::

:::: example Bunty's four claims
State, with reasons, whether each of Bunty's statements is correct or incorrect.

(i) "Statistics is for researchers. I pick the vouchers that look interesting."

(ii) "Tax laws are the tax consultant's job; the auditor only checks the accounts."

(iii) "Since the financial statements are the result of the accounting process, a sound knowledge of accounting principles is a fundamental requirement for the auditor."

(iv) "Knowing how the factory runs is the cost accountant's business, not the auditor's."

::: answer Model answer
**(i) Incorrect.** It is not possible for an auditor to check each and every transaction, so auditors use statistical methods to draw samples in a scientific manner. The auditor is expected to have knowledge of statistical sampling for meaningful conclusions.

**(ii) Incorrect.** An auditor should have a good knowledge of business laws affecting the entity. Good knowledge of business laws and various taxation laws helps the auditor to understand the financial statements in a better way, in accordance with applicable laws.

**(iii) Correct.** Auditing reviews the financial statements, which are nothing but a result of the overall accounting process. Since the audit is concerned with financial information, a sound knowledge of accounting principles is a fundamental requirement to conduct the audit and express an opinion.

**(iv) Incorrect.** A good auditor is one who understands the client and his business functions, such as production, cost system and marketing.

## Answer frame

### "Explain why the auditor needs knowledge of X, Y and Z" (a case)

1. One opening line: auditing is interdisciplinary in nature; it draws from diverse subjects and makes use of them.
2. For each discipline asked: its one-line link in the Study Material's words ("Auditing and Behavioural Science: knowledge of human behaviour is essential for an auditor to effectively discharge his duties").
3. Then the fact of the case it explains, and what the auditor does with it.
4. A closing line: mere verification of the records is not enough to form the opinion.

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: the essay that forgot the case
Writing a page on "auditing is a vast subject" and never mentioning the contractor disputes or the working capital practice. **Fix:** in May 2026 the marks were for each discipline's link *and* the fact it explains: two sentences per discipline.
:::

::: mistake Statistics without mathematics
Writing only "sampling" under Statistics & Mathematics. **Fix:** ICAI's line has two halves: statistical sampling for meaningful conclusions, and mathematics for verification of inventories.
:::

::: mistake Six disciplines, or the link made up from the name
Listing only the six subjects of the opening paragraph and forgetting data processing and production, which the chart and the one-liners add; or inventing a link from the subject's name ("Economics: the auditor should know demand and supply"). **Fix:** learn the eight one-liners in ICAI's words ("familiar with the overall economic environment of the client"), and keep their emphasis: knowledge of accounting principles is called a fundamental requirement, and knowledge of human behaviour is called essential.
:::

## Recap

- Auditing is interdisciplinary: it draws from accountancy, law, behavioural science, statistics, economics and financial management; ICAI's chart adds data processing and production.
- Accounting: auditing reviews financial statements, the result of the accounting process; sound knowledge of accounting principles is the fundamental requirement.
- Law: good knowledge of business laws (and taxation laws) affecting the entity.
- Economics: the overall economic environment of the client.
- Behavioural science: human behaviour, because the auditor seeks information and makes inquiries of many people.
- Statistics and mathematics: statistical sampling for meaningful conclusions; mathematics for verification of inventories.
- Data processing: EDP auditing is developing as a discipline in itself. Financial management: working capital, funds flow, ratios, capital budgeting, and financial markets.
- Production: understand the client's business functions, production, cost system, marketing. (Kesar's samosa line counts.)
