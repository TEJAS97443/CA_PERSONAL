## In plain words

### Forty cities, one opinion

Tapri Tea Co. Ltd now runs cafés in forty cities, a depot-and-café branch in Guwahati and, since last year, an export branch in Dubai. Iyer & Associates sign one opinion on the whole company. Lakshmi cannot be in Guwahati, Dubai and Indore in the same week, so a branch can be audited by a **branch auditor**, who reports to the company's auditor.

> **Kesar:** For Dubai, my nephew Rehan will do the audit. MBA from Dubai, very sharp. Sab adjust ho jayega.
> **Lakshmi:** Then we read what section 143(8) asks of an auditor abroad, Kesar-ji.
> **Bunty:** And once the branch auditor sends a report, Ma'am, we just staple it on?
> **Lakshmi:** We use it, Bunty. We do not swallow it whole.

That is **branch audit**: the company's auditor still reports on the whole company, using another auditor's work for a part of it. The Standard on Auditing for this is **SA 600, "Using the Work of Another Auditor"**: the company's auditor is the **principal auditor**, the branch auditor is the **other auditor**, and the branch is a **component**.

**Joint audit** is a different arrangement. Two or more firms are appointed together as the auditors of one entity. They plan together, share the work and normally sign one common report. **SA 299 (Revised), "Joint Audit of Financial Statements"**, says which work each firm answers for alone and which work they all answer for together.

::: analogy The wedding caterers
At a big wedding, the head caterer may let another caterer run the sweets counter, but tastes before serving: the principal auditor using a branch auditor's work. Or two caterers are hired jointly: each answers for its own dishes, both together for the shared buffet and the guests' plates, and if they cannot agree on the menu card, each prints its own and mentions the other's: a joint audit.
:::

::: key
Branch audit: the branch auditor's report goes to the company's auditor, who deals with it as he considers necessary, after SA 600 procedures show the work is adequate. Joint audit: each firm answers alone for its allotted work and all jointly and severally for the shared list; a firm that disagrees issues a separate report, and the reports refer to each other.
:::

## How it works

### Part A: branch audit

#### 1. Books of account of branches: section 128

As per section 128(1) of the Companies Act, 2013, every company shall prepare and keep at its registered office books of account and other relevant books and papers and financial statement for every financial year which give a true and fair view of the state of the affairs of the company, **including that of its branch office or offices**, if any, and explain the transactions effected both at the registered office and its branches; such books shall be kept on accrual basis and according to the double entry system of accounting (SM 8.62).

- All or any of the books may be kept at such other place in India as the Board of Directors may decide; the company shall, within 7 days of the decision, file with the Registrar a notice in writing giving the full address of that other place. The books may also be kept in electronic mode in the manner prescribed (SM 8.62).
- **Section 128(2):** where a company has a branch office in India or outside India, it shall be deemed to have complied with sub-section (1) if proper books of account relating to the transactions effected at the branch office are kept at that office and **proper summarised returns** periodically are sent by the branch office to the company at its registered office or the other place (SM 8.62–8.63).

#### 2. Who audits a branch: section 143(8)

Section 143(8) prescribes the duties and powers of the company's auditor with reference to the audit of the branch and the branch auditor. Where a company has a branch office, its accounts shall be audited (SM 8.63):

- **In India:** either by the auditor appointed for the company (the company's auditor), or by any other person qualified for appointment as an auditor of the company under the Act and appointed as such under section 139.
- **Outside India:** either by the company's auditor, or by an accountant, or by any other person duly qualified to act as an auditor of the accounts of the branch office in accordance with the laws of that country.

#### 3. Where the branch report goes

- The branch auditor shall **prepare a report** on the accounts of the branch examined by him and **send it to the auditor of the company**, who shall **deal with it in his report in such manner as he considers necessary** (SM 8.63).
- As per **rule 12 of the Companies (Audit and Auditors) Rules, 2014**, the branch auditor shall submit his report to the company's auditor, and ==reporting of fraud by the auditor shall also extend to such branch auditor to the extent it relates to the concerned branch== (SM 8.63).

#### 4. What the company's auditor reports about branches

Under section 143(3), the company's auditor's report shall state, among other matters (SM 8.69–8.70):

- (b) whether, in his opinion, proper books of account as required by law have been kept by the company so far as appears from his examination of those books and **proper returns adequate for the purposes of his audit have been received from branches not visited by him**;
- (c) whether the report on the accounts of any branch office of the company audited under sub-section (8) by a person other than the company's auditor **has been sent to him** under the proviso to that sub-section and **the manner in which he has dealt with it** in preparing his report;
- (d) whether the company's balance sheet and profit and loss account dealt with in the report are in agreement with the books of account **and returns**.

ICAI's illustrative report shows where branches appear in the words of the report: see Formats and formulas below.

#### 5. SA 600: using the work of another auditor

::: definition Principal auditor, other auditor, component
**Principal auditor** means the auditor with responsibility for reporting on the financial information of an entity when that financial information includes the financial information of one or more components audited by another auditor. **Other auditor** means an auditor, other than the principal auditor, with responsibility for reporting on the financial information of a component which is included in the financial information audited by the principal auditor. **Component** means a division, branch, subsidiary, joint venture, associated enterprises or other entity whose financial information is included in the financial information audited by the principal auditor (SM 8.63–8.64).
:::

When the accounts of the branch are audited by a person other than the company's auditor, there is need for a **clear understanding** of the role of such other auditor and the company's auditor in relation to the audit of the branch and of the company as a whole, and great necessity for a **proper rapport** between the two for an effective audit. SA 600 deals with these needs (SM 8.64):

1. **Right to visit.** In certain situations, the statute governing the entity may confer a right on the principal auditor to visit a component and examine the books of account and other records of the component, if he thinks it necessary to do so.
2. **Normally entitled to rely.** Where another auditor has been appointed for the component, the principal auditor would normally be entitled to rely upon the work of such auditor, unless there are special circumstances to make it essential for him to visit the component and/or to examine its books of account and other records.
3. **But satisfied first.** The principal auditor should perform procedures to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence that the work of the other auditor is adequate for the principal auditor's purposes, in the context of the specific assignment.

When using the work of another auditor, the principal auditor should **ordinarily** perform the following procedures (SM 8.64):

- (a) **advise the other auditor of the use** that is to be made of the other auditor's work and report, and make sufficient arrangements for **co-ordination** of their efforts at the planning stage of the audit. The principal auditor would inform the other auditor of matters such as areas requiring special consideration, procedures for the identification of inter-component transactions that may require disclosure, and the time-table for completion of audit; and
- (b) **advise the other auditor of the significant accounting, auditing and reporting requirements** and **obtain representation** as to compliance with them.

The principal auditor **might discuss** with the other auditor the audit procedures applied, or **review a written summary** of the other auditor's procedures and findings, which may be in the form of a completed questionnaire or check-list. The principal auditor may also wish to **visit the other auditor**. The nature, timing and extent of procedures will depend on the circumstances of the engagement and the principal auditor's knowledge of the professional competence of the other auditor, which may have been enhanced from the review of the previous audit work of the other auditor (SM 8.64–8.65).

Two consequences in the report: when SA 600 applies, the auditor's responsibilities section states the division of responsibility, indicating the extent to which components audited by other auditors are included, e.g. the number of branches (SM 8.19); and ICAI's illustrative report carries an Other Matter paragraph on the branches audited by branch auditors (SM 8.30; [[aud.report.sa706|SA 706]]).

#### 6. The branch auditor is a full auditor of the branch

The Study Material's Case Scenario 6 settles two points (SM 11.58–11.61). A branch auditor of only some branches must still **determine materiality and identify and assess the risks of material misstatement** for the auditee branches, with documentation; substantive procedures alone are not enough. And the company's auditor **can** advise the branch auditor of significant requirements and ask for a representation of compliance: that is SA 600's procedure (b).

### Part B: joint audit

#### 7. What it is, and why

Joint audit basically implies **pooling together the resources and expertise** of more than one firm of auditors to render an expert job in a given time period which may be difficult to accomplish acting individually. It essentially involves **sharing of the total work** (SM 8.65). Its legal foothold: under section 139(3), the members of a company may resolve to provide that the audit shall be conducted by more than one auditor (SM 8.92).

| Advantages (SM 8.65–8.66) | Disadvantages (SM 8.66) |
|---|---|
| (i) Sharing of expertise | (i) The fees being shared |
| (ii) Advantage of mutual consultation | (ii) Psychological problem where firms of different standing are associated |
| (iii) Lower workload | (iii) General superiority complex of some auditors |
| (iv) Better quality of performance | (iv) Problems of co-ordination of the work |
| (v) Improved service to the client | (v) Areas of work of common concern being neglected |
| (vi) For multi-national companies, work spread using local firms better placed for detailed work and local laws and regulations | (vi) Uncertainty about the liability for the work done |
| (vii) Lower staff development costs | |
| (viii) Lower costs to carry out the work | |
| (ix) A sense of healthy competition towards a better performance | |

#### 8. SA 299 (Revised): planning together

SA 299 (Revised) lays down the principles for effective conduct of a joint audit to achieve the overall objectives of the auditor laid down in SA 200. It requires that (SM 8.66–8.67):

1. the engagement partner and other key members of the engagement team from **each** of the joint auditors should be involved in planning the audit;
2. the joint auditors should **jointly establish an overall audit strategy** which sets the scope, timing and direction of the audit, and guides the development of the audit plan;
3. before the commencement of the audit, the joint auditors should discuss and develop a **joint audit plan**. In developing it, the joint auditors should:
    - (a) identify division of audit areas and common audit areas;
    - (b) ascertain the reporting objectives of the engagement;
    - (c) consider and communicate among all joint auditors the factors that are significant in directing the engagement team's efforts;
    - (d) consider the results of preliminary engagement activities, or similar engagements performed earlier;
    - (e) ascertain the nature, timing and extent of resources necessary to accomplish the engagement;
4. each of the joint auditors should consider and **assess the risks of material misstatement** and communicate them to the other joint auditors;
5. the joint auditors should discuss and document the nature, timing, and extent of the audit procedures for (I) common and (II) specific allotted areas of audit to be performed;
6. the joint auditors should obtain a **common engagement letter** and a **common management representation letter**;
7. the **work allocation document** should be signed by all the joint auditors and communicated to those charged with governance.

#### 9. Who answers for what

In respect of audit work **divided** among the joint auditors, each joint auditor shall be responsible **only for the work allocated** to such joint auditor, including proper execution of the audit procedures. On the other hand, all the joint auditors shall be **jointly and severally responsible** for (SM 8.67):

- (i) the audit work which is not divided among the joint auditors and is carried out by all joint auditors;
- (ii) decisions taken by all the joint auditors under audit planning in respect of common audit areas;
- (iii) matters which are brought to the notice of the joint auditors by any one of them and there is an agreement among the joint auditors on such matters;
- (iv) examining that the financial statements of the entity comply with the requirements of the relevant statutes;
- (v) presentation and disclosure of the financial statements as required by the applicable financial reporting framework;
- (vi) ensuring that the audit report complies with the requirements of the relevant statutes, applicable Standards on Auditing and other relevant pronouncements issued by ICAI.

#### 10. Telling each other, and the report

- In case a joint auditor comes across matters which are relevant to the areas of responsibility of other joint auditors and which deserve their attention, or which require disclosure or discussion with, or application of judgment by, other joint auditors, the said joint auditor shall **communicate the same to all the other joint auditors in writing prior to the completion of the audit** (SM 8.67–8.68).
- The joint auditors are required to issue a **common audit report**. However, where the joint auditors are in **disagreement** with regard to the opinion or any matters to be covered by the audit report, they shall express their opinion in a **separate audit report**. In such circumstances, the audit report(s) issued by the joint auditor(s) shall **make a reference to each other's audit report(s)** (SM 8.68).

## Worked examples

:::: example Kesar's branch plans
Tapri Tea Co. Ltd has two branches whose accounts will be audited by persons other than Iyer & Associates for 2025-26:

- the **Dubai branch**: Kesar proposes his nephew Rehan Mehra, an MBA who is not an accountant and is not qualified to act as an auditor under the laws of the UAE;
- the **Guwahati branch**: the members have appointed CA Priya Barua under section 139; she is qualified for appointment as auditor of the company.

Kesar also wants both branch reports sent "to me and the board only, not to Lakshmi-ji, she has enough to read". During her audit, Priya finds that the Guwahati branch cashier has been diverting cash.

The two branches report total assets of ₹18.40 crore (Guwahati) and ₹11.25 crore (Dubai) at 31 March 2026, and revenue of ₹26.75 crore and ₹14.90 crore for the year.

Required:

(i) Examine whether Rehan Mehra and CA Priya Barua may audit the branches.

(ii) Comment on Kesar's instruction on the branch reports, and on Priya's duty regarding the cash diversion.

(iii) Assuming both branches are finally audited by eligible branch auditors, state how Iyer & Associates deal with the branches in their audit report, with the amounts for the Other Matter paragraph.

::: answer Model answer
**Relevant provision.** As per section 143(8) of the Companies Act, 2013, a branch office's accounts shall be audited by the company's auditor or by a person qualified for appointment as auditor of the company and appointed under section 139; for a branch outside India, by the company's auditor or by an accountant or by any other person duly qualified to act as an auditor of the branch accounts under the laws of that country. The branch auditor sends the branch report to the company's auditor, who deals with it in his report as he considers necessary; under rule 12 of the Companies (Audit and Auditors) Rules, 2014, reporting of fraud extends to the branch auditor to the extent it relates to the branch. Section 143(3)(b), (c) and (d) require the company's auditor to report on branch returns and branch reports.

**Facts and analysis.**

(i) Rehan is neither the company's auditor, nor an accountant, nor a person duly qualified under UAE law: he **cannot** audit the Dubai branch. The Dubai branch may be audited by Iyer & Associates or by an accountant or other person duly qualified under the laws of the UAE. CA Priya Barua is qualified for appointment as the company's auditor and appointed under section 139: she **may** audit the Guwahati branch.

(ii) Kesar's instruction is contrary to section 143(8) and rule 12: the branch auditor **sends the report to the company's auditor**. The report must reach Iyer & Associates, who deal with it in their report as they consider necessary. The cash diversion relates to the Guwahati branch, so the duty to report fraud **extends to Priya** to that extent.

(iii) The branch working, computed:

| Branch | Total assets (₹ crore) | Revenue (₹ crore) |
|---|---|---|
| Guwahati | 18.40 | 26.75 |
| Dubai | 11.25 | 14.90 |
| **Total** | **29.65** | **41.65** |

**Conclusion.** Iyer & Associates should: (a) in the Opinion paragraph, state that the financial statements include the returns audited by the branch auditors of the branches at Guwahati and Dubai; (b) include an **Other Matter** paragraph: "We did not audit the financial statements/information of 2 branches included in the standalone financial statements of the Company whose financial statements/financial information reflect total assets of ₹29.65 crore as at 31st March 2026 and the total revenue of ₹41.65 crore for the year ended on that date… our opinion in so far as it relates to the amounts and disclosures included in respect of branches, is based solely on the report of such branch auditors. Our opinion is not modified in respect of these matters."; and (c) make the section 143(3)(b), (c) and (d) statements: proper returns received from the branches not visited, the branch auditors' reports sent to them and properly dealt with, and agreement with the books and the branch returns. The Other Matter paragraph is first communicated, with its wording, to those charged with governance.
:::

:::: example Two branch auditors, one principal auditor
For 2025-26, Lakshmi plans to use the work of two branch auditors of Tapri Tea Co. Ltd. CA Priya Barua audits the Guwahati branch; Lakshmi has reviewed Priya's branch audit work for the last four years and found it thorough. CA Arjun Saikia audits the new Shillong branch, opened this year; Lakshmi has never seen his work. Tapri's overall materiality is ₹1.50 crore. Consider each of the following:

(a) Kesar suggests that Lakshmi should not write to the branch auditors before their audits begin: "Wait for their reports. Less paperwork for everyone."

(b) Bunty plans exactly the same set of procedures on the work of both branch auditors, "to be fair to both".

(c) Priya's report on the Guwahati branch is qualified: she could not attend the year-end count of branch inventory of ₹0.42 crore, and no alternative procedure was possible. No other matter affects Tapri's opinion. Bunty proposes to copy Priya's qualification word for word into Tapri's audit report.

(d) Kesar argues that Lakshmi may now either re-audit the whole Shillong branch herself or rely on Arjun's report without doing anything at all.

Advise Lakshmi, with reasons.

::: answer Model answer
**Relevant provision.** As per section 143(8), the branch auditor sends the branch report to the company's auditor, who shall deal with it in his report in such manner as he considers necessary (SM 8.63). As per SA 600, "Using the Work of Another Auditor", the principal auditor would normally be entitled to rely on the other auditor's work, unless special circumstances make it essential to visit the component or examine its records, but should perform procedures to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence that the work is adequate for the principal auditor's purposes: ordinarily procedures (a) and (b) set out in How it works, and possibly a discussion of the procedures applied, a review of a written summary of procedures and findings, or a visit. Their nature, timing and extent depend on the circumstances and on the principal auditor's knowledge of the other auditor's professional competence, which may have been enhanced from the review of the other auditor's previous audit work (SM 8.64–8.65).

**Analysis and conclusion.**

(a) Kesar's suggestion would skip both "ordinarily" procedures, which belong to the planning stage. Lakshmi should write to both branch auditors before their audits: the use she will make of their work and reports, the areas requiring special consideration, the procedures for identifying inter-branch transactions that may require disclosure, and the time-table for completion; and the significant accounting, auditing and reporting requirements, with a **representation** of compliance with them.

(b) Procedures (a) and (b) apply to both, but the nature, timing and extent of the rest need not be the same. Four years of reviewing Priya's work have enhanced Lakshmi's knowledge of her competence, so a discussion of the procedures applied may be enough. For Arjun she has no such knowledge and should do more: review a written summary of his procedures and findings (a completed questionnaire or check-list), discuss them and, if necessary, visit him.

(c) The branch report is not copied: Lakshmi deals with it as she considers necessary. Her opinion is on Tapri's financial statements as a whole, so under SA 705 the question is whether the possible effect of the unverified inventory is material to them. At ₹0.42 crore against overall materiality of ₹1.50 crore, with no other matter, it is not, so Tapri's opinion is not modified on this account. Under section 143(3)(c) her report states that the branch report has been sent to her and the manner in which she has dealt with it.

(d) Neither extreme is right. Lakshmi is **normally entitled to rely** on Arjun's work, so no full re-audit unless special circumstances make it essential to visit the branch or examine its records; but she must still obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence that his work is **adequate** for her purposes, as in (b).
:::

:::: example The cold-chain company with two auditors
The members of Narmada Cold Chain Ltd have appointed Iyer & Associates and Bose & Co. as joint auditors for 2025-26. The signed work allocation document gives revenue, receivables, cash and bank to Iyer & Associates, and property, plant and equipment, inventories and payables to Bose & Co.; borrowings, related parties and the presentation and disclosure of the financial statements are common areas.

(a) After the report is issued, inventory at the Nagpur depot is found to have been overstated by ₹0.90 crore. Bose & Co.'s team did not attend that depot's count.

(b) While verifying receivables, Iyer & Associates notice credit notes for goods returned by customers that do not appear in the inventory records.

(c) The financial statements omitted a disclosure the financial reporting framework requires; neither firm noticed.

(d) Bose & Co. propose separate management representation letters, one for each firm's areas.

(e) Iyer & Associates conclude that the opinion must be qualified; Bose & Co. disagree. To avoid confusing the shareholders, Bose & Co. suggest that each firm issue its own report without mentioning the other's.

Comment on each with reference to SA 299 (Revised).

::: answer Model answer
**Relevant provision.** As per SA 299 (Revised), for audit work divided among the joint auditors, each joint auditor shall be responsible only for the work allocated to it, including proper execution of the audit procedures; all of them shall be jointly and severally responsible for, among other matters, matters brought to their notice by any one of them and agreed, and presentation and disclosure as required by the applicable financial reporting framework. A joint auditor who comes across matters relevant to another's area shall communicate them to all the other joint auditors in writing prior to the completion of the audit. The joint auditors obtain a common engagement letter and a common management representation letter and issue a common audit report; where they disagree on the opinion or any matter to be covered by the report, each expresses its opinion in a separate audit report, and the reports make a reference to each other's reports.

**Analysis and conclusion.**

(a) Inventories were allotted to Bose & Co., and the count attendance was part of executing the procedures in that area. **Bose & Co. alone** are responsible.

(b) The matter is relevant to Bose & Co.'s area. Iyer & Associates shall **communicate it in writing** to all the other joint auditors before the audit is completed. Once brought to notice and agreed upon, it becomes a joint and several responsibility.

(c) Presentation and disclosure is a joint and several responsibility, and a common area here: **both firms** are responsible.

(d) SA 299 (Revised) requires a **common management representation letter** (and a common engagement letter); separate letters are not in order.

(e) Disagreement on the opinion does call for a **separate audit report** from each firm, but the reports shall **make a reference to each other's** report: Bose & Co.'s suggestion of silence is not in order.
:::

## Answer frame

### A case question: provision, facts, analysis, conclusion

1. **Relevant provision**: section 143(8) and rule 12 for "who audits and to whom"; SA 600 for "what may the principal auditor do"; SA 299 (Revised) for "who is responsible" and "what about the report".
2. **Facts of the case**: where the branch is (India or outside); who was appointed and how; which areas were allotted to whom; what the firms disagree on.
3. **Analysis**: apply the provision line by line.
4. **Conclusion**: eligible or not; report to the company's auditor; responsibility alone or joint and several; one report or separate reports referring to each other.

### "Distinguish between a branch auditor and a joint auditor"

| Point | Branch auditor | Joint auditor |
|---|---|---|
| Appointed for | The accounts of a branch office | The audit of the entity as a whole, with other auditors |
| Reports to | The company's auditor | The members, in a common report |
| Responsibility | For the branch report; the company's auditor uses it under SA 600 | Allotted work alone; listed matters jointly and severally |
| Governing rule | Section 143(8), rule 12, SA 600 | Section 139(3), SA 299 (Revised) |

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: the majority verdict
Writing "the joint auditor in the minority must agree with the majority". **Fix:** where joint auditors disagree on the opinion or any matter in the report, each expresses its opinion in a separate report, and the reports refer to each other (SM 8.68).
:::

::: mistake "Anyone can audit a foreign branch"
**Fix:** outside India, only the company's auditor, an accountant, or a person duly qualified under that country's laws (SM 8.63).
:::

::: mistake The branch report that went to the board
**Fix:** the branch auditor sends the report to the company's auditor (section 143(8), rule 12), who deals with it in his report as he considers necessary (SM 8.63).
:::

::: mistake Relying without checking, or checking without relying
**Fix:** the principal auditor is normally entitled to rely, but should perform procedures to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence that the work is adequate for his purposes (SM 8.64).
:::

## Practice

::: quiz Check yourself
Q: Case scenario. Tapri Tea Co. Ltd opens a branch in Colombo, Sri Lanka. The board wants the branch accounts audited locally. Which of the following may audit them under section 143(8)?
- [ ] Only Iyer & Associates, the company's auditor
- [x] Iyer & Associates, or an accountant, or any other person duly qualified to act as an auditor of the branch accounts under the laws of Sri Lanka
- [ ] Any chartered accountant in India nominated by the managing director
- [ ] The branch's own internal audit team, whose report goes to the board
> Outside India: the company's auditor, an accountant, or a person duly qualified under that country's laws. In India it would be the company's auditor or a person qualified for appointment as auditor of the company and appointed under section 139.

Q: Under SA 600, which procedures should the principal auditor ordinarily perform? Select all that apply.
- [x] Advise the other auditor of the use to be made of the other auditor's work and report, and arrange co-ordination at the planning stage
- [x] Advise the other auditor of significant accounting, auditing and reporting requirements and obtain representation as to compliance
- [ ] Re-perform every procedure applied by the other auditor before relying on the report
- [ ] Obtain photocopies of all the other auditor's working papers as of right
> The two "ordinarily" procedures are (a) and (b). The principal auditor is normally entitled to rely, so re-performing everything is not required; the working papers belong to the other auditor.

Q: Joint auditors of a company have divided the audit areas. For which of the following are ALL the joint auditors jointly and severally responsible? Select all that apply.
- [x] Decisions taken by all of them under audit planning for common audit areas
- [x] Presentation and disclosure of the financial statements as required by the framework
- [ ] Proper execution of audit procedures in an area allotted to one joint auditor
- [x] Ensuring that the audit report complies with the relevant statutes, SAs and ICAI pronouncements
> Allocated work, including its execution, is the allotted auditor's alone; the other three are on SA 299's joint and several list.

Q: Case scenario. Joint auditors Patel & Co. and Rao & Co. agree that the opinion is unmodified, but disagree on whether the report should include an Other Matter paragraph on branches audited by branch auditors. Which of the following is correct?
- [ ] They must issue a common report, because separate reports are allowed only when the opinion differs
- [ ] The view of the firm with the larger share of allotted work prevails
- [x] Each expresses its opinion in a separate audit report, and the reports refer to each other
- [ ] The matter goes to those charged with governance, whose decision binds both firms
> SA 299 (Revised): disagreement "with regard to the opinion or any matters to be covered by the audit report" leads to separate reports, each referring to the other's. There is no majority, seniority or share-of-work rule.

## Recap

- Section 128: books at the registered office include the branches'; a branch keeping proper books and sending proper summarised returns periodically is deemed compliance.
- Section 143(8): branch in India: company's auditor or a person qualified to be the company's auditor and appointed under section 139; outside India: company's auditor, an accountant, or a person duly qualified under that country's laws.
- The branch auditor reports to the company's auditor, who deals with it as he considers necessary; rule 12: fraud reporting extends to the branch auditor for the branch.
- Section 143(3)(b), (c), (d): returns from branches not visited; branch reports received and how dealt with; agreement with books and returns.
- SA 600: normally entitled to rely, but procedures to be satisfied the work is adequate; ordinarily (a) advise the use and co-ordinate, (b) advise requirements and obtain representation; discuss, review a summary or visit.
- Joint audit: pooling resources and expertise; nine advantages, six disadvantages; legal foothold in section 139(3).
- SA 299 (Revised): joint planning and strategy; joint audit plan (areas, reporting objectives, significant factors, preliminary results, resources); common engagement and representation letters; signed work allocation communicated to those charged with governance.
- Allocated work: alone. Undivided work, common decisions, agreed matters, statutes, presentation, report: jointly and severally. Disagreement: separate reports referring to each other. (Two caterers, two menu cards, each mentioning the other.)

## Flashcards

Q:: Section 143(8): who may audit the accounts of a branch office in India?
A:: The company's auditor, or any other person qualified for appointment as an auditor of the company under the Act and appointed as such under section 139.

Q:: Section 143(8): who may audit the accounts of a branch office outside India?
A:: The company's auditor, or an accountant, or any other person duly qualified to act as an auditor of the branch accounts in accordance with the laws of that country.

Q:: Branch audit: to whom does the branch auditor send the report, and what does the recipient do with it?
A:: To the company's auditor, who deals with it in his report in such manner as he considers necessary (section 143(8); rule 12 of the Companies (Audit and Auditors) Rules, 2014).

Q:: Rule 12: how far does fraud reporting extend to a branch auditor?
A:: Reporting of fraud by the auditor extends to the branch auditor to the extent it relates to the concerned branch.

Q:: Section 128(2): when is a company with a branch deemed to comply with section 128(1)?
A:: If proper books of account relating to the branch's transactions are kept at the branch and proper summarised returns are sent periodically to the registered office or the other place where books are kept.

Q:: SA 600: define principal auditor.
A:: The auditor with responsibility for reporting on the financial information of an entity when that financial information includes the financial information of one or more components audited by another auditor.

Q:: SA 600: which two procedures should the principal auditor ordinarily perform?
A:: (a) Advise the other auditor of the use to be made of its work and report and arrange co-ordination at the planning stage (special areas, inter-component transactions, time-table); (b) advise the significant accounting, auditing and reporting requirements and obtain representation as to compliance.

Q:: Joint audit: name any five advantages.
A:: Sharing of expertise; mutual consultation; lower workload; better quality of performance; improved service to the client (also: local expertise for multinationals, lower staff development costs, lower costs, healthy competition).

Q:: SA 299: what five things do joint auditors consider in developing the joint audit plan?
A:: (a) Division of audit areas and common areas; (b) reporting objectives; (c) factors significant in directing the team's efforts; (d) results of preliminary engagement activities or similar earlier engagements; (e) nature, timing and extent of resources needed.

Q:: SA 299: for what are all joint auditors jointly and severally responsible?
A:: Undivided work done by all; planning decisions on common areas; matters brought to notice by one and agreed; compliance of the statements with statutes; presentation and disclosure under the framework; compliance of the audit report with statutes, SAs and ICAI pronouncements.

Q:: SA 299: joint auditors disagree on the opinion. What happens to the report?
A:: Each expresses its opinion in a separate audit report, and the reports make a reference to each other's reports.

Q:: Joint audit: where is its legal foothold in the Companies Act, 2013?
A:: Section 139(3): the members of a company may resolve that the audit shall be conducted by more than one auditor.
