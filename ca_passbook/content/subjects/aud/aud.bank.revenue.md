## In plain words

### The interest that was charged but never earned

At the end of March, the CBS of Narmada Bank's Indore Main Branch runs its interest batch: every loan account, good or bad, is debited with a month's interest, and the branch's income goes up by the same amount.

> **Bunty:** Ma'am, interest income is the easiest line. The system charged it to every borrower, so it is all income.
> **Lakshmi:** Charging it and earning it are two different things, Bunty.
> **Bunty:** But it is on the borrowers' statements.
> **Lakshmi:** So are the unpaid instalments.

A bank earns most of its income as **interest** on advances and investments, and the rest as **other income**: commissions, fees, locker rent, profits on investments. It spends on **interest** on deposits and borrowings, on **operating expenses** (salaries, rent, technology) and on **provisions and contingencies** (for bad loans, taxes, falling investments).

Income is normally booked as it is earned (on accrual), because the bank expects to collect it. But a loan that has become an NPA has shown that it may not pay, so the rule changes: **income on an NPA is booked only when it is actually received**, and interest already credited but not realised is taken back out. On the expenses side, the auditor cannot re-compute interest on lakhs of deposits, so the main test is analytical: does the interest paid make sense for the deposits held and the rates offered?

::: analogy The tapri's credit book
A roadside tapri lets regulars drink on credit and writes each cup in a book. A wise owner counts a regular's cups as earnings because they pay at month-end. When one regular has not paid for four months, the owner stops counting that customer's cups as earnings, crosses out the ones counted earlier and still unpaid, and keeps a separate pencil note of the cups only to remember them. The day the customer pays, the cash becomes earnings. That pencil note is a bank's memorandum account.
:::

::: key
A bank recognises income on accrual, but income from non-performing assets only when actually realised: when an advance becomes an NPA, unrealised interest, fees and commission already credited are reversed or provided for, and further interest is kept in a memorandum account outside income and outside gross advances.
:::

## How it works

### 1. What sits in the Profit and Loss Account

Section 29(1) of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949 requires the Profit and Loss Account to be prepared in **Form B of the Third Schedule**, or as near thereto as circumstances admit (SM 10.38). Its income and expenditure heads (SM 10.40–10.41, 10.45–10.46, 10.49):

**Interest earned** comprises (SM 10.40): interest/discount on advances/bills; interest income on investments; interest on balances with the RBI and other inter-bank funds; and others (any other interest or discount income).

**Other income** comprises (SM 10.40–10.41):

- **Commission, exchange and brokerage**: (a) commission on bills for collection; (b) commission/exchange on remittances and transfers (demand drafts, NEFT, RTGS); (c) commission on letters of credit and guarantees, letters of comfort; (d) loan processing, arranger and syndication fees; (e) mobile banking fees; (f) credit/debit card fees, including annual fees, merchant acquiring income and interchange fees; (g) rent from letting out lockers; (h) commission on Government business; (i) commission on other permitted agency business, including consultancy; (j) brokerage on securities; (k) fee on insurance referral; (l) commission on referral of mutual fund clients; (m) service/transaction charges, including for transactions at other branches; (n) income from other services such as custodian, demat, investment advisory and cash management.
- Profit on sale of investments; profit/loss on revaluation of investments.
- Profit on sale of land, buildings and other assets; profit/loss on revaluation of fixed assets.
- Profit on exchange transactions (revaluation gains/losses on forward exchange and derivative contracts, premium on options).
- Dividends etc. from subsidiaries and joint ventures abroad/in India.
- Miscellaneous income.

**Income from investments** (SM 10.45): interest income on investments includes all interest and dividend from Government securities, bonds, debentures and other investments, except dividends from subsidiaries and joint ventures; **broken period interest paid** on securities purchased and **amortisation of premium on SLR investments** are netted off from it. Investments are dealt in as banking activity, so the **net** profit or loss on their sale goes to profit and loss; investments are valued periodically as per RBI guidelines and the depreciation or appreciation is taken to profit and loss.

### 2. Auditing income: the general rules

The Study Material's audit approach for income (SM 10.41–10.42):

1. **Auditor's concern**: reasonable assurance that the recorded income arose from transactions that took place during the period and pertain to the bank, that there is no unrecorded income, and that income is recorded at the appropriate amount.
2. **RBI's direction**: any income which exceeds **one per cent of the total income** of the bank (if reckoned on a gross basis), or **one per cent of the net profit before taxes** (if reckoned net of costs), should be considered on accrual as per AS 9.
3. **Materiality**: an item of income that is not material under these norms may be recognised when received, and the auditors need not qualify their report.
4. **Revenue certainty**: banks recognise interest, fees and commission on accrual, as earned; it is an essential condition for accrual that ultimate collection is not unreasonable to expect. Interest on advances is generated automatically through a batch process in the CBS.
5. **Revenue uncertainty**: because of significant uncertainty about collection, banks should not recognise income on NPAs until it is actually realised. When a facility is classified as an NPA for the first time, interest accrued and credited to income in the corresponding previous year and not realised should be reversed or provided for. This applies to **Government guaranteed accounts** too.
6. **Advances against securities**: interest on advances against term deposits, NSCs, IVPs, KVPs and life policies may be taken to income on the due date, provided adequate margin is available.
7. **Bills purchased**: discount received on bills outstanding at the year-end is apportioned between the two years; the **unexpired discount** (relating to the period after the year-end) is recorded as **"Other Liabilities"**. Interest paid on **rediscounting** bills with other institutions is **not netted off** from the discount earned.
8. **Bills for collection**: the customer's account is credited only after the bill is actually collected from the drawee, and the branch's **commission becomes due only when the bill has been collected**.
9. **Renegotiations**: fees and commissions from re-negotiating or rescheduling debts are recognised on accrual over the period of the re-negotiated or rescheduled credit. The auditor test-checks interest for a sample, and fees and commissions on bills for collection, letters of credit and bank guarantees.

### 3. Reversal of income on NPAs

The Study Material sets these rules (SM 10.43–10.44):

- (a) If any advance, including bills purchased and discounted, becomes an NPA as at the close of any year, ==the entire interest accrued and credited to income account in the past periods, should be reversed or provided for if the same is not realised==. This applies to Government guaranteed accounts also.
- (b) For NPAs, fees, commission and similar income that have accrued should **cease to accrue** in the current period, and be reversed or provided for with respect to past periods, if uncollected.
- (c) Banks that have **wrongly recognised** income in the past should **reverse** the interest if it was recognised in the current year, or **make a provision** for an equivalent amount if it was recognised in previous year(s).
- (d) The auditor should enquire into any **large unexplained debits** in the interest income account, and whether borrowers have written about differences in interest charged and whether action has been taken.

Particular cases:

- **Leased assets**: the finance income component (as defined in AS 19) accrued and credited before the asset became an NPA, and unrealised, is reversed or provided for in the current period.
- **Take-out finance** (a long-term loan, promised for a date or for the completion of a project, that replaces the initial loan; common in property development): if, on the record of recovery, the lending bank classifies the account as an NPA, it should not recognise income unless realised from the borrower or the taking-over institution (if the arrangement so provides).
- **Partial recoveries in NPAs**: without a clear agreement on appropriation (to principal or interest), banks adopt an accounting policy and apply the right of appropriation **uniformly and consistently**; income is recognised as per AS 9 when certainty attaches to realisation. Interest realised in NPAs can be taken to income, but the credits towards interest must **not come out of fresh or additional credit facilities** sanctioned to the borrower.
- **Memorandum account**: on an account turning NPA, banks reverse the interest charged and not collected by debiting profit and loss, and stop applying further interest. They may continue to record the accrued interest in a **memorandum account** for control; it is **not taken into account in computing gross advances**.

### 4. Auditing the expenses

Expenditure is shown under three heads (SM 10.45–10.46):

- **Interest expended**: interest on deposits; interest on RBI/inter-bank borrowings; others.
- **Operating expenses**: payments to and provisions for employees; rent, taxes and lighting; printing and stationery; advertisement and publicity; depreciation on the bank's property; directors' fees, allowances and expenses; auditors' fees and expenses; legal expenses; postage, telegrams, telephones etc.; repairs and maintenance; insurance; marketing expenses; other expenses.
- **Provisions and contingencies**: provisions for non-performing assets; provisions for taxation; provisions for diminution in the value of investments; provisions for contingencies.

**Interest expense** (SM 10.46–10.47). The auditor is primarily concerned with the **overall reasonableness** of interest expense, by analysing the ratios of interest paid on different types of deposits and borrowings to the **average quantum** of those liabilities; the entries are generated by a CBS batch process. The procedures:

1. Obtain an analysis of the types of deposits outstanding at the **end of each quarter**, work out a **weighted average interest rate**, compare it with the **actual average rate** paid as per the annual accounts, and enquire into a material difference.
2. Compare the average rate paid with the **previous years'** figures and analyse material differences.
3. Obtain the general ledger break-up of interest on deposits (savings and term) and borrowings each month or quarter; analyse month-on-month (or quarter-on-quarter) costs and document reasons for variances against the benchmark; agree the cost analysis with the ledger; understand and re-compute the average balance on a sample basis.
4. Test check the calculation of interest to ensure that (a) interest has been provided on all deposits up to the balance sheet date; (b) rates are as per the bank's internal regulations, RBI directives and agreements with depositors; (c) interest on savings accounts follows the bank's/RBI's rules; (d) interest on inter-branch balances is at the rates prescribed by the head office/RBI.
5. Ascertain changes in savings and term deposit rates during the period, obtain the **interest rate card** and analyse the cost accordingly; check that interest has been accrued on the **entire borrowing portfolio** and agrees with the ledgers; re-compute the accrual from the term sheet, deal ticket or agreement (frequency, rate, period elapsed).

**Operating expenses** (SM 10.48): study and evaluate internal control over expenses, including authorisation procedures; examine divergent trends in major items; perform substantive analytical procedures, for example each item's ratio to total operating expenses compared with previous years; verify expenses with supporting documents and check calculations.

**Provisions and contingencies** (SM 10.48): the auditor should

1. ensure compliance with the regulatory requirements for provisioning in the various circulars;
2. understand how the bank computes provisions on standard assets and NPAs, mainly by checking the basis of classification into standard, sub-standard, doubtful, loss and non-performing assets, verified on a sample basis ([[aud.bank.advances|advances and NPAs]]);
3. obtain the detailed break-up of standard and non-performing loans and agree the balances with the general ledger;
4. obtain management's tax provision computation and check that the items debited and credited to profit and loss are appropriately considered in it;
5. examine other provisions for expenses against the circumstances warranting them, and their adequacy, through discussion and explanations from management.

**Prior period items** (SM 10.49): Form B has no specific line for the impact of prior period items on the current year's profit, so such disclosures, wherever warranted, may be given.

::: formula Weighted average interest rate on deposits
$$\bar r = \frac{\sum (B \times r)}{\sum B}$$

Here $B$ is the average balance of each type of deposit, worked out from its quarter-end balances, and $r$ is that type's rate. Compare $\bar r$ with the actual average rate (interest on deposits as per the accounts ÷ total average deposits) and with the previous year's rate (SM 10.46–10.47).
:::

::: formula Discount on a bill purchased, at the year-end
$$\text{Earned} = \text{Discount} \times \frac{\text{Days to year-end}}{\text{Days of the bill}}$$

Earned: the discount income of the year; the rest is unexpired discount, shown under Other Liabilities (SM 10.42). The Study Material gives no day-count rule; this lesson counts from the day after the purchase up to the year-end (15 February to 31 March is 44 days). State the convention in an answer.
:::

## Worked examples

:::: example What the branch may keep as income
Iyer & Associates are auditing the income of Narmada Bank's Indore Main Branch for 2025-26. The following amounts (₹ lakh) were credited to income during the year and have not been received, except where stated:

1. **Rewa Polymers** (an NPA since 20 November 2024): the CBS went on applying interest, ₹16.80 lakh, all year. Interest of ₹4.10 lakh credited in 2024-25 before the account turned NPA is also unrealised and was never reversed.
2. **Shivam Agro Foods** (an NPA for the first time, from 5 March 2026): interest of ₹5.20 lakh credited during 2025-26.
3. **Omkar Electricals** (overdraft against its own term deposits, adequate margin): interest of ₹1.44 lakh, due and unpaid.
4. **Narmada Sugar Mills** (term loan guaranteed by the Central Government, an instalment overdue for 120 days, guarantee not invoked): interest of ₹3.60 lakh.
5. **Kshipra Textiles** (a doubtful asset): ₹6.00 lakh **received** and credited to interest under the bank's uniform policy of appropriating recoveries first to interest. ₹2.50 lakh of it was drawn from an ad hoc limit sanctioned to Kshipra in February 2026.
6. **Discount** of ₹2.70 lakh, received on a 90-day bill purchased on 15 February 2026 and maturing on 16 May 2026, credited in full.
7. **Commission** of ₹0.85 lakh on bills sent for collection, which had not been collected by 31 March 2026.

**Required:** State, with reasons, how much of each item may be recognised as income for 2025-26, and the adjustments needed.

::: answer Show the working
**Working notes**

1. **Rewa Polymers.** Income on NPAs is not recognised until realised; a bank that has wrongly recognised income should reverse it if recognised in the current year, or make a provision for an equivalent amount if recognised in previous years (SM 10.42–10.43). Reverse ₹16.80 lakh; provide ₹4.10 lakh for the 2024-25 interest. The interest may be kept in a memorandum account for control, outside gross advances.
2. **Shivam Agro Foods.** On becoming an NPA, the entire interest accrued and credited in past periods and not realised is reversed or provided for (SM 10.43). Reverse ₹5.20 lakh.
3. **Omkar Electricals.** Interest on advances against term deposits may be taken to income on the due date, provided adequate margin is available (SM 10.42). Recognise ₹1.44 lakh.
4. **Narmada Sugar Mills.** A Central Government guaranteed advance with the guarantee not invoked is a standard asset but an NPA for income recognition (SM 10.29), and the reversal rules apply to Government guaranteed accounts also (SM 10.43). Reverse ₹3.60 lakh.
5. **Kshipra Textiles.** Interest realised in NPAs can be taken to income under a uniform and consistent appropriation policy, but the credits must not come out of fresh or additional facilities sanctioned to the borrower (SM 10.44). Recognise ₹3.50 lakh; reverse ₹2.50 lakh.
6. **Bill purchased.** 15 February to 31 March is 44 days of the 90. Discount earned = 2.70 × 44 ÷ 90 = **₹1.32 lakh**; unexpired discount ₹1.38 lakh goes to **Other Liabilities** (SM 10.42).
7. **Bills for collection.** The branch's commission becomes due only when the bill has been collected (SM 10.42). Reverse ₹0.85 lakh.

**Working notes** (₹ crore)

| Type | Average balance | Rate | Interest at that rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|

## Under the hood

### Two percentages that are not the same test

The RBI's 1% rule decides **whether an income item must be accrued at all**: an item above 1% of total income (gross basis) or 1% of net profit before taxes (net basis) is accrued under AS 9; a smaller one may be recognised when received without a qualification. The NPA rules decide **whether interest on a particular advance can be accrued**: never on an NPA, whatever the amount. A question on a ₹2 crore processing fee asks about the first; a question on interest from a doubtful borrower asks about the second.

### Where the income checks meet the advances checks

Every reversal in this lesson starts with a classification from [[aud.bank.advances|audit of advances]]: a wrong NPA date gives a wrong reversal. And because interest is applied by a CBS batch, the auditor's first check is the one in [[aud.bank.approach|the bank audit approach]]: are the RBI's parameters built into the system that stops interest on NPAs?

## Answer frame

### "Write a short note on reversal of income"

1. NPA as at the close of any year: entire unrealised interest credited in past periods reversed or provided for; also for Government guaranteed accounts.
2. Fees, commission and similar income cease to accrue and are reversed or provided for, if uncollected.
3. Wrongly recognised income: reverse if in the current year; provide an equivalent amount if in previous years.
4. Enquire into large unexplained debits to interest income and borrowers' complaints about interest charged.
5. Add, where relevant: leased assets, take-out finance, partial recoveries, memorandum account.

### "Discuss two analytical procedures to verify interest on deposits"

| Procedure | What the auditor does |
|---|---|
| Weighted average rate | From quarter-end deposit analysis, compute the weighted average rate; compare with the actual average rate as per the accounts; enquire into a material difference |
| Trend | Compare the average rate paid with previous years; analyse material differences |
| Period analysis | Month-on-month or quarter-on-quarter cost analysis from the ledger break-up; document reasons for variances |

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: "the system charged it, so it is income"
Bunty's tick on the interest batch. **Fix:** on an NPA, interest is income only when actually realised; unrealised interest already credited is reversed (current year) or provided for (previous years).
:::

::: mistake The Central Government guarantee that saves the income
Keeping accrued interest on a Central Government guaranteed account because it is a standard asset. **Fix:** it is standard for classification but an NPA for income recognition; the reversal rules apply to Government guaranteed accounts also.
:::

::: mistake Netting the rediscount
Deducting interest paid on rediscounting bills from the discount earned. **Fix:** the rediscount interest is not to be netted off; and only the discount for the period up to the year-end is income, the unexpired part going to Other Liabilities.
:::

::: mistake Commission on a bill nobody has paid yet
Crediting commission when a bill is sent for collection. **Fix:** the commission becomes due only when the bill has been collected.
:::

> When an advance becomes an NPA as at the close of a year, the entire interest accrued and credited in past periods is reversed or provided for if not realised (SM 10.43). Security plays no part.

## Recap

- Form B has two income heads (interest earned, other income) and three expenditure heads (interest expended, operating expenses, provisions and contingencies).
- Income is accrued when collection is reasonably expected, but income on NPAs only when actually realised; this also covers Government guaranteed accounts.
- On an NPA: reverse or provide for unrealised interest, fees and commission; reverse current-year wrong income, provide for earlier years'; memorandum account outside gross advances.
- Partial recoveries: uniform, consistent appropriation policy; never income if paid out of a fresh facility to the same borrower.
- Advances against deposits, NSCs, KVPs and life policies with adequate margin: interest on the due date.
- Bills purchased: apportion the discount; unexpired part to Other Liabilities; rediscount interest not netted. Bills for collection: commission due when collected.
- RBI 1% norm: accrue items above 1% of total income (gross) or of net profit before tax (net); smaller ones may be on receipt without qualification.
- Interest expense: weighted average rate from quarter-end deposits against the actual average rate and last year's; test checks against the rate card. Provisions: RBI circulars, classification sample, ledger agreement, tax computation. (Cross out the unpaid cups.)

## Flashcards

Q:: Bank audit: what are the heads of income and expenditure in Form B?
A:: Income: interest earned; other income. Expenditure: interest expended; operating expenses; provisions and contingencies.

Q:: Bank audit: when is income on an NPA recognised?
A:: Only when it is actually realised; it is not recognised on accrual.

Q:: Reversal of income: what happens when an advance becomes an NPA as at the close of a year?
A:: The entire interest accrued and credited to income in past periods is reversed or provided for if not realised; fees and commission cease to accrue and are reversed or provided for if uncollected. This applies to Government guaranteed accounts also.

Q:: Reversal of income: how is income wrongly recognised in the past corrected?
A:: Reverse it if it was recognised in the current year; make a provision for an equivalent amount if it was recognised in previous years.

Q:: Bank audit: what is the memorandum account for interest on NPAs?
A:: A record of interest accrued on NPAs kept for control after the interest is reversed and stopped; it is not taken into account in computing gross advances.

Q:: Bank audit: how are partial recoveries in NPAs treated?
A:: Without an agreement on appropriation, the bank applies a uniform and consistent policy; realised interest can be taken to income, but not if the credit comes out of a fresh or additional facility to the borrower.

Q:: Bank audit: how is discount on bills purchased treated at the year-end?
A:: Apportioned between the two years; the unexpired discount is recorded under Other Liabilities; interest paid on rediscounting is not netted off from the discount earned.

Q:: Bank audit: when does commission on bills for collection become due?
A:: Only when the bill has been collected from the drawee; the customer is credited only after collection.

Q:: RBI norm: which income items must a bank accrue under AS 9?
A:: Items exceeding 1% of total income (if reckoned gross) or 1% of net profit before taxes (if reckoned net of costs); smaller items may be recognised when received, without a qualification.

Q:: Bank audit: name two analytical procedures for interest on deposits.
A:: Compute a weighted average rate from the quarter-end deposit analysis and compare it with the actual average rate as per the accounts; compare the average rate with previous years. Enquire into material differences.

Q:: Bank audit: what does the auditor do for provisions and contingencies?
A:: Ensure compliance with RBI provisioning circulars; understand the computation for standard assets and NPAs, checking classification on a sample; agree the loan break-up with the ledger; check the tax computation; examine other provisions for adequacy.
