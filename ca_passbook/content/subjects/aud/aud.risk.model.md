## In plain words

### How does a CA sign a clean report on fake sales?

Bunty reads the news at his desk: a regulator has acted against the auditor of a listed snack company whose profits were pumped up with fake sales. The report was clean.

> **Bunty:** Ma'am, how does a CA miss crores of fake sales?
> **Lakshmi:** Three things had to go wrong, Bunty. The sales figure was easy to fake. The company's own checks did not stop it. And the auditor's tests did not catch it.
> **Bunty:** And if any one of them had worked?
> **Lakshmi:** No news story. Also, no Bunty reading the news at 11 a.m.

The chance that an auditor gives a clean opinion on accounts that are materially wrong is **audit risk**. It is built from three smaller risks:

- **Inherent risk**: the item itself is easy to get wrong, before anyone checks it (a complex calculation, an industry where companies keep failing).
- **Control risk**: the company's own checks (its **internal control**) fail to stop the error or to catch and fix it in time.
- **Detection risk**: the auditor's own tests fail to find it.

The first two belong to the company. Together they are the **risk of material misstatement**: the chance that the accounts are wrong before the audit starts. Only the third is in the auditor's hands, so when the accounts are likely to be wrong, the auditor must test harder to keep audit risk acceptably low. It never reaches zero.

::: analogy Three strainers for sour milk
Milk can arrive sour, more often in May than in December (inherent risk). Tapri's cook sniffs every can, unless the morning rush makes him skip it (control risk). The food inspector tastes a few cups and may pick only the good ones (detection risk). A customer drinks sour chai only when all three fail. The inspector cannot change the weather or the cook. He can only taste more cups, taste them across the whole day, and send his best taster. That is all an auditor can do about detection risk too.
:::

::: key
Audit risk is a function of the risk of material misstatement (inherent risk and control risk, the entity's risks) and detection risk (the auditor's). The auditor can influence only detection risk, so the higher the assessed risk of material misstatement, the lower detection risk must be kept, through more and better audit evidence.
:::

## How it works

### 1. Audit risk

::: definition Audit risk
Audit risk means the risk that the auditor gives an inappropriate audit opinion when the financial statements are materially misstated (SM 3.4).
:::

It means the auditor expresses an **unmodified opinion** when the financial statements are materially misstated. Not only would the auditor's reputation be damaged; the auditor could invite regulatory action from the professional body and probable legal action by intended users. To avoid this, SA 200 requires the auditor to obtain ==sufficient appropriate audit evidence to reduce audit risk to an acceptably low level==, and so draw reasonable conclusions on which to base the opinion (SM 3.4). **Audit risk is a function of the risks of material misstatement and detection risk.**

### 2. Misstatement, and the risk of material misstatement

::: definition Misstatement
A difference between the amount, classification, presentation, or disclosure of a reported financial statement item and the amount, classification, presentation, or disclosure that is required for the item to be in accordance with the applicable financial reporting framework. Misstatements can arise from error or fraud (SM 3.4).
:::

The Study Material's examples of misstatements (SM 3.5):

1. Charging an item of capital expenditure to revenue, or vice versa.
2. A disclosure that differs from what the applicable financial reporting framework requires.
3. Selection or application of inappropriate accounting policies.
4. An accounting estimate that differs from what is appropriate under the framework.
5. Intentional booking of fake expenses in the statement of profit and loss.
6. Overstating receivables by not writing off irrecoverable debts.
7. Overstating or understating inventories.

::: definition Risk of material misstatement
As per SA 200, the risk that the financial statements are materially misstated prior to audit: a probability of frauds or errors in the financial statements before the audit (SM 3.4).
:::

It exists at **two levels** (SM 3.5):

- **The overall financial statement level**: risks that relate pervasively to the financial statements as a whole and potentially affect many assertions.
- **The assertion level**, for classes of transactions, account balances and disclosures: assessed to determine the nature, timing and extent of further audit procedures needed for sufficient appropriate audit evidence.

At the assertion level the risk of material misstatement has **two components, inherent risk and control risk**. Both are the entity's risks: they exist independently of the audit and are influenced by the client, not by the auditor (SM 3.5).

### 3. Inherent risk: the item is slippery

::: definition Inherent risk
The susceptibility of an assertion about a class of transaction, account balance or disclosure to a misstatement that could be material, either individually or when aggregated with other misstatements, before consideration of any related controls (SA 200; SM 3.6).
:::

- It is **higher for some assertions** than for others, for example for complex calculations.
- Inherent risk factors are considered while designing tests of controls and substantive procedures. Whether the auditor's assessment is lower or higher, each category covers a range of degrees of inherent risk: the auditor may assess two assertions as lower while recognising that one has less inherent risk than the other.
- It is important to consider the reason for each identified inherent risk, even if the risk is lower, when designing tests of controls and substantive procedures.
- **External circumstances** giving rise to business risks may also influence it: technological developments might make a product obsolete. Factors in the entity and its environment may also influence the inherent risk of a specific assertion (SM 3.6).
- Examples (SM 3.6): an accounting standard gives guidance on a complex issue that management might not understand; a large number of business failures in the entity's industry.

### 4. Control risk: the entity's guard is asleep

::: definition Control risk
The risk that a misstatement that could occur in an assertion about a class of transaction, account balance or disclosure and that could be material, either individually or when aggregated with other misstatements, will not be prevented, or detected and corrected, on a timely basis by the entity's internal control (SA 200; SM 3.7).
:::

There is an **inverse relation** between control risk and the efficiency of internal control: when the efficiency of internal control is high, control risk is low, and when it is low, control risk is high (SM 3.7). The Study Material's examples are controls that exist on paper and may not be followed (SM 3.7):

- cash and cheque books to be kept in a locked safe with access for authorised personnel only;
- fire extinguishers and smoke detectors to be in place and working at all times, to reduce the risk of fire damage to inventories (they may have expired, or may not work);
- only items of expenditure below ₹10,000 to be routed through the imprest system of petty cash.

How internal control is built is covered in [[aud.risk.ic|internal control: components and limitations]].

### 5. Detection risk: the auditor's own blind spot

::: definition Detection risk
The risk that the procedures performed by the auditor to reduce audit risk to an acceptably low level will not detect a misstatement that exists and that could be material, either individually or when aggregated with other misstatements (SA 200; SM 3.7).
:::

Detection risk comprises (SM 3.8):

- **Sampling risk**: the risk that the auditor's conclusion based on a sample may be different from the conclusion if the entire population were subjected to the same audit procedure; the sample was not representative of the population.
- **Non-sampling risk**: the risk that the auditor reaches an erroneous conclusion for any reason not related to sampling risk, such as applying an inappropriate audit procedure.

Examples (SM 3.8): sizeable work-in-progress inventories are expected, but the auditor does not attend the inventory count and relies on alternative audit procedures; revenue is audited on a sample that may not be representative of overall revenue.

**The auditor can only influence detection risk.** Inherent and control risk belong to the entity, so the auditor must reduce detection risk to keep audit risk low. Detection risk may be reduced by **increasing the area of checking, testing larger samples and including competent and experienced persons in the engagement team** (SM 3.8). Sampling itself is in [[aud.evid.sa530|SA 530: audit sampling]].

### 6. What audit risk does not include

- The **auditor's business risks**, such as loss from litigation, adverse publicity or other events arising in connection with the audit.
- The risk that the auditor expresses an opinion that the financial statements are materially misstated **when they are not**. This risk is ordinarily insignificant (SM 3.8).

### 7. Judgment, not a calculator

The assessment of risks is based on audit procedures to obtain the necessary information and on evidence obtained throughout the audit. It is ==a matter of professional judgment, rather than a matter capable of precise measurement==, exercised by an auditor whose training, knowledge and experience have developed the competence to make reasonable judgments (SM 3.9).

Standards on Auditing do not ordinarily refer to inherent risk and control risk separately, but to a **combined assessment of the "risks of material misstatement"**. The auditor may make separate or combined assessments, depending on preferred audit techniques or methodologies and practical considerations, expressed in quantitative terms (such as percentages) or in non-quantitative terms. In any case, the need to make appropriate risk assessments is more important than the approach used. Hence, writing AR for audit risk, RMM for the risks of material misstatement, IR, CR and DR for inherent, control and detection risk (SM 3.10):

$$AR = RMM \times DR \qquad AR = IR \times CR \times DR$$

## Worked examples

:::: example TapriNow and its festival cashbacks
In 2025-26 TapriNow, Tapri Tea Co. Ltd's delivery app, earned revenue of ₹84 crore from about 31 lakh orders. Iyer & Associates note:

(i) Orders carry layered festival cashbacks and loyalty points, and a new 12-month "chai subscription" whose revenue must be spread over the subscription period. The accounts team is applying the revenue standard to subscriptions for the first time.

(ii) Policy requires the regional manager's approval in the app for any cashback above ₹200 on one order. The approval step was switched off for six weeks during Diwali "to avoid delays".

(iii) Bunty tested 25 orders, all from April, and, misreading the policy, accepted subscription receipts as revenue on receipt.

Identify and explain the risk in each of (i), (ii) and (iii), and state how the auditor should respond to (iii).

::: answer Model answer
**(i) Inherent risk.** As per SA 200, inherent risk is the susceptibility of an assertion about a class of transaction, account balance or disclosure to a misstatement that could be material, before consideration of any related controls. It is higher for complex calculations, and where an accounting standard gives guidance on a complex issue that management may not understand. Layered cashbacks and first-time recognition of subscription revenue over a period make revenue susceptible to material misstatement even before controls are considered: the inherent risk is high.

**(ii) Control risk.** Control risk is the risk that a misstatement that could be material will not be prevented, or detected and corrected, on a timely basis by the entity's internal control. The approval control exists but did not operate for six weeks in the busiest season, so unapproved cashbacks could misstate revenue undetected: control risk is high for that period.

**(iii) Detection risk.** Detection risk is the risk that the procedures performed by the auditor to reduce audit risk to an acceptably low level will not detect a material misstatement. It comprises sampling risk and non-sampling risk. A sample of 25 orders from April alone may not represent 31 lakh orders across the year, especially the Diwali weeks: **sampling risk**. Treating subscription receipts as revenue on receipt is an erroneous conclusion from an inappropriate procedure, not caused by sampling: **non-sampling risk**.

**Response.** The auditor can influence only detection risk, and with high inherent and control risk it must be kept low: increase the area of checking (the whole year, and the Diwali weeks specifically), test a larger sample, and assign competent and experienced persons who understand the revenue policy.
:::

:::: example The debtors who paid too fast
Tapri's premix division reports these figures:

| Particulars | 2024-25 (₹ crore) | 2025-26 (₹ crore) |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue (all on credit) | 72.00 | 70.20 |
| Trade receivables at 31 March | 11.84 | 4.81 |
| Inventory at 31 March | 9.60 | 15.60 |

The CFO explains: "We moved our distributors to 30-day terms in January, and they paid up before the year end." Bunty proposes: "The CFO has explained it, so let us assess inherent risk as low, test the credit-terms control, and skip the debtors work this year."

Required:

(a) Compute the trade receivables collection period for both years (closing receivables, 365-day year), and list possible reasons, genuine and otherwise, for the movements in the table.

(b) Comment on Bunty's proposal with reference to the components of audit risk.

::: answer Model answer
**(a) Collection period.**

| Year | Working | Days |
|---|---|---|
| 2024-25 | 11.84 ÷ 72.00 × 365 | 60 |
| 2025-26 | 4.81 ÷ 70.20 × 365 | 25 |

Revenue fell by 2.5%, receivables fell from 60 days to 25 days of revenue, and inventory rose by 62.5% (₹9.60 crore to ₹15.60 crore).

- *Genuine reasons*: the change to 30-day terms; a collection drive before the year end; receivables discounted or factored with a bank.
- *Possible misstatements*: receipts of April recorded as received in March (cut-off); receivables reduced by unsupported credit notes or write-offs; goods dispatched to distributors but not invoiced, which would lower revenue and receivables and, if the dispatches were not removed from stock records, also explain the rise in inventory; receivables reclassified under another head or set off against amounts payable without a right to do so.

**(b) Relevant provision.** Audit risk is a function of the risks of material misstatement and detection risk. The risk of material misstatement at the assertion level comprises inherent risk and control risk; both are the entity's risks, which exist independently of the audit and are not influenced by the auditor. The auditor can only influence detection risk, and must reduce it to keep audit risk at a low level, by increasing the area of checking, testing larger samples and including competent and experienced persons in the engagement team. The assessment of risks is a matter of professional judgment based on the audit procedures performed and the evidence obtained. Irrespective of the assessed risks, the auditor shall design and perform substantive procedures for each material class of transactions, account balance and disclosure.

**Facts and analysis.**

1. *An explanation does not lower inherent risk.* The unusual movements are indicators of risks of material misstatement in the occurrence and cut-off of receipts, the completeness of revenue and the valuation of inventory. The CFO's explanation is a representation to be corroborated (the January circular to distributors, the revised agreements, receipts in the bank statements of March and April), not evidence that the risk is low.
2. *A control test is not enough.* Testing the credit-terms control may give evidence about control risk, but not about whether receipts, revenue and inventory are correctly recorded at the year end; and substantive procedures on material receivables cannot be skipped.
3. *Detection risk must be kept low.* With the risk of material misstatement high, the team should test cut-off of receipts and dispatches around 31 March, confirm balances with distributors, examine credit notes and write-offs, match dispatch records with invoices, and assign experienced persons to these areas.

**Conclusion.** Bunty's proposal is not proper. The auditor cannot change the entity's risks by accepting an explanation; the only lever is detection risk, and here it must be kept low.
:::

## Under the hood

### Two levels, two kinds of response

A **financial statement level** risk spreads across many assertions: in MTP January 2026 Series I, bonuses pushing the CEO and CFO to overstate profit are one. Such risks get **overall responses**; assertion-level risks get **further audit procedures** ([[aud.risk.sa330|SA 330]]). Identifying them is [[aud.risk.sa315|SA 315]].

### Detection risk and sample size

The higher the assessed risk of material misstatement, the larger the sample for tests of details. If the auditor does not test controls, the assessment cannot be reduced for effective controls, so detection risk must be low and more evidence comes from tests of details (SM 4.58).

::: lab audit-risk {"mode":"numeric","ar":5,"irp":80,"crp":50,"crp2":80}
Put illustrative numbers on the formula: hold audit risk at 5%, let control risk rise from 50% to 80%, and watch the acceptable detection risk fall.
:::

### A new client raises detection risk

In the Study Material's Case Study 2, a new client means increased detection risk and more substantive procedures (SM 3.76–3.77).

### Materiality and audit risk move in opposite directions

There is an **inverse relationship** between materiality and the degree of audit risk (SM 3.81); see [[aud.risk.sa320|SA 320]]. Audit risk can never be zero, because of the [[aud.nature.limits|inherent limitations of an audit]] (SM 1.16).

## Answer frame

### "Identify and explain the risk" in a case

1. **Name the risk** and give its SA 200 definition in ICAI's words.
2. **Explain it** with the Study Material's points (inherent risk: higher for complex calculations, external circumstances; control risk: inverse relation with efficiency of internal control; detection risk: sampling and non-sampling).
3. **Apply it to the facts**: "In the given case, ..."
4. **Conclude**, and add the consequence: "Hence detection risk must be kept low by increasing the area of checking, testing larger samples and using competent and experienced staff."

### "Distinguish between inherent, control and detection risk"

| Point | Inherent risk | Control risk | Detection risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Key words | Susceptibility of an assertion, "before consideration of any related controls" | Not "prevented, or detected and corrected, on a timely basis" by internal control | Auditor's procedures "will not detect" a material misstatement |
| Owner | Entity | Entity | Auditor |
| Auditor can change it? | No, only assess it | No, only assess it | Yes |
| Moves with | Complexity, external circumstances | Inversely with efficiency of internal control | Area of checking, sample size, staff competence |
| Example | Complex calculation; failing industry | Locked-safe rule not followed | Unrepresentative sample |

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: the definition swap
Writing "control risk is the susceptibility of an account balance to misstatement, assuming there were no related internal controls". That is the definition of **inherent** risk; ICAI's own Test Your Knowledge marks this statement incorrect (SM 3.81–3.82). **Fix:** "susceptibility ... before consideration of controls" is inherent risk; "not prevented, or detected and corrected, on a timely basis by internal control" is control risk.
:::

::: mistake "The risk is high, so keep detection risk high"
**Fix:** they move in opposite directions. High risk of material misstatement means detection risk must be kept low, which means more and better evidence (MTP September 2024 Series I).
:::

::: mistake The auditor who promises to cut control risk
Writing that the auditor "reduces inherent or control risk". **Fix:** those are the entity's risks, influenced by the client and not by the auditor (SM 3.5); the auditor assesses them and can reduce only detection risk.
:::

::: mistake Counting the lawsuit
**Fix:** litigation, adverse publicity, and calling correct accounts wrong are not audit risk for the SAs.
:::

= 10

- [ ] No, detection risk must be eliminated altogether

- [x] Increasing the area of checking
- [x] Testing larger samples
- [x] Including competent and experienced persons in the engagement team

The other three are erroneous conclusions for reasons not related to sampling (accepting unreliable evidence, applying the wrong rates, using an inappropriate procedure): non-sampling risk.

## Recap

- Audit risk: the risk that the auditor gives an inappropriate opinion when the financial statements are materially misstated. It is a function of the risk of material misstatement and detection risk.
- Risk of material misstatement: misstated before audit; at the financial statement level and the assertion level; components inherent risk and control risk, both the entity's.
- Inherent risk: susceptibility to material misstatement before considering controls; higher for complex calculations and in failing industries.
- Control risk: internal control will not prevent, or detect and correct, a material misstatement on time; inverse to the efficiency of internal control.
- Detection risk: the auditor's procedures will not detect it; sampling and non-sampling risk; the only one the auditor can influence.
- Reduce detection risk: wider checking, larger samples, competent and experienced staff.
- Not audit risk: the auditor's business risks; calling correct accounts wrong. Assessment is judgment, not measurement.
- Audit risk = inherent risk × control risk × detection risk. (Sour chai needs all three strainers to fail.)

Q:: SA 200: define control risk.
A:: The risk that a misstatement that could occur in an assertion and could be material, individually or in aggregate, will not be prevented, or detected and corrected, on a timely basis by the entity's internal control.

Q:: SA 200: define detection risk.
A:: The risk that the procedures performed by the auditor to reduce audit risk to an acceptably low level will not detect a misstatement that exists and that could be material, individually or in aggregate.

Q:: What are the two parts of detection risk?
A:: Sampling risk (the sample's conclusion differs from what testing the whole population would show) and non-sampling risk (an erroneous conclusion for any other reason, such as an inappropriate procedure).

Q:: Which component of audit risk can the auditor influence, and how is it reduced?
A:: Only detection risk: by increasing the area of checking, testing larger samples and including competent and experienced persons in the engagement team.

Q:: Which two matters are not included in audit risk?
A:: The auditor's business risks (litigation, adverse publicity), and the risk of expressing an opinion that the financial statements are materially misstated when they are not.

Q:: What is the relation between control risk and the efficiency of internal control?
A:: Inverse: the more efficient the internal control, the lower the control risk.

Q:: Audit risk as a formula, per the Study Material?
A:: Audit risk = risks of material misstatement × detection risk = inherent risk × control risk × detection risk.
