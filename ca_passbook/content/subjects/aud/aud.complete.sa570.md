## In plain words

### Will the shop open tomorrow?

Iyer & Associates also audit Mehra Mithai Ltd, the sweets company of Kesar's cousin. It has made losses two years running, its ₹20 crore of debentures fall due on 30 September 2026 with no refinancing in place, and its sugar supplier now insists on cash on delivery.

> **Bunty:** Ma'am, the plant and machinery are in the books at ₹18 crore, cost less depreciation. That is fine, na?
> **Lakshmi:** Fine if the factory runs next year. If it shuts, who pays ₹18 crore for a halwa line?
> **Kesar:** *(on the phone)* My cousin will manage. I have told him I will help.
> **Lakshmi:** "I will help" is a feeling, Kesar-ji. I need an arrangement.

Accounts are normally prepared on the **going concern basis**: the assumption that the business will carry on for the foreseeable future, with neither the intention nor the need to close down or shrink drastically. On that basis, the factory is worth what it will earn over its working life. If the business is about to close, the same factory is worth only what a buyer will pay for it now, and the accounts must be prepared on a liquidation basis.

**Management** must assess whether the entity can continue as a going concern. The **auditor** obtains evidence about whether management's use of the going concern basis is appropriate, and concludes whether there is a **material uncertainty**: events or conditions that may cast significant doubt on the entity's ability to continue, serious enough that readers must be told about them. Those two conclusions, and whether the uncertainty is properly disclosed, decide what the auditor's report says.

One more thing: the auditor cannot see the future. A report that says nothing about going concern is not a guarantee that the business will survive.

::: analogy The new sofa
If you will live in your flat for years, the sofa you just bought is worth what it gives you over those years. If you are moving out next week, it is worth what a buyer on a resale site will pay. Same sofa, two values: the going concern basis is the first, the liquidation basis the second. And if you might move out, depending on whether your landlord renews the lease, your flatmates deserve to be told: that is the material uncertainty.
:::

::: key
Two questions decide the report: is the going concern basis appropriate, and does a material uncertainty exist? If it does, the third question is whether the financial statements disclose it adequately.
:::

## How it works

### 1. Meaning and significance

Going concern is one of the fundamental accounting assumptions. The enterprise is normally viewed as a going concern, that is, as continuing in operation for the foreseeable future. It is assumed that the enterprise has neither the intention nor the necessity of liquidation or of curtailing materially the scale of the operations (SM 7.13). General purpose financial statements are prepared using the going concern basis of accounting, unless management either intends to liquidate the entity or to cease operations, or has no realistic alternative but to do so.

When the use of the going concern basis is appropriate, assets and liabilities are recorded on the basis that the entity will be able to realise its assets and discharge its liabilities in the normal course of business. When an enterprise is not viewed as a going concern, the financial statements are prepared on liquidation basis: inventories may need to be written down, and assets may have to be recorded at the likely prices they will fetch (SM 7.14).

Mehra Mithai's main assets show the difference (₹ crore; liquidation values as estimated by a valuer):

| Asset | Going concern basis | Liquidation basis |
|---|---:|---:|
| Plant and machinery | 18.00 | 6.50 |
| Goodwill of the acquired brand | 12.00 | 0.00 |
| Inventories | 2.40 | 1.10 |
| Trade receivables | 1.80 | 1.40 |
| **Total** | **34.20** | **9.00** |

₹25.20 crore, or 73.7% of these assets, depends on the single assumption that the business carries on.

### 2. Management's responsibility

The preparation of the financial statements requires management to assess the entity's ability to continue as a going concern even if the financial reporting framework does not include an explicit requirement to do so. The assessment involves making a judgment, at a particular point in time, about inherently uncertain future outcomes. Relevant to that judgment (SM 7.14–7.15):

- The degree of uncertainty increases significantly the further into the future an event or condition or its outcome occurs.
- The size and complexity of the entity, the nature and condition of its business and the degree to which it is affected by external factors affect the judgment.
- Any judgment about the future is based on information available at the time it is made; subsequent events may produce outcomes inconsistent with judgments that were reasonable at the time.

### 3. The auditor's responsibilities and objectives

The auditor's responsibilities are to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence regarding, and conclude on, the appropriateness of management's use of the going concern basis of accounting, and to conclude, based on the evidence, whether a material uncertainty exists about the entity's ability to continue as a going concern. These responsibilities exist even if the framework does not explicitly require management to make a specific assessment (SM 7.15).

However, as described in SA 200, the potential effects of inherent limitations on the auditor's ability to detect material misstatements are greater for future events or conditions; the auditor cannot predict them. Accordingly, ==the absence of any reference to a material uncertainty== about the entity's ability to continue as a going concern in an auditor's report cannot be viewed as a guarantee as to the entity's ability to continue as a going concern (SM 7.15).

The objectives of the auditor are (SM 7.15):

- (a) to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence regarding and conclude on the appropriateness of management's use of the going concern basis of accounting in the preparation of the financial statements;
- (b) to conclude, based on the audit evidence obtained, whether a material uncertainty exists related to events or conditions that may cast significant doubt on the entity's ability to continue as a going concern; and
- (c) to report in accordance with this SA.

### 4. Risk assessment: look for events or conditions

When performing risk assessment procedures under [[aud.risk.sa315|SA 315]], the auditor shall consider whether events or conditions exist that may cast significant doubt on the entity's ability to continue as a going concern, and shall determine whether management has already performed a preliminary assessment (SM 7.16):

- (a) if it has, discuss the assessment with management and determine whether management has identified such events or conditions, individually or collectively, and, if so, management's plans to address them; or
- (b) if it has not, discuss with management the basis for the intended use of the going concern basis, and inquire whether such events or conditions exist.

The auditor shall remain alert throughout the audit for audit evidence of such events or conditions.

**Events or conditions that, individually or collectively, may cast significant doubt** (SM 7.17–7.18). *ICAI asks for these by category; learn them as three lists.*

**Financial**

- Net liability or net current liability position.
- Fixed-term borrowings approaching maturity without realistic prospects of renewal or repayment; or excessive reliance on short-term borrowings to finance long-term assets.
- Indications of withdrawal of financial support by creditors.
- Negative operating cash flows indicated by historical or prospective financial statements.
- Adverse key financial ratios.
- Substantial operating losses or significant deterioration in the value of assets used to generate cash flows.
- Arrears or discontinuance of dividends.
- Inability to pay creditors on due dates.
- Inability to comply with the terms of loan agreements.
- Change from credit to cash-on-delivery transactions with suppliers.
- Inability to obtain financing for essential new product development or other essential investments.

**Operating**

- Management intentions to liquidate the entity or to cease operations.
- Loss of key management without replacement.
- Loss of a major market, key customer(s), franchise, license, or principal supplier(s).
- Labour difficulties.
- Shortages of important supplies.
- Emergence of a highly successful competitor.

**Other**

- Non-compliance with capital or other statutory or regulatory requirements, such as solvency or liquidity requirements for financial institutions.
- Pending legal or regulatory proceedings against the entity that may, if successful, result in claims that the entity is unlikely to be able to satisfy.
- Changes in law or regulation or government policy expected to adversely affect the entity.
- Uninsured or underinsured catastrophes when they occur.

### 5. Evaluating management's assessment

The auditor shall evaluate management's assessment of the entity's ability to continue as a going concern; it is a key part of the auditor's consideration of management's use of the going concern basis (SM 7.18).

- It is not the auditor's responsibility to rectify the lack of analysis by management. When there is a history of profitable operations and a ready access to financial resources, management may make its assessment without detailed analysis, and the auditor's evaluation may be made without detailed evaluation procedures if the auditor's other procedures are sufficient to conclude on the appropriateness of the basis.
- In other circumstances, the evaluation may include the process management followed, the assumptions on which the assessment is based, and management's plans for future action and whether they are feasible in the circumstances.
- The auditor shall cover the same period as that used by management, as required by the framework, or by law or regulation if it specifies a longer period. If management's assessment covers less than **twelve months from the date of the financial statements**, the auditor shall request management to extend it to at least twelve months from that date (SM 7.19).

### 6. Additional procedures when events or conditions are identified

If events or conditions have been identified that may cast significant doubt, the auditor shall obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence to determine whether or not a material uncertainty exists, through performing additional audit procedures, including consideration of mitigating factors. These procedures shall include (SM 7.19–7.20):

- (a) where management has not yet performed an assessment, requesting management to make its assessment;
- (b) evaluating management's plans for future actions in relation to its going concern assessment, whether the outcome of these plans is likely to improve the situation and whether they are feasible in the circumstances;
- (c) where the entity has prepared a cash flow forecast, and analysis of the forecast is a significant factor in evaluating management's plans: (i) evaluating the reliability of the underlying data generated to prepare the forecast; and (ii) determining whether there is adequate support for the assumptions underlying the forecast;
- (d) considering whether any additional facts or information have become available since the date on which management made its assessment;
- (e) requesting written representations from management and, where appropriate, those charged with governance, regarding their plans for future actions and the feasibility of these plans.

**Examples of such procedures** (SM 7.20):

- Analysing and discussing cash flow, profit and other relevant forecasts with management.
- Analysing and discussing the entity's latest available interim financial statements.
- Reading the terms of debentures and loan agreements and determining whether any have been breached.
- Reading minutes of the meetings of shareholders, those charged with governance and relevant committees for reference to financing difficulties.
- Inquiring of the entity's legal counsel regarding litigation and claims and the reasonableness of management's assessments of their outcome and financial implications.
- Confirming the existence, legality and enforceability of arrangements to provide or maintain financial support with related and third parties, and assessing their financial ability to provide additional funds.
- Evaluating the entity's plans to deal with unfilled customer orders.
- Performing audit procedures regarding subsequent events ([[aud.complete.sa560|SA 560]]) to identify those that mitigate or otherwise affect going concern.
- Confirming the existence, terms and adequacy of borrowing facilities.
- Obtaining and reviewing reports of regulatory actions.
- Determining the adequacy of support for any planned disposals of assets.

### 7. The auditor's conclusions

The auditor shall evaluate whether sufficient appropriate audit evidence has been obtained regarding, and shall conclude on, the appropriateness of management's use of the going concern basis of accounting, and shall conclude whether, in the auditor's judgment, a material uncertainty exists (SM 7.20–7.21).

::: definition Material uncertainty
A material uncertainty exists when the magnitude of its potential impact and likelihood of occurrence is such that, in the auditor's judgment, appropriate disclosure of the nature and implications of the uncertainty is necessary (SM 7.21).
:::

**Disclosures when a material uncertainty exists.** If the basis is appropriate but a material uncertainty exists, the auditor shall determine whether the financial statements (SM 7.21):

- (a) adequately disclose the principal events or conditions that may cast significant doubt on the entity's ability to continue as a going concern and management's plans to deal with them; and
- (b) disclose clearly that there is a material uncertainty related to events or conditions that may cast significant doubt on the entity's ability to continue as a going concern and, therefore, that it may be unable to realise its assets and discharge its liabilities in the normal course of business.

**Disclosures when no material uncertainty exists.** If events or conditions have been identified but the auditor concludes that no material uncertainty exists, the auditor shall evaluate whether, in view of the requirements of the framework, the financial statements provide adequate disclosures about these events or conditions (SM 7.21).

### 8. Implications for the auditor's report

1. **Basis inappropriate.** If the financial statements have been prepared using the going concern basis but, in the auditor's judgment, its use is inappropriate, the auditor shall express an **adverse opinion** (SM 7.21–7.22).
2. **Basis appropriate, material uncertainty adequately disclosed.** The auditor shall express an **unmodified opinion** and the report shall include a separate section under the heading **"Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern"** to (a) draw attention to the note in the financial statements that discloses such matters; and (b) state that these events or conditions indicate that a material uncertainty exists that may cast significant doubt on the entity's ability to continue as a going concern and that the auditor's opinion is not modified in respect of the matter (SM 7.22).
3. **Basis appropriate, material uncertainty not adequately disclosed.** The auditor shall (a) express a **qualified opinion or adverse opinion**, as appropriate, in accordance with [[aud.report.sa705|SA 705]]; and (b) in the Basis for Qualified (Adverse) Opinion section, state that a material uncertainty exists that may cast significant doubt on the entity's ability to continue as a going concern and that the financial statements do not adequately disclose this matter (SM 7.22).
4. **Management unwilling to make or extend its assessment** when requested. The auditor shall consider the implications for the report: a **qualified opinion or a disclaimer of opinion** may be appropriate, because it may not be possible to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence regarding the use of the going concern basis (SM 7.22–7.23).

### The "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" section

Its two contents are listed in point 2 above: (a) draw attention to the note; (b) state that a material uncertainty exists and that the opinion is not modified in respect of it (SM 7.22). Neither an Emphasis of Matter paragraph ([[aud.report.sa706|SA 706]]) nor a key audit matter ([[aud.report.sa701|SA 701]]) is a substitute for this reporting (SM 8.50, 8.54).

## Worked examples

:::: example Mehra Mithai's sweet nothings
Mehra Mithai Ltd's draft financial statements for 2025-26 show a loss of ₹6.80 crore (₹5.10 crore last year), current assets of ₹14.20 crore against current liabilities of ₹21.60 crore, and operating cash flow of ₹(3.10) crore. Its 14% debentures of ₹20 crore are due for redemption on 30 September 2026; no refinancing has been agreed. The dividend was skipped, and the sugar mill now supplies only on cash on delivery. Management prepares the accounts on the going concern basis and gives this cash forecast for 2026-27 (₹ crore):

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Opening cash | 0.60 |
| Operating cash flow (turnaround expected) | 0.40 |
| Sale of the acquired brand (offer letter received) | 9.00 |
| New term loan (application under consideration) | 12.00 |
| Redemption of debentures | (20.00) |
| Debenture interest to 30 September | (1.40) |
| **Closing cash** | **0.60** |

Kesar has told his cousin, orally, that he will "help if needed".

Required:

(i) Identify the events or conditions that may cast significant doubt on going concern.

(ii) State the additional audit procedures Iyer & Associates should perform.

(iii) Recompute the closing cash if the loan is not sanctioned, and if operating cash flow repeats this year's figure. Does a material uncertainty exist?

(iv) How should the auditor report if the uncertainty is (a) adequately disclosed; (b) not disclosed?

::: answer Model answer
**(i) Events or conditions.** As per SA 570, the following, individually or collectively, may cast significant doubt on the entity's ability to continue as a going concern:

- **Financial:** net current liability position of ₹7.40 crore (current ratio 0.66); fixed-term borrowings (₹20 crore debentures) approaching maturity without realistic prospects of renewal or repayment yet; negative operating cash flows; substantial operating losses in two years; discontinuance of dividends; change from credit to cash-on-delivery transactions with a supplier.

**(ii) Additional procedures.** Since events or conditions have been identified, the auditor shall perform additional procedures, including consideration of mitigating factors, to determine whether a material uncertainty exists:

1. Evaluate management's plans (brand sale, new loan, turnaround): whether their outcome is likely to improve the situation and whether they are feasible.
2. As the forecast is significant to that evaluation: evaluate the reliability of the underlying data, and determine whether there is adequate support for the assumptions, in particular a positive operating cash flow of ₹0.40 crore after a negative ₹3.10 crore this year. The forecast also shows no interest or repayment on the ₹12 crore term loan it assumes.
3. Determine the adequacy of support for the planned disposal of the brand (the offer letter, its terms and the buyer's ability to pay); confirm the existence, terms and adequacy of the borrowing facility applied for; read the debenture trust deed and loan agreements for breaches; read board and shareholder minutes for financing difficulties.
4. Confirm the existence, legality and enforceability of any arrangement for financial support from related and third parties, and assess their financial ability to provide funds. Kesar's oral promise is not, without more, such an arrangement.
5. Consider whether additional facts have become available since management's assessment, including subsequent events; request written representations from management and those charged with governance on their plans and their feasibility.

**(iii) Working note: the forecast under the auditor's sensitivities (₹ crore)**

| Scenario | Closing cash |
|---|---:|
| Management's forecast | 0.60 |
| Loan not sanctioned: 0.60 − 12.00 | (11.40) |
| Operating cash flow as in 2025-26: 0.60 − 0.40 − 3.10 | (2.90) |
| Both | (14.90) |

Each closing figure = 0.60 opening + operating cash flow + 9.00 brand sale + loan − 20.00 redemption − 1.40 interest. Management does not intend to liquidate, and its plans, if achieved, meet the redemption: the going concern basis can be appropriate. But the outcome depends on a loan not yet sanctioned and a sale not yet completed, and either failure leaves the company unable to redeem. The magnitude of the potential impact and its likelihood are such that disclosure is necessary: **a material uncertainty exists**.

**(iv)(a) Adequately disclosed.** The auditor shall express an **unmodified opinion** with a separate section headed "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern", drawing attention to the note that discloses the matter and stating that these events or conditions indicate that a material uncertainty exists that may cast significant doubt on the entity's ability to continue as a going concern and that the opinion is not modified in respect of the matter.

**(iv)(b) Not disclosed.** The auditor shall express a **qualified or adverse opinion**, as appropriate, in accordance with SA 705, and in the Basis for Qualified (Adverse) Opinion section state that a material uncertainty exists that may cast significant doubt on the entity's ability to continue as a going concern and that the financial statements do not adequately disclose this matter. If, after the procedures, the auditor concluded that the basis itself was inappropriate, the opinion would be **adverse**.

:::: example The assessment that went stale, and the rival that did not sink Tapri
(a) Tapri Cold Chain Pvt Ltd, Tapri's subsidiary, made losses in 2024-25 and 2025-26, and its current liabilities exceed its current assets by ₹7.40 crore at 31 March 2026. Its board approved management's going concern assessment, covering the twelve months to 31 March 2027, on 25 April 2026. The assessment assumes that Narmada Co-operative Bank will renew the company's ₹15 crore working capital limit in full in June 2026. On 20 May 2026, nine days before the auditor's report is due, the bank writes that it will renew only ₹9 crore. Management's response: "Our assessment was approved in April. A May letter cannot reopen it."

(b) BrewBucks opened 60 coffee bars in Tapri's ten largest cities during 2025-26, and Tapri's sales at its existing cafés there fell by 12%. Given Tapri's profits, its cash of ₹85 crore and its undrawn bank limits, Iyer & Associates conclude that no material uncertainty exists. Bunty notes in the file: "No material uncertainty, so going concern needs no mention anywhere."

Required: Advise Iyer & Associates under SA 570 on (a) management's response and the further audit work, and (b) Bunty's note.

::: answer Model answer
**Relevant provision.** As per SA 570, if events or conditions have been identified that may cast significant doubt on the entity's ability to continue as a going concern, the auditor shall obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence to determine whether or not a material uncertainty exists, through performing additional audit procedures, including consideration of mitigating factors. These include evaluating management's plans for future actions, whether their outcome is likely to improve the situation and whether they are feasible; where a cash flow forecast is significant, evaluating the reliability of the underlying data and whether there is adequate support for the assumptions; **considering whether any additional facts or information have become available since the date on which management made its assessment**; and requesting written representations regarding the plans and their feasibility (SM 7.19–7.20). If events or conditions have been identified but the auditor concludes that no material uncertainty exists, the auditor shall evaluate whether, in view of the requirements of the applicable financial reporting framework, the financial statements provide adequate disclosures about these events or conditions (SM 7.21).

**(a) Facts and analysis.** The losses and the net current liability position of ₹7.40 crore are financial events or conditions in SA 570's list, so the additional procedures apply. Management's response is **not correct**: the bank's letter of 20 May is additional information that has become available since the assessment was approved on 25 April, and the auditor must consider it. It removes ₹6 crore (₹15 crore − ₹9 crore) of the funding on which the assessment rests. Iyer & Associates should:

1. ask management how it will meet the ₹6 crore gap (for example, funds from Tapri, another lender, or a sale of assets), and evaluate whether those plans are likely to improve the situation and are feasible;
2. evaluate the reliability of the data in the cash flow forecast and whether its other assumptions are adequately supported;
3. confirm the existence, terms and adequacy of the borrowing facilities with the bank; if the plan relies on Tapri's support, confirm the existence, legality and enforceability of that arrangement and assess Tapri's financial ability to provide the funds; read board minutes for references to financing difficulties;
4. request written representations from management and, where appropriate, those charged with governance, about the plans and their feasibility.

On that evidence they conclude whether a material uncertainty exists, and then evaluate the disclosure and report under SA 570.

**(b) Facts and analysis.** The emergence of a highly successful competitor is an operating event or condition in SA 570's list. The conclusion that no material uncertainty exists means that the report carries no "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" section; it does not end the auditor's work on disclosure. Bunty's note is **not correct**: Iyer & Associates shall evaluate whether, in view of the requirements of the applicable financial reporting framework, the financial statements provide adequate disclosures about BrewBucks' expansion and its effects.
:::

## Under the hood

### Going concern in the rest of the report

- **SA 700.** Where applicable, the auditor reports in accordance with SA 570 (Revised); the report includes, where applicable, a section addressing the going concern reporting requirements (SM 8.13–8.14, 8.25), and the auditor's responsibilities section describes the conclusion on the going concern basis and on material uncertainty (SM 8.19). See [[aud.report.sa700|SA 700]].
- **Related parties.** The procedure of confirming financial support "with related and third parties" ties SA 570 to [[aud.evid.sa550|SA 550]]: a promise from a promoter is evidence only if an enforceable arrangement exists and the promoter can pay.

### Twelve months from which date?

The twelve months run from the **date of the financial statements**, not from the date of the auditor's report or the date management made its assessment (SM 7.19). Where the framework, or law or regulation, specifies a longer period, the auditor covers that period.

::: exam The traps
- **The category trap.** Shortage of important supplies, labour difficulties and loss of key management are *operating*; arrears of dividends and cash-on-delivery suppliers are *financial*; non-compliance with regulatory requirements and pending litigation are *other*.
- **"Net loss means not a going concern"** is false (Study Material case study MCQ 2; MTP September 2024 Series I Q2(a)).
- **Its own section, never Key Audit Matters.** In January 2025 MCQ 15, management says the going concern assumption "will not be suitable" and that the uncertainty will be "suitably disclosed" in the notes. No option offered an adverse opinion; three options put the matter under Key Audit Matters, and ICAI's key is (A), a separate "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" section under SA 570. Keep the two rules of SM 7.22 apart in your own answers: an appropriate basis with an adequately disclosed material uncertainty gives that section; statements prepared on the going concern basis when its use is inappropriate get an adverse opinion.
:::

::: trick Three questions, five reports
"Basis appropriate?" No: **adverse**. "Material uncertainty?" No: **unmodified**. "Adequately disclosed?" Yes: **unmodified + Material Uncertainty section**; No: **qualified or adverse**. And the odd one out: management will not assess: **qualified or disclaimer**. Condition: the first branch assumes the statements were prepared on the going concern basis.

## Answer frame

### A case on going concern

1. **Relevant provision**: the part of SA 570 the case turns on (events or conditions, additional procedures, the twelve-month rule, or the reporting requirement), in the Standard's words.
2. **Facts**: list the events or conditions with their category and figures; management's plans and assessment period; the disclosure made.
3. **Analysis**: is the basis appropriate? Do mitigating factors and feasible plans remove the doubt, or does a material uncertainty remain? Is it adequately disclosed?
4. **Conclusion**: the opinion and the report element ("Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" section, or the statement in the Basis section), or the procedure demanded.

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: Bunty's "qualified, because of going concern doubts"
Bunty qualifies every report with a material uncertainty. **Fix:** an adequately disclosed material uncertainty leaves the opinion **unmodified**, with a separate "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" section. Modification follows only inadequate disclosure, an inappropriate basis, or management's refusal to assess.
:::

::: mistake Using an Emphasis of Matter paragraph for a material uncertainty
**Fix:** SA 570 prescribes its own separate section; an Emphasis of Matter paragraph, or a key audit matter, is not a substitute (SM 8.50, 8.54).
:::

::: mistake Twelve months from the wrong date
**Fix:** management's assessment must cover at least twelve months from the **date of the financial statements**; if shorter, the auditor requests an extension.
:::

::: mistake "The clean report promised survival"
**Fix:** the absence of a reference to a material uncertainty cannot be viewed as a guarantee of the entity's ability to continue: inherent limitations are greater for future events.
:::

## Practice

::: quiz Check yourself
Q: Case scenario. The state pollution control board has claimed ₹38 crore from Shipra Agro Chemicals Ltd for river contamination; the company's net worth is ₹22 crore, and the tribunal hears the case in September 2026. Iyer & Associates conclude that the going concern basis is appropriate but that a material uncertainty exists. Note 41 describes the claim and management's plan to contest it, but nowhere states that a material uncertainty exists or that the company may be unable to realise its assets and discharge its liabilities in the normal course of business. Which of the following is correct?
- [x] A qualified or adverse opinion, as appropriate, with the Basis section stating that a material uncertainty exists and that the financial statements do not adequately disclose this matter
- [ ] An unmodified opinion with a "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" section, since the claim and the plans are disclosed
- [ ] An unmodified opinion with an Emphasis of Matter paragraph on Note 41
- [ ] A disclaimer of opinion, because the outcome of the hearing cannot be predicted
> Adequate disclosure needs both the principal events or conditions with management's plans **and** a clear statement that a material uncertainty exists (SM 7.21). Note 41 has only the first, so SA 570 requires a qualified or adverse opinion under SA 705, with the Basis for Qualified (Adverse) Opinion section stating the material uncertainty and the inadequate disclosure (SM 7.22). The separate section is for adequately disclosed uncertainties; an Emphasis of Matter paragraph is never the vehicle.

Q: Case scenario. Tapri's head of samosa production resigned in January 2026; in February a production head with ten years' experience at a large snack maker joined in his place. In listing events or conditions that may cast significant doubt on going concern, how should Iyer & Associates treat the resignation?
- [ ] As an operating event or condition: loss of key management counts whatever happens next
- [ ] As a financial event or condition, because recruiting a replacement costs money
- [x] Not as "loss of key management without replacement": the post was filled, so that condition does not exist
- [ ] As an event that alone requires a "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" section
> SA 570's operating example is "loss of key management **without replacement**" (SM 7.17). A prompt, experienced replacement removes that condition. The auditor still considers all events and conditions individually and collectively, and none of them leads straight to a report section.

Q: Case scenario. Tapri's management assessed its ability to continue as a going concern for the fifteen months from 1 April 2026 to 30 June 2027, to take in a debenture redemption due in June 2027. What period should Iyer & Associates' evaluation of the assessment cover?
- [ ] The twelve months to 31 March 2027, since that is the minimum SA 570 requires
- [ ] The twelve months from the date of the auditor's report
- [ ] Only the period up to the date of the auditor's report, since later events are for next year's audit
- [x] The same fifteen months to 30 June 2027 that management used
> In evaluating management's assessment, the auditor shall cover the same period as that used by management (SM 7.19). Twelve months from the date of the financial statements is the minimum the assessment must cover: had it covered less, the auditor would request management to extend it.

Q: Select all the additional audit procedures SA 570 requires when events or conditions casting significant doubt are identified.
- [x] Where management has not yet made an assessment, requesting it to make one
- [x] Evaluating management's plans for future actions and their feasibility
- [x] Requesting written representations regarding those plans and their feasibility
- [ ] Obtaining a guarantee from the auditor's firm that the entity will survive
> (a), (b), (e) of the five required procedures, with (c) the forecast and (d) new information. The auditor never guarantees survival.

Q: The financial statements are prepared on the going concern basis, but the board resolved in May to close the business, and the auditor concludes the basis is inappropriate. The opinion is:
- [ ] Qualified opinion
- [ ] Unmodified with a "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" section
- [x] Adverse opinion
- [ ] Disclaimer of opinion
> An inappropriate use of the going concern basis requires an adverse opinion; there is nothing uncertain to disclose about a decided closure.

## Recap

- Going concern: the entity continues in operation for the foreseeable future, with neither the intention nor the necessity of liquidation or of materially curtailing operations; otherwise, the liquidation basis.
- Management assesses; the auditor obtains evidence on the appropriateness of the basis, concludes on material uncertainty, and reports. No reference to a material uncertainty is not a guarantee.
- Events or conditions: financial (net current liabilities, maturing debt, negative cash flows, losses, dividend arrears, cash-on-delivery suppliers), operating (key management, major market or customer, labour, supplies, competitor), other (regulatory non-compliance, litigation, law changes, uninsured catastrophes).
- Management's assessment covers at least twelve months from the date of the financial statements, or the auditor requests an extension.
- Additional procedures: request an assessment; evaluate plans; test the forecast's data and assumptions; consider new information; written representations.
- Material uncertainty: potential impact and likelihood such that disclosure of its nature and implications is necessary.
- Basis inappropriate: adverse. Uncertainty adequately disclosed: unmodified + "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" section. Not adequately disclosed: qualified or adverse. Management will not assess: qualified or disclaimer may be appropriate.
- Kesar's "I will help" is a feeling, not an arrangement: confirm support is enforceable and affordable.

## Flashcards

Q:: SA 570: what does the going concern assumption mean?
A:: The enterprise is viewed as continuing in operation for the foreseeable future, having neither the intention nor the necessity of liquidation or of curtailing materially the scale of its operations.

Q:: SA 570: what are the objectives of the auditor?
A:: (a) To obtain sufficient appropriate evidence and conclude on the appropriateness of management's use of the going concern basis; (b) to conclude whether a material uncertainty exists; (c) to report in accordance with SA 570.

Q:: SA 570: is an auditor's report without a material uncertainty reference a guarantee of going concern?
A:: No. Inherent limitations are greater for future events or conditions that the auditor cannot predict.

Q:: SA 570: give five operating events or conditions that may cast significant doubt.
A:: Management intentions to liquidate or cease operations; loss of key management without replacement; loss of a major market, key customer, franchise, licence or principal supplier; labour difficulties; shortages of important supplies; emergence of a highly successful competitor.

Q:: SA 570: give five financial events or conditions that may cast significant doubt.
A:: Net (current) liability position; maturing fixed-term borrowings without realistic prospects of renewal or repayment; negative operating cash flows; adverse key financial ratios; arrears or discontinuance of dividends (also: losses, inability to pay creditors, loan-term breaches, cash-on-delivery suppliers).

Q:: SA 570: the period management's assessment must cover?
A:: The same period as required by the framework or law (if longer); if less than twelve months from the date of the financial statements, the auditor requests an extension to at least twelve months.

Q:: SA 570: the additional procedures required when events or conditions are identified?
A:: Request an assessment if none; evaluate management's plans and their feasibility; for a significant cash flow forecast, evaluate data reliability and support for assumptions; consider new facts since the assessment; obtain written representations on plans.

Q:: SA 570: when does a material uncertainty exist?
A:: When the magnitude of its potential impact and likelihood of occurrence is such that, in the auditor's judgment, appropriate disclosure of its nature and implications is necessary.

Q:: SA 570: report when the basis is appropriate and a material uncertainty is adequately disclosed?
A:: Unmodified opinion with a separate section headed "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" that draws attention to the note and states that the opinion is not modified in respect of the matter.

Q:: SA 570: report when a material uncertainty is not adequately disclosed?
A:: Qualified or adverse opinion under SA 705, stating in the Basis section that a material uncertainty exists and that the financial statements do not adequately disclose it.

Q:: SA 570: report when the going concern basis is inappropriate?
A:: An adverse opinion.

Q:: SA 570: report when management is unwilling to make or extend its assessment?
A:: The auditor considers the implications: a qualified opinion or a disclaimer of opinion may be appropriate.
