## In plain words

### The factory that worked every hour and still fell short

Tapri's samosa factory has fixed overheads of ₹6,00,000 a month: rent, supervisors' salaries, depreciation of the fryers. They stay the same whatever the factory makes. To put them into the cost of each batch, Ritu fixes a rate in advance from the budget: 15,000 batches a month (30,000 machine hours in 25 days), so **₹40 a batch**, or ₹20 a machine hour. Each batch made carries ₹40; that is the **absorbed** fixed overhead.

In October the factory worked 24 days (Diwali took one), made 14,100 batches, and spent ₹5,88,000.

> **Kesar:** We spent ₹12,000 less than the budget, and the fryers ran all 30,000 budgeted hours. Why is the fixed overhead report ₹24,000 adverse?
> **Ritu:** Per unit, please. Each batch carries ₹40. The budget expected 15,000 batches; we made 14,100. The 900 missing batches could not carry their ₹40.
> **Kesar:** Diwali. Sab adjust ho jayega.
> **Ritu:** Diwali took one day. Slow frying took the rest.

The 14,100 batches absorbed 14,100 × ₹40 = ₹5,64,000, against the ₹5,88,000 spent: the **fixed overhead cost variance** is ₹24,000 adverse. Two questions explain it. **Did we spend what the budget said?** ₹6,00,000 budgeted, ₹5,88,000 spent: ₹12,000 favourable, the **expenditure variance**. **Did we make what the budget said?** ₹5,64,000 absorbed against ₹6,00,000 budgeted: ₹36,000 adverse, the **volume variance**, fixed overhead left unrecovered because output fell short.

Volume has three causes. **Calendar**: one working day lost, ₹24,000 (A). **Capacity**: in the 24 days the fryers ran 30,000 hours, 1,200 more than 24 days were budgeted for: ₹24,000 (F). **Efficiency**: those 30,000 hours produced only what 28,200 standard hours should: ₹36,000 (A). The longer shifts made up for Diwali; slow frying made the month adverse.

::: analogy The leased auto-rickshaw
Raju leases an auto for ₹30,000 a month and plans 25 days × 10 hours × 2 trips an hour = 500 trips, so each fare must carry ₹60 of lease. If the owner raises the lease, that is expenditure. A city bandh that shuts him out for a day is calendar. Driving 8 hours instead of 10 on working days is capacity. Traffic that allows 1.5 trips an hour is efficiency. Every trip he does not make leaves ₹60 of lease unrecovered: the volume variance.
:::

::: key
Fixed overheads are absorbed on output at a rate fixed from the budget. The cost variance (absorbed − actual) splits into **expenditure** (budgeted − actual: spending) and **volume** (absorbed − budgeted: output), and volume splits into **efficiency**, **capacity** and **calendar**. A fixed overhead variance is an under- or over-absorption.
:::

## How it works

The recovery of fixed overheads depends on capacity utilisation: a company that produces less than the projected utilisation cannot recover all the budgeted fixed overheads (SM 13.38). With fixed overheads of ₹10,000, the cost per unit is ₹1.25 at 8,000 units and ₹0.91 at 11,000 (SM 13.39), so standard costing fixes a **predetermined rate** on a predetermined capacity. ICAI's own words: ==overhead variance is nothing but under or over-absorption of overhead== (SM 13.40).

ICAI's definitions, in its order (SM 13.40–13.41):

- **Fixed overhead cost variance**: the difference between actual fixed overhead and absorbed fixed overhead. It is divided into two parts:
- (A) **Fixed overhead expenditure variance**: the difference between the actual fixed overhead incurred and budgeted fixed overhead. Fig 13.1 calls it the "expenditure or **budget** variance" (SM 13.13).
- (B) **Fixed overhead volume variance**: variance in fixed overhead which arises due to the volume of production. It is further divided into three:
  - (a) **Efficiency variance**: the difference between fixed overhead absorbed and standard fixed overhead.
  - (b) **Capacity variance**: the difference between standard fixed overhead and budgeted overhead.
  - (c) **Calendar variance**: arises due to the difference in number of actual working days and the standard working days.

When the calendar variance is computed, the capacity variance is modified to a **revised capacity variance** = (actual hours − revised budgeted hours) × standard fixed rate per hour (SM 13.41). Revised budgeted hours are the budgeted hours for the days actually worked: budgeted hours ÷ budgeted days × actual days (SM 13.76).

### Seven steps through the Diwali month

Budget: ₹6,00,000; 25 days; 30,000 machine hours; 15,000 batches. Actual: ₹5,88,000; 24 days; 30,000 hours; 14,100 batches.

::: steps
1. **Rates** (SM 13.42): per hour ₹6,00,000 ÷ 30,000 = ₹20; per batch ₹6,00,000 ÷ 15,000 = ₹40; per day ₹6,00,000 ÷ 25 = ₹24,000. Standard time = 30,000 ÷ 15,000 = 2 hours a batch.
2. **Standard hours for actual output** = 14,100 × 2 = 28,200. **Absorbed** = 28,200 × ₹20 = **₹5,64,000** (also 14,100 × ₹40).
3. **Standard fixed overhead** = actual hours × SR = 30,000 × ₹20 = **₹6,00,000**. **Revised budgeted fixed overhead** = (30,000 ÷ 25 × 24 = 28,800 hours) × ₹20 = **₹5,76,000**.
4. **Cost variance** = absorbed − actual = ₹5,64,000 − ₹5,88,000 = **₹24,000 (A)**. **Expenditure** = budgeted − actual = ₹6,00,000 − ₹5,88,000 = **₹12,000 (F)**. **Volume** = absorbed − budgeted = ₹5,64,000 − ₹6,00,000 = **₹36,000 (A)**.
5. **Efficiency** = (SH − AH) × SR = (28,200 − 30,000) × ₹20 = **₹36,000 (A)**.
6. **Revised capacity** = (AH − revised budgeted hours) × SR = (30,000 − 28,800) × ₹20 = **₹24,000 (F)**. **Calendar** = (actual days − budgeted days) × rate per day = (24 − 25) × ₹24,000 = **₹24,000 (A)**.
7. **Checks** (SM 13.42): volume = efficiency + capacity + calendar: ₹36,000 (A) + ₹24,000 (F) + ₹24,000 (A) = ₹36,000 (A). Cost = expenditure + volume: ₹12,000 (F) + ₹36,000 (A) = ₹24,000 (A).
:::

Without the calendar split the capacity variance is (30,000 − 30,000) × ₹20 = nil: the factory did work its budgeted hours. The calendar variance shows the lost day, the revised capacity variance the longer shifts that made up for it.

## Formats and formulas

*Reproduce the formulas and the working-note table from memory.* SH = standard hours for actual output, AH = actual hours, BH = budgeted hours, RBH = revised budgeted hours, SR = standard fixed overhead rate per hour, BF = budgeted fixed overheads, AF = actual fixed overheads, AD and BD = actual and budgeted working days. Each variance is the first amount minus the second: positive is (F), negative is (A).

::: formula Fixed overhead cost variance
$$\text{Cost} = (SH \times SR) - AF$$
Absorbed (recovered) fixed overheads − actual fixed overheads. Absorbed = SH × SR, or actual output × standard rate per unit (SM 13.41–13.42).
:::

::: formula Expenditure and volume variances
$$\text{Expenditure} = BF - AF \qquad \text{Volume} = (SH \times SR) - BF$$
Budgeted − actual; absorbed − budgeted. BF = BH × SR (SM 13.41).
:::

::: formula Efficiency, capacity and calendar variances
$$\text{Efficiency} = SR\,(SH - AH) \qquad \text{Capacity} = SR\,(AH - BH)$$
$$\text{Calendar} = SR_{\text{day}} \times (AD - BD)$$
SR per day = standard fixed overhead rate per day. With a calendar variance, use the revised capacity variance = SR (AH − RBH) (SM 13.41).
:::

::: formula Rates and revised budgeted hours
$$SR = \frac{BF}{BH} \qquad RBH = \frac{BH}{BD} \times AD$$
Rate per unit = BF ÷ budgeted output; rate per day = BF ÷ BD. Revised budgeted fixed overheads (the Study Material also calls them "possible fixed overheads") = RBH × SR = BF ÷ BD × AD (SM 13.42, 13.76, 13.87).
:::

::: formula Verification
$$\text{Cost} = \text{Expenditure} + \text{Volume}$$
And volume = efficiency + capacity + calendar (SM 13.42).
:::

The summary in the Study Material uses the names **recovered**, **standard**, **budgeted** and **actual** overhead: cost = recovered − actual, expenditure = budgeted − actual, volume = recovered − budgeted, efficiency = recovered − standard, capacity = standard − budgeted (SM 13.54).

## Worked examples

::::: example Two cup sizes, one kiln down and a Sunday shift
Tapri's kulhad unit fires its cups in a shed of 6 kilns. The monthly budget: 24 working days, each kiln firing 16 hours a day, and fixed overheads (kiln-shed rent, depreciation, supervisors) of ₹6,91,200. Standard kiln time is 1.5 kiln-hours for 1,000 small kulhads and 2.4 kiln-hours for 1,000 large ones.

In November the shed worked 25 days, the last one a Sunday added for wedding orders. One kiln was under repair for 5 of those days; every other kiln fired its full 16 hours each day. Output was 7,00,000 small and 5,20,000 large kulhads, and actual fixed overheads were ₹7,02,000.

**Required:**

(i) Calculate the standard kiln-hours for the actual output.

(ii) Calculate the fixed overhead efficiency, capacity and calendar variances.

(iii) Calculate the fixed overhead volume, expenditure and cost variances, verify them, and say what each part of the volume variance tells Ritu about November.

:::: answer Show the working
**Working notes**

| Particulars | Budget | Actual |
|---|---:|---:|
| Fixed overheads (₹) | 6,91,200 | 7,02,000 |
| Working days | 24 | 25 |
| Kiln-hours | 6 × 16 × 24 = 2,304 | 6 × 16 × 25 − 16 × 5 = 2,320 |

1. Standard rate = ₹6,91,200 ÷ 2,304 = **₹300 per kiln-hour**; rate per day = ₹6,91,200 ÷ 24 = **₹28,800**.
2. Revised budgeted hours = 2,304 ÷ 24 × 25 = **2,400**.

**(i)** Standard hours for actual output = 700 × 1.5 + 520 × 2.4 = 1,050 + 1,248 = **2,298 kiln-hours**. With two products, standard hours (not units) are the common measure of output.

**(ii)**

- Efficiency = SR (SH − AH) = ₹300 × (2,298 − 2,320) = **₹6,600 (A)**.
- Revised capacity = SR (AH − RBH) = ₹300 × (2,320 − 2,400) = **₹24,000 (A)**.
- Calendar = (actual days − budgeted days) × rate per day = (25 − 24) × ₹28,800 = **₹28,800 (F)**.

**(iii)**

- Volume = absorbed − budgeted = (2,298 × ₹300 = ₹6,89,400) − ₹6,91,200 = **₹1,800 (A)**.
- Expenditure = budgeted − actual = ₹6,91,200 − ₹7,02,000 = **₹10,800 (A)**.
- Cost = absorbed − actual = ₹6,89,400 − ₹7,02,000 = **₹12,600 (A)**.

Verification: efficiency + capacity + calendar = ₹6,600 (A) + ₹24,000 (A) + ₹28,800 (F) = ₹1,800 (A), the volume variance; expenditure + volume = ₹10,800 (A) + ₹1,800 (A) = ₹12,600 (A), the cost variance.

:::: example A flexible budget, a boiler repair and the October report
Tapri's premix plant prepares an annual flexible budget of overheads. It plans 1,80,000 cartons a year (its normal capacity), and works 25 days a month:

| Annual activity | 1,50,000 cartons | 1,80,000 cartons |
|---|---:|---:|
| Total overheads (₹) | 33,00,000 | 36,90,000 |

In October a boiler repair shut the plant for 3 days, and it made 13,800 cartons. October's overheads came to ₹3,08,600; of these, rent, salaries, insurance and depreciation (the fixed heads) were ₹1,16,000 and the rest varied with output.

**Required:**

(i) Separate the budgeted overheads into fixed and variable, and find the standard rates.

(ii) Calculate the variable overhead cost variance.

(iii) Calculate the fixed overhead cost, expenditure, volume and calendar variances, and explain the part of the volume variance the calendar does not explain.

(iv) Calculate the total overhead cost variance.

::: answer Show the working
**Working notes**

1. Variable rate = change in overheads ÷ change in activity = (₹36,90,000 − ₹33,00,000) ÷ (1,80,000 − 1,50,000) = **₹13 per carton**. Annual fixed overheads = ₹36,90,000 − 1,80,000 × ₹13 = **₹13,50,000**.
2. Fixed rate = ₹13,50,000 ÷ 1,80,000 = **₹7.50 per carton**. Monthly budget = ₹13,50,000 ÷ 12 = **₹1,12,500**; per day = ₹1,12,500 ÷ 25 = **₹4,500**.
3. Actual fixed = ₹1,16,000; actual variable = ₹3,08,600 − ₹1,16,000 = **₹1,92,600**.
4. Days worked = 25 − 3 = 22. Revised budgeted ("possible") fixed overheads = ₹1,12,500 ÷ 25 × 22 = **₹99,000**.

**(i)** Variable ₹13 a carton; fixed ₹13,50,000 a year, ₹7.50 a carton.

**(ii)** VOH cost variance = 13,800 × ₹13 − ₹1,92,600 = ₹1,79,400 − ₹1,92,600 = **₹13,200 (A)**.

**(iii)**

- Absorbed fixed overheads = 13,800 × ₹7.50 = ₹1,03,500.
- Cost = ₹1,03,500 − ₹1,16,000 = **₹12,500 (A)**.
- Expenditure = ₹1,12,500 − ₹1,16,000 = **₹3,500 (A)**.
- Volume = ₹1,03,500 − ₹1,12,500 = **₹9,000 (A)**.
- Calendar = (22 − 25) × ₹4,500 = **₹13,500 (A)**, which is ₹99,000 − ₹1,12,500.

Check: ₹3,500 (A) + ₹9,000 (A) = ₹12,500 (A). The rest of the volume variance is ₹1,03,500 − ₹99,000 = **₹4,500 (F)**: in 22 days the budget expected 15,000 ÷ 25 × 22 = 13,200 cartons and the plant made 13,800, 600 more, × ₹7.50. The question gives no hours, so this cannot be split into efficiency and capacity.

**(iv)** Total overhead cost variance = ₹13,200 (A) + ₹12,500 (A) = **₹25,700 (A)** = (₹1,79,400 + ₹1,03,500) − ₹3,08,600.

Two traps in this layout: the monthly budget is one-twelfth of the annual fixed amount, and a flexible budget ([[cma.bud.flexible|flexible budgets]]) at two levels is split like a semi-variable cost (change in cost ÷ change in activity) before any rate is computed.

On an audit visit, Bunty has only these figures for the kulhad unit's machine shop for June: actual fixed overheads ₹3,10,500 for 14,400 actual machine hours; fixed overhead expenditure variance ₹10,500 (A); capacity variance ₹12,000 (A); efficiency variance ₹9,000 (A). Standard machine time is 0.75 hour per batch of cups, and no days are given.

**Required:** Calculate:

(i) the budgeted fixed overheads;

(ii) the standard rate per machine hour and the budgeted machine hours;

(iii) the standard hours for actual output, the actual output and the budgeted output;

(iv) the volume and cost variances, and the overhead to be written off to Costing Profit and Loss Account.

::: answer Show the working
**(i)** Expenditure = budgeted − actual, so budgeted = ₹3,10,500 − ₹10,500 = **₹3,00,000**.

**(ii)** Capacity = SR × AH − SR × BH = SR × 14,400 − ₹3,00,000 = −₹12,000, so SR × 14,400 = ₹2,88,000 and **SR = ₹20 per machine hour**. Budgeted hours = ₹3,00,000 ÷ ₹20 = **15,000**.

**(iii)** Efficiency = SR (SH − AH): ₹20 × (SH − 14,400) = −₹9,000, so SH = 14,400 − 450 = **13,950 hours**. Actual output = 13,950 ÷ 0.75 = **18,600 batches**; budgeted output = 15,000 ÷ 0.75 = **20,000 batches**.

**(iv)** Volume = SR (SH − BH) = ₹20 × (13,950 − 15,000) = **₹21,000 (A)**; check: capacity ₹12,000 (A) + efficiency ₹9,000 (A). Cost = absorbed − actual = 13,950 × ₹20 − ₹3,10,500 = ₹2,79,000 − ₹3,10,500 = **₹31,500 (A)**; check: expenditure ₹10,500 (A) + volume ₹21,000 (A). The ₹31,500 is the fixed overhead under-absorbed in June, written off to the Costing Profit and Loss Account.

## Under the hood

### Per unit or per hour: the same volume variance

The volume variance is the same on either rate: (actual output − budgeted output) × rate per unit equals (SH − BH) × rate per hour. Diwali month: (14,100 − 15,000) × ₹40 = (28,200 − 30,000) × ₹20 = ₹36,000 (A). Efficiency and capacity need hours; calendar needs days. The Study Material also defines a **standard output for actual hours** (budgeted output ÷ budgeted hours × actual hours) for the per-unit method (SM 13.42).

### The overhead expenses variance

Paragraph 7.4 opens with two variances (SM 13.38–13.39), before the expenditure and volume analysis: the **production volume variance**, the fixed overheads left unrecovered because output fell short, and the **overhead expenses variance**, the variations in actual spending on both fixed and variable overheads. Illustration 12 (SM 13.46–13.47) and Practical Problem 7 (SM 13.60–13.61, answer 13.75) compute the second as budgeted fixed overheads − (total actual overheads − standard variable overheads for actual output). In effect it compares total actual overheads with the fixed budget plus the standard variable overheads for the actual output, so one figure covers the spending on both.

A plant budgeted for 5,000 units has fixed overheads of ₹2,00,000 (₹40 a unit) and variable overheads of ₹30 a unit. It makes 4,500 units, and its total overheads are ₹3,50,000. Production volume variance = (4,500 − 5,000) × ₹40 = ₹20,000 (A). Overhead expenses variance = ₹2,00,000 − (₹3,50,000 − 4,500 × ₹30) = ₹2,00,000 − ₹2,15,000 = ₹15,000 (A). Together they are the total overhead cost variance: 4,500 × ₹70 − ₹3,50,000 = ₹35,000 (A). Use these two names only when the question asks for them; otherwise split fixed and variable overheads as in the rest of this lesson.

### Budgeted hours after normal down time

Budgeted hours are the hours expected to be worked. 52 workers × 25 days × 8 hours less a normal down time of 15% gives 8,840 budgeted hours, and the fixed and variable rates are computed on 8,840 (MTP September 2025 Series II Q1(c)).

### Where the variance goes

Because a fixed overhead variance is an under- or over-absorption (SM 13.40), its accounting follows [[cma.oh.underover|under- and over-absorption of overheads]]: normally to the Costing Profit and Loss Account. How the variances close the gap between budgeted and actual profit is in [[cma.std.recon|reconciling budgeted and actual profit]]. Variable overheads, which move with output, are in [[cma.std.voh|variable overhead variances]].

::: trick Five amounts, six subtractions
Write SH × SR, AH × SR, RBH × SR, BH × SR and actual in a column. Neighbours give efficiency, capacity, calendar and expenditure; first minus fourth is volume; first minus last is cost. Condition: SR is the standard fixed rate per hour from the budget, and RBH is used only when days differ.
:::

## Answer frame

### "Discuss the various types of fixed overhead variances" (theory)

1. **Why they arise**: the recovery of fixed overheads depends on capacity utilisation; producing less than the projected level leaves part of the budgeted fixed overheads unrecovered (SM 13.38).
1. **Fixed overhead cost variance**: the difference between actual fixed overhead and absorbed fixed overhead; overhead variance is an under- or over-absorption.
1. **Expenditure variance**: actual fixed overhead incurred against budgeted fixed overhead.
1. **Volume variance**: arises due to the volume of production; absorbed against budgeted. It shows the cost of failing to produce at the planned activity, or the benefit of producing above it (SM 13.39).
1. Volume splits into **efficiency** (absorbed against standard fixed overhead), **capacity** (standard fixed overhead against budgeted) and **calendar** (actual against standard working days).
1. When the calendar variance is computed, the capacity variance is revised to (actual hours − revised budgeted hours) × standard rate.
1. Verification: cost = expenditure + volume; volume = efficiency + capacity + calendar.
1. The formula chart, or a tree.

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: the day lost twice
Computing the calendar variance and then the capacity variance on the original budgeted hours. The kiln shed's Sunday sits inside both: ₹4,800 (F) capacity + ₹28,800 (F) calendar + ₹6,600 (A) efficiency = ₹27,000 (F), against a true volume variance of ₹1,800 (A). **Fix:** with a calendar variance, capacity is SR (AH − revised budgeted hours) (SM 13.41).
:::

::: mistake The expenditure variance measured against absorption
"Expenditure = absorbed ₹5,64,000 − actual ₹5,88,000 = ₹24,000 (A)." That is the cost variance. **Fix:** expenditure compares actual with the **budget** (₹6,00,000), because fixed overheads are not expected to move with output; only absorption does.
:::

::: mistake The annual budget in a monthly question
Using the annual ₹13,50,000 as October's fixed budget gives an expenditure variance of ₹12,34,000 (F) instead of ₹3,500 (A). **Fix:** split the budget into fixed and variable first, then convert it to the period of the actuals (÷ 12).
:::

## Practice

::: quiz Check yourself
Q: Tapri's kulhad unit absorbs fixed overheads at ₹15 a tray, on a monthly budget of ₹6,00,000. Its fixed overhead volume variance for March was ₹45,000 (A). How many trays did it make?
= 37000
> Budgeted output = ₹6,00,000 ÷ ₹15 = 40,000 trays. Volume = (actual − budgeted output) × rate, so actual − 40,000 = −₹45,000 ÷ ₹15 = −3,000: 37,000 trays. The ₹45,000 is the fixed overhead the 3,000 missing trays could not carry.

Q: Case scenario. Dev reviews the samosa factory's May report. Budget: 25 days, 800 machine hours a day, fixed overheads ₹4,00,000. Actual: 23 days, 19,000 machine hours, output worth 18,500 standard hours. The report prepared by Ritu's intern shows a calendar variance of ₹32,000 (A). Which capacity variance belongs beside it?
- [ ] ₹20,000 (A)
- [x] ₹12,000 (F)
- [ ] ₹10,000 (A)
- [ ] ₹32,000 (F)
> SR = ₹4,00,000 ÷ 20,000 = ₹20; revised budgeted hours = 800 × 23 = 18,400. Revised capacity = ₹20 × (19,000 − 18,400) = ₹12,000 (F). ₹20,000 (A) uses the original 20,000 hours and counts the two lost days again; ₹10,000 (A) is the efficiency variance. Check: ₹10,000 (A) + ₹12,000 (F) + ₹32,000 (A) = ₹30,000 (A) = 18,500 × ₹20 − ₹4,00,000.

Q: Case scenario. The samosa factory budgets fixed overheads of ₹3,60,000 for 18,000 machine hours. In August it worked 17,000 hours, its output was worth 16,500 standard hours, and it spent ₹3,48,000 on fixed overheads. The report prepared by Ritu's intern says "Fixed overhead expenditure variance ₹18,000 (A)". Which statement is correct?
- [ ] The intern is right: the factory overspent by ₹18,000
- [ ] The expenditure variance is ₹30,000 (A): absorbed less budgeted
- [ ] The expenditure variance is ₹8,000 (A): the budget for 17,000 hours less actual
- [x] The expenditure variance is ₹12,000 (F); the intern's ₹18,000 (A) is the cost variance
> SR = ₹3,60,000 ÷ 18,000 = ₹20. Expenditure = budgeted − actual = ₹3,60,000 − ₹3,48,000 = ₹12,000 (F). The intern compared actual with absorbed (16,500 × ₹20 = ₹3,30,000): that is the cost variance, ₹18,000 (A). ₹30,000 (A) is the volume variance; ₹8,000 (A) compares actual with the standard fixed overhead for actual hours, which no fixed overhead variance does.

Q: Which variance is "the difference between standard fixed overhead and budgeted overhead" in the Study Material?
- [ ] Efficiency variance
- [x] Capacity variance
- [ ] Expenditure variance
- [ ] Volume variance
> Capacity = standard fixed overhead (AH × SR) − budgeted (BH × SR) (SM 13.41). Efficiency compares absorbed with standard; expenditure compares budgeted with actual; volume compares absorbed with budgeted.

Q: Which of these make up the fixed overhead volume variance? Select all that apply.
- [x] Efficiency variance
- [x] Capacity variance
- [x] Calendar variance
- [ ] Expenditure variance
> Volume = efficiency + capacity + calendar (SM 13.42). Expenditure is the other half of the cost variance.

## Recap

- FOH cost variance = absorbed (SH × SR) − actual: an under- or over-absorption.
- Expenditure = budgeted − actual (spending). Volume = absorbed − budgeted (output).
- Volume = efficiency SR (SH − AH) + capacity SR (AH − BH) + calendar (actual days − budgeted days) × rate per day.
- With a calendar variance, capacity = SR (AH − RBH); RBH = BH ÷ budgeted days × actual days.
- Checks: cost = expenditure + volume; volume = efficiency + capacity + calendar.
- Rates: per hour BF ÷ BH, per unit BF ÷ budgeted output, per day BF ÷ budgeted days; monthly budget = annual ÷ 12.
- Five amounts: SH × SR, AH × SR, RBH × SR, BH × SR, actual. (Diwali month: worked every budgeted hour, still ₹24,000 (A).)

## Flashcards

Q:: ICAI's definition: fixed overhead cost variance?
A:: The difference between actual fixed overhead and absorbed fixed overhead: (SH × SR) − actual fixed overheads.

Q:: Formula: fixed overhead expenditure variance?
A:: Budgeted fixed overheads − actual fixed overheads, i.e. (BH × SR) − actual.

Q:: Formula: fixed overhead volume variance?
A:: Absorbed fixed overheads − budgeted fixed overheads, i.e. (SH × SR) − (BH × SR).

Q:: Fixed overhead volume variance is divided into which three variances?
A:: Efficiency variance, capacity variance and calendar variance.

Q:: Formula: fixed overhead efficiency variance?
A:: SR (SH − AH): absorbed fixed overhead − standard fixed overhead.

Q:: Formula: fixed overhead capacity variance, and the revised one?
A:: SR (AH − BH): standard − budgeted fixed overhead. With a calendar variance: SR (AH − revised budgeted hours).

Q:: Formula: fixed overhead calendar variance?
A:: Standard fixed overhead rate per day × (actual working days − budgeted working days).

Q:: Revised budgeted hours?
A:: Budgeted hours ÷ budgeted days × actual days.

Q:: The fixed overhead "budget variance" is another name for which variance?
A:: The fixed overhead expenditure variance: budgeted fixed overheads − actual fixed overheads (Fig 13.1: "Expenditure or Budget Variance").

Q:: Overhead expenses variance (Illustration 12)?
A:: Budgeted fixed overheads − (total actual overheads − standard variable overheads for actual output); with the production volume variance it makes up the total overhead cost variance.

Q:: The two verification formulas for fixed overhead variances?
A:: Cost variance = expenditure + volume; volume = efficiency + capacity + calendar.

Q:: Fixed overheads ₹10,000; output 8,000 units against a budget of 10,000. Production volume variance?
A:: (8,000 − 10,000) × ₹1 = ₹2,000 (A): the cost of failing to produce at the planned activity (SM 13.39).
