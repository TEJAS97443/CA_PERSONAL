## In plain words

### The CEO who loved coupons

When Tapri was one stall, Kesar owned it and ran it: nobody could put Kesar's interests ahead of Kesar's. Since the 2024 listing, Tapri has thousands of shareholders, and none of them pours chai. Its delivery app, TapriNow, is run by a hired chief executive, Arjun, whose bonus is 1% of the app's revenue.

> **Dev:** Arjun's revenue is up 60%. So is his bonus.
> **Kesar:** Wonderful! So the app is making money?
> **Dev:** It is giving away ₹20 crore of free-chai coupons to buy that revenue. And he has asked for a ₹6 crore office with a rooftop café.
> **Kesar:** Whose café is it, his or ours?

That is the **agency problem**. The shareholders (the **principals**) hire managers (their **agents**) to run the firm for them. In theory the managers act in the shareholders' best interest; in reality they may chase their own goals: salary, perks, a bigger bonus. Watching and controlling them costs the shareholders money, the **agency cost**: board reviews, audits of the coupon spend, a new bonus formula. Lenders face the same problem, and protect themselves with **negative covenants**: promises written into the loan, such as "no more borrowing beyond a point".

Two more ideas complete the chapter. The finance manager works inside an environment: accounting feeds the decisions, marketing, production and quantitative methods support them, and stakeholders watch the results. And when cash coming in cannot meet the bills falling due, the firm is in **financial distress**; if that continues until the firm cannot repay its debts, it is **insolvent**.

::: analogy The driver and the fuel card
You hire a driver and give him a fuel card. He may take the long route to clock overtime: that gap between his interest and yours is the agency problem. A GPS tracker you pay for, a deposit he offers, the trips you cannot take because every detour needs your approval, and the time you spend designing a fair bonus are all costs you would not bear if you drove yourself. Together they are the agency cost.
:::

::: key
The agency problem is the chance that managers place personal goals ahead of the owners' goals; the extra cost shareholders bear to monitor and control them is the agency cost. Financial distress is cash inflows too small for current obligations; insolvency, the inability to repay debts, is the result of continuous distress.
:::

- **Accounting** is a necessary input into the financial management function: financial statements help the finance manager gauge past performance and future directions (SM 1.21–1.22).
- **Marketing and production**: new products, promotion plans and changes in the production process need capital outlays and change the projected cash flows, which the finance manager must evaluate and finance (SM 1.23).
- **Quantitative methods** supply the tools for analysing complex financial problems (SM 1.23).
- **Economics** helps the finance manager know what goes on in the world outside the business (SM 1.23).

**Financial management and accounting** are closely related but differ in two ways (SM 1.22–1.23):

::: compare
| Point | Accounting | Financial management |
|---|---|---|
| Treatment of funds | Accrual principle: revenue when sold, expenses when incurred | Cash flows: revenue when cash is received, expenses when paid |
| Why it matters | A firm profitable in the accounting sense may still be unable to meet its current obligations (say, receivables not collected) | Cash flow based returns help the finance manager maintain solvency and avoid insolvency |
| Decision-making | Chief focus: to collect data and present it | Chief focus: financial planning, controlling and decision-making |
| Sequence | Supplies the data | ==Financial management begins where accounting ends== |

**The people around it.** Where outsiders hold shares or lend money, management is under the constant supervision of stakeholders (employees, creditors, customers, government), each judging it by the fulfilment of its own objective ([[fm.intro.objectives|objectives of financial management]], SM 1.16). And the opening of the economy, with liberalisation, deregulation and globalisation, has given finance managers access to global resources and widened their role ([[fm.intro.role|the finance function]], SM 1.18).

### 2. The agency problem

In a sole proprietorship or a partnership the owners participate in management. In a company the owners are not active in management, so there is a **separation between owners (shareholders) and managers**. In theory managers should act in the best interest of shareholders; in reality they may try to maximise their individual goals like salary and perks. So there is a **principal–agent relationship** between owners and managers, known as the agency problem (SM 1.24).

::: definition Agency problem
The chances that managers may place personal goals ahead of the goal of owners. (SM 1.24)
:::

::: definition Agency cost
The additional cost borne by the shareholders to monitor the manager and control their behaviour so as to maximise shareholders' wealth. Agency costs are generally of four types: (i) monitoring, (ii) bonding, (iii) opportunity, (iv) structuring. (SM 1.24)
:::

### 3. Addressing the agency problem

The agency problem arises when managers' interests are not aligned to the interests of the **debt lenders** and the **equity investors** (SM 1.24).

**With lenders.** The agency problem of debt lenders is addressed by imposing **negative covenants**, i.e. the managers cannot borrow beyond a point. The Study Material calls this one of the most important concepts of modern finance, applied in the credit risk management of banks, fund raising and valuing distressed companies (SM 1.24).

**With shareholders.** It can be addressed if the managers' interests are aligned to the shareholders' interests, which is "easier said than done". The efforts made so far (SM 1.25):

1. **Managerial compensation is linked to the profit** of the company to some extent, and also to the **long-term objectives** of the company.
2. **"Employee is also designed** to address the issue with the underlying assumption that maximisation of the stock price is the objective of the investors."
3. **Effective monitoring** can be done.

::: note How the second point is printed
The Study Material (SM 1.25) and every ICAI Suggested Answer that reproduces it print the second point exactly as quoted: the word naming the scheme is missing. Read in context, it describes rewards for employees that are tied to the share price, an employee stock option scheme being the usual example. In an answer, write the point the way ICAI prints it, or as "employee schemes linked to the share price".
:::

### 4. Financial distress and insolvency

A firm must manage, continuously, the price of its product or service, demand, and the prices of inputs such as raw material and labour. It must also manage the proportion of **debt** very delicately: higher debt means higher interest, and if cash inflows are not sufficient, both short-term and long-term creditors put pressure on the firm. If these factors are not well managed, the firm reaches **distress** (SM 1.21).

::: definition Financial distress
A position where cash inflows of a firm are inadequate to meet all its current obligations. (SM 1.21)
:::

::: definition Insolvency
The inability of a firm to repay various debts; it is a result of continuous financial distress. (SM 1.21)
:::

If distress continues for a long period, the firm may have to sell its assets, many times at a lower price. When revenue is inadequate to revive the situation, the firm will not be able to meet its obligations and becomes insolvent (SM 1.21). Debt needs the extra care the Study Material asks for: financial leverage helps equity while the return on investment exceeds the cost of debt, and when the cost of debt is more than the return, the firm can be under financial distress, which is why it is called a "double-edged sword" ([[fm.lev.leverage|leverage]], FM Study Material 6.18).

## Worked examples

:::: example TapriNow's coupon bonus
TapriNow Pvt Ltd, Tapri's delivery-app subsidiary, is run by a hired CEO, Arjun Sethi, whose annual bonus is 1% of the app's gross revenue. This year he spent ₹20 crore on free-chai coupons, which raised revenue by 60% and the app's loss by ₹14 crore, and he has proposed a ₹6 crore office fit-out. Tapri's board has appointed an outside firm to review all marketing spend, now requires board approval for any spend above ₹1 crore, and is redesigning Arjun's pay. TapriNow's bank loan agreement prohibits further borrowing while the debt-equity ratio exceeds 1:1.

(i) Explain the agency problem in this case and the agency cost it creates.

(ii) Advise how the agency problem between Tapri and Arjun, and with the bank, can be addressed.

(2 + 2 = 4 marks)

::: answer Model answer
**(i) Agency problem and agency cost.** In a company, owners are not active in management, so there is a separation between owners and managers. In theory managers should act in the best interest of shareholders; in reality they may try to maximise their individual goals like salary and perks, so there is a principal–agent relationship, known as the agency problem: the chances that managers place personal goals ahead of the goal of owners.

In the given case, Arjun's bonus depends on gross revenue, not on shareholders' wealth. The coupons raised revenue, and his bonus, while increasing the app's loss by ₹14 crore; the office fit-out serves his perks. His goals and the shareholders' goal have diverged.

The agency problem leads to agency cost: the additional cost borne by the shareholders to monitor the manager and control his behaviour so as to maximise shareholders' wealth. Here it includes the outside review of marketing spend, the delay and board time that the ₹1 crore approval limit imposes, and the cost of redesigning Arjun's pay. ICAI classifies agency costs into four types: monitoring, bonding, opportunity and structuring.

**(ii) Addressing the problem.** The agency problem between managers and shareholders can be addressed if the managers' interests are aligned to the shareholders' interests:

1. **Compensation**: link Arjun's pay to the profit of the company to some extent and to its long-term objectives, not to gross revenue.
2. **Employee schemes linked to the share price**: designed on the assumption that maximising the stock price is the investors' objective. TapriNow is unlisted, so such a scheme for Arjun would use the shares of its listed parent, Tapri.
3. **Effective monitoring**: the outside review and the approval limit already in place.

The bank's agency problem is addressed by **negative covenants**: managers cannot borrow beyond a point. Its condition that no further borrowing is allowed while the debt-equity ratio exceeds 1:1 is such a covenant, and protects the bank against TapriNow's managers adding risk at the lender's expense.
:::

Gokul Doodh Pvt Ltd is a dairy owned by Kesar's cousin, financed largely by a bank term loan and an overdraft. Its cash position for five quarters (₹ crore):

In Q4 the bank refused to renew the overdraft. In Q5 the dairy missed two loan instalments, and with demand still falling it sees no way to raise its revenue. (ii) State, with reasons, when it became insolvent.

:::: example Profitable and still short of cash
Tapri's institutional catering division began 2025-26 with no cash of its own. For the year it reports sales of ₹48 crore and expenses of ₹43 crore: a profit of ₹5 crore. All the expenses have been paid in cash, any gap being met by advances from head office. ₹9 crore of the sales were made to a railway caterer that has not paid and is disputing the bills; the rest has been collected. In the first week of April, ₹3 crore falls due to the division's tea suppliers. The division head says: "We made ₹5 crore, so we can pay the ₹3 crore without help from head office."

Comment on the division head's statement, distinguishing between the accounting and the financial management view of funds. (4 marks)

::: answer Model answer
**Relevant concept.** Financial management and accounting differ in the treatment of funds. In accounting, funds are measured on the accrual principle: revenue is recognised at the point of sale and not when collected, and expenses when incurred rather than when paid. Accrual-based data do not fully reflect the financial condition of an organisation: one that is profitable in the accounting sense may be unable to meet its current obligations because of a shortage of liquidity, for example from uncollectible receivables. In financial management the treatment of funds is based on cash flows: revenue is recognised only when cash is received and expenses when paid, because the finance manager is concerned with maintaining solvency.

**Facts of the case.** Accounting profit = ₹48 crore − ₹43 crore = ₹5 crore. Cash received = ₹48 crore − ₹9 crore uncollected = ₹39 crore; cash paid = ₹43 crore.

**Analysis.** On a cash basis the division has a shortfall of ₹39 crore − ₹43 crore = **₹4 crore**, already met by head office's advances, so it has no cash of its own to pay the ₹3 crore due to suppliers. The ₹5 crore profit includes ₹9 crore of receivables that may never be collected.

**Conclusion.** The statement is not correct. It reads the accounting profit as if it were cash. The financial manager, whose focus is planning, controlling and decision-making on the basis of cash flows, would arrange funds for the ₹3 crore payment and pursue the ₹9 crore receivable; financial management begins where accounting ends.
:::

## Under the hood

### What the four agency costs usually mean

The Study Material lists the four types and stops (SM 1.24). For understanding, the meanings usually given in finance texts are below; they are not in the Study Material, so in an answer name the four types as ICAI does and keep explanations short.

| Type | Usual meaning | In the TapriNow case |
|---|---|---|
| Monitoring | Cost of watching managers: audits, reviews, reporting, board oversight | The outside review of marketing spend |
| Bonding | Cost of assuring owners that managers will act in their interest, such as contractual limits managers accept | A clause Arjun might accept capping perks |
| Opportunity | Value lost because restrictions on managers slow or block good decisions | Projects delayed by the ₹1 crore approval limit |
| Structuring | Cost of structuring managers' compensation to align it with owners' goals | Designing Arjun's new pay |

### Distress in other chapters

The same ideas return later in the paper:

- **Capital structure, trade-off theory** (FM Study Material 5.27–5.28): the benefit of debt (its tax benefit) is traded off against the costs of financial distress, including bankruptcy costs and non-bankruptcy costs (staff leaving, suppliers demanding disadvantageous payment terms, bondholder/stockholder infighting), and against agency costs from two conflicts, shareholders–managers and shareholders–debt holders. The direct cost of distress is the cost of insolvency: assets sold at a distress price, much below their current value, plus administrative and legal costs. See [[fm.cs.theories|capital structure theories]].
- **Liquidity ratios** (FM Study Material 3.5): continuous default on short-term liabilities leads to commercial bankruptcy, and eventually to sickness and dissolution. See [[fm.ratio.liquidity|liquidity ratios]].

### The two sides of one sentence

"Financial distress is a position where cash inflows of a firm are inadequate to meet all its current obligations" is a statement about **cash** and **current obligations**. ICAI's May 2024 true-or-false question turned the sentence round ("when accounting profits of a firm are sufficient to meet its long-term obligations"): profit instead of cash, long-term instead of current obligations, so the statement is false.

## Answer frame

### "Explain the agency problem and agency cost; how can it be addressed?" (4 marks)

1. **Separation**: owners participate in management in a proprietorship or partnership; in a company they do not, so owners and managers are separate.
2. **Principal–agent relationship**: managers may maximise salary and perks; agency problem = the chances that managers place personal goals ahead of the owners' goal.
3. **Agency cost**: the additional cost borne by shareholders to monitor and control managers; four types: monitoring, bonding, opportunity, structuring.
4. **Addressing it**: debt lenders, negative covenants (no borrowing beyond a point); shareholders, align interests: compensation linked to profit and long-term objectives; employee schemes linked to the share price; effective monitoring.

### "Write a short note on financial distress and insolvency"

Factors to manage (price, demand, input prices, the proportion of debt; creditors' pressure) → definition of distress (cash inflows inadequate to meet all current obligations) → if continued, assets sold, often at a lower price → revenue inadequate to revive → definition of insolvency (inability to repay various debts, a result of continuous distress).

### "Differentiate financial management and financial accounting"

Two heads, as in the comparison above: **treatment of funds** (accrual against cash flow, with the uncollectible-receivables example and the solvency reason) and **decision-making** (collecting and presenting data against planning, controlling and decision-making), ending with "financial management begins where accounting ends".

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: "profitable, so not in distress"
Gokul Doodh showed profits in Q2 and Q3 and was in distress in both. **Fix:** distress is about cash inflows against current obligations; accounting profit is on the accrual basis and can coexist with a cash shortfall.
:::

::: mistake Using "distress" and "insolvency" as synonyms
**Fix:** distress is a cash shortfall against current obligations; insolvency is the inability to repay debts, the result of distress that continues and that revenue cannot reverse. A firm can come out of distress; insolvency is where distress ends if it does not.
:::

::: mistake Saying the agency cost is borne by the managers
**Fix:** ICAI's definition: the additional cost borne by the **shareholders** to monitor and control the managers (SM 1.24).
:::

::: mistake Forgetting the lender
Answers that discuss only shareholders and managers lose the point on debt. **Fix:** the agency problem arises when managers' interests are not aligned to those of the debt lenders as well as the equity investors; lenders use negative covenants.
:::

## Recap

- Agency problem: the chances that managers place personal goals ahead of the owners' goal; it arises from the separation of ownership and management in companies.
- Agency cost: the additional cost borne by shareholders to monitor and control managers; four types: monitoring, bonding, opportunity, structuring.
- Lenders' agency problem: negative covenants (managers cannot borrow beyond a point).
- Shareholders: link pay to profit and long-term objectives; employee schemes tied to the share price; effective monitoring.
- Financial distress: cash inflows inadequate to meet all current obligations; prolonged, it forces asset sales, often at lower prices.
- Insolvency: inability to repay various debts, the result of continuous distress when revenue cannot revive the situation.

## Flashcards

Q:: Define the agency problem (ICAI's "in a nutshell" line).
A:: The chances that managers may place personal goals ahead of the goal of owners.

Q:: Why does the agency problem arise in companies but not in a sole proprietorship?
A:: In a proprietorship or partnership the owners participate in management; in a company the owners are not active in management, so owners and managers are separate (a principal–agent relationship).

Q:: Define agency cost.
A:: The additional cost borne by the shareholders to monitor the manager and control their behaviour so as to maximise shareholders' wealth.

Q:: What are the four types of agency cost, in ICAI's order?
A:: Monitoring, bonding, opportunity, structuring.

Q:: How is the agency problem of debt lenders addressed?
A:: By imposing negative covenants, i.e. the managers cannot borrow beyond a point.

Q:: What three efforts does the Study Material list for addressing the agency problem between managers and shareholders?
A:: Managerial compensation linked to profit and to long-term objectives; employee schemes based on the assumption that maximising the stock price is the investors' objective; effective monitoring.

Q:: Define financial distress.
A:: A position where cash inflows of a firm are inadequate to meet all its current obligations.

Q:: Define insolvency, and state how it relates to distress.
A:: The inability of a firm to repay various debts; it is a result of continuous financial distress.

Q:: What happens if financial distress continues for a long period?
A:: The firm may have to sell its assets, many times at a lower price; when revenue is inadequate to revive the situation, it cannot meet its obligations and becomes insolvent.

Q:: Financial management versus accounting: how do they differ in the treatment of funds?
A:: Accounting measures funds on the accrual principle (revenue at sale, expenses when incurred); financial management on cash flows (revenue when received, expenses when paid), to maintain solvency.

Q:: Which discipline is primary for the financial manager, and which are supportive?
A:: Accounting is primary; marketing, production and quantitative methods are supportive; economics helps the manager know the world outside the business.
