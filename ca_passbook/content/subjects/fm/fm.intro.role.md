## In plain words

### One tapri, three questions

Twenty years ago Tapri Tea Co. was one roadside stall in Indore. Before the first cup was sold, Kesar had to answer four questions: which things to buy (a stove, a cart, kettles), how much they would cost in all, how much cash he would need every day for milk, tea leaves and his helper's wages, and where all that money would come from (his savings, and a loan from his uncle at 2% a month).

> **Kesar:** Back then I did all of it myself, on the back of a matchbox.
> **Dev:** And today the same questions need a CFO, a treasurer, a controller and ten managers.
> **Kesar:** Same questions? Then why are you so expensive?
> **Dev:** Because the matchbox had one stall. We have 40 cities.

Those questions, and one more that arrived with the first profit (how much of it to take home), are the whole of **financial management**: getting money (the **financing decision**), putting it to work in assets (the **investment decision**), and deciding how much of the profit to hand back to the owners (the **dividend decision**). Running the daily cash, stock and credit is **working capital management**. Together these are the **finance functions**, and the value of the firm depends on how well all of them are taken.

Money comes from different sources, each with its own **risk, cost and control**: owners' equity is safe for the firm but expensive; debentures are cheaper but must be repaid with interest come what may. And money raised is only useful if it earns more than it costs, so the finance manager also watches how it is used: no idle cash, no excess stock, no machine that cannot pay its way.

The person in charge has grown too. Once the finance department was called in only for big events (a merger, an expansion). Today the **chief financial officer (CFO)** is a strategic partner of the chief executive, and under the CFO sit a **treasurer** (who raises, invests and guards the money) and a **controller** (who accounts for it, costs it, taxes it and audits it).

::: analogy The town's water engineer
A town's water engineer brings water in from the river, the borewell and the tanker, each with its own price and reliability (financing); pipes it to the fields and factories that will make the best use of it (investment); and decides how much to release to households now and how much to keep in the dam for next summer (dividend versus retention). A leaking pipe is idle money: water paid for that waters nothing.
:::

::: key
Financial management is the efficient acquisition (financing) and allocation (investment) of funds, with the dividend decision on top: three inter-related long-term decisions plus working capital management. The finance executive who takes them has grown from a fund-raiser for big events into the CEO's strategic partner.
:::

While deciding how much to take from each source, the entrepreneur keeps in mind the **cost of capital** of each source (interest, dividend) and tries to keep it low. So financial management is concerned with ==efficient acquisition (financing) and allocation (investment in assets, working capital etc.) of funds== with an objective to make profit (dividend) for the owners. Any business requiring money asks three key questions (SM 1.3):

1. Where to get the money from? (**Financing decision**)
2. Where to invest the money? (**Investment decision**)
3. How much to distribute amongst shareholders to keep them satisfied? (**Dividend decision**)

### 2. What financial management means

::: definition Financial management
That managerial activity which is concerned with planning and controlling of the firm's financial resources; in other words, acquiring, financing and managing assets to accomplish the overall goal of a business enterprise (mainly to maximise the shareholder's wealth). (SM 1.3)
:::

Where positive cash flow matters more than book profit, it can also be defined as planning for the future of a business enterprise to ensure a positive cash flow; some experts call it the science of money management. Two formal definitions (SM 1.3–1.4):

- "Financial Management comprises of forecasting, planning, organizing, directing, co-ordinating and controlling of all activities relating to acquisition and application of the financial resources of an undertaking in keeping with its financial objective."
- **Phillippatus**: "Financial Management is concerned with the managerial decisions that result in the acquisition and financing of short term and long term credits for the firm." It deals with the selection of specific assets (or combinations of assets) and the size and growth of an enterprise, and the analysis is based on the expected inflows and outflows of funds and their effect on managerial objectives.

### 3. The two aspects: getting funds and using them

There are two basic aspects of financial management: **procurement of funds** and the **effective utilisation** of these funds to achieve business objectives (SM 1.4).

**Procurement.** Because funds can be obtained from many sources, procurement is always a complex problem (SM 1.4). New and creative sources keep appearing (trading in **carbon credits** is one, SM 1.5). Funds from different sources have different characteristics in terms of **risk, cost and control**: the cost of funds should be at the minimum, and for that a proper balancing of risk and control must be carried out; the finance manager must also strike a balance between equity and debt so that the funding structure suits the business (SM 1.5).

| Source | From the firm's point of view (SM 1.5–1.6) |
|---|---|
| **Equity** | Best from the risk point of view: no repayment except in liquidation. Usually the most expensive: shareholders expect more than the prevalent interest rate, and dividends are an appropriation of profit, not an expense under the Income Tax Act. A public issue may dilute existing control. |
| **Debentures** | Comparatively cheaper than shares because of their tax advantage: interest is allowed for tax, a dividend comes out of taxed profit. But interest must be paid even in hard times, whether or not there are profits, and the debentures must be repaid as agreed: a high degree of risk. |
| **Banks** | Meet long-term and short-term needs. Fund-based: cash credit, overdraft, term loans, working capital term loan, bill purchase/discounting. Non-fund-based: guarantees, letters of credit. |
| **International funding** | FDI and FII are the two major routes, besides ADRs and GDRs; procurement must suit the requirements of foreign investors. |
| **Angel financing** | Equity from a wealthy individual for a start-up or expansion; sometimes the last option for a start-up that does not qualify for bank funding and is too small for venture capital. |

The sources are taught in [[fm.sources.long|long-term sources]], [[fm.sources.short|short-term sources]] and [[fm.sources.modern|contemporary and international sources]].

**Utilisation.** The finance manager must point out where funds are idle or not properly used. All funds are procured at a cost and with some risk; if they do not generate an income higher than the cost of procuring them, there is no point in running the business (SM 1.7). Funds go to:

- **Fixed assets**: invested so that the company can produce at its optimum level without endangering its financial solvency. This needs sound knowledge of **capital budgeting** (investment appraisal), the planning process used to decide whether long-term investments such as new or replacement machinery, new plants, new products and research projects would provide the desired return (SM 1.7; [[fm.capbud.cashflows|capital budgeting]]).
- **Working capital**: enough of it, but not too much blocked in inventories, book debts and cash (SM 1.7; [[fm.wc.basics|working capital]]).

### 4. The finance functions: V = f(I, F, D)

The value of a firm depends on its finance functions or decisions (SM 1.8):

$$V = f(I, F, D)$$

 {"label": "Short-term", "children": [{"label": "Working\ncapital", "color": "g"}]}]},
 "caption": "The three long-term decisions decide V = f(I, F, D); short-term decisions reduce to working capital management (current assets and current liabilities)."}
:::

**(a) Investment decisions (I)** relate to the **selection of assets** in which funds will be invested. Long-term funds go into fixed assets and also current assets, after careful assessment of the projects through capital budgeting; a part of long-term funds is kept for working capital. Asset management policies are laid down for current assets: the **inventory policy** is decided by the production manager and the finance manager in view of production needs, future raw material prices and the availability of funds (SM 1.8).

**(b) Financing decisions (F)** relate to **acquiring the optimum finance** to meet financial objectives and seeing that fixed and working capital are effectively managed. The finance manager needs (SM 1.8–1.9):

- a good knowledge of the sources of funds and their costs, and a **sound capital structure**: a proper balance between equity capital and debt;
- a clear understanding of the difference between **profit and cash flow**: profit is of little avail unless the organisation has cash to pay for assets and sustain the working capital cycle;
- the evaluation of **risk**: excessive debt carries high risk for equity because of the priority rights of lenders;
- in overseas trading, protection against currency fluctuations by **hedging**, a strategy designed to minimise, reduce or cancel out the risk in another investment (as a shopkeeper hedges the risk of fire with a fire insurance contract).

**(c) Dividend decisions (D)** relate to **how much and how frequently cash can be paid out** of the profits as income for the owners. An owner is rewarded in two ways, the growth of the capital invested and the cash paid out (drawings for a sole trader, dividends for a company). The dividend decision has two elements: the amount paid out and the amount retained to support growth, ==the latter being also a financing decision==. The level and regular growth of dividends is a significant factor in a company's market value (SM 1.9; [[fm.div.basics|dividend policy]]).

All three are inter-related. The first two arise in any organisation; the dividend decision only in profit-making ones (SM 1.9).

**Short-term finance decisions** are generally reduced to the management of current assets and current liabilities: **working capital management (WCM)** (SM 1.10).

### 5. Why the three decisions must be taken together

ICAI's Suggested Answers explain the inter-relationship this way (MTP Series II for May 2024 and for September 2026, Q4(a)): the underlying objective of all three is the same, the **maximisation of shareholders' wealth**, so their joint impact on the market price of the share must be considered and they should be solved jointly.

- **Investment**: long-term funds are invested after careful assessment through capital budgeting and uncertainty analysis, and only a proposal expected to yield at least enough to meet **its cost of financing** is accepted.
- **Financing**: a proper balance between long-term and short-term funds, and within long-term funds a proper mix of loan funds and owners' funds; the optimum mix increases the return to equity shareholders.
- **Dividend**: what part of profit to pay out and what to retain; an optimal dividend pay-out ratio maximises shareholders' wealth.

### 6. Importance and scope

The importance of financial management cannot be over-emphasised: it is the key to successful business operations, and without proper administration of finance no business enterprise can reach its full potential for growth and success. "Money is to an enterprise, what oil is to an engine." Financial management is all about planning investment, funding the investment, monitoring expenses against budget and managing gains from the investments; it means the management of all matters related to an organisation's finances (SM 1.10). The best way to show its importance is to list the tasks good financial management involves (SM 1.10):

- Taking care not to over-invest in fixed assets
- Balancing cash outflows with cash inflows
- Ensuring a sufficient level of short-term working capital
- Setting sales revenue targets that will deliver growth
- Increasing gross profit by setting the correct pricing for products or services
- Controlling general and administrative expenses by finding more cost-efficient ways of running day-to-day operations
- Tax planning that will minimise the taxes the business has to pay

**Scope.** Based on **Ezra Solomon's** concept, financial management studies (SM 1.11):

1. Determination of the size of the enterprise and its rate of growth.
2. Determining the composition of its assets.
3. Determining the mix of its financing, i.e. the level of debt to equity.
4. Analysis, planning and control of its financial affairs.

### 7. Three phases of evolution

Financial management evolved as a separate field of study at the beginning of the century and gradually over the past 50 years, in three phases (SM 1.7–1.8):

::: diagram Evolution of financial management
{"type": "layers", "layers": [
 {"label": "Traditional phase", "note": "only occasional events, e.g. mergers, liquidation"},
 {"label": "Transitional phase", "note": "day-to-day funds analysis, planning, control"},
 {"label": "Modern phase", "note": "still going on: analysis and new theories"}],
 "caption": "Top to bottom: the three phases in order (SM 1.7–1.8)."}
:::

- **Traditional phase**: financial management was considered necessary only during occasional events such as takeovers, mergers, expansion and liquidation, and financial decisions kept in mind the needs of outsiders (investment bankers, lenders and the like).
- **Transitional phase**: the day-to-day problems financial managers faced were given importance: funds analysis, planning and control.
- **Modern phase**: still going on. The scope has greatly increased; financial analysis helps decision making, and many theories have been developed on efficient markets, capital budgeting, option pricing, valuation models and other fields.

### 8. The role of the financial controller

The role of the **financial controller** has changed over the years. Until the middle of the century (in the Study Material's words), its scope was limited to **procurement of funds** under major events in the life of the enterprise such as promotion, expansion or merger. In modern times it includes, besides procurement of funds, the three decisions: investment, financing and dividend (SM 1.11).

In a bid to maximise shareholders' wealth, the financial controller should ==strive to maximise returns in relation to the given risk==, and seek courses of action that avoid unnecessary risks. To ensure maximum return, funds flowing in and out of the firm should be constantly monitored to ensure that they are safeguarded and properly utilised (SM 1.11).

### 9. The finance executive and the CFO

With liberalisation, deregulation and globalisation, the opportunities before finance managers have virtually no limits; the finance executive goes by different names in different organisations, but the role and functions are similar (SM 1.18). ICAI quotes Jeff Thomson, IMA President and CEO: today's CFO team is expected to add value well beyond the traditional roles of cost management, controls and acting as the conscience of the organisation, by serving as the **integration hub** for key business processes, a **catalyst for change** including business transformation, and a **consultant or trusted business advisor** in creating sustainable growth (SM 1.19).

The finance executive's responsibilities include (SM 1.19):

1. **Financial analysis and planning**: determining the proper amount of funds to employ in the firm, i.e. designating the size of the firm and its rate of growth.
2. **Investment decisions**: the efficient allocation of funds to specific assets.
3. **Financing and capital structure decisions**: raising funds on terms as favourable as possible, i.e. determining the composition of liabilities.
4. **Management of financial resources** (such as working capital).
5. **Risk management**: protecting assets.

## Worked examples

:::: example One export plant, three decisions
Tapri's board is considering a ₹75 crore plant at Indore to make frozen samosas for export. Dev expects it to earn 16% a year; Tapri's cost of capital is 12%. He proposes to fund it with ₹25 crore of this year's profit, cutting this year's dividend from ₹32 crore to ₹7 crore, and ₹50 crore by either 9% non-convertible debentures or a rights issue of equity shares. A director asks why the board cannot simply approve the plant today and "sort out the money and the dividend later".

Explain to the director, with reference to this proposal, how the investment, financing and dividend decisions are inter-related. (4 marks)

::: answer Model answer
**Inter-relationship.** The finance functions are divided into three major decisions: investment, financing and dividend. They are inter-related because the underlying objective of all three is the same, the maximisation of shareholders' wealth. Since they are inter-related, their joint impact on the market price of the company's shares must be considered, and they should be solved jointly.

**Investment decision.** The plant is a long-term investment to be assessed through capital budgeting. It should be accepted only if it is expected to yield at least a return adequate to meet its cost of financing. The expected 16% exceeds the 12% cost of capital, but the cost depends on how the ₹75 crore is raised, so the investment cannot be approved without the financing decision.

**Financing decision.** The finance manager has to ensure a proper mix of loan funds and owners' funds. Raising ₹50 crore through 9% debentures is cheaper because interest is tax-deductible, but it adds fixed interest and repayment risk; a rights issue avoids that risk but is costlier. The optimum mix increases the return to equity shareholders and maximises their wealth.

**Dividend decision.** Retaining ₹25 crore of profit is internal financing, so it is also a financing decision: it deprives shareholders of ₹25 crore of dividend now in exchange for growth. The level and regular growth of dividends affect the market price of the share, so the pay-out must be set together with the plant and its financing.

**Conclusion.** The three decisions are inter-related and must be taken jointly, judging each by its effect on shareholders' wealth. The board should approve the plant, its financing mix and the dividend together.
:::

## Under the hood

### Two "controllers" in one chapter

The Study Material uses the word in two senses. "Role of Financial Controller" (SM 1.11) means the head of finance, the person who takes the investment, financing and dividend decisions. In the organisation chart (SM 1.19), "Controller" is one of the two arms under the CFO, beside the treasurer, in charge of accounting, costing, tax, data processing and internal audit. Answer a "role of the financial controller" question with SM 1.11 (procurement then, the three decisions now, maximising return for the given risk), not with the chart.

### Emerging issues for the CFO (an RTP extra)

ICAI's RTP for January 2025 (Q11(a)) asked for "the emerging issues (any four) affecting the future role of CFO". Its answer lists nine priorities: **regulation** (a growing personal stake in compliance); **globalisation** (a finance function that works on the global stage and embraces diversity); **technology** ("big data" and analytics); **risk** (more effective risk management, an appropriate corporate ethos); **transformation** (a better service to the business at zero cost impact); **stakeholder management** (the CFO as the face of the corporate brand); **strategy** (validation and execution); **reporting** (broader and burdensome requirements); **talent and capability** in the top finance role. This list is not in the Study Material edition that applies for January 2027 (the applicable RTP is the one for January 2027, not this one), so it is not core; read it once, because ICAI has asked it.

### Procurement: the considerations in one line

Theory question 4 at the end of the chapter asks for "three main considerations in procuring funds"; ICAI's answer refers to paragraph 2.1 (SM 1.29). Paragraph 2.1 names three characteristics in which every source differs, **risk, cost and control** (SM 1.5), and adds the balance between equity and debt: those are the points to write.

::: trick Treasurer holds the money, controller holds the records
**Treasurer**: three Cs and two money jobs: **C**ash, **C**redit, **C**apital budgeting; **F**und raising, **P**ortfolio. **Controller**: everything that is recorded or checked: financial accounting, cost accounting, tax, data processing, internal audit. Condition: this is ICAI's chart of how the finance function *may* be organised in a large organisation (SM 1.19); a real company may organise it differently.
:::

## Answer frame

### "Discuss the role (responsibilities) of the finance executive / CFO"

Open with one sentence: the finance executive plays an important role in the company's goals, policies and financial success. Then the five responsibilities, each with its ICAI gloss: financial analysis and planning (size and rate of growth); investment decisions (efficient allocation of funds to specific assets); financing and capital structure decisions (composition of liabilities); management of financial resources (working capital); risk management (protecting assets). If the question says "in today's world", add the line on the strategic business partner of the CEO and the "now does" list.

### "Explain the inter-relationship between investment, financing and dividend decisions"

1. The three decisions; inter-related because their objective is the same, maximisation of shareholders' wealth; consider their joint impact on the share price and solve them jointly.
2. Investment: capital budgeting; accept only if the return at least meets the cost of financing.
3. Financing: balance of long-term and short-term funds; mix of loan and owners' funds; the optimum mix maximises return to equity.
4. Dividend: payout against retention; retained earnings used for internal financing deprive shareholders of dividends; optimal payout maximises wealth.
5. Conclusion: take them jointly, keeping in view their joint effect on shareholders' wealth.

### "Distinguish the treasurer's and the controller's functions"

| Treasurer | Controller |

| Cash manager | Financial accounting manager |
| Credit manager | Cost accounting manager |
| Capital budgeting manager | Tax manager |
| Fund raising manager | Data processing manager |
| Portfolio manager | Internal auditor |

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: the retained profit that was "only a dividend decision"
Bunty classifies "retain 70% of profit to fund new cafés" as a dividend decision and stops. **Fix:** the amount retained to support growth is also a financing decision (SM 1.9). Say both.
:::

::: mistake Answering "role of the financial controller" with the organisation chart
**Fix:** SM 1.11's financial controller is the head of finance: once limited to procuring funds for major events, now taking the investment, financing and dividend decisions, maximising returns in relation to the given risk and monitoring the flow of funds. The chart's "Controller" is a different post.
:::

::: mistake Calling equity "the cheapest source because there is no interest"
**Fix:** equity is the best source from the **risk** point of view (no repayment except in liquidation) but usually the most **expensive**: shareholders expect more than the interest rate, and dividends are not tax-deductible (SM 1.5). Debentures are cheaper but riskier.
:::

::: mistake Listing the CFO's old jobs as the "role today"
Accounting, tax filing and tracking payables and receivables are in the "used to do" column. **Fix:** the "now does" list adds managing M&As, profitability and pricing analysis, outsourcing decisions, overseeing IT and HR, strategic planning, regulatory compliance and risk management, on top of budgeting and forecasting.
:::

## Recap

- Financial management is the efficient acquisition (financing) and allocation (investment) of funds, with the objective of making profit (dividend) for owners; mainly to maximise shareholders' wealth.
- Two aspects: procurement of funds (each source differs in risk, cost and control) and effective utilisation (fixed assets via capital budgeting, and working capital without too much blocked in it).
- V = f(I, F, D): investment (selection of assets), financing (optimum finance, sound capital structure, profit versus cash, risk, hedging) and dividend (payout and retention, the retention being also a financing decision); short-term: working capital management.
- The three decisions are inter-related through one objective, shareholders' wealth, and must be taken jointly.
- Scope (Ezra Solomon): size and growth; composition of assets; mix of financing; analysis, planning and control.
- Evolution: traditional (occasional events), transitional (day-to-day problems), modern (analysis and theories).
- Financial controller: once procurement for major events, now the three decisions; maximise returns for the given risk. Finance executive: planning, investment, financing and capital structure, management of financial resources, risk management.
- Under the CFO: treasurer (cash, credit, capital budgeting, fund raising, portfolio) and controller (financial accounting, cost accounting, tax, data processing, internal audit); today's CFO is the CEO's strategic partner. The matchbox has been retired.

## Flashcards

Q:: What are the four stages of decision making for a new venture in the Study Material?
A:: (1) Decide which assets to buy; (2) determine the total investment needed for them; (3) determine the cash needed to run daily operations (working capital requirement); (4) decide the sources to finance the total investment.

Q:: Define financial management (ICAI's first meaning).
A:: That managerial activity which is concerned with planning and controlling of the firm's financial resources: acquiring, financing and managing assets to accomplish the overall goal of a business enterprise (mainly to maximise shareholders' wealth).

Q:: What are the two basic aspects of financial management?
A:: Procurement of funds, and effective utilisation of those funds to achieve business objectives.

Q:: In which three characteristics do funds from different sources differ?
A:: Risk, cost and control.

Q:: Why is equity the best source from the risk point of view but usually the most expensive?
A:: No repayment except in liquidation; but shareholders expect more than the prevalent interest rate, dividends are an appropriation of profit not allowed as an expense under the Income Tax Act, and a new issue may dilute control.

Q:: State the expression for the value of a firm in terms of finance decisions, and name the decisions.
A:: V = f(I, F, D): investment, financing and dividend decisions (long term); short term, working capital management.

Q:: Which part of the dividend decision is also a financing decision?
A:: The amount retained to support the growth of the organisation.

Q:: Ezra Solomon's four aspects of the scope of financial management?
A:: Size and rate of growth of the enterprise; composition of its assets; mix of its financing (debt to equity); analysis, planning and control of its financial affairs.

Q:: What are the three phases in the evolution of financial management?
A:: Traditional (needed only for occasional events such as mergers and liquidation), transitional (day-to-day problems of funds analysis, planning and control), modern (still going on; financial analysis and theories).

Q:: The five responsibilities of the finance executive?
A:: Financial analysis and planning; investment decisions; financing and capital structure decisions; management of financial resources (such as working capital); risk management.

Q:: Which managers report to the treasurer, and which to the controller, in ICAI's organisation chart?
A:: Treasurer: cash, credit, capital budgeting, fund raising and portfolio managers. Controller: financial accounting, cost accounting, tax and data processing managers, and the internal auditor.

Q:: How has the role of the financial controller changed?
A:: Once limited to procurement of funds for major events (promotion, expansion, merger); now also the investment, financing and dividend decisions, maximising returns in relation to the given risk.
