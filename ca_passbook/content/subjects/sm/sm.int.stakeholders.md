## In plain words

### Forty pages for everyone

Tapri Tea Co. Ltd is building a second samosa factory near Bhopal. Kesar wants the plan sent to everybody.

> **Kesar:** Ananya, print the Bhopal plan. Forty pages each: the board, the bank, the workers, the milk co-op, the pollution board, that business magazine. Sab ko khush rakhenge.
> **Ananya:** Forty pages to a magazine that writes about us once a year, and the same forty to the board that must approve the money?
> **Kesar:** Equal treatment, no?
> **Ananya:** Equal respect, Kesar-ji. Not equal effort. Two questions first.

Everyone who has a stake in Tapri's success, and can affect it, is a **stakeholder**: the people inside (the board, managers, employees, the shareholders who own it) and outside (the bank, suppliers, customers, regulators, the town around the factory). They do not all matter equally, and they do not all want the same thing.

Ananya's two questions are: **how much power** does this stakeholder have over Tapri's strategy or resources, and **how interested** is it in Tapri succeeding? Mendelow's matrix crosses the two answers into four boxes. High power and high interest: **key players**, managed closely and involved in decisions (the board). High power, low interest: **keep satisfied**, with enough regular information that they never become a hurdle (the bank, the pollution board). Low power, high interest: **keep informed**, so that no major issue arises and their feedback is used (the factory workers, the milk co-operative). Low power, low interest: **low priority**, monitored with minimum effort (the magazine).

And the boxes are not permanent. If the factory breaks a pollution rule, the pollution board stops being a quiet giant and becomes a very interested one.

::: analogy The wedding guest list
A big Indian wedding has its own Mendelow grid. The two families are key players: every decision goes past them. Phupha-ji, who does not care about the menu but can spoil the whole evening if he feels ignored, is "keep satisfied": greet him first, seat him well. The cousins' group chat wants every detail and decides nothing: keep them informed. The neighbour three buildings away is low priority, until the band plays past midnight and he calls the police. Now he has power and interest, and the grid has to be redrawn.
:::

::: key
Mendelow's matrix sorts stakeholders by **power** (ability to influence the organisation's strategy or resources) and **interest** (how interested they are in its success), so that effort follows importance: manage key players closely, keep the powerful satisfied, keep the interested informed, monitor the rest, and re-analyse the grid whenever the environment changes.
:::

## How it works

### The internal environment starts with people

::: definition Internal environment
The sum total of people (individuals and groups, stakeholders), processes (input-throughput-output), physical infrastructure (space, equipment and physical conditions of work), administrative apparatus (lines of authority and power, responsibility, accountability) and organisational culture (the intangible aspects of working: relationships, philosophy, values, ethics) that shape an organisation's identity (SM 3.2).
:::

It is specific to each organisation, based on its structure and business model, and includes all stakeholders such as top management, investors, employees and the board of directors (SM 3.3). The first internal question is therefore: who are they?

### Who is a stakeholder?

A firm may be viewed as a **coalition of stakeholders**: all the individuals and entities that have a stake in its success and can impact it as well. This view contrasts with the earlier view of the firm as an extension of its owners and shareholders alone (SM 3.3).

::: definition Stakeholders
Any person or group of individuals, internal or external, that has an interest in, or impact on, the business or corporate strategy of the organisation. They have the power to influence the strategy or performance of that organisation (SM 3.3).
:::

- Generally, stakeholders include **management, employees, shareholders, customers and vendors**. Governments, labour unions and local groups are often considered stakeholders too, **depending on their impact** on the particular organisation. Each is affected by the strategy the organisation chooses (SM 3.3).
- It is important to **first identify the key stakeholders**: each exerts a different level of influence and has a differing level of interest in the organisation (SM 3.3).
- Their expectations can influence strategy, and a **clash of objectives** may have unfavourable consequences. The Study Material's example: a healthcare innovator needs a long-term view of its return on investment, because research takes years to reach a commercial outcome, while shareholders whose main concern is quick profits may hesitate to fund it (SM 3.3–3.4).

The Study Material lists what each key stakeholder of an OTT platform wants (SM 3.4):

| Stakeholder | Requirements |
|---|---|
| Shareholders | Innovation and continuous creative content; total shareholder return (RoI); corporate social responsibility; top rankings; highest market share |
| CEO and board of directors | Prestige; market share; revenue and profit growth; market rankings |
| Major vendors (production houses) | Growth; stability of ordering; stable margins |
| Consumers (viewers) | New content (innovation); better deals (pricing benefits); value for money; continuous supply |
| Employees | Wages and benefits; stability of employment; pride of working for a reputed organisation |

### Mendelow's matrix: two questions

::: definition Mendelow's matrix
The Mendelow stakeholder matrix, also known as the **stakeholder analysis matrix** and the **power-interest matrix**, is a simple framework to help manage key stakeholders (SM 3.5).
:::

Managing a project means managing the competing interests of many stakeholders: who needs to know what and when, who needs to give feedback and who has the final approval. Mendelow suggests analysing stakeholder groups on two dimensions (SM 3.5):

- **Power**: the ability to influence organisation strategy or resources.
- **Interest**: how interested they are in the organisation succeeding.

In reality some stakeholders hold more power than others and some have more interest than others. A **big shareholder** is likely to have high power and high interest; a **big competitor** has high power to impact strategy but potentially less interest in the success of its rival. The CEO has high power and high interest, so keeping the CEO informed almost daily should be a priority; a research institute seeking the organisation's data has low power and low interest, so it is monitored rarely, with minimum time and money spent on it (SM 3.5–3.6).

### Stakeholders move between quadrants

The environment is highly dynamic, and events can make stakeholders **suddenly move between quadrants**. If an organisation inadvertently contravenes a regulation, say GST compliance, the regulatory body (the Indirect Taxes Department) moves from high power, low interest to **high power, high interest**, and needs a different way of managing and communicating. Media houses move from low power, low interest to **low power, high interest**. So it is always worth ==re-analysing Mendelow's grid in the event of a change in the environment== (SM 3.7).

### Using the matrix, step by step

The Study Material describes the grid; ICAI's suggested answers to "How can Mendelow's matrix be used to analyse and manage the stakeholders effectively?" set out five steps (MTP May 2024 Series I Q4(b); RTP September 2024 Q12; RTP January 2026 Q11):

::: steps
1. **Identify stakeholders**: all individuals, groups or organisations that may be impacted by, or have an impact on, the organisation's activities.
2. **Assess power and interest**: power from authority, resources and expertise; interest from involvement, expectations and the potential benefits or risks to them.
3. **Plot them on the grid**: power on one axis, interest on the other; each stakeholder lands in one of the four quadrants.
4. **Develop a strategy for each quadrant**: fully engage key players; keep the powerful satisfied with regular updates so that they do not become detractors; keep the interested informed and use their feedback; monitor the low-priority group with minimal resources.
5. **Monitor and adapt**: power and interest change, so review the grid and adjust the strategies.
:::

## Under the hood

### Customers: which quadrant?

The Study Material lists customers among its "keep satisfied" examples (SM 3.6), but ICAI's own case answers place customers wherever the facts put them: with investors as **key players** where they are high-power and high-interest (MTP January 2026 Series II), with employees and suppliers as **keep informed** where the company plans to "regularly update" them (MTP September 2026 Series I Q3(a)). A real estate developer that wants to build a supplier's panels into all its projects is a **key player** for that supplier (September 2025 MCQ 13). In a case, never classify from a list of examples: read the case for its power cue ("can stop", "must approve", "strong political ties") and its interest cue ("deeply involved", "urging", "unaffected").

### Interest is about the organisation

Interest is "how interested they are in the organisation succeeding" (SM 3.5). Activists who keep raising concerns but cannot influence decisions, or a community group that campaigns hard but cannot change one café's menu, are **high interest, low power** in ICAI's MCQ answers (MTP May 2025 Series II (B)(ii); RTP September 2025 MCQ 4). A powerful environmental NGO urging greener practices is a **key player** (RTP May 2026 Q1(v)).

### Where the matrix turns up in other chapters

Case scenarios mix Mendelow with other topics: a museum company identifying local communities as key stakeholders before it can grow (RTP September 2024 MCQ 1(i)); a company's stakeholder analysis next to its BCG portfolio (RTP May 2026 Q1). The idea of a firm as a coalition of stakeholders also explains why a clearly articulated strategic vision "communicates management's aspirations to stakeholders" (SM 1.16; [[sm.intro.intent|strategic intent]]).

## Answer frame

### "Explain how Mendelow's matrix helps in managing stakeholders and categorising them" (5 marks)

1. **The tool**: Mendelow's matrix (stakeholder analysis matrix, power-interest matrix) is a simple framework to help manage key stakeholders.
2. **Managing stakeholders**: a project involves the competing interests of various stakeholders; the matrix categorises them on **power** (ability to influence strategy or resources) and **interest** (in the organisation succeeding), identifies which are most important and prioritises engagement.
3. **The four groups**, each with its action and an example: key players (manage closely, involve in decisions: CEO, shareholders, board); keep satisfied (enough regular information to keep them satisfied: banks, government); keep informed (inform adequately, use their feedback: employees, suppliers); low priority (monitor only, minimal effort: business magazines).
4. **The grid**, drawn with power up the side and interest along the bottom.
5. **Closing line**: stakeholders move between quadrants as the environment changes, so the grid is re-analysed; the matrix helps allocate time and resources and improves the chances of success.

### "How can the matrix be used to analyse and manage stakeholders?" (5 marks)

The five steps: identify stakeholders; assess power and interest; plot them on the grid; develop strategies for each quadrant (the four actions); monitor and adapt.

### A case: "classify and advise"

|---|---|

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: BrewBucks, key player
Kesar files the big rival as a key player because "they matter most". **Fix:** a big competitor has high power to impact strategy but potentially less interest in its rival's success: **keep satisfied** (SM 3.5).
:::

::: mistake Keep satisfied and keep informed swapped
Interested employees written under "keep satisfied", the powerful bank under "keep informed". **Fix:** satisfied goes with high power, informed with high interest. Check the axes before the names.
:::

::: mistake Classifying from the list, not the case
"Customers are keep satisfied, the Study Material says so." **Fix:** the examples illustrate; the case decides. The same customers can be key players, keep satisfied or keep informed in different ICAI answers.
:::

::: mistake The four names with nothing in them
Writing "key players, keep satisfied, keep informed, low priority" and stopping. **Fix:** each group earns its marks with its power-interest pair and the action the organisation takes, plus the line that positions change and the grid must be re-analysed.
:::

## Recap

- Stakeholders: any person or group, internal or external, with an interest in, or impact on, the organisation's business or corporate strategy; the firm is a coalition of stakeholders, not just an extension of its owners.
- Identify the key stakeholders first: influence and interest differ, and clashing objectives (long-term research versus quick profits) can hurt.
- Mendelow's matrix (stakeholder analysis matrix, power-interest matrix): power = ability to influence strategy or resources; interest = interest in the organisation succeeding.
- Key players (high, high): manage closely, involve in decisions (shareholders, CEO, board).
- Keep satisfied (high power, low interest): enough regular information to keep them satisfied (banks, government).
- Keep informed (low power, high interest): inform adequately, use their feedback (employees, suppliers). Low priority (low, low): monitor only (business magazines).
- Stakeholders move: a GST lapse turns the tax department into a key player and the media into keep informed; re-analyse the grid when the environment changes. (Phupha-ji is always keep satisfied.)

## Flashcards

Q:: Define stakeholders as the Study Material does.
A:: Any person or group of individuals, internal or external, that has an interest in, or impact on, the business or corporate strategy of the organisation; they have the power to influence its strategy or performance.

Q:: What is the "coalition of stakeholders" view of the firm?
A:: The firm is a coalition of all individuals and entities that have a stake in its success and can impact it, in contrast to the earlier view of the firm as an extension of its owners and shareholders alone.

Q:: Mendelow's matrix: its other two names?
A:: The stakeholder analysis matrix and the power-interest matrix.

Q:: Mendelow: what do "power" and "interest" mean?
A:: Power: the ability to influence organisation strategy or resources. Interest: how interested the stakeholders are in the organisation succeeding.

Q:: Mendelow: key players?
A:: High power, high interest (shareholders, CEO, board): fully engage them, make the greatest efforts to satisfy them, take their advice and keep them informed with all information regularly.

Q:: Mendelow: keep satisfied?
A:: High power, low interest (banks, government, customers in the Study Material's examples): put in enough work to keep them satisfied with their intended information on a regular basis.

Q:: Mendelow: keep informed?
A:: Low power, high interest (employees, vendors, suppliers, legal experts): inform them adequately so that no major issues arise; they give real-time feedback and areas of improvement.

Q:: Mendelow: low priority?
A:: Low power, low interest (business magazines, media houses): only monitor them, with minimal effort, watching for changes in their power or interest.

Q:: Where does a big competitor sit in Mendelow's matrix, per the Study Material?
A:: High power to impact strategy but potentially less interest in the rival's success: keep satisfied.

Q:: Mendelow: what happens after an inadvertent GST contravention?
A:: The Indirect Taxes Department moves from high power, low interest to high power, high interest, and media houses from low power, low interest to low power, high interest; the grid must be re-analysed.

Q:: The five steps ICAI's answers give for using Mendelow's matrix?
A:: Identify stakeholders; assess power and interest; plot them on the grid; develop strategies for each quadrant; monitor and adapt.
