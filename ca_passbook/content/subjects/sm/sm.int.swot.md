## In plain words

### Two lists, one sheet

Kesar wants Tapri's chai in bottles: "Tapri Thanda", cold chai for supermarkets and quick-delivery apps, by next summer.

> **Kesar:** Our brand, our cold chain, our taste. What can go wrong?
> **Dev:** BrewBucks' canned coffee, new sugar labelling rules, milk prices. And we have never bottled anything in our lives.
> **Kesar:** Dev, you are discounting my dream.
> **Ananya:** You are both right, and each of you has half the picture. One sheet, four boxes.

Ananya draws a square and splits it twice. The top row is **inside** Tapri, things it has or lacks and can do something about: its brand and cold chain are **strengths**; its lack of bottling experience is a **weakness**. The bottom row is **outside** Tapri, things it does not control: young buyers wanting ready-to-drink beverages and fast-growing delivery apps are **opportunities**; BrewBucks' cans and stricter sugar rules are **threats**. The left column helps the plan; the right column harms it.

That is **SWOT analysis**: strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, threats. It **combines** the external analysis (what PESTLE and the five forces found out there) with the internal analysis (stakeholders, strategic drivers, core competence) into one simple framework, so that a decision is taken with full awareness of every factor. The point is not the list. It is the next step: use the strengths and the opportunities to overcome the weaknesses and the threats.

::: analogy Your own SWOT before 12 January
Strength: you score well in SM case MCQs. Weakness: your FM capital budgeting numericals are unfinished. Opportunity: ICAI's RTPs and MTPs show what is asked. Threat: the paper may be lengthy, and your cousin's sangeet is on 11 January. The first two are about you, the last two about the world. A plan that only celebrates the strength, or only fears the wedding, fails; a good plan uses the strength and the RTPs to cover the weak chapters and the time pressure.
:::

::: key
SWOT analysis puts the organisation's internal **strengths and weaknesses** and its external **opportunities and threats** in one framework, so that a decision is taken with full awareness of all the factors. Do it before every major action, and use strengths and opportunities to overcome weaknesses and threats.
:::

## How it works

### What SWOT analysis is

::: definition SWOT analysis
The analysis of a business's strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats. Its primary objective is ==to help organisations develop a full awareness of all the factors (external as well as internal) involved in making a business decision== (SM 3.22).
:::

- It should be implemented **before all company actions**: exploring new initiatives, revamping internal policies, considering opportunities to grow, or altering a plan midway (SM 3.22).
- It is used to **discover recommendations and strategies**, with a focus on **leveraging strengths and opportunities to overcome weaknesses and threats** (SM 3.22).
- Since its creation it has been the most widely used tool for business owners to grow their companies. It can also be done just to **check on the current landscape** of the business and improve operations, showing the areas where the organisation performs well and the areas that need improvement (SM 3.22).

The chapter's own answers define each box (SM 3.51):

- **Strength**: an inherent capability of the organisation which it can use to gain strategic advantage over its competitors.
- **Weakness**: an inherent limitation or constraint of the organisation which creates strategic disadvantage to it.
- **Opportunity**: a favourable condition in the organisation's environment which enables it to strengthen its position.
- **Threat**: an unfavourable condition in the organisation's environment which causes a risk for, or damage to, the organisation's position.

Opportunities and threats are **largely uncontrollable activities outside the organisation** (SM 3.36, answer 3.42). The goal of SWOT is to **exploit** the organisation's opportunities and strengths while **neutralising** its threats and **correcting** its weaknesses (SM 3.36, answer 3.42).

### Combining external and internal analysis

Internal analysis focuses on the existing structure and competencies of the business, and so highlights the **strengths and weaknesses**; external analysis identifies and prepares for the uncontrollable, the **opportunities or threats**. SWOT is therefore a tool used for **both** internal and external analysis (SM 3.23). The Study Material's framework of strategic analysis shows the same flow: external analysis yields opportunities, threats, trends and strategic uncertainties; internal analysis yields strategic strengths, weaknesses, problems, constraints and uncertainties; both lead to strategy identification and selection (SM 2.7).

### The benefit and the criticism

- **Benefit**: it identifies the complex issues for an organisation and puts them into a **simple framework** (SM 3.23).
- **Criticism**: it does not generally provide for **evaluation of strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats in the competitive context**. An organisation using it should therefore consider relative competitors and the external factors affecting it. Although a simple tool, it is a **useful starting point** for analysis (SM 3.23).

### Why do SWOT before choosing a strategy?

An important part of strategic thinking is generating **strategic alternatives**, given the company's internal strengths and weaknesses and its external opportunities and threats; comparing them is SWOT analysis. It helps managers **craft a business model** that will let the company gain a competitive advantage in its industry; competitive advantage leads to increased profitability, which maximises the company's chances of surviving in a fast-changing, competitive environment (SM 3.51–3.52). The key reasons for SWOT analysis (SM 3.52):

1. It provides a **logical framework**.
2. It presents a **comparative account**.
3. It **guides the strategist in strategy identification**.

## Worked examples

::::: example Tapri Frozen: which road first?
Tapri Frozen makes frozen samosas and kachoris at its Bhopal plant, which runs at 55% of its capacity. Its recipes are the ones café customers already love, and every batch is made to one standard. Its own cold-chain fleet already reaches every city Tapri serves. But it sells almost only through Tapri's cafés: it has no freezer cabinets in shops, its cost per piece is high because volumes are low, and its two-person sales team has never sold to institutions.

Two routes are on the table:

- **Route A**: supermarket and kirana freezers in ten cities. FrostBite Foods, a national frozen-food major, controls most freezer space in these shops and has just cut its prices by 15%; power cuts in smaller towns often thaw the stock in shop freezers.

- **Route B**: tenders from airline caterers and railway e-catering kitchens for 40 lakh frozen snacks a year, awarded on taste, consistency and price, with bulk delivery to the caterers' own kitchens.

Required:

(i) Prepare a SWOT analysis of Tapri Frozen from the facts.

(ii) Using it, advise which route Tapri Frozen should take first.

:::: answer Model answer
**(i) SWOT analysis of Tapri Frozen.** SWOT analysis puts the organisation's internal strengths and weaknesses and its external opportunities and threats in one framework, so that the decision is taken with full awareness of all the factors involved.

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|
| Recipes customers already love | No freezer cabinets or presence in shops |
| Every batch made to one standard | High cost per piece from low volumes |
| Spare capacity: the plant runs at 55% | Sales team has never sold to institutions |
| Own cold-chain fleet in every Tapri city | |

| Opportunities | Threats |
|---|---|
| Airline and railway catering tenders for 40 lakh pieces a year | FrostBite's control of shop freezer space |
| Tenders awarded on taste and consistency, not only price | FrostBite's 15% price cut |
| | Power cuts that thaw stock in shop freezers |

**(ii) Advice: Route B first.** SWOT is used to discover strategies that leverage strengths and opportunities to overcome weaknesses and threats.

1. **Strengths meet the opportunity**: the tenders reward taste and consistency, which Tapri Frozen has, and its spare capacity and cold-chain fleet can supply bulk deliveries to the caterers' kitchens.
2. **A weakness is corrected**: 40 lakh pieces a year would fill idle capacity and bring down the high cost per piece.
3. **The threats are side-stepped**: bulk delivery to kitchens needs no shop freezers, so FrostBite's grip on freezer space and the power cuts in small towns do not matter on this route.
4. **The remaining weakness must be corrected first**: Tapri Frozen needs a trained tender team before it bids, as its sales team has never sold to institutions.

Route A runs straight into the weaknesses (no freezer cabinets, high cost per piece) and the threats (FrostBite's shelf control and price cut, power cuts); it can be reconsidered once volumes from the tenders have lowered costs. Since SWOT does not by itself evaluate these factors in the competitive context, Tapri Frozen should also judge FrostBite's ability to bid for the same tenders before committing.
::::

:::: example Rocky's first SWOT
Kesar's nephew Rocky, a summer intern, has prepared a SWOT for Tapri's cafés:

(a) BrewBucks is opening 30 new cafés in Tapri's cities: **weakness**.

(b) Tapri's café staff have never been trained to make cold beverages: **threat**.

(c) The state government now allows cafés to stay open 24 hours: **strength**.

(d) Tapri owns a cold-chain fleet: **opportunity**.

(e) Quick-delivery apps are growing fast, but TapriNow crashes at peak hours while BrewBucks' app does not: **opportunity**, nothing more.

Correct Rocky's classification, with reasons.

::: answer Model answer
Strengths and weaknesses are internal (inherent capabilities, or limitations, of the organisation); opportunities and threats are external (favourable, or unfavourable, conditions in its environment).

**(a) Threat.** BrewBucks' expansion is an unfavourable condition in Tapri's environment that causes a risk to its position. It is external, so it cannot be a weakness.

**(b) Weakness.** Untrained staff are an inherent limitation of Tapri itself, creating a strategic disadvantage. It is internal, so it cannot be a threat.

**(c) Opportunity.** A rule change is a favourable condition in the environment that enables Tapri to strengthen its position; Tapri does not own it.

**(d) Strength.** The fleet is an inherent capability Tapri can use to gain strategic advantage over competitors.

**(e) An opportunity that is also a threat, plus a weakness.** The growth of delivery apps is an opportunity, but an opportunity is also a threat where internal weaknesses do not allow the organisation to take advantage of it in the manner a rival can (SM 3.46). TapriNow's crashes are an internal weakness that BrewBucks can exploit.
:::

## Under the hood

### Is it SWOT, PESTLE or a strategic group map?

ICAI's MCQs put these tools side by side as options. Pick by what the case does:

| The case... | Tool |
|---|---|
| Lists the firm's own strengths and weaknesses with external opportunities and threats | SWOT analysis |
| Studies only macro-environmental factors (political, economic, social, technological, legal, environmental), e.g. subsidies and fuel prices | PESTLE analysis ([[sm.ext.pestle|PESTLE]]) |
| Groups rival firms by their competitive positions | Strategic group mapping ([[sm.int.drivers|strategic drivers]]) |
| Sorts stakeholders by power and interest | Mendelow's matrix ([[sm.int.stakeholders|stakeholders]]) |

In RTP May 2026 Q1(i), external factors such as EV subsidies and rising fuel costs called for **PESTLE**, "more suitable than SWOT in this context"; in RTP May 2025 MCQ 4 and MTP September 2025 Series I (A)(i), positioning against rival brands called for **strategic group mapping**, with SWOT as the wrong option.

### The line ICAI's short notes add

ICAI's answers to "Write a short note on SWOT analysis" (RTP May 2025 Q12; MTP May 2026 Series II Q2(b)) close with a sentence that is not in the July 2024 Study Material: "The major purpose of SWOT analysis is to enable the management to create a firm-specific business model that will best align, fit or match organisational resources and capabilities to the demands of the environment in which it operates." It agrees with the Study Material's "craft a business model" (SM 3.52) and is safe to write.

### Strengths are only worth what the environment rewards

A core competence is a source of competitive advantage only when it lets the firm exploit opportunities in its external environment (SM 3.21): in SWOT terms, a strength with no matching opportunity changes nothing.

::: trick Inside or outside? Then good or bad?
Ask two questions of each item, in this order. Is it **inside** the organisation (it owns it, does it, lacks it) or **outside** (a market, a rival, a rule, a trend)? Then, does it **help** or **harm** the objective? Inside-help: S; inside-harm: W; outside-help: O; outside-harm: T. Condition: a rival's move is always outside, even when it hurts.
:::

## Answer frame

### "Why is it necessary to do SWOT analysis before selecting a strategy?" (5 marks)

1. Open with the primary objective: full awareness of all the factors, internal and external, involved in a business decision.
2. It identifies strengths and weaknesses (internal) and opportunities and threats (external), so that management considers all aspects before a strategic move.
3. It helps discover recommendations and strategies that leverage strengths and opportunities to overcome weaknesses and threats.
4. It shows where the business performs well and where it needs improvement; done periodically, it checks the current landscape.
5. It puts complex issues into a simple framework; key reasons: a logical framework, a comparative account, guidance in strategy identification.
6. Close: strategy is then based on internal capabilities and external realities, raising the chances of success.

### A case: "Conduct (or evaluate through) a SWOT analysis"

| Part | What to write |
|---|---|
| Describe (1 mark where asked) | Definition and primary objective; S and W internal, O and T external |
| The grid | Two tables or one 2 × 2: every case fact in its box, in short phrases |
| Evaluation | Which strengths meet which opportunities; how weaknesses and threats can be addressed |
| Caution | SWOT alone does not evaluate the factors in the competitive context: compare with rivals |

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: the rival filed as a weakness
"BrewBucks is opening 30 cafés: weakness." **Fix:** a competitor's action is outside the organisation: a **threat**. A weakness is an inherent limitation of the organisation itself.
:::

::: mistake A grid with no conclusion
Four boxes of bullets and a full stop. **Fix:** SWOT exists to discover strategies; end with how the strengths and opportunities will be used to overcome the weaknesses and threats.
:::

::: mistake "SWOT is an internal analysis tool"
Because it sits in the internal-environment chapter, students call it internal. **Fix:** internal analysis gives the strengths and weaknesses, external analysis the opportunities and threats: SWOT is used for both, and combines them (SM 3.23).
:::

## Practice

::: quiz Check yourself
Q: Case scenario. A dairy finds that its brand is trusted (A), its plant is old (B), demand for protein drinks is rising (C), and a large rival has cut prices (D). Which classification is correct?
- [x] A strength, B weakness, C opportunity, D threat
- [ ] A strength, B threat, C opportunity, D weakness
- [ ] A opportunity, B weakness, C strength, D threat
- [ ] A strength, B weakness, C threat, D opportunity
> Brand and plant are internal (strength, weakness); demand and the rival's price cut are external (opportunity, threat).

Q: The primary objective of SWOT analysis, per the Study Material, is to:
- [ ] Group rival firms by their competitive approaches and positions
- [x] Help organisations develop a full awareness of all the factors, external as well as internal, involved in making a business decision
- [ ] Classify stakeholders by power and interest
- [ ] Measure the macro environment only
> The other options describe strategic group mapping, Mendelow's matrix and PESTLE.

Q: The major criticism of SWOT analysis in the Study Material is that it:
- [ ] Ignores external factors
- [ ] Is too complex for small firms
- [x] Does not generally provide for evaluating strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats in the competitive context
- [ ] Can be used only once a year
> Hence the advice to consider relative competitors and external factors; it remains a useful starting point.

Q: Case scenario. A new rule will require every pack of tea to carry a QR code tracing the tea to its garden from 2027. Tapri already records every batch from estate to pack; Kadak Chai buys loose tea from traders and keeps no such records. In each company's SWOT analysis, the new rule is:
- [ ] A strength for Tapri and a weakness for Kadak Chai
- [ ] A threat for both, since both must change their packs
- [x] An opportunity for Tapri and a threat for Kadak Chai
- [ ] An opportunity for both, since the whole industry gains credibility
> The rule is a condition in the environment, so it is an opportunity or a threat, never a strength or a weakness. It is favourable for Tapri, whose records let it strengthen its position, and unfavourable for Kadak Chai, whose lack of records puts its position at risk. Tapri's records are its strength and Kadak Chai's lack of them its weakness: the same external change is an opportunity or a threat depending on the firm's internal position.

Q: Which of the following is NOT one of the key reasons for SWOT analysis given in the Study Material?
- [ ] It provides a logical framework
- [ ] It presents a comparative account
- [ ] It guides the strategist in strategy identification
- [x] It evaluates each factor in the competitive context
> The three key reasons are a logical framework, a comparative account and guidance in strategy identification (SM 3.52). Evaluation in the competitive context is exactly what SWOT does not generally provide: that is its main criticism, which is why relative competitors must also be considered (SM 3.23).

## Recap

- SWOT: strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, threats; primary objective: full awareness of all the factors (external as well as internal) in a business decision.
- Use it before all company actions (new initiatives, internal policies, growth, altering a plan midway), to find strategies that leverage strengths and opportunities to overcome weaknesses and threats.
- Strengths and weaknesses: internal origin, attributes of the organisation. Opportunities and threats: external origin, attributes of the environment, largely uncontrollable.
- Helpful: strengths, opportunities. Harmful: weaknesses, threats.
- Benefit: complex issues in a simple framework. Criticism: no evaluation in the competitive context, so consider relative competitors; a useful starting point.
- Key reasons: a logical framework, a comparative account, guidance in strategy identification.
- An opportunity is also a threat if internal weaknesses stop the firm using it as a rival can. (Your cousin's sangeet on 11 January is a threat, not a weakness.)
