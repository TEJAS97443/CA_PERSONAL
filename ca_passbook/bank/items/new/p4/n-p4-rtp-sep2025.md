#### Question
Quantity of materials used and direct material cost-
#### Options
(A) 3,875 kg and ₹ 1,50,000
(B) 3,875 kg and ₹ 1,25,000
(C) 4,000 kg and ₹ 1,25,000
(D) 4,000 kg and ₹ 1,50,000
#### Answer
**(c) Calculation of actual quantity of materials used:**

Standard quantity of material used per units of output

= $\left(\dfrac{\text{budgeted usage}}{\text{budgeted production}}\right)$

= $\left(\dfrac{\text{3,750 kg}}{\text{7,500 units}}\right)$ = 0.5 kg

Standard price = $\left(\dfrac{\text{budgeted material cost}}{\text{budgeted usage}}\right)$

= $\left(\dfrac{\text{₹ 1,12,500}}{\text{3,750 kg}}\right)$ = ₹ 30

Material usage variance = (Std. qty. for actual output - Actual qty.) × Std. price

₹ 3,750A = [(7,750 x 0.5kg) - AQ] x ₹ 30

\- ₹ 3,750 = ₹ 1,16,250 - 30AQ

30AQ = ₹ 1,20,000

AQ = 4,000 kg

Actual quantity of materials used = **4,000 kg**

**Calculation of actual direct material cost:**

Material Price Variance = Actual Quantity purchased (AQ) x {Std. Price (SP) – Actual Price(AP)}

₹ 25,000F = 5,000 kg x (₹ 30 - AP)

₹ 25,000 = ₹ 1,50,000 - 5,000 AP

5,000 AP = ₹ 1,25,000

AP = ₹ 25

Actual direct material cost = 5,000 kg x ₹ 25 = ₹ **1,25,000**
#### Approach
Get the standard quantity per unit and the standard price from the budget, then work backwards: the usage variance gives the actual quantity used, and the price variance (on the 5,000 kg purchased) gives the actual price.

#### Question
Direct labour hours-
#### Options
(A) 7,937.50 hours
(B) 6,000 hours
(C) 5,812.50 hours
(D) 5,000 hours
#### Answer
**(b)** Standard hours per unit of output = $\left(\dfrac{\text{Budgeted hours}}{\text{Budgeted output}}\right)$

= $\left(\dfrac{\text{5,625 hours}}{\text{7,500 units}}\right)$ = 0.75 hours

Standard wage rate = $\left(\dfrac{\text{budgeted labour cost}}{\text{budgeted hours}}\right)$

= $\left(\dfrac{\text{₹ 1,12,500}}{\text{5,625 hours}}\right)$ = ₹ 20

Labour Efficiency Variance = Std. Rate (SR) x {Std. Hours (SH) – Actual Hours (AH)}

₹ 3,750A = ₹ 20 x [(7,750 x 0.75 hours) - AH]

\- ₹ 3,750 = ₹ 1,16,250 - 20AH

20AH = ₹ 1,20,000

AH = 6,000

Actual Hours = **6,000**
#### Approach
The efficiency variance is at the standard rate, so set it equal to SR × (standard hours for the actual output − actual hours) and solve for the actual hours.

#### Question
Suppose the units of a product are produced at the rate of 4 units per useful direct labour hour. Direct labour idle time is 10% of hours paid for. Sales of 1,000 units are budgeted and finished goods stock is expected to rise by 100 units. The budgeted direct labour hours for the production would be:
#### Options
(A) 306
(B) 250
(C) 275
(D) 400
#### Answer
**(a) 306**

Total production required = Budgeted sales + Increase in finished goods stock

= 1,000+100=1,100 units

Production rate = 4 units per useful direct labour hour

Useful hours = $\dfrac{\text{1,100 units}}{\text{4 units/hour}}$ = 275 hours

Useful hours = 90% of paid hours:

#### Question
A company’s sales could decrease by 50% before it starts incurring losses. And, for every Re. 1 of sales, it can contribute 40 paise towards fixed costs and generating profit. For the current year, the company’s fixed cost amounts to ₹ 5,00,000.
