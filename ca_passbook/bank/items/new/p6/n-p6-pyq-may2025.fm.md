#### Question
The following information is available for S Ltd. for the year ended 31st March, 2025:

| | |
|---|---:|
| Raw Material consumed | 20% of COGS |
| Raw Material Inventory turnover ratio | 4.00 |
| Finished Goods Inventory holding period | 0.75 month |
| Gross profit (based on COGS) | 12.50% |
| Debtor collection period (all sales are credit sales) | 3 months |
| Proprietary ratio | 0.3125 |
| Fixed Assets turnover ratio (based on sales) | 3.00 |
| Fixed Assets to Total Assets | 40% |

You are required to prepare a Balance Sheet as on 31st March, 2025 in the following format:

| Liabilities | ₹ | Assets | ₹ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Shareholders’ Fund (assume no Preference Shares) | | Fixed Assets | 12,00,000 |
| Long-term Debt | 15,00,000 | Stock of Raw Material | |
| Current Liabilities | | Stock of Finished Goods | |
| | | Debtors | |
| | | Cash | |
| Total | | Total | |
#### Answer
**Workings:**

**(i) Calculation of Sales, Cost of Goods Sold and Gross Profit**

Fixed Assets Turnover = $\dfrac{\text{Sales}}{\text{Fixed Assets}}$

3 = $\dfrac{\text{Sales}}{12{,}00{,}000}$

So, Sales = 12,00,000 x 3 = ₹ 36,00,000

And Cost of Goods Sold = 36,00,000 x $\dfrac{100}{(100 + 12.5)}$ = ₹ 32,00,000

Therefore, Gross Profit = 32,00,000 x 12.5% = ₹ 4,00,000

**(ii) Calculation of Stock of Raw Materials**

Raw Material Consumed = COGS x 20%

= 32,00,000 x 20%

= ₹ 6,40,000

So, stock of raw material = $\dfrac{6{,}40{,}000}{4}$ = ₹ 1,60,000

**(iii) Calculation of Debtors**

Debtor Collection Period = $\dfrac{12}{\text{Receivable Turnover Ratio}}$ = 3 months

Or Debtors = 36,00,000 X $\dfrac{3}{12}$

= ₹ 9,00,000

**(iv) Calculation of Total Assets**

Total Asset = $\dfrac{\text{Fixed Assets}}{40\%}$

= 12,00,000 x $\dfrac{100}{40}$ = ₹ 30,00,000

So Total Liabilities including shareholder’s fund = ₹ 30,00,000

**(v) Calculation of Stock of Finished Goods**

Stock of Finished Goods = 32,00,000 x $\dfrac{0.75}{12}$ = ₹ 2,00,000

**(vi) Calculation of Current Assets and Fixed Assets**

Current Assets = Total Assets – Fixed Assets

= 30,00,000 – 12,00,000 = ₹ 18,00,000

And Cash Balance = Current Assets – (Raw Material + Finished Goods + Debtors)

= 18,00,000 – (1,60,000 + 2,00,000 + 9,00,000)

= ₹ 5,40,000

**(vii) Calculation of Shareholder’s Fund**

Proprietary Ratio = $\dfrac{\text{Shareholders Fund}}{\text{Total Assets}}$

0.3125 = $\dfrac{\text{Shareholders Fund}}{30{,}00{,}000}$

Shareholders Fund = ₹ 9,37,500

**Balance Sheet as on 31st March 2025**

| Liabilities | (₹) | Assets | (₹) |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Shareholders’ Fund | 9,37,500 | Fixed Assets | 12,00,000 |
| Long-term Debt | 15,00,000 | Stock of Raw Material | 1,60,000 |
| Current Liabilities (Balancing Figure) | 5,62,500 | Stock of Finished Goods | 2,00,000 |
| | | Debtors | 9,00,000 |
| | | Cash | 5,40,000 |
| | **30,00,000** | | **30,00,000** |
#### Approach
Start from the one figure in rupees: fixed assets give sales (turnover 3) and total assets (40%). The gross profit on COGS gives COGS, and every stock and debtor figure follows from it; cash and current liabilities are balancing figures.

#### Question
What will be retention ratio if the company wants to maintain its P/E ratio to 12 in current year, given that the expected rate of return for an investor is 20%?
#### Answer
**Calculation of Retention Ratio**

P/E = $\dfrac{1-b}{K_e-br}$

12 = $\dfrac{1-b}{0.20-0.25b}$

Or 2.4 – 3b =1-b

Or 2.4 – 1 = 3b – b

Or 2b = 1.4

Or b = 0.70 or 70%

So, Retention Ratio is 70%, if the company wants to maintain its P/E ratio to 12 in the current year.
#### Approach
Under Gordon's model the justified P/E is (1 – b) ÷ (Ke – br); put in the target P/E, Ke and r and solve for b.

#### Question
What will be the expected price per share after one year if Y Ltd. achieves above-mentioned targeted P/E ratio?
#### Answer
**Calculation of Expected Price Per Share**

P/E = $\dfrac{\text{Market Price Share}}{\text{Earning Per Share}}$

P/E Ratio is 12 and EPS is ₹ 10

So, Market Price Share = P/E Ratio x Earning Per Share

= 12 x 10 = ₹ 120

Retention Ratio is 70%, when P/E ratio is 12, Return on Earnings is 25%

So, New growth rate = 0.70 X 0.25 = 0.175 = 17.5%

Expected price after one year = ₹ 120 X (1+17.5%) = ₹ 141

Therefore, the expected price per share after one year is ₹ 141 if Y Ltd. achieves the targeted P/E ratio of 12.
#### Approach
Today's price is the target P/E times EPS; it grows at g = b × r, with the retention ratio found in (i).

Calculate percentage change in earnings per share, if sales increase by 5%.

**Calculation of % change in EPS if Sales increase by 5%**

= 2 x 1.5 = 3

#### Approach
Combined leverage (operating × financial) is the percentage change in EPS for a 1% change in sales.

#### Answer
**Calculation of Post-tax Cost of Convertible Debentures:**

Calculation of Interest rate on Bank term Loan & Debentures

Let the rate of interest on debentures be ‘X’

Therefore, Rate of Interest on bank loan = 1.3X x (0.7) = 0.91X

Kd (Debentures) = $\dfrac{I(1-t)+\dfrac{(RV-NP)}{n}}{\dfrac{(RV+NP)}{2}}$

= $\dfrac{100X(1-0.3)+\dfrac{(100-96)}{5}}{\dfrac{(100+96)}{2}}$

= $\dfrac{70X+0.8}{98}$

| Sources of capital | Amount of capital | Weights (W) | Cost (K) | W x K |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Equity share capital | 14,00,000 | 0.35 | 0.20 | 0.07 |
| Preference share capital | 10,00,000 | 0.25 | 0.12 | 0.03 |
| Debentures | 9,60,000 | 0.24 | $\dfrac{70X+ 0.8}{98}$ | $\dfrac{16.8X + 0.192}{98}$ |
| Bank Loan | 6,40,000 | 0.16 | 0.91X | 0.16 x 0.91X |
| | 40,00,000 | 1.00 | | 0.14 |

0.14 = 0.07 + 0.03 + $\dfrac{16.8X+0.192}{98}$ + 0.16x(0.91X)

0.04 x 98 = 16.8 X +0.192 + 14.2688 X

3.728 = 31.0688X

#### Question
Z Ltd. is an unlevered company. It wants to repurchase its equity shares of ₹ 300 lakhs by issue of 12% debentures of same amount. Current market value of Z Ltd. is ₹ 1,400 lakhs. Its cost of capital is 18%. The company will maintain same level of EBIT in future years. Dividend pay-out ratio is 100%. Company pays tax at a rate of 30%.

As per Modigliani and Miller approach, due to such change in capital structure, what will be impact on the following?

(i) Market Value of Z Ltd.

(ii) Overall cost of capital

(iii) Cost of equity
#### Answer
Market Value of Equity = $\dfrac{\text{Net income (NI) for equity holders}}{K_e}$

₹ 1,400 lakhs = $\dfrac{\text{Net income (NI) for equity holders}}{0.18}$

Net Income to equity holders/EAT = ₹ 252 lakhs

Therefore, EBIT = $\dfrac{\text{EAT}}{(1-t)}$ = $\dfrac{252 \text{ lakhs}}{(1 - 0.3)}$ = ₹ 360 lakhs

**Income Statement**

| | All Equity (₹ In lakhs) | Equity & Debt (₹ In lakhs) |
|---|---:|---:|
| EBIT (as calculated above) | 360 | 360 |
| Interest on ₹ 300 lakhs @ 12% | - | 36 |
| EBT | 360 | 324 |
| Tax @ 30% | 108 | 97.2 |
| Income available to equity holders | 252 | 226.8 |

**(i) Market value of Z Ltd**

Market value of levered firm = Value of unlevered firm + Tax Advantage

= ₹ 1,400 lakhs + (₹ 300 lakhs x 0.3)

= ₹ 1,490 lakhs

Change in market value of the company = 1,490 lakhs – 1,400 lakhs

= ₹ 90 lakhs

The impact is that the market value of the company has increased by ₹ 90 lakhs due to replacement of equity with debt.

**(ii) Overall Cost of Capital**

Market Value of Equity = Market value of levered firm - Equity repurchased

= ₹ 1,490 lakhs – ₹ 300 lakhs = ₹ 1,190 lakhs

Cost of Equity (K$_e$) = $\dfrac{\text{Net Income to equity holders}}{\text{Market value of equity}}$ × 100

= $\dfrac{\text{₹ 226.8 lakhs}}{\text{₹ 1190 lakhs}}$ × 100 =19.06% (approx.)

Cost of debt (K$_d$) = I (1 - t) = 12 (1 - 0.3) = 8.4%

| Components | Amount (₹ In lakhs) | Cost of Capital % | Weight | WACC (K$_o$) % |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Equity | 1,190 | 19.06 | 0.80(approx.) | 15.25 |
| Debt | 300 | 8.4 | 0.20(approx.) | 1.68 |
| | 1490 | | 1 | 16.93 |

(**Note:** If we take the weight of Equity as 0.7986 and weight of Debt as 0.2013 then WACC will be 16.91%).

Impact on Market value and Overall cost of capital of Z Ltd.

| Particulars | Before restructure | After restructure | Impact |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Market value of company | ₹ 1,400 lakhs | ₹ 1,490 Lakhs | Increase due to import of debt |
| Cost of equity | 18% | 19.06% | Increase |
| Overall cost of capital | 18% | 16.93% | Decrease |

So, the impact is that the Overall Cost of Capital or K$_o$ has fallen by 1.07% (18% - 16.93%) due to the benefit of tax relief on debt interest payment.

**(iii) Cost of Equity**

The impact is that cost of equity has risen by 1.06% (19.06% - 18%) due to the presence of financial risk i.e. introduction of debt in capital structure.

**Note:** Cost of Capital and Cost of equity can also be calculated with the help of following formulas,

Cost of Capital (K$_o$) = K$_{eu}$ [1 – (t x L)]

Where,

K$_{eu}$ = Cost of equity in an unlevered company

t = Tax rate

L = $\dfrac{\text{Debt}}{\text{Debt + Equity}}$

So, K$_o$ = 0.18 $\left[1- \left(0.3 \times \dfrac{\text{₹ 300 lakhs}}{\text{₹ 1490 lakhs}}\right)\right]$ = 0.1691 or 16.91% (approx.)

Cost of Equity (K$_e$) = K$_{eu}$ + (K$_{eu}$ – K$_d$) $\dfrac{\text{Debt (1-t)}}{\text{Equity}}$

Where,

K$_{eu}$ = Cost of equity in an unlevered company

K$_d$ = Cost of debt

t = Tax rate

So, K$_e$ = 0.18 + $\left((0.18-0.12) \times \dfrac{\text{₹ 300 lakhs (1-0.3)}}{\text{₹ 1190 lakhs}}\right)$

= 0.1906 or 19.06%
#### Approach
With 100% payout, equity value = net income ÷ Ke gives EAT and then EBIT. Under MM with tax the levered value is the unlevered value plus the tax shield (debt × tax rate); Ke and Ko follow from the new values.

What is hierarchy of financing under 'Pecking Order' theory, and why does it exist?
#### Answer
**Hierarchy of financing Pecking order:**

Pecking order theory suggests that managers may use various sources for raising of fund in the following order:
