## Lesson 3.1: Key stakeholders and Mendelow's matrix

*Who the stakeholders are, and how Mendelow's power-interest grid sorts them into key players, keep satisfied, keep informed and low priority, and why the grid must be redrawn when things change.*

### Key idea

Mendelow's matrix sorts stakeholders by **power** (ability to influence the organisation's strategy or resources) and **interest** (how interested they are in its success), so that effort follows importance: manage key players closely, keep the powerful satisfied, keep the interested informed, monitor the rest, and re-analyse the grid whenever the environment changes.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where this lesson appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
Of the 32 new-scheme ICAI documents searched (eight past papers, eight RTPs, sixteen MTPs), 21 ask about Mendelow's matrix, in 27 questions:

- **"'Managing stakeholders is critical to the success of a project'. Explain how Mendelow's Matrix helps in managing stakeholders and categorizing the stakeholders into groups", 5 marks**: January 2025 Q6(b); MTP January 2026 Series I Q4(b); MTP May 2026 Series II Q4(a).
- **"How can Mendelow's Matrix be used to analyze and manage the stakeholders effectively?", 5 marks**: MTP May 2024 Series I Q4(b); MTP May 2025 Series I Q4(b); MTP September 2025 Series II Q4(b); RTP September 2024 Q12.
- **Case, 5 marks**: categorise and manage a company's stakeholders (MTP September 2026 Series I Q3(a), an online grocer; RTP January 2026 Q11, a construction company); why a company prioritises one group, plus the four categories (RTP September 2025 Q11, a politically connected business coalition: key players).
- **Case MCQs, 2 marks**: the developer (September 2025 MCQ 13: key players); a CEO whom the company makes the greatest efforts to satisfy (May 2025 MCQ 13: key player); the CEO of a smaller company that a software firm plans to acquire (RTP May 2024 MCQ 5: key player); dealers with significant influence (MTP September 2025 Series II (A)(i): key players); a powerful NGO (RTP May 2026 Q1(v): key players); a community group and a group of activists (RTP September 2025 MCQ 4; MTP May 2025 Series II (B)(ii): high interest, low power).

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
ICAI's suggested answers open with one line on the tool ("a simple framework to help manage key stakeholders"), state the two dimensions, then give **each of the four groups with what the organisation does about it**, usually with an example and often with the grid drawn. A case answer names the quadrant, quotes the case's power and interest cues, then states the engagement approach.

**[trick] S for Strong, I for Interested**
Keep **S**atisfied: the **S**trong (high power) but sleepy (low interest). Keep **I**nformed: the **I**nterested but not strong. Both high: key players; both low: low priority. Condition: this only names the box; the marks are for what the organisation does in it.

### Answer frame

### "Explain how Mendelow's matrix helps in managing stakeholders and categorising them" (5 marks)

1. **The tool**: Mendelow's matrix (stakeholder analysis matrix, power-interest matrix) is a simple framework to help manage key stakeholders.
2. **Managing stakeholders**: a project involves the competing interests of various stakeholders; the matrix categorises them on **power** (ability to influence strategy or resources) and **interest** (in the organisation succeeding), identifies which are most important and prioritises engagement.
3. **The four groups**, each with its action and an example: key players (manage closely, involve in decisions: CEO, shareholders, board); keep satisfied (enough regular information to keep them satisfied: banks, government); keep informed (inform adequately, use their feedback: employees, suppliers); low priority (monitor only, minimal effort: business magazines).
4. **The grid**, drawn with power up the side and interest along the bottom.
5. **Closing line**: stakeholders move between quadrants as the environment changes, so the grid is re-analysed; the matrix helps allocate time and resources and improves the chances of success.

### "How can the matrix be used to analyse and manage stakeholders?" (5 marks)

The five steps: identify stakeholders; assess power and interest; plot them on the grid; develop strategies for each quadrant (the four actions); monitor and adapt.

### A case: "classify and advise"

| Step | What to write |
|---|---|
| Tool | One line: the matrix and its two dimensions |
| Facts | Each stakeholder's power cue and interest cue, quoted from the case |
| Quadrant | High or low on each, so the quadrant |
| Action | What the organisation should do with that quadrant |
| Change | If the case changes something (a lapse, a campaign), the move and the new action |

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: BrewBucks, key player** **Fix:** a big competitor has high power to impact strategy but potentially less interest in its rival's success: **keep satisfied** (SM 3.5).
- **Keep satisfied and keep informed swapped** **Fix:** satisfied goes with high power, informed with high interest. Check the axes before the names.
- **Classifying from the list, not the case** **Fix:** the examples illustrate; the case decides. The same customers can be key players, keep satisfied or keep informed in different ICAI answers.
- **The four names with nothing in them** **Fix:** each group earns its marks with its power-interest pair and the action the organisation takes, plus the line that positions change and the grid must be re-analysed.

### Recap

- Stakeholders: any person or group, internal or external, with an interest in, or impact on, the organisation's business or corporate strategy; the firm is a coalition of stakeholders, not just an extension of its owners.
- Identify the key stakeholders first: influence and interest differ, and clashing objectives (long-term research versus quick profits) can hurt.
- Mendelow's matrix (stakeholder analysis matrix, power-interest matrix): power = ability to influence strategy or resources; interest = interest in the organisation succeeding.
- Key players (high, high): manage closely, involve in decisions (shareholders, CEO, board).
- Keep satisfied (high power, low interest): enough regular information to keep them satisfied (banks, government).
- Keep informed (low power, high interest): inform adequately, use their feedback (employees, suppliers). Low priority (low, low): monitor only (business magazines).
- Stakeholders move: a GST lapse turns the tax department into a key player and the media into keep informed; re-analyse the grid when the environment changes. (Phupha-ji is always keep satisfied.)

## Lesson 3.2: Strategic drivers

*The four strategic drivers (industry and markets, customers, products/services, channels) that show how a business is performing and what sets it apart, with strategic group mapping and ICAI's list of marketing strategies.*

### Key idea

Strategic drivers assess current performance by asking what differentiates the organisation from its competitors. The four key drivers are **industry and markets, customers, products/services and channels**; some are interlinked (markets with products, channels with the key customers in each channel).

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where the drivers appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
Of the 32 new-scheme ICAI documents searched (eight past papers, eight RTPs, sixteen MTPs), 19 ask about this lesson:

- **"Write a short note on the key strategic drivers of an organization", 5 marks**: January 2025 Q8(b); RTP January 2026 Q12; RTP May 2026 Q12; RTP September 2025 Q12; RTP September 2026 Q12; MTP September 2025 Series I Q3(a); MTP May 2026 Series I Q3(a). Case versions: a timber trader whose customers switched to polyester furnishings, "identify and explain the strategic driver overlooked ... also, state other strategic drivers" (MTP September 2026 Series I Q1(b): customers); a startup revising its model to follow user behaviour and platforms (MTP January 2026 Series II Q1(b)).
- **Channels, 5 marks**: "What are channels? Why is channel analysis important? Explain the different types of channels" (May 2024 Q8(a), 1 + 1 + 3; MTP January 2026 Series II Q3(b); RTP January 2025 Q12); May 2026 Q7(b); MTP September 2024 Series II Q2(b).
- **Strategic group mapping, 5 marks**: "recommend a tool to analyse the competitive position of rival companies and outline the procedure" (May 2024 Q8(b), an OR choice; January 2026 Q5(a), a beverage company in a saturated market; the same task in MTP May 2024 Series I, MTP September 2024 Series II, MTP September 2025 Series II and MTP September 2026 Series II); smartphone makers on price and features (MTP May 2026 Series I Q1(a)); identify the tool from a two-wheeler grouping (RTP May 2026 Q11).
- **MCQs, 1 or 2 marks**: the tool for identifying the strongest and weakest competitors, or the market positions of rivals (January 2025 MCQ 10; RTP January 2026 MCQ 5; RTP May 2025 MCQ 4; MTP September 2025 Series I (A)(i): strategic group mapping each time); enlightened marketing (January 2025 MCQ 13); augmented marketing (May 2025 MCQ 12; MTP September 2026 Series I); the product driver (January 2026 MCQ 13); a café overcrowded on weekends offering weekday discounts (January 2026 MCQ 16: synchro-marketing); frequent-flyer lounges (MTP January 2026 Series II: relationship marketing).

**[exam] The cue words**
Marketing-strategy MCQs are decided by one phrase in the case. "Irregular demand, idle capacity, off-peak discount": synchro. "Overfull demand, reduce or shift it": demarketing. "Additional services beyond the core product, unprecedented level": augmented. "Long-run performance of the marketing system, customer orientation, sense of mission": enlightened. "Several segments, a separate offer for each": differential. "One or a few sub-markets": concentrated.

**[trick] I-C-P-C: "I Count Profit Carefully"**
**I**ndustry and markets, **C**ustomers, **P**roducts/services, **C**hannels, the Study Material's order (SM 3.8). For channels: **S**ell, **P**hysically carry, **S**upport (sales, product, service). Condition: the mnemonic gives the order; the marks are for the explanation of each.

### Answer frame

### "Write a short note on the key strategic drivers" (5 marks)

1. **Opening**: strategic drivers are essential elements that influence an organisation's ability to differentiate itself from its competitors; they assess the current performance of the business (key markets, key customers, products and services, channels and competitive advantage).
2. **Industry and markets**: relative position; industries group companies by primary product; a market is all buyers and sellers in a region; analysed through strategic group mapping.
3. **Customers**: types of customers and their needs; the first step in deciding the product; customer versus consumer.
4. **Products/services**: what business are we in and how to win in each product; branding, packaging, features, pricing; marketing strategies.
5. **Channels**: sales, product and service channels; wide and strong channels win and act as barriers to entry.
6. **Closing line**: by aligning these drivers with its goals, a business achieves sustained growth and a competitive edge.

### "What are channels? Why is channel analysis important? Explain the types" (1 + 1 + 3)

| Part | Marks | Write |
|---|---|---|
| What are channels | 1 | The distribution system by which an organisation distributes its product or provides its service; wide, strong channels help win and act as barriers to entry |
| Why channel analysis | 1 | Important when the strategy is to scale up and expand beyond current geographies and markets: new markets need new or leveraged channels to reach new customers |
| The three types | 3 | Sales channel (who sells to whom; designers' agencies), product channel (who physically handles it; Australia Post), service channel (who supports it during and after the sale; the Bosch plumber) |

### "Recommend a tool ... and outline the procedure" (5 marks)

Name the tool (strategic group mapping) and define a strategic group; say why it suits the case (many rivals, need to group them); give the four steps in order, with the list of typical variables in step 1; close with what the map shows (relative positions, the strongest and weakest rivals, direct competitors).

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the weekend café that got "demarketing"** **Fix:** irregular demand with idle and overworked capacity, evened out by pricing and incentives, is **synchro-marketing** (January 2026 MCQ 16). Demarketing reduces overfull demand.
- **Lounges are not "augmented"** A frequent-flyer lounge strengthens a bond with select customers: **relationship marketing**. Augmented marketing adds services beyond the core product (online repair, on-site service, a better customer interface: May 2025 MCQ 12).
- **Industry and market used as synonyms** **Fix:** an industry groups companies by their primary product; a market is the buyers and sellers in a region, and each product of a business has its own market.

### Recap

- Strategic drivers assess current performance: what differentiates the organisation from its competitors. Four: industry and markets, customers, products/services, channels (some interlinked).
- Industry: companies grouped by primary product. Market: all buyers and sellers in a region; each product of a business has its own market.
- Strategic group: rivals with similar competitive approaches and positions. Map: identify characteristics; plot two at a time; group firms in the same space; circles proportional to each group's share of industry sales.
- Customers: types of customers are the first step in deciding the product; customer buys, consumer uses; pricing for the customer, design for the consumer.
- Products: "what business are we in and how do we win in each product?"; differentiation may be real or psychological; new-product pricing: customer-centric, reasonable margin, market share.
- Marketing strategies: social, augmented, direct, relationship, services, person, organisation, place, enlightened, differential, synchro, concentrated, demarketing.
- Channels: sales (who sells), product (who carries), service (who supports); wide, strong channels win and block entrants; analyse them before expanding. (The dhaba's puncture mechanic is its service channel.)

## Lesson 3.3: Resources, capabilities and core competence

*How resources and capabilities combine into a core competence, Prahalad and Hamel's three conditions, the four criteria (valuable, rare, costly to imitate, non-substitutable), and the four characteristics that decide whether an advantage lasts.*

### Key idea

A core competence is not one skill or one asset: it is the integration of many resources and capabilities (5–15 areas of developed expertise) that differentiates the firm from competitors, delivers customer value and applies across the organisation. Only a capability that is **valuable, rare, costly to imitate and non-substitutable** is a core competence, and the advantage lasts according to its **durability, transferability, imitability and appropriability**.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where this lesson appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
Of the 32 new-scheme ICAI documents searched (eight past papers, eight RTPs, sixteen MTPs), 15 ask about this lesson:

- **The four criteria, 5 marks**: "Explain the four specific criteria of sustainable competitive advantages that a company can use to determine the capabilities that are core competencies" (September 2024 Q7(a); MTP May 2025 Series I Q4(b); RTP May 2024 Q12). A case: a hospital whose surgical expertise rivals cannot copy despite buying the same robots (RTP September 2026 Q11: costly to imitate).
- **Prahalad and Hamel's three areas, 5 marks**: "Major core competencies are identified in three areas - competitor differentiation, customer value and application to other markets. Discuss" (MTP September 2024 Series I Q4(b); MTP January 2025 Series I Q4(b); MTP May 2025 Series II Q3(a); MTP January 2026 Series I Q4(b), an OR choice). "Explain the concept of core competency. What are the conditions required to be met for a company to regard its competence as core competency?" (September 2026 Q8(a); answers not yet published).
- **The four characteristics, 5 marks**: "explain the major characteristics of resources and capabilities" for sustainable competitive advantage (May 2025 Q6(b)).
- **Build competencies, 5 marks**: a small chemist shop against online sellers (MTP January 2025 Series II Q3(a)).
- **MCQs, 1 or 2 marks**: a product crucial across many industries (RTP September 2024 MCQ 4: application to other markets); rapid prototyping that beats rivals to market (MTP January 2025 Series I (B)(iii) and MTP September 2026 Series II (B)(iii): core competency); building AI strength through R&D, talent and training (MTP September 2024 Series I (B)(i): core competency building); easily copied R&D but an inimitable culture of innovation (MTP May 2026 Series I (B)(ii): the culture is the core competency); a fashion company's trend forecasting (RTP May 2025 MCQ 1(v)).

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
ICAI's suggested answers open with the list's own sentence ("Capabilities that are valuable, rare, costly to imitate, and non-substitutable are core competencies"; "major core competencies are identified in three areas"; "the sustainability of competitive advantage ... depends upon four major characteristics"), then give each item with its Study Material explanation. In a case, apply each item to a fact and end with a conclusion.

**[trick] VRIN and "Dear Tapri, Invest Appropriately"**
Core competence test: **V**aluable, **R**are, **I**nimitable (costly to imitate), **N**on-substitutable. Sustainability: **D**urability, **T**ransferability, **I**mitability, **A**ppropriability. Condition: ICAI's answers use the full words ("costly to imitate", not "inimitable"); the letters are only for recall.

### Answer frame

### "Explain the four criteria ... core competencies" (5 marks)

1. Opening: four specific criteria of sustainable competitive advantage determine which capabilities are core competencies; capabilities that are valuable, rare, costly to imitate and non-substitutable are core competencies.
2. Each criterion with its meaning, in this order: valuable (exploit opportunities or avert threats; human capital), rare (few competitors possess it; shared capabilities give no advantage), costly to imitate (rivals cannot develop it easily; Intel), non-substitutable (no strategic equivalent; Tata, Apple).
3. Closing: only when all four are met is a capability a core competence and a source of competitive advantage; it must be supported over time.

### "Major core competencies are identified in three areas ... Discuss" (5 marks)

1. Opening: according to C.K. Prahalad and Gary Hamel, major core competencies are identified in three areas.
2. Competitor differentiation; customer value; application of competencies to other markets: each with its Study Material explanation.
3. Closing: if the three conditions are met, the company can regard its competence as a core competency.

### Case: "Is X a core competence?" or "Can the advantage be sustained?"

| Step | What to write |
|---|---|
| The list | The opening sentence of the right list (criteria, conditions or characteristics) |
| Each item | Its meaning in the Study Material's words, then the case fact that meets or fails it |
| Conclusion | Core competence or not; advantage sustainable or not, and what the firm should do |

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: "Our core competence is our new machine"** **Fix:** a core competence cannot be built on one capability or a single technological know-how; it is the integration of many resources, 5–15 areas of developed expertise (SM 3.18).
- **The criteria answered with the characteristics** **Fix:** criteria for core competencies = valuable, rare, costly to imitate, non-substitutable. Durability and the rest decide whether the advantage is sustained.
- **"Imitability" written as "costly to imitate"** **Fix:** in the core-competence criteria write "costly to imitate"; in the sustainability characteristics write "imitability" with its test: how easily and quickly can competitors build the resources from scratch?

### Recap

- Core competency (Prahalad and Hamel): the collective learning in the organisation, especially coordinating diverse production skills and integrating multiple streams of technologies.
- Never one skill or one know-how: the integration of many resources; 5–15 areas of developed expertise; superior integration of technological, physical and human resources plus cultural capabilities (managing change, learning, teamwork).
- Three areas: competitor differentiation (unique, hard to imitate), customer value (fundamental benefit to the end customer), application to other markets (whole organisation, opens new markets).
- Four criteria: valuable, rare, costly to imitate, non-substitutable; only all four make a core competence, and only when it exploits opportunities in the external environment.
- Sustainability of competitive advantage: durability, transferability, imitability, appropriability.
- Examples to quote: HUL (marketing and sales), Wal-Mart (low operating costs), Intel (R&D cycle time), Tata (culture and talent), Apple (iOS), Airtel (customer service). (A star bowler can be bought at auction; the team's system cannot.)

## Lesson 3.4: SWOT analysis: combining external and internal analysis

*SWOT puts the internal strengths and weaknesses and the external opportunities and threats on one sheet, so that a decision is taken with full awareness of all the factors; what it is for, and its one big criticism.*

### Key idea

SWOT analysis puts the organisation's internal **strengths and weaknesses** and its external **opportunities and threats** in one framework, so that a decision is taken with full awareness of all the factors. Do it before every major action, and use strengths and opportunities to overcome weaknesses and threats.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where SWOT appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
Of the 32 new-scheme ICAI documents searched (eight past papers, eight RTPs, sixteen MTPs), 9 ask about SWOT analysis:

- **"The primary objective of SWOT analysis is to help organizations develop a full awareness of all factors involved in making a business decision. In the light of the above statement, explain why is it necessary to do SWOT analysis for business organization before using the strategy", 5 marks**: September 2025 Q7(a).
- **"Write short note on SWOT analysis", 5 marks**: RTP May 2025 Q12; MTP May 2026 Series II Q2(b).
- **A case, 5 marks**: conduct a SWOT for a new line of smart home devices (MTP May 2024 Series II Q3(a); RTP January 2025 Q11); identify the analysis an organic farm should conduct and outline the grid (MTP September 2024 Series I Q1(b)); describe SWOT and evaluate a training company's online expansion through it (September 2026 Q5(c), 1 + 4; answers not yet published).
- **MCQs, 2 marks**: a startup listing its unique technology, high competition, an untapped segment and inefficiencies (RTP September 2025 MCQ 3: SWOT); a company using its branding strength and AI opportunities against global competition (MTP May 2025 Series I (A)(v): SWOT).

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
In a case, ICAI's answers draw the grid (Strengths and Weaknesses on top, Opportunities and Threats below), fill it with the case's facts in short bullets, and close with a paragraph on what the company should do: leverage the strengths, address the weaknesses and threats. For "why SWOT before strategy", ICAI's suggested answer (September 2025 Q7(a)) makes these points: full awareness of internal and external factors; strategies leveraging strengths and opportunities to overcome weaknesses and threats; areas performing well and needing improvement; checking the current landscape; complex issues in a simple framework.

**[trick] Inside or outside? Then good or bad?**
Ask two questions of each item, in this order. Is it **inside** the organisation (it owns it, does it, lacks it) or **outside** (a market, a rival, a rule, a trend)? Then, does it **help** or **harm** the objective? Inside-help: S; inside-harm: W; outside-help: O; outside-harm: T. Condition: a rival's move is always outside, even when it hurts.

### Answer frame

### "Why is it necessary to do SWOT analysis before selecting a strategy?" (5 marks)

1. Open with the primary objective: full awareness of all the factors, internal and external, involved in a business decision.
2. It identifies strengths and weaknesses (internal) and opportunities and threats (external), so that management considers all aspects before a strategic move.
3. It helps discover recommendations and strategies that leverage strengths and opportunities to overcome weaknesses and threats.
4. It shows where the business performs well and where it needs improvement; done periodically, it checks the current landscape.
5. It puts complex issues into a simple framework; key reasons: a logical framework, a comparative account, guidance in strategy identification.
6. Close: strategy is then based on internal capabilities and external realities, raising the chances of success.

### A case: "Conduct (or evaluate through) a SWOT analysis"

| Part | What to write |
|---|---|
| Describe (1 mark where asked) | Definition and primary objective; S and W internal, O and T external |
| The grid | Two tables or one 2 × 2: every case fact in its box, in short phrases |
| Evaluation | Which strengths meet which opportunities; how weaknesses and threats can be addressed |
| Caution | SWOT alone does not evaluate the factors in the competitive context: compare with rivals |

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the rival filed as a weakness** **Fix:** a competitor's action is outside the organisation: a **threat**. A weakness is an inherent limitation of the organisation itself.
- **A grid with no conclusion** **Fix:** SWOT exists to discover strategies; end with how the strengths and opportunities will be used to overcome the weaknesses and threats.
- **"SWOT is an internal analysis tool"** **Fix:** internal analysis gives the strengths and weaknesses, external analysis the opportunities and threats: SWOT is used for both, and combines them (SM 3.23).

### Recap

- SWOT: strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, threats; primary objective: full awareness of all the factors (external as well as internal) in a business decision.
- Use it before all company actions (new initiatives, internal policies, growth, altering a plan midway), to find strategies that leverage strengths and opportunities to overcome weaknesses and threats.
- Strengths and weaknesses: internal origin, attributes of the organisation. Opportunities and threats: external origin, attributes of the environment, largely uncontrollable.
- Helpful: strengths, opportunities. Harmful: weaknesses, threats.
- Benefit: complex issues in a simple framework. Criticism: no evaluation in the competitive context, so consider relative competitors; a useful starting point.
- Key reasons: a logical framework, a comparative account, guidance in strategy identification.
- An opportunity is also a threat if internal weaknesses stop the firm using it as a rival can. (Your cousin's sangeet on 11 January is a threat, not a weakness.)

## Lesson 3.5: Porter's generic strategies

*Competitive advantage and Porter's three bases (cost leadership, differentiation, focus), with how to achieve each, its advantages against the five forces and its disadvantages, plus the best-cost provider strategy.*

### Key idea

Competitive advantage is profitability above the industry average that rivals cannot duplicate. Porter's three generic strategies, **cost leadership, differentiation and focus** (focused cost leadership or focused differentiation), are three bases for it; the **best-cost provider** gives more value for money by combining low cost with upscale features. Each needs different organisational arrangements, controls and incentives.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where the generic strategies appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
Of the 32 new-scheme ICAI documents searched (eight past papers, eight RTPs, sixteen MTPs), 23 ask about this lesson:

- **Descriptive, 5 marks**: cost leadership, its conditions and disadvantages (January 2026 Q6(b), 1 + 4); differentiation, its bases and the measures to achieve it (May 2025 Q7(b), 1 + 2 + 2); "Explain in brief the various basis of differentiation strategies" (MTP January 2025 Series I Q3(b)).
- **Identify and explain, 5 marks**: differentiation with its disadvantages (May 2026 Q5(b)), the measures to achieve it (RTP May 2024 Q11) or its advantages against the five forces (RTP September 2024 Q11); focused strategies with advantages and disadvantages, a baby-care firm pricing low for one niche and premium for another (September 2024 Q5(c)); best-cost provider, "explain the strategy briefly and enumerate the key ways" it was implemented (September 2025 Q5(a)); focused differentiation (RTP May 2025 Q11; RTP September 2026 Q13); three companies, three strategies (MTP May 2025 Series II Q1(b)).
- **MCQs, 1 or 2 marks**: focused differentiation (September 2024 MCQ 12; May 2026 MCQ 9; MTP May 2024 Series II (A)(v)); best-cost provider (May 2026 MCQ 14, choose the actions that fit; MTP September 2024 Series I (A)(iii); MTP September 2024 Series II (A)(v); MTP January 2026 Series I (A)(ii); RTP September 2026 MCQ 1(iii)); cost leadership (RTP May 2024 MCQ 4, the risk from technological breakthroughs; MTP September 2024 Series II (B)(ii); MTP September 2025 Series II (A)(v); MTP May 2026 Series II (A)(v); RTP September 2024 MCQ 1(ii)); differentiation (MTP January 2025 Series I (A)(ii); MTP January 2026 Series II (A)(v); MTP May 2024 Series I (A)(iii), why it fell short; RTP September 2025 MCQ 1(iii); RTP January 2026 MCQ 1(v); RTP May 2026 MCQ 5); September 2026 MCQ 12 (answers not yet published).

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
ICAI's suggested answers start with the same line ("According to Porter, strategies allow organizations to gain competitive advantage from three different bases: cost leadership, differentiation, and focus. Porter called these base generic strategies"), name the strategy, apply its definition to the case facts, then give the list asked for (conditions, measures, advantages against the five forces, or disadvantages) in the Study Material's order.

**[trick] Two questions, then a third**
**How wide?** The whole market, or one niche? **Win how?** On cost and price, or on uniqueness? Broad and price: cost leadership. Broad and unique: differentiation. Niche and price: focused cost leadership. Niche and unique: focused differentiation. Then ask: **against rivals' comparable products, the same quality at a lower price, or much better quality at a similar price?** Best-cost provider. Condition: a niche player's prices are judged against rivals in its niche, not the whole industry.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the cheap niche player called a cost leader** **Fix:** it serves a narrow market and charges low prices relative to firms in that market, not the lowest prices in the industry: **focused cost leadership**.
- **Premium means focus** **Fix:** a premium product sold to the broad market (all cities, all customer groups) is **differentiation**; it is focused differentiation only when the target is a narrow segment.
- **Best-cost provider confused with cost leadership** **Fix:** a cost leader sells standardised products cheaply; the best-cost provider gives more value for money: a lower price for comparable quality, or a similar price for much higher quality.
- **Advantages written as a general list** **Fix:** the Study Material's answer is the five forces, one by one: rivalry, buyers, suppliers, entrants, substitutes.

### Recap

- Competitive advantage: profitability above the industry average, from unique features the target market sees as superior, which rivals fail to duplicate.
- Porter's generic strategies: cost leadership, differentiation, focus; generic because any type or size of firm, even not-for-profit, can pursue them; each needs different arrangements, controls and incentives.
- Cost leadership: standardised products, very low unit cost, price-sensitive broad market; effective with many price-sensitive buyers and few ways to differentiate; 6 measures, five-force advantages, 4 disadvantages.
- Differentiation: unique industry-wide, premium price, price-insensitive buyers; bases: product, pricing, organisation; 6 measures, five-force advantages, 3 disadvantages.
- Focus: a narrow segment of sufficient size and growth, not crucial to major rivals; focused cost leadership (low prices relative to the niche's rivals) or focused differentiation (unique features for the niche); 4 measures, 2 advantages, 3 disadvantages.
- Best-cost provider: more value for money, a lower price for comparable quality or a similar price for much better quality (Android flagships).
- Answer pattern: Porter's three bases, the strategy named, its definition applied to the case, then the list asked for. (The vada pav leads on cost; the Udaipur wedding caterer focuses on uniqueness.)
