## Lesson 11.1: Professional ethics and the fundamental principles

*What ethics means for a Chartered Accountant, why the profession needs a code, the principles-based approach, and the five fundamental principles ICAI asks you to spot in a case.*

### Key idea

A Chartered Accountant shall comply with **each** of the five fundamental principles: integrity, objectivity, professional competence and due care, confidentiality and professional behaviour. Where no specific rule fits, the principles still apply: ICAI expects the spirit of the Code to be followed, not just its letter.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where the fundamental principles appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
In the ICAI documents read for this lesson, the principles were examined in the 15 listed here:

- **Descriptive, 3 to 5 marks.** Situations to classify, "Explain which fundamental principle … is violated": May 2024 Q5(c) (3 marks, three situations) and RTP January 2026 Q20 (three situations). A principle described in words, "Identify and explain": January 2025 Q6(c) (2 + 2 marks). A confidentiality case for a CA in service: September 2024 Q6(b) (4 marks). Principles-based versus rules-based approach: May 2026 Q1(a)(ii) (2 marks). Meaning of professional behaviour with an example: MTP May 2024 Series I Q5(d) (3 marks). No engagement letter: MTP September 2024 Series I Q4(b) (5 marks).
- **Multiple choice, 2 marks each.** Too little time to do the work, yet a clean report signed: competence and due care (September 2024 MCQ 4). Outgoing auditor's silence: professional behaviour (January 2026 MCQ 1; RTP September 2025 MCQ 1). Non-compliances deliberately left unreported: professional behaviour (RTP September 2026 MCQ 1). A request to present disclosures misleadingly: integrity (MTP May 2025 Series I MCQ 9; MTP September 2025 Series II MCQ 10). A bank's non-disclosure declaration: confidentiality (MTP September 2024 Series II MCQ 10). A situation no rule covers: principles-based approach (MTP January 2026 Series II MCQ 13; MTP May 2026 Series II MCQ 9).

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
ICAI's suggested answers name the principle, quote its definition in the Study Material's words ("straightforward and honest", "bias, conflict of interest or undue influence of others", "on a timely basis", "required by law", "might discredit the profession"), apply it to the facts and end with "violated" or "not violated". A bare name earns little.

**[trick] In Our Chai, Cardamom Please**
**I**ntegrity, **O**bjectivity, professional **C**ompetence and due care, **C**onfidentiality, **P**rofessional behaviour: the five principles in the Study Material's order. Condition: it is a memory aid for the list only; each answer still needs the definition.

### Answer frame

### "Identify and explain the principle" (1 to 2 marks per situation)

1. **Name the principle** as a heading: "(a) Integrity".
2. **Quote the definition**: only the limb that fits ("shall not knowingly be associated with information that omits required information where such omission would be misleading").
3. **Apply it to the facts**: "In the given case, Harsh knowingly omitted …".
4. **Conclude**: "Hence, the principle of integrity is violated." (Or "not violated", as in a disclosure required by law.)

### "Distinguish between the principles-based and the rules-based approach" (2 marks)

Write the comparison table of §3 as points: essence (spirit versus established rules); professional judgment in every situation versus following rules; narrow outlook and rigidity of rules; conclusion that the spirit of the Code must be followed.

### Three answers to file (b)

**[cols] **Loses marks****

"Harsh is dishonest."

A judgment, not an answer: no principle named, no definition.
---
**Passes**

"Integrity is violated because Harsh hid the demand."

Right principle, but no definition and no link to "misleading".
---
**Full marks**

Integrity named; "straightforward and honest" and the omission limb quoted; the facts applied (required by the format, knowingly omitted, decisive to the bank); "Hence, integrity is violated"; in service is no exemption.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: independence as the sixth principle** **Fix:** there are five: integrity, objectivity, professional competence and due care, confidentiality, professional behaviour. Independence, its threats and safeguards are a separate topic.
- **Integrity and objectivity swapped** **Fix:** false or misleading information is integrity; bias, conflict of interest or undue influence is objectivity. When both are present, name both.
- **Confidentiality as a vow of silence** **Fix:** disclosure is allowed when required by law, when permitted by law and authorised by the client or employer, or under a professional duty or right not prohibited by law.
- **The client's say-so is not always enough** **Fix:** the second exception needs *both* permission of law and the client's authority; and the auditor's working papers remain the firm's, shared at its discretion.

### Recap

- Ethics: moral principles governing a person's behaviour; a state of mind strong enough to resist selfish motives.
- Need: society, governments, clients, taxing authorities, employees, investors and the business and financial community trust Chartered Accountants; assurance exists to enhance users' confidence; the profession acts in the public interest.
- Deviation brings the disciplinary mechanism: fines, suspension or removal from membership, or other action.
- Principles-based approach: the spirit of ethics and professional judgment in every situation; rules-based: rigid, may be narrow. Follow the spirit of the Code.
- Five principles: integrity, objectivity, professional competence and due care, confidentiality, professional behaviour. Comply with each.
- Integrity covers false, negligent **and** misleadingly incomplete information; diligence means carefully, thoroughly and on a timely basis.
- Confidential information may be disclosed when required by law, permitted by law and authorised by the client or employer, or under a professional duty or right not prohibited by law. (The umpire may tell the court; he may not tell the bookie.)

## Lesson 11.2: Independence, threats and safeguards

*Independence of mind and in appearance, the five threats with ICAI's examples, what an auditor does when a threat exists, and professional skepticism, the questioning mind every audit needs.*

### Key idea

Independence must exist in fact (independence of mind) **and** appear to exist to a reasonable and informed third party (independence in appearance). Five threats attack it: self-interest, self-review, advocacy, familiarity and intimidation. When one exists, desist, eliminate it, or reduce it to an acceptable level with recorded safeguards; if credible and adequate safeguards cannot be fully implemented, do not accept the work.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where independence and skepticism appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
In the ICAI documents read for this lesson (cited here and under the hood), threats to independence were examined in 19 documents and professional skepticism in 6.

- **Classify the threats, 3 to 5 marks.** Six observations of a firm, plus what the auditor should do: January 2025 Q2(b) (3 + 2 marks). Five threats from the Study Material's own examples, with reasons: September 2025 Q6(c) (4 marks, an OR question). A partner's shares, a loan to a director, fee dependence, internal audit and consultancy, an ex-Finance Manager on the team: RTP September 2026 Q20. Familiarity threats explained: MTP January 2025 Series II Q5(c) (4 marks), MTP May 2026 Series II Q6 (5 marks, with rotation under the Companies Act), MTP May 2024 Series I (3 marks, free holidays and air travel). Inherited shares before appointment: RTP January 2025 Q20. The threat when one firm audits a listed company for ever, the Companies Act safeguard and the general situations of that threat: September 2026 Q3(b) (1 + 1 + 3 marks; in the Study Material's terms a familiarity threat, addressed by rotation of auditors, SM 11.11; its suggested answer was not yet published when this lesson was checked). A team member's close business relationship with a client executive, the meaning of independence and the engagement partner's responsibilities: September 2026 Q2(c) (1 + 3 marks).
- **Multiple choice, 2 marks each.** Undue fee dependence is self-interest (September 2024 MCQ 5; MTP January 2025 Series I MCQ 12 and Series II MCQ 13). Gifts and a loan (January 2026 MCQ 9). Shares bought to benefit from the opinion; reviewing a method one designed (MTP May 2025 Series I MCQ 6 and 8; MTP September 2025 Series II MCQ 9). A client threatening future work: intimidation (RTP January 2025 MCQ 7; MTP September 2026 Series II MCQ 11). Ten years with a CEO, plus gifts: familiarity (RTP May 2025 MCQ 7). Book-keeping for an audit client: self-review (RTP September 2024 MCQ 6; RTP September 2025 MCQ 3). The engagement partner's steps on threats (January 2026 MCQ 7).
- **Professional skepticism, 3 to 5 marks.** Unusual year-end sales despite invoices, and the risks reduced: May 2026 Q1(a)(i) (3 marks). Contradicting invoices, evasive answers, altered documents: MTP January 2026 Series II (4 marks). One internal document for a material purchase: MTP September 2025 Series II (5 marks). Five examples of skepticism: MTP May 2025 Series II (5 marks). MCQs: reliance on a representation letter alone shows a lack of skepticism (RTP September 2025 MCQ 2), and tracing an undisclosed related party through past annual reports shows skepticism (RTP May 2024 general MCQ 4).

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
For a classification question ICAI's answers give a table (situation, threat), each threat's definition as the reason, and then the guiding principles on what to do: desist, eliminate, or safeguards reducing the threat to an acceptable level, recorded; if credible and adequate safeguards cannot be fully implemented, do not accept. For skepticism, the definition, the "alert to" list or the "reduces the risks of" list, and the facts applied.

**[trick] Money, Mirror, Megaphone, Mate, Menace**
Self-interest (money), self-review (mirror: you see your own work), advocacy (megaphone), familiarity (mate), intimidation (menace). Condition: the cue picks the threat, but the answer must give ICAI's definition and example, and a single fact can create more than one threat.

### Answer frame

### "Classify the threats and state what the auditor should do"

1. A table: **situation → threat**, one line each.
2. For each, the **reason** in the Study Material's words ("could benefit from a financial interest", "services that are themselves subject matters of audit", "client's advocate in litigation", "close relative … in a senior position", "pressure to disproportionately reduce work").
3. **What to do**, in the guiding principles' words: consider threats before taking on work; desist, eliminate, or safeguards to an acceptable level, recorded; if credible and adequate safeguards cannot be fully implemented, do not accept the work.
4. Where marks allow, the specific step for each threat (sell the interest, remove the person, decline the service).

### A skepticism case: provision, facts, analysis, conclusion

Definition → the "alert to" item the facts trigger (contradicting evidence, doubtful reliability, fraud conditions) → what the auditor should do (investigate further, modify or add procedures; past honesty does not excuse) → the risks it reduces → conclusion on the auditor's approach.

### "Distinguish between independence of mind and independence in appearance"

| Point | Independence of mind | Independence in appearance |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | State of mind permitting an opinion without influences that compromise judgment | Avoiding facts and circumstances so significant that an informed third party would conclude independence is compromised |
| Test | The auditor is himself satisfied | No unbiased person would be forced to conclude independence is likely abridged |
| Enables | Integrity, objectivity and professional skepticism | Users' confidence in the report |

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: "I know I am independent, so there is no threat"** **Fix:** independence must also exist in appearance: the test is whether a reasonable and informed third party, knowing all the facts, would conclude that integrity, objectivity or skepticism had been compromised.
- **The share dealer filed under self-interest** **Fix:** a financial interest in the client is self-interest, but the Study Material's example of an auditor who **deals in the shares or securities** of the audited company is **advocacy**: he is seen to promote the client.
- **Self-review and familiarity reversed** **Fix:** the client's former director now on the audit team is self-review; the audit firm's former partner now a director of the client is familiarity.
- **Safeguards as an afterthought** **Fix:** say which, that they must reduce the threat to an acceptable level and be recorded, and that if credible and adequate safeguards cannot be fully implemented, the work must not be accepted.
- **Skepticism as suspicion of everyone** **Fix:** the auditor may accept records and documents as genuine unless he has reason to believe the contrary; skepticism means a questioning mind, alertness and critical assessment, and investigating further when there is reason to doubt.

### Recap

- Independence: judgment not subordinate to the wishes or direction of another who engaged him, or to his own self-interest; a condition of mind and character that rules alone cannot create.
- Two perspectives: independence of mind and independence in appearance (the reasonable and informed third party); it must exist in fact and appear to exist.
- Self-interest: financial interest, loan or guarantee, fee dependence, close business relationship, potential employment, contingent audit fees.
- Self-review: reviewing one's own earlier judgment or service; audit team member recently a director or senior employee of the client.
- Advocacy: dealing in the client's shares, acting as its advocate in litigation. Familiarity: close relative in a senior post, former firm partner at the client, long association, significant gifts. Intimidation: threat of replacement, pressure to cut work with fees, threat of litigation.
- Threat exists: desist, eliminate, or safeguards to an acceptable level, recorded; if credible and adequate safeguards cannot be fully implemented, do not accept.
- Professional skepticism: questioning mind, alertness to misstatement from error or fraud, critical assessment of evidence; documents may be accepted as genuine unless there is reason to doubt; past honesty does not excuse it. (Bunty returned the gold card. He now buys his own chai.)

## Lesson 11.3: SA 210: agreeing the terms of audit engagements

*The preconditions the auditor must establish before accepting an audit, the engagement letter and what it contains, refusing a crippling scope limitation, requests to change the terms, and when recurring audits need revised terms.*

### Key idea

Accept or continue an audit only when its basis is agreed: the preconditions are present (an acceptable framework, and management's acknowledgement of its responsibilities) and the terms are commonly understood and recorded in an engagement letter or other written agreement.

### Formats and formulas

### ICAI's example engagement letter (SM 11.17–11.20)

*Reproduce the headings in order, and the auditor's and management's responsibilities point by point: May 2026 asked for five of the auditor's responsibilities for 5 marks. ICAI's example is from PJ Shrimali & Co. to Pristine Products Limited; the wording below is ICAI's, with Tapri's names put in.*

*Iyer & Associates, Chartered Accountants, Indore. [Date]*

To the Board of Directors of Tapri Tea Co. Ltd

**The objective and scope of the audit**

You have requested that we audit the financial statements of Tapri Tea Co. Ltd, which comprise the Balance Sheet as at March 31st, 20XX, the Statement of Profit & Loss, Cash Flow Statement for the year then ended, and notes to the financial statements, including a summary of significant accounting policies and other explanatory information.

We are pleased to confirm our acceptance and our understanding of this audit engagement by means of this letter. The objectives of our audit are to obtain reasonable assurance about whether the financial statements as a whole are free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, and to issue an auditor's report that includes our opinion. Reasonable assurance is a high level of assurance, but is not a guarantee that an audit conducted in accordance with Standards on Auditing (SAs) will always detect a material misstatement when it exists. Misstatements can arise from fraud or error and are considered material if, individually or in the aggregate, they could reasonably be expected to influence the economic decisions of users taken on the basis of these financial statements.

**The responsibilities of the auditor**

We will conduct our audit in accordance with Standards on Auditing (SAs) issued by the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India (ICAI). Those Standards require that we comply with ethical requirements. As part of an audit in accordance with SAs, we exercise professional judgment and maintain professional skepticism throughout the audit. We also:

- Identify and assess the risks of material misstatement of the financial statements, whether due to fraud or error, design and perform audit procedures responsive to those risks, and obtain audit evidence that is sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for our opinion. The risk of not detecting a material misstatement resulting from fraud is higher than for one resulting from error, as fraud may involve collusion, forgery, intentional omissions, misrepresentations, or the override of internal control.
- Obtain an understanding of internal control relevant to the audit in order to design audit procedures that are appropriate in the circumstances but not for the purpose of expressing an opinion on the effectiveness of the company's internal control. However, we will communicate to you in writing concerning any significant deficiencies in internal control relevant to the audit of the financial statements that we have identified during the audit.
- Evaluate the appropriateness of accounting policies used and the reasonableness of accounting estimates and related disclosures made by management.
- Conclude on the appropriateness of management's use of the going concern basis of accounting and, based on the audit evidence obtained, whether a material uncertainty exists related to events or conditions that may cast significant doubt on the company's ability to continue as a going concern. If we conclude that a material uncertainty exists, we are required to draw attention in our auditor's report to the related disclosures in the financial statements or, if such disclosures are inadequate, to modify our opinion. Our conclusions are based on the audit evidence obtained up to the date of our auditor's report. However, future events or conditions may cause the company to cease to continue as a going concern.
- Evaluate the overall presentation, structure and content of the financial statements, including the disclosures, and whether the financial statements represent the underlying transactions and events in a manner that achieves fair presentation.

Because of the inherent limitations of an audit, together with the inherent limitations of internal control, there is an unavoidable risk that some material misstatements may not be detected, even though the audit is properly planned and performed in accordance with SAs.

**The responsibilities of management**

Our audit will be conducted on the basis that management and, where appropriate, those charged with governance acknowledge and understand that they have responsibility:

- (a) For the preparation of financial statements that give a true and fair view in accordance with the financial reporting Standards. This includes:
  - The responsibility for the preparation of financial statements on a going concern basis.
  - The responsibility for selection and consistent application of appropriate accounting policies, including implementation of applicable accounting standards along with proper explanation relating to any material departures from those accounting standards.
  - The responsibility for making judgments and estimates that are reasonable and prudent so as to give a true and fair view of the state of affairs of the entity at the end of the financial year and of the profit or loss of the entity for that period.
- (b) For such internal control as management determines is necessary to enable the preparation of financial statements that are free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error; and
- (c) To provide us with:
  - (i) Access, at all times, to all information, including the books, accounts, vouchers and other records and documentation, of the company, whether kept at the head office of the company or elsewhere, of which management is aware that is relevant to the preparation of the financial statements such as records, documentation and other matters;
  - (ii) Additional information that we may request from management for the purpose of the audit; and
  - (iii) Unrestricted access to persons within the entity from whom we determine it necessary to obtain audit evidence. This includes our entitlement to require from the officers of the company such information and explanations as we may think necessary for the performance of our duties as auditor. As part of our audit process, we will request from management and, where appropriate, those charged with governance, written confirmation concerning representations made to us in connection with the audit.

**Fees**

Our fees bill for ₹ XXXXXX (plus applicable taxes) and out of pocket expenses will be raised after completion of audit work.

**Reporting**

We will report to the members of Tapri Tea Co. Ltd as a body, whether in our opinion, the financial statements give the information required by the Companies Act, 2013 in the manner so required and give a true and fair view in conformity with the accounting principles generally accepted in India, of the state of affairs of the company as at March 31, 20XX, and its profit/loss, and its cash flows for the year ended on that date. The form and content of our report may need to be amended in the light of our audit findings.

Please sign and return the attached copy of this letter to indicate your acknowledgement of, and agreement with, the arrangements for our audit of the financial statements including our respective responsibilities.

For Iyer & Associates, Chartered Accountants. Firm's Registration Number. (Signature) (Name of the Member) (Designation)

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where SA 210 appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
In the documents read for this lesson, SA 210 was examined in 18.

- **Preconditions, 4 or 5 marks**: May 2025 Q3(c) (4 marks), MTP September 2025 Series I Q4(b), MTP January 2026 Series II Q4(b), MTP May 2026 Series I Q6(b) (5 marks each; refusal to acknowledge responsibilities, access only on request, an internally designed framework), RTP May 2024 Q24 (a partnership firm that calls internal control the auditor's job).
- **The engagement letter, 3 to 5 marks.** What it is and contains: September 2024 Q5(d) (3 marks), MTP May 2024 Series II Q5(d) (3 marks), MTP January 2025 Series I Q2(a) (5 marks, with the law-prescribed exception). The auditor's responsibilities in the letter, any five: May 2026 Q6(a) (5 marks).
- **Change of terms to a review, 5 marks**: September 2026 Q1(a) (inventory inconsistencies found, then a request citing time and cost), MTP January 2025 Series II Q2(a) and MTP September 2026 Series I Q6(b) (receivables unsupported: not justified); RTP May 2025 Q20 (a request after diversification).
- **Recurring audits**: the eight factors, September 2025 Q3(b) (5 marks); RTP September 2025 Q20; MCQs in January 2025 (MCQ 1: the replacement of the engagement partner within the audit firm is not one of the factors), MTP January 2025 Series I MCQ 13, MTP May 2024 Series II MCQ 2.3 (a "modest" change is not a factor: the word is "significant") and RTP September 2026 MCQ 5.
- **Other MCQs**: the letter is sent by the auditor and states that the report may differ, MTP September 2024 Series I MCQ 10; a scope limitation before acceptance, MTP September 2025 Series I MCQ 14 and MTP May 2024 Series II MCQ 2.1 (do not accept).

**[exam] The traps**
Who sends the letter (the auditor, to the client: the Study Material's Correct/Incorrect question); "significant", not "modest", change in nature or size; the missing preconditions in MCQ options are usually responsibility for internal control and for preparing the financial statements; and "unless required by law or regulation" belongs in every answer on not accepting.

**[trick] Framework + PIA, and "Only Auditors Make Fine Reports"**
Preconditions: an acceptable **Framework**, plus management's acknowledgement of **PIA**: **P**reparation of the financial statements, **I**nternal control, **A**ccess (information and persons). The letter's five, in order: **O**bjective and scope, **A**uditor's responsibilities, **M**anagement's responsibilities, **F**ramework, **R**eport (expected form, and that it may differ). Condition: both follow the Study Material's order.

### Answer frame

### A case question on acceptance or change of terms

1. **Relevant provision**: "As per SA 210, …": the preconditions and the consequence of their absence; or the reasonable-justification rule and its examples; or the recurring-audit factors.
2. **Facts of the case**: what management refused, limited or requested, and why.
3. **Analysis**: which precondition fails; would the limitation lead to a disclaimer; is the reason a change in circumstances or misunderstanding (reasonable), or unsatisfactory information (not reasonable); which recurring-audit factor is present.
4. **Conclusion**: accept or not (with "unless required by law or regulation"); agree or not, and the consequences (new terms in writing; or withdraw where possible and consider reporting); revise or remind.

### "What should the auditor consider before agreeing to change to a lower level of assurance?"

| Point | What earns the mark |
|---|---|
| Justification | Determine whether there is reasonable justification |
| Implications | Assess any legal or contractual implications |
| Work done | May be relevant to the changed engagement |
| Report | Appropriate to the revised engagement; no reference to the original audit or its procedures (except agreed-upon procedures) |
| Terms | Agree and record the new terms in writing |
| If refused | Withdraw where possible under law; decide whether to report to those charged with governance, owners or regulators |

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the engagement letter sent by the client** **Fix:** the audit engagement letter is sent **by the auditor to the client**. (The Study Material's own Correct/Incorrect question.)
- **Accepting first, fixing the preconditions later** **Fix:** discuss the matter, and unless required by law or regulation, do not accept the engagement.
- **Agreeing to a review to rescue the client from a qualified opinion** **Fix:** a request that relates to incorrect, incomplete or unsatisfactory information has no reasonable justification. If the client then refuses to let the audit continue, withdraw where possible under law and consider reporting the circumstances.
- **"We audit them every year, so no new letter"** **Fix:** a new letter is not needed every period, but the auditor must assess whether to revise the terms or remind the entity, and the eight factors (a significant change in ownership or business, new laws, a new framework, new reporting requirements …) make it appropriate.

### Recap

- Objective: accept or continue an audit only when its basis is agreed: preconditions present, terms commonly understood.
- Preconditions: an acceptable framework, and management's acknowledgement of responsibility for preparing the financial statements, for internal control, and for giving access to information and unrestricted access to persons.
- Preconditions absent: discuss; unless required by law or regulation, do not accept. A proposed limitation leading to a disclaimer: do not accept as an audit.
- Terms recorded in an engagement letter (or other written agreement) sent by the auditor to the client: objective and scope; auditor's and management's responsibilities; framework; expected report and that it may differ.
- Law prescribing the terms: record only that the law applies and that management acknowledges its responsibilities.
- Change of terms: only with reasonable justification (changed circumstances, misunderstanding), never to escape a modified opinion; new terms in writing; if refused and the audit is stopped, withdraw where possible and consider reporting.
- Recurring audits: assess revising or reminding; eight factors, from misunderstanding to new reporting requirements.
- No trials, no fitted suit.

## Lesson 11.4: SQC 1 and SA 220: quality control

*Why audit quality needs a system: SQC 1's six elements for the whole firm, SA 220's mirror for each audit and its engagement partner, the engagement quality control review, and the 60-day and seven-year rules.*

### Key idea

SQC 1 applies to the **entire firm** and to **all** engagements; SA 220 applies to a **particular audit** and is premised on the basis that the firm is subject to SQC 1. Both rest on six elements, and both aim at reasonable assurance that the work complies with professional standards and regulatory and legal requirements and that the reports issued are appropriate in the circumstances.

### Formats and formulas

### Two clocks that start on the date of the auditor's report

*Reproduce this from memory: both limits run from the date of the auditor's report, not from the year-end or the end of fieldwork.*

| Rule (SQC 1) | Limit | Report dated 22 May 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Assembly of the final audit file | Not more than 60 days after the report date | By 21 July 2026 |
| Retention of audit documentation | Ordinarily not shorter than 7 years from the report date (or the group auditor's report, if later) | At least until 22 May 2033 |

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where SQC 1 and SA 220 appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
In the ICAI documents read for this lesson, SQC 1 or SA 220 was examined in 16 new-scheme documents:

- **Engagement quality control review, 3 to 5 marks.** The engagement partner's duties for an EQCR: May 2024 Q6(a) (4 marks). Direction and review, plus EQCR duties, with management pressing for an early report and a disagreement with the reviewer: January 2026 Q1(a) (5 marks). Why the review is needed and when it is mandatory: MTP May 2024 Series I (3 marks).
- **SQC 1 policies.** Independence mechanism, whether EQCR is mandatory for all engagements, annual written confirmation from an articled clerk: January 2026 Q4(c) (2 + 1 + 1 marks). The six elements and why they are communicated: MTP January 2026 Series I (5 marks). Leadership responsibilities: MTP September 2024 Series I Q3(c) (4 marks). Integrity of a prospective client: September 2025 Q4(b) (5 marks), RTP September 2024 Q20, MTP May 2025 Series I (5 marks), MTP September 2026 Series II (4 marks, OR). Assembly after three months and retention for four years: May 2024 Q1(d) (4 marks); assembly months after an August report: May 2025 Q1(c)(ii).

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
The answers list the Study Material's points in its order, in its words ("ultimate responsibility", "sufficient and appropriate experience, ability and the necessary authority", "at least annually … written confirmation", "mandatory for all audits of financial statements of listed entities", "not date the auditor's report until the completion of the engagement quality control review", "without fear of reprisals"), then apply them to the facts with a one-line conclusion.

**[trick] Like Every Auditor, Have Every Measure**
**L**eadership, **E**thical requirements, **A**cceptance and continuance, **H**uman resources (SA 220: assignment of engagement teams), **E**ngagement performance, **M**onitoring: the six elements in order. Condition: in SA 220 the fourth element is "assignment of engagement teams", and each element's wording differs slightly between the two pronouncements.

### Answer frame

### A case on SQC 1 or SA 220

1. **Provision**: "As per SQC 1 …" or "As per SA 220 …", only the element the facts touch, in its words.
2. **Facts**: "In the given case, …" with the dates, the client's status (listed or not) and who did what.
3. **Analysis**: compliant or not, and why (the 41 days, the listed status, the missing authority).
4. **Conclusion and action**: "Hence, the firm has not complied …; it should …".

### "Distinguish between SQC 1 and SA 220"

| Point | SQC 1 | SA 220 |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | All engagements: audits, reviews, other assurance and related services | Audit engagements only |
| Level | The entire firm | A particular audit engagement |
| Responsibility | Firm; CEO or managing partners assume ultimate responsibility | Engagement partner |
| Basis | The firm's system of quality control | Premised on the basis that the firm is subject to SQC 1 |
| Fourth element | Human resources | Assignment of engagement teams |

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the firm-wide SA 220** **Fix:** the reverse. SQC 1 applies to the entire firm and all engagements; SA 220 to a particular audit, and it is premised on SQC 1.
- **The fee-sized review** **Fix:** EQCR is mandatory for all audits of listed entities, whatever the fee; the firm devises criteria only for other engagements.
- **Clocks started at the year-end** **Fix:** assembly within 60 days after the date of the auditor's report; retention ordinarily not shorter than seven years from the date of the auditor's report (or the group auditor's report, if later).
- **The reviewer as the new signatory** **Fix:** the review does not reduce the responsibilities of the engagement partner; he still may not date the report until the review is complete.

### Recap

- SQC 1: the entire firm, all engagements; SA 220: a particular audit, the engagement partner, premised on SQC 1.
- SQC 1 objective: reasonable assurance that the firm and its personnel comply with professional standards and regulatory and legal requirements, and that reports are appropriate.
- Six elements: leadership, ethical requirements, acceptance and continuance, human resources (SA 220: assignment of engagement teams), engagement performance, monitoring.
- Leadership: CEO or managing partners hold ultimate responsibility; operational people need experience, ability and authority. Independence: communicate, identify and evaluate threats, notify breaches, written confirmation at least annually.
- Acceptance: integrity of the client, competence (capabilities, time, resources), ethical requirements; seven integrity matters, including money laundering indications and why the previous firm left.
- EQCR: mandatory for audits of listed entities; does not reduce the engagement partner's responsibilities; the report is not dated until it is complete.
- Files assembled within 60 days after the report date; retained at least seven years from it (or the group auditor's report, if later); the property of the firm.
- SA 220 engagement partner: overall quality, raising concerns without reprisals, competent team with experts, direction, supervision and review, monitoring results, four documentation matters. (Kesar now calls his café inspectors "EQC reviewers".)
