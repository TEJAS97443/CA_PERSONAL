## Lesson 8.1: SA 700: forming an opinion and the auditor's report

*How the auditor concludes that the financial statements are true and fair, and the fourteen elements of the written report, heading by heading, in ICAI's order.*

### Key idea

An unmodified opinion is earned, not assumed: reasonable assurance from sufficient appropriate evidence, immaterial uncorrected misstatements and an evaluation of the statements as a whole. The report says so under SA 700's headings, opinion first, dated no earlier than the evidence is complete.

### Formats and formulas

### Opinion and Basis for Opinion, ready to draft

*Reproduce from memory (ICAI's Illustrations 3 and 4, SM 8.12–8.13, 8.26–8.27).*

**Opinion.** We have audited the standalone financial statements of Tapri Tea Co. Ltd ("the Company"), which comprise the Balance Sheet as at 31st March 2026, the Statement of Profit and Loss, the Statement of Changes in Equity and the Statement of Cash Flows for the year then ended, and notes to the financial statements, including a summary of significant accounting policies and other explanatory information. In our opinion and to the best of our information and according to the explanations given to us, the aforesaid standalone financial statements give the information required by the Act in the manner so required and give a true and fair view in conformity with the accounting principles generally accepted in India, of the state of affairs of the Company as at 31st March 2026, and profit, changes in equity and its cash flows for the year ended on that date.

**Basis for Opinion.** We conducted our audit in accordance with the Standards on Auditing (SAs) specified under section 143(10) of the Companies Act, 2013. Our responsibilities under those Standards are further described in the Auditor's Responsibilities for the Audit of the Financial Statements section of our report. We are independent of the Company in accordance with the Code of Ethics issued by the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India together with the ethical requirements that are relevant to our audit of the financial statements under the provisions of the Companies Act, 2013 and the Rules thereunder, and we have fulfilled our other ethical responsibilities in accordance with these requirements and the Code of Ethics. We believe that the audit evidence we have obtained is sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for our opinion.

ICAI's "according to the explanations given to us" is not a "subject to": the opinion stays unconditional.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where SA 700 appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
SA 700 appeared in 9 of the new-scheme documents read:

- **Descriptive, 3 to 5 marks.** Basis for Opinion: MTP January 2026 Series I Q4(c) (4 marks); May 2026 Q3(b) (1 + 4). Factors in the reasonable assurance conclusion: MTP May 2024 Series II Q4(b) (4). Specific evaluations and fair presentation: MTP May 2026 Series I Q5(a) (5). Management's responsibilities: MTP May 2025 Series I Q4(a) (5). Consistency, flexibility and the objectives: MTP September 2026 Series I Q5(a) (5). Opinion phrases: September 2024 Q4(c) (3).
- **UDIN**: RTP January 2025 Q17; September 2024 MCQ 15 (ICAI's key: UDIN for the export sales certificate, not for balance confirmation letters).
- **Via SA 705**: elements dropped in a disclaimer, January 2025 Q3(c) (4).

Old-scheme RTPs and MTPs asked for the lack-of-neutrality indicators, "with examples".

**[trick] Fourteen elements in six moves**
"Name it, judge it, flag it, split the blame, add the law, sign it." Title, addressee; opinion, basis; going concern, key audit matters, other information; management's and auditor's responsibilities and where the latter sit; other reporting responsibilities; signature, place, date. Condition: the order for an audit under SAs (SM 8.9); a layout prescribed by law may differ.

### Answer frame

### "Explain what this section contains"

Heading in quotation marks, its position ("directly following the Opinion section"), its points in ICAI's order and words, then why it matters ("provides important context about the auditor's opinion"). A draft-report case: provision, facts, analysis, the corrected element.

### "Distinguish between a fair presentation framework and a compliance framework"

| Point | Fair presentation framework | Compliance framework |
|---|---|---|
| Compliance with the framework | Required | Required |
| Disclosures beyond the framework | Acknowledged as possibly necessary | No such acknowledgement |
| Departure from a requirement | Acknowledged explicitly, extremely rare | No such acknowledgement |
| Auditor's extra evaluation | Whether fair presentation is achieved | Not required |

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the clean opinion "subject to" Kesar's explanations** **Fix:** "give a true and fair view" or "present fairly, in all material respects"; "subject to" suggests a conditional opinion (SM 8.11).
- **"The opinion is clean, so no Basis for Opinion"** **Fix:** the Basis for Opinion section is in every report under SA 700, modified or not (SM 8.12, 8.100).
- **Dating the report on the last day of fieldwork** **Fix:** no earlier than sufficient appropriate evidence, including that those with the recognised authority have taken responsibility for the statements (SM 8.22).

### Recap

- Objectives: form an opinion from an evaluation of the conclusions drawn from the evidence; express it clearly in a written report.
- Reasonable assurance: sufficient appropriate evidence; uncorrected misstatements not material; the evaluations, including bias.
- Lack of neutrality: selective correction of misstatements; possible bias in estimates.
- Specific evaluations: policies disclosed and appropriate, estimates reasonable, information relevant, reliable, comparable and understandable, disclosures adequate, terminology appropriate.
- Unmodified: "true and fair view" or "present fairly, in all material respects"; never "subject to" or "with the foregoing explanation".
- Fourteen elements, title to date; Basis for Opinion follows the Opinion in every report.
- Dated no earlier than complete evidence; partner's signature, membership number, FRN, UDIN. (No certificate before the check-up.)

## Lesson 8.2: SA 701: key audit matters

*Which matters a listed company's auditor describes in the report, how they are filtered from matters discussed with those charged with governance, and what they never replace.*

### Key idea

Key audit matters are chosen from the matters communicated with those charged with governance: first those that required significant auditor attention, then those of most significance in the current period's audit. They add transparency; they are never a substitute for disclosures, a modified opinion or going concern reporting, and never a separate opinion.

### Formats and formulas

### The Key Audit Matters section

*Reproduce from memory (SM 8.27, 8.53–8.54).*

**Key Audit Matters**

Key audit matters are those matters that, in our professional judgment, were of most significance in our audit of the financial statements of the current period. These matters were addressed in the context of our audit of the financial statements as a whole, and in forming our opinion thereon, and we do not provide a separate opinion on these matters.

[Description of each key audit matter in accordance with SA 701.]

Each key audit matter goes under an appropriate subheading of its own (SM 8.53).

**When there are none** (SM 8.54):

**Key Audit Matters**

[Except for the matter described in the Basis for Qualified (Adverse) Opinion section or Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern section,] We have determined that there are no [other] key audit matters to communicate in our report.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where SA 701 appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
In the new-scheme documents read for this lesson, key audit matters were examined in 7 documents:

- **Descriptive.** Definition and how key audit matters are determined: May 2025 Q3(b) (5 marks). Purpose (2 marks) and "not a substitute" (3 marks): September 2025 Q2(b). "Not a substitute": MTP May 2025 Series II Q6(c) (4 marks). Factors in determining, and how they are communicated: RTP May 2025 Q16. Purpose: RTP January 2026 Q17.
- **Multiple choice, 2 marks**, as the tempting wrong option: an adequately disclosed going concern uncertainty goes in the "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" section, not under Key Audit Matters (January 2025 MCQ 15); management refusing a going concern assessment leads to a qualified opinion or disclaimer, not a KAM-only mention (MTP January 2026 Series I MCQ 15).

Old-scheme papers asked the same few things: determining KAMs (November 2020 Q5(c), 3 marks; MTP December 2021 Series I Q6(d), 3 marks); "not a substitute" (MTP May 2022 Series II Q5(d), 3 marks; RTPs November 2019, November 2023); correct-or-incorrect on "a KAM substitutes a modified opinion" (January 2021) and "a KAM is a separate opinion" (May 2023), both incorrect; a disclaimer and the KAM section (November 2023 Q2(a), 4 marks); and a case MCQ where a draft describing revenue cut-off risk and the procedures that addressed it belonged under "Key Audit Matters" (MTP November 2022 Series II, MCQ 1.5).

**[trick] Three reasons a matter needs attention: "Risk, Judgment, Event"**
**R**isk (higher assessed or significant risks), **J**udgement (significant judgments on significant management judgment, including high-uncertainty estimates), **E**vent (significant events or transactions of the period). Condition: these decide cut 1 only; cut 2, "of most significance in the current period", is still the auditor's professional judgment.

**[trick] Four things a KAM is not: "D-M-G-S"**
**D**isclosure substitute, **M**odified-opinion substitute, **G**oing concern (SA 570) substitute, **S**eparate opinion. Write them in this order: it is ICAI's order (SM 8.54).

### Answer frame

### "Explain how the auditor determines key audit matters"

1. The definition, in ICAI's words, with "selected from matters communicated with those charged with governance".
2. Cut 1: "required significant auditor attention", with the three considerations (a), (b), (c).
3. Cut 2: "of most significance in the audit of the financial statements of the current period".
4. For "how communicated": separate section, heading "Key Audit Matters", a subheading for each, the two introductory statements.

### "Distinguish between a key audit matter and an Emphasis of Matter paragraph"

| Point | Key audit matter (SA 701) | Emphasis of Matter (SA 706) |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A matter of most significance in the audit of the current period | A matter presented or disclosed in the statements, fundamental to users' understanding |
| Where it comes from | Matters communicated with those charged with governance | The statements' presentation or disclosures |
| When included | Listed entities; or law, or the auditor decides | When the auditor considers it necessary, no modification is required for it, and it is not a key audit matter |
| Effect on opinion | None; no separate opinion | Opinion not modified in respect of it |

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the KAM that tried to be a qualification** **Fix:** a KAM is not a substitute for a modified opinion required by SA 705. Qualify, describe the matter in the Basis for Qualified Opinion section, and let the KAM section except it ("Except for the matter described in the Basis for Qualified Opinion section…") (SM 8.54).
- **A Key Audit Matters section in a disclaimer** **Fix:** when the auditor disclaims an opinion, the report shall not include a Key Audit Matters section, unless law or regulation requires it (SM 8.47).
- **Stopping at the first cut** **Fix:** those are only the matters that required significant attention; KAMs are the ones **of most significance** in the current period's audit (SM 8.53).
- **"KAMs are only for listed companies"** **Fix:** listed entities must; law or regulation may require them for others; and the auditor may decide to communicate them, for example for banks, insurance companies, pension funds or charitable institutions (SM 8.14).

### Recap

- Key audit matters: matters that, in the auditor's professional judgment, were of most significance in the audit of the current period, selected from matters communicated with those charged with governance.
- Purpose: enhance the communicative value of the report through greater transparency about the audit performed.
- Objectives: determine key audit matters and, having formed an opinion, communicate them by describing them in the report.
- Applies to listed entities; also where law requires or the auditor decides; no KAM section with a disclaimer unless law requires.
- Cut 1, significant attention: higher or significant risks; significant judgments and high-uncertainty estimates; significant events or transactions. Cut 2: of most significance.
- Separate "Key Audit Matters" section, a subheading for each, two introductory statements; no separate opinion.
- Not a substitute for disclosures, a modified opinion or SA 570 reporting; not a separate opinion. Tell those charged with governance the KAMs, or that there are none. (Mentioning the storm never makes a wrong-city landing safe.)

## Lesson 8.3: SA 705: qualified, adverse and disclaimer of opinion

*When the auditor must modify the opinion, how two questions (misstatement or missing evidence; pervasive or not) pick qualified, adverse or disclaimer, and how each changes the report.*

### Key idea

Two things decide the modified opinion: the nature of the matter (misstatement, or no evidence) and whether its effects are pervasive. A disclaimer is not "the worst accounts": it is the opinion for pervasive missing evidence.

### Formats and formulas

### The modified report, section by section

*Reproduce this table from memory: each heading and each change carries marks.*

| Element | Qualified | Adverse | Disclaimer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion heading | Qualified Opinion | Adverse Opinion | Disclaimer of Opinion |
| Basis heading | Basis for Qualified Opinion | Basis for Adverse Opinion | Basis for Disclaimer of Opinion |
| Statement that the evidence is sufficient and appropriate | Kept, "qualified" added | Kept, "adverse" added | Left out |
| Reference to where the auditor's responsibilities are described | Kept | Kept | Left out |
| Auditor's responsibilities section | As in SA 700 | As in SA 700 | Three statements only |
| Key Audit Matters section | Where SA 701 applies | Where SA 701 applies | None, unless law requires |

**Opinion wording** (fair presentation framework):

- **Qualified**: ==except for the effects of the matter(s) described in the Basis for Qualified Opinion section==, the financial statements give a true and fair view; for missing evidence, "except for the **possible** effects".
- **Adverse**: because of the significance of the matter(s) described in the Basis for Adverse Opinion section, the financial statements do not give a true and fair view.
- **Disclaimer**: the auditor (a) does not express an opinion; (b) because of the significance of the matter(s) described in the Basis for Disclaimer of Opinion section, has not been able to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence to provide a basis for an audit opinion; and (c) **was engaged to audit** the financial statements (amending SA 700's "have audited").

**The Basis section must contain** (SM 8.45–8.46):

- Misstated amounts: a **description and quantification** of the financial effects, unless impracticable (and if so, a statement saying so).
- Misstated narrative disclosures: how they are misstated.
- A required disclosure omitted: discuss it with those charged with governance; describe the omitted information; unless law prohibits it, include the disclosure where practicable and supported by sufficient appropriate audit evidence.
- Missing evidence: the **reasons** for the inability.
- Even with an adverse opinion or a disclaimer: any other matters that would have required a modification, and their effects.

**A disclaimer's responsibilities section** keeps only (a) the responsibility to conduct an audit in accordance with Standards on Auditing and issue a report; (b) that, because of the matter(s) described, sufficient appropriate audit evidence for an opinion could not be obtained; (c) the independence and ethics statement (SM 8.46).

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where SA 705 appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
In the ICAI documents read for this lesson, SA 705 was examined 13 times, once in each of 13 documents.

- **Descriptive, 4 or 5 marks.** When each opinion is expressed: MTP January 2025 Series I Q2(b) (5 marks), RTP September 2025 Q17. Circumstances and objective: MTP January 2026 Series II Q4(c) (4 marks), MTP May 2026 Series II Q5(a) (5 marks). Factors deciding the type: MTP September 2026 Series II Q5(c) (4 marks). Management-imposed limitation: MTP January 2026 Series I Q6(a) (5 marks), RTP May 2024 Q19 (a case). A disclaimer case on the Basis section and the SA 700 elements left out: January 2025 Q3(c) (4 marks).
- **Multiple choice, 2 marks each**, usually through another Standard: unreliable or refused written representations (September 2024 MCQ 12; May 2026 MCQ 4, where the wrong action is to treat it as a disagreement and qualify; September 2026 MCQ 6, answers not yet published); an inadequately disclosed going concern uncertainty (January 2026 MCQ 11); an uncounted inventory location, alternative procedures first (MTP September 2025 Series II MCQ 5).

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
ICAI's suggested answers quote the provision in the Standard's words, apply it to the facts and end by naming the opinion. The phrases carry marks: "sufficient appropriate audit evidence", "material but not pervasive", "possible effects on the financial statements of undetected misstatements", "inadequate to communicate the gravity of the situation", "those charged with governance".

**[trick] Two questions, three opinions**
"Evidence or no evidence?", then "Pervasive or not?". Evidence and pervasive: **adverse**. No evidence and pervasive: **disclaimer**. Not pervasive: **qualified**. Condition: the matter must be material; an immaterial one needs no modification.

### Answer frame

### A case question: provision, facts, analysis, conclusion

1. **Relevant provision**: "As per SA 705, …" in the Standard's words, only the parts the case needs.
2. **Facts of the case**: "In the given case, …" the facts that decide it, with their figures.
3. **Analysis**: misstatement or missing evidence? Material? Pervasive, and why?
4. **Conclusion**: "Hence, the auditor should express a … opinion", then whatever else is asked (headings, wording, the Basis section, communication).

### "Distinguish between a qualified and an adverse opinion"

| Point | Qualified opinion | Adverse opinion |
|---|---|---|
| When | Misstatements material, not pervasive; or evidence missing and possible effects material, not pervasive | Misstatements both material and pervasive |
| Evidence | Obtained, or not obtained | Obtained |
| Wording | "Except for the effects (possible effects) … give a true and fair view" | "Because of the significance of … do not give a true and fair view" |
| Headings | Qualified Opinion; Basis for Qualified Opinion | Adverse Opinion; Basis for Adverse Opinion |

### Three answers to the samosa-factory case

**[cols] **Loses marks****

"Disclaimer of opinion, because Kesar did not co-operate."

Right conclusion; no provision, no reasoning, no steps, no report changes.
---
**Passes**

"As per SA 705, when sufficient appropriate audit evidence cannot be obtained and the possible effects could be material and pervasive, the auditor disclaims. The factory and the cold-chain division are 61% of assets, so: disclaimer."

Provision and facts, but no limitation steps and no report changes.
---
**Full marks**

The steps in order (request, those charged with governance, alternative procedures, withdraw or disclaim), the facts applied to "material and pervasive", the conclusion, then each change to the report.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the disclaimer for accounts that are merely terrible** **Fix:** a misstatement with evidence, material and pervasive, is **adverse**. A disclaimer is for evidence that could not be obtained.
- **The "except for" that excepted everything** **Fix:** "except for" works only if the rest can still be relied on; pervasive effects need an adverse opinion or a disclaimer.
- **Hiding the problem in a key audit matter or an Emphasis of Matter paragraph** **Fix:** neither replaces a modified opinion the circumstances require (SM 8.50, 8.54).
- **Jumping straight to the disclaimer** **Fix:** write SA 705's steps first (request removal, those charged with governance, alternative procedures, withdrawal); they carry most of the marks.

### Recap

- Modify when the financial statements as a whole are not free from material misstatement, or when sufficient appropriate audit evidence cannot be obtained to conclude that they are.
- Objective: to express clearly an appropriately modified opinion.
- Material, not pervasive: **qualified** ("except for the effects", or "possible effects" when evidence is missing).
- Misstatement, material and pervasive: **adverse**. Missing evidence, possible effects material and pervasive: **disclaimer** (also multiple uncertainties, extremely rarely).
- Pervasive: not confined to specific items; if confined, a substantial proportion; or disclosures fundamental to users.
- Limitation after acceptance: request removal, tell those charged with governance, try alternatives; then qualify, or withdraw if practicable, else disclaim.
- Disclaimer: "engaged to audit"; reasons in the Basis section; no sufficiency statement, no responsibilities reference, no key audit matters unless law requires.
- Tell those charged with governance the circumstances and wording first. (The mechanic kept out of the bonnet never calls the engine bad.)

## Lesson 8.4: SA 706: emphasis of matter and other matter paragraphs

*How an auditor who has already formed the opinion points users to a matter inside the financial statements (Emphasis of Matter) or outside them (Other Matter), and why neither changes the opinion.*

### Key idea

Emphasis of Matter: "look at this, it is already in the financial statements, and it is fundamental". Other Matter: "here is something outside the financial statements about the audit, our responsibilities or this report". Neither modifies the opinion, and an Emphasis of Matter is never a substitute for a modified opinion, a missing disclosure or the going concern section.

### Formats and formulas

### The two drafts, word for word

*Reproduce these from memory: the heading, the reference to the note and the last sentence each carry marks.*

**Emphasis of Matter** (ICAI's draft in the Study Material, SM 8.50):

> **Emphasis of Matter – [what the matter is]**
>
> We draw attention to Note [X] of the financial statements, which describes [the matter]. Our opinion is not modified in respect of this matter.

**Other Matter** (ICAI's illustrative company report, for branches audited by branch auditors, SM 8.30):

> **Other Matter**
>
> We did not audit the financial statements/information of [number] branches included in the standalone financial statements of the Company whose financial statements/financial information reflect total assets of ₹[…] as at 31st March 20XX and the total revenue of ₹[…] for the year ended on that date, as considered in the standalone financial statements/information of these branches have been audited by the branch auditors whose reports have been furnished to us, and our opinion in so far as it relates to the amounts and disclosures included in respect of branches, is based solely on the report of such branch auditors.
>
> Our opinion is not modified in respect of these matters.

The Other Matter text is as printed in the Study Material. In ICAI's illustration it sits after the Auditor's Responsibilities section and before "Report on Other Legal and Regulatory Requirements" (SM 8.30–8.31); the branch side of it is in [[aud.report.branch|branch audit and joint audit]].

### Emphasis of Matter or Other Matter: the side-by-side

| Point | Emphasis of Matter | Other Matter |
|---|---|---|
| Refers to | A matter presented or disclosed in the financial statements | A matter other than those presented or disclosed |
| Test | Fundamental to users' understanding of the financial statements | Relevant to users' understanding of the audit, the auditor's responsibilities or the report |
| Provided | No SA 705 modification is required for it; not a key audit matter | Not prohibited by law or regulation; not a key audit matter |
| Heading | Includes the term "Emphasis of Matter" | "Other Matter", or other appropriate heading |
| Content | Clear reference to the matter and to the note; only information in the financial statements; opinion not modified | The matter, in a separate section |

("Not a key audit matter" applies when SA 701 applies.)

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where SA 706 appeared in the documents read**
New scheme, May 2024 to September 2026:

- **Identify and explain.** January 2026 Q3(a): a fire after the year-end, fully disclosed, with an unmodified opinion; identify the paragraph (1 mark), explain its meaning and other circumstances (4 marks). September 2024 MTP Series II Q2(b) (5 marks): litigation with a regulator in the High Court; the considerations when an Emphasis of Matter is included, and two examples.
- **The four examples alone.** MTP January 2026 Series II Q6(a) (5 marks); RTP May 2024 Q18.
- **A case on pending litigation.** January 2025 Q4(c) (4 marks): ICAI's answer combined SA 501 (communication with legal counsel), SA 706 and the section 143(3) statement on pending litigations, and printed an "Alternative Solution" built on SA 706's three requirements alone.
- **Other Matter through SA 710.** May 2026 Q6(b) (2 + 3 marks): a predecessor's report referred to; name the paragraph and list its contents.
- **The tempting wrong option in MCQs.** September 2024 MCQ 1 (inventory count impossible: modify under SA 705, not a representation plus an Emphasis of Matter), MCQ 6 (a dissenting joint auditor issues a separate report, not "a view in the Emphasis of Matter paragraph") and MCQ 12 (unreliable written representations: disclaimer, not an unmodified opinion with a paragraph).

Old scheme: an Emphasis of Matter draft for properties received in barter deals on possession letters (May 2022 Q6(a), 4 marks, with an alternative answer under CARO); a draft describing revenue cut-off risk and the procedures that addressed it, which belonged under Key Audit Matters, not Emphasis of Matter or Other Matter (MTP November 2022 Series II MCQ 1.5); the four examples (MTP December 2021 Series II Q5(d), 3 marks; MTP November 2023 Series I Q4(a), 4 marks); correct-or-incorrect on "an Emphasis of Matter affects the opinion" (RTP November 2023, incorrect).

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
ICAI's suggested answers name the paragraph first, then give its definition, the three requirements (separate section with "Emphasis of Matter" in the heading; clear reference to the matter and the note, only information in the financial statements; opinion not modified) and, where asked, the four examples in ICAI's order. A drafted paragraph in ICAI's format ("We draw attention to Note … Our opinion is not modified in respect of this matter") is how ICAI itself answered the fire and barter cases.

**[trick] Four examples: "Litigation, Later event, Early standard, Earthquake"**
**L**itigation or regulatory action (exceptional, uncertain outcome); **L**ater event (significant, between the date of the financial statements and the report date); **E**arly application of a new accounting standard (where permitted, material effect); **E**arthquake, standing for any major catastrophe with a significant effect on the financial position. Condition: these are examples of when the auditor *may* emphasise; the matter must still be disclosed in the statements, need no modification, and not be a key audit matter.

### Answer frame

### A case question: provision, facts, analysis, conclusion

1. **Relevant provision**: "As per SA 706 (Revised), …" the definition or the "provided" conditions, whichever the case turns on.
2. **Facts of the case**: what is disclosed, where (which note), and whether any modification is needed.
3. **Analysis**: inside the statements (Emphasis of Matter) or outside (Other Matter)? A key audit matter? A modification, a missing disclosure or going concern in disguise?
4. **Conclusion**: name the paragraph; draft it in ICAI's format; add the communication with those charged with governance.

### "Distinguish between a key audit matter and an Emphasis of Matter"

Use the table in [[aud.report.sa701|SA 701]]; add one line that carries marks: a matter determined to be a key audit matter is not also given an Emphasis of Matter paragraph (SM 8.49).

### Three answers to "Define an Emphasis of Matter paragraph and state how it is presented"

**[cols] **Loses marks****

"A paragraph to highlight important things in the report."

No definition, no "fundamental", no requirements.
---
**Passes**

The definition in ICAI's words, and "a separate section, heading Emphasis of Matter".

Two of the three requirements missing.
---
**Full marks**

The definition; the three requirements in order (separate section and heading; clear reference, only information in the statements; opinion not modified); the two provisos if the question is a case.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the Emphasis of Matter that tried to be a qualification** **Fix:** an Emphasis of Matter is included only if no modification is required, and it is not a substitute for one (SM 8.49–8.50). Modify under SA 705.
- **The paragraph that told users something the notes do not** **Fix:** the Emphasis of Matter paragraph shall refer only to information presented or disclosed in the financial statements (SM 8.49).
- **Going concern dressed up as Emphasis of Matter** **Fix:** an adequately disclosed material uncertainty goes in the separate "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" section; an Emphasis of Matter is not a substitute for SA 570 reporting (SM 7.22, 8.50).
- **Mixing up the two** **Fix:** ask one question: is the matter presented or disclosed in the financial statements? Yes: Emphasis of Matter. No: Other Matter.

### Recap

- Emphasis of Matter: refers to a matter **appropriately presented or disclosed** in the financial statements that is **fundamental to users' understanding**.
- Included provided no SA 705 modification is required for it and, when SA 701 applies, it is not a key audit matter.
- Written in a separate section headed with "Emphasis of Matter"; clear reference to the matter and the note; only information in the statements; "our opinion is not modified".
- Examples: exceptional litigation or regulatory action; significant event after the date of the financial statements and before the report; early application of a new standard; major catastrophe.
- Not a substitute for a modified opinion, required disclosures, or SA 570's going concern section.
- Other Matter: a matter **not** presented or disclosed, relevant to understanding the audit, the auditor's responsibilities or the report; provided law does not prohibit it and it is not a key audit matter; heading "Other Matter".
- Tell those charged with governance the expectation and the wording first. (The bowler stays fit to play.)

## Lesson 8.5: SA 710: comparative information

*What the auditor checks in last year's column, and when the report must mention it: corresponding figures (opinion on this year only) versus comparative financial statements (opinion on every year shown).*

### Key idea

Corresponding figures: the opinion refers to the current period only, and mentions the corresponding figures only in the exceptions SA 710 lists. Comparative financial statements: the opinion refers to each period presented. Either way, the auditor audits the comparison.

### Formats and formulas

### When the report mentions last year: the master table

*Reproduce this table from memory: the situation, the reporting and the paragraph carry the marks.*

| Situation | Corresponding figures | Comparative financial statements |
|---|---|---|
| Normal case | Opinion silent on them | Opinion on each period |
| Prior opinion modified, matter unresolved | Modify current opinion; refer to both, or to comparability | No separate rule in the Study Material (see Under the hood) |
| Prior misstatement, prior opinion unmodified | Not dealt with: qualified or adverse, on corresponding figures | (Predecessor's report) communicate; predecessor informed; amended and reissued: report on current period only |
| Prior period not audited | Other Matter: corresponding figures unaudited | Other Matter: comparative statements unaudited |
| Predecessor audited prior period | Other Matter, if law permits and the auditor decides to refer | Other Matter, unless the predecessor's report is revised |
| Auditor's new opinion on prior period differs | Not applicable | Other Matter: substantive reasons |

Both Other Matter paragraphs about a predecessor contain the same three things, in this order: **(a)** the prior period was audited by a predecessor auditor; **(b)** the type of opinion expressed and, if modified, the reasons; **(c)** the date of that report.

### The Basis for Modification: two ways to write it

| Effect on current figures | Basis for Modification paragraph |
|---|---|
| Material | Refers to both the current and the corresponding figures |
| Other cases | Explains the modification is due to the effect on comparability |

### Answer frame

### A case question: provision, facts, analysis, conclusion

1. **Relevant provision**: "As per SA 710, …" name the approach, then quote the circumstance the case turns on.
2. **Facts of the case**: which approach the entity uses; last year's opinion; who audited last year; what has been found.
3. **Analysis**: is the matter unresolved? Is the effect on the current period's figures material? Which paragraph?
4. **Conclusion**: the opinion, and the content of the Basis for Modification or Other Matter paragraph.

### "Distinguish between corresponding figures and comparative financial statements"

| Point | Corresponding figures | Comparative financial statements |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Integral part of the current period statements | Included for comparison with the current period statements |
| Read | Only in relation to the current period figures | As a set comparable with the current period |
| Level of detail | Dictated by relevance to the current period figures | Comparable with the current period statements |
| Opinion refers to | Current period only | Each period presented and audited |
| Prior period unaudited | Other Matter: corresponding figures unaudited | Other Matter: comparative statements unaudited |

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: "last year's problem is last year's auditor's problem"** **Fix:** an unresolved prior modification requires the current opinion to be modified, with the Basis paragraph referring to both periods or to comparability (SM 8.60).
- **The Emphasis of Matter for unaudited comparatives** **Fix:** that the prior period was not audited is not disclosed in the statements; it is about the audit. The paragraph is an **Other Matter** paragraph (SM 8.60, 8.62).
- **"Unaudited" as an excuse** **Fix:** the statement does not relieve the auditor of obtaining sufficient appropriate audit evidence on opening balances (SM 8.60).
- **One opinion for two years** **Fix:** the opinion refers to each period presented and audited (SM 8.61).

### Recap

- Comparative information: prior period amounts and disclosures included under the applicable framework. Approach: set by law or regulation, or by the engagement terms.
- Corresponding figures: opinion on the **current period only**. Comparative financial statements: opinion on **each period** presented.
- Procedures: required and classified; agrees with prior period; policies consistent or changes accounted for and disclosed; possible misstatement: extra procedures (and SA 560 if we audited it); representations for all periods, plus prior period items.
- Corresponding figures exceptions: prior modification unresolved (refer to both, or to comparability); prior misstatement not dealt with (qualified or adverse, on the corresponding figures); unaudited (Other Matter); predecessor referred to (Other Matter).
- Predecessor Other Matter contents: predecessor audited; type of opinion (reasons if modified); date of the report.
- Comparative statements: a different opinion on the prior period: Other Matter with the substantive reasons; predecessor's missed misstatement: tell management and TCWG, ask that the predecessor be told; amended and reissued: report on the current period only.
- "Unaudited" never relieves the opening-balance work. (Today's commentary still checks last match's score.)

A company's auditor inquires into some matters and speaks only if the answer is adverse; states others every year, with reasons for every negative or qualified answer; and reports frauds by officers or employees outside the report, to the Central Government or to the audit committee or board depending on the amount.

- **Hall of Shame: the six "we are satisfied" sentences** **Fix:** inquire into all six; report only the adverse ones, as special comments. Saying "satisfied" is not required.
- **The two small frauds that became one big one** **Fix:** the ₹1 crore test is applied individually, to each offence; and an amount the fraud is *expected* to reach counts.
- **Putting CARO clauses in section 143(3)** **Fix:** those are CARO 2020 clauses (xiv) and (vii). Rule 11 has six matters only: litigations, foreseeable losses, IEPF, intermediaries, dividend, audit trail.
- **Reporting the internal financial controls of every company** **Fix:** section 143(3)(i) does not apply to the exempt private companies (One Person Company, small company, or turnover below ₹50 crore with borrowings below ₹25 crore).

### Recap

- Section 143(1): inquire into six matters (secured loans, book entries, securities sold below cost, loans shown as deposits, personal expenses, shares allotted for cash); report only special comments.
- Section 143(2): report to the members whether the financial statements give a true and fair view of the state of affairs, profit or loss and cash flow.
- Section 143(3): (a) information, (b) books and branch returns, (c) branch reports, (d) agreement with books, (e) accounting standards, (f) adverse effects, (g) directors under 164(2), (h) remarks on accounts, (i) IFC, (j) Rule 11.
- Rule 11: pending litigations, foreseeable losses, IEPF delay, intermediaries representations, dividend under section 123, audit trail.
- Section 143(4): reasons for every negative or qualified answer; 143(9): comply with auditing standards; 143(11): CARO 2020.
- Section 143(12): fraud by officers or employees, ₹1 crore or above individually (or expected), to the Central Government; less, to the audit committee or Board; also CARO clause (xi).
- In the report: a separate "Report on Other Legal and Regulatory Requirements": CARO annexure, then 143(3) (a) to (j). (The bus inspector calls the authorities, not just the newspaper.)

### A case question: provision, facts, analysis, conclusion

1. **Relevant provision**: section 143(8) and rule 12 for "who audits and to whom"; SA 600 for "what may the principal auditor do"; SA 299 (Revised) for "who is responsible" and "what about the report".
2. **Facts of the case**: where the branch is (India or outside); who was appointed and how; which areas were allotted to whom; what the firms disagree on.
3. **Analysis**: apply the provision line by line.
4. **Conclusion**: eligible or not; report to the company's auditor; responsibility alone or joint and several; one report or separate reports referring to each other.

### "Distinguish between a branch auditor and a joint auditor"

| Point | Branch auditor | Joint auditor |
|---|---|---|
| Appointed for | The accounts of a branch office | The audit of the entity as a whole, with other auditors |
| Reports to | The company's auditor | The members, in a common report |
| Responsibility | For the branch report; the company's auditor uses it under SA 600 | Allotted work alone; listed matters jointly and severally |
| Governing rule | Section 143(8), rule 12, SA 600 | Section 139(3), SA 299 (Revised) |

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the majority verdict** **Fix:** where joint auditors disagree on the opinion or any matter in the report, each expresses its opinion in a separate report, and the reports refer to each other (SM 8.68).
- **"Anyone can audit a foreign branch"** **Fix:** outside India, only the company's auditor, an accountant, or a person duly qualified under that country's laws (SM 8.63).
- **The branch report that went to the board** **Fix:** the branch auditor sends the report to the company's auditor (section 143(8), rule 12), who deals with it in his report as he considers necessary (SM 8.63).
- **Relying without checking, or checking without relying** **Fix:** the principal auditor is normally entitled to rely, but should perform procedures to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence that the work is adequate for his purposes (SM 8.64).

### Recap

- Section 128: books at the registered office include the branches'; a branch keeping proper books and sending proper summarised returns periodically is deemed compliance.
- Section 143(8): branch in India: company's auditor or a person qualified to be the company's auditor and appointed under section 139; outside India: company's auditor, an accountant, or a person duly qualified under that country's laws.
- The branch auditor reports to the company's auditor, who deals with it as he considers necessary; rule 12: fraud reporting extends to the branch auditor for the branch.
- Section 143(3)(b), (c), (d): returns from branches not visited; branch reports received and how dealt with; agreement with books and returns.
- SA 600: normally entitled to rely, but procedures to be satisfied the work is adequate; ordinarily (a) advise the use and co-ordinate, (b) advise requirements and obtain representation; discuss, review a summary or visit.
- Joint audit: pooling resources and expertise; nine advantages, six disadvantages; legal foothold in section 139(3).
- SA 299 (Revised): joint planning and strategy; joint audit plan (areas, reporting objectives, significant factors, preliminary results, resources); common engagement and representation letters; signed work allocation communicated to those charged with governance.
- Allocated work: alone. Undivided work, common decisions, agreed matters, statutes, presentation, report: jointly and severally. Disagreement: separate reports referring to each other. (Two caterers, two menu cards, each mentioning the other.)
