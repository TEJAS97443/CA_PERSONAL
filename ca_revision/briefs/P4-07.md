## Lesson 7.1: Non-integrated and integrated accounting systems

*How a separate cost ledger keeps its double entry through the Cost Ledger Control Account, the control accounts and journal entries ICAI expects, and how integrated accounts replace that account with real assets and liabilities in one set of books.*

### Key idea

In a non-integrated system the cost ledger is made self-balancing by the Cost Ledger Control Account, which stands in for every account kept only in the financial books. Integrated accounts use the real accounts instead, in one set of books, so no reconciliation is needed.

### Formats and formulas

*Reproduce the two-sided control account and the cost-ledger trial balance from memory: ICAI asks for both, and for "what each balance represents".*

**[cols] Stores Ledger Control Account (format)**
| Dr. | ₹ |
|---|---:|
| To Balance b/d | xxx |
| To Cost Ledger Control A/c (purchases) | xxx |
| To WIP Control A/c (returns from shop) | xxx |
---
| Cr. | ₹ |
|---|---:|
| By WIP Control A/c (direct issues) | xxx |
| By Production OH Control A/c (indirect issues) | xxx |
| By Cost Ledger Control A/c (returns to vendor) | xxx |
| By Balance c/d | xxx |

**Trial balance of the cost ledger**: debit balances are Stores Ledger Control, WIP Control and Finished Goods Control (plus any overhead control left open with an under-absorbed balance, and the Cost of Sales A/c if it has not been closed to Costing P&L, as in Illustration 1, SM 7.14); the credit balance is the Cost Ledger Control A/c (plus any over-absorbed balance). The Cost Ledger Control balance equals the net of all the others.

**Missing figures in incomplete control accounts** (SM 7.28–7.30, 7.51–7.53):

| To find | Use |
|---|---|
| Materials purchased | Payments to creditors + closing creditors − opening creditors |
| Cost of goods completed | Cost of goods sold + closing finished goods − opening finished goods |
| Overhead applied | Hours worked × predetermined rate (budgeted overheads ÷ budgeted hours) |
| Closing WIP | Materials + labour hours × wage rate + labour hours × overhead rate, for the unfinished jobs |
| Materials consumed | Balancing figure of the WIP Control A/c |
| Closing stores | Opening stores + purchases − materials consumed |

### Exam angle

**[exam] How ICAI asked this, May 2024 to September 2026**
In the documents read for this lesson, cost accounting systems (other than the reconciliation) appeared in 6 questions:

- **Journal entries, non-integrated**: September 2025 Q4(b), 5 marks: materials purchased on credit, materials issued, direct wages allocated, factory overheads over-absorbed, administration overheads under-absorbed. The answer showed both forms of the purchase entry (via Material Control, or straight to Stores Ledger Control).
- **Ledgers**: MTP May 2024 Series I Q3(a), 4 marks: "What are the important ledgers to be maintained under non-integrated accounting system?" (the four ledgers of SM 7.3).
- **Pre-requisites**: MTP September 2024 Series I Q6(c), 4 marks: "What are the essential pre-requisites for integrated accounts?"
- **Reconciliation under integration**: RTP May 2024 Q13(a): "Is reconciliation of cost accounts and financial accounts necessary in case of integrated accounting system?" ICAI's answer: one set of books, so no.
- **Integrated control accounts**: RTP May 2025 Q12: Amy Ltd's batch costing fully integrated with the financial accounts; prepare six accounts from Stores Ledger Control to Costing P&L, with non-productive time and a physical WIP value.
- **MCQ**: RTP September 2025 MCQ 4: the journal entry for overheads incurred (Production and Administrative OH Control Dr. / To Cost Ledger Control A/c).

**[exam] The traps**
- Under a non-integrated system nothing is credited to Bank or Creditors: the credit goes to the Cost Ledger Control A/c (SM MCQ 5: purchases are debited to Material or Stores Ledger Control).
- Materials bought for a special job go straight to WIP (job) A/c, and returns to vendor are a debit to the Cost Ledger Control A/c (SM MCQs 6 and 7).
- Notional costs may be included in non-integrated accounts (SM MCQ 2); the cost ledger can carry them because it is separate.
- Over-absorbed: debit the overhead control, credit Costing P&L; under-absorbed: the reverse (September 2025 Q4(b)).

**[trick] Does it cross the border?**
For each transaction ask: does it involve anything kept only in the financial books (cash, bank, debtors, creditors, sales, the final profit)? If yes, one side of the entry is the Cost Ledger Control A/c in a non-integrated system, or that real account in an integrated one. If no, both sides are cost accounts and the entry is identical in both systems. Condition: the cost ledger in question records the item at all (purely financial items such as interest are not recorded in the cost ledger).

### Answer frame

### "Distinguish between integrated and non-integrated accounting systems"

**[compare] | Point | Non-integrated system | Integrated system |**
|---|---|---|
| Books | Separate ledgers for cost and financial accounts (SM 7.2) | Cost and financial accounts in the same set of books (SM 7.19) |
| Linking account | Cost Ledger Control A/c (General Ledger Adjustment A/c) completes the double entry (SM 7.3) | No notional account; bank, debtors, creditors, provision for depreciation, fixed assets and share capital accounts used instead (SM 7.21) |
| Coverage | Only transactions relating to the product or service; excludes purely financial items and balance sheet items (SM 7.2–7.3) | Complete analysis of cost and sales; complete details of cash payments, assets and liabilities (SM 7.21) |
| Profit | Two profits; reconciliation necessary (SM 7.2) | One profit; no reconciliation (SM 7.2, 7.20) |
| Effort | Two sets of books to maintain | Less effort, less time, economical: centralisation of accounting function (SM 7.20) |

### Other questions, in ICAI's order

- **Ledgers under non-integrated accounts** (4 marks): cost ledger, stores ledger, work-in-process ledger, finished goods ledger, each with ICAI's one-line description.
- **Advantages of integrated accounts**: no need for reconciliation; less efforts; less time consuming; economical process.
- **Essential pre-requisites**: extent of integration; suitable coding system; agreed routine for accruals, prepaid expenses and adjustments; perfect coordination between cost and financial staff.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the bank account in the cost ledger** **Fix:** the cost ledger has no bank account; the credit is the Cost Ledger Control A/c. Bank appears only when the books are integrated.
- **Receipts from debtors in the cost books** **Fix:** these touch only financial accounts and are not recorded at all; in integrated accounts they are Bank Dr. / To Receivables, and Payables Dr. / To Bank.
- **Under- and over-absorption the wrong way round** **Fix:** over-absorbed means the control has a credit balance (absorbed > incurred); close it by debiting the control and crediting Costing P&L. Under-absorbed: Costing P&L Dr. / To the control.
- **Non-productive time charged to WIP** **Fix:** non-productive (idle) time of direct workers is a production overhead: debit Production OH Control, not WIP (SM 7.27); abnormal idle time goes to Costing P&L (SM 7.4).

### Recap

- Non-integrated: separate cost ledger; records only product-related transactions; excludes purely financial items and balance sheet items.
- Cost Ledger Control A/c (General Ledger Adjustment A/c): credited with all expenditure, debited with sales, receives the costing profit; its balance is the net of all impersonal account balances.
- Four ledgers: cost ledger, stores ledger, work-in-process (job) ledger, finished goods ledger.
- Over-absorbed: overhead control Dr. / To Costing P&L; under-absorbed: the reverse; Overhead Adjustment A/c may be dispensed with.
- Integrated: one set of books; bank, debtors, creditors, provision for depreciation, fixed assets, share capital replace the CLC.
- Advantages: no reconciliation, less effort, less time, economical. Pre-requisites: extent of integration, coding system, agreed routine for accruals, coordination of staff.
- Incomplete records: purchases from creditors; goods completed from finished goods; materials consumed as the WIP balancing figure. (No bank account in the embassy.)

## Lesson 7.2: Reconciling cost and financial profit

*Why the cost books and the financial books show different profits, the four causes ICAI lists, and how to reconcile them item by item in a reconciliation statement or a Memorandum Reconciliation Account.*

### Key idea

Each difference changes one profit and not the other. For every item ask: did it make the financial profit higher or lower than the costing profit? Starting from the costing profit, add what made the financial profit higher and subtract what made it lower.

### Formats and formulas

*Reproduce both layouts from memory. Use the one the question names; when it only says "reconcile", ICAI's answers use the statement (SM Illustrations 6 to 8).*

**Reconciliation statement** (SM 7.35–7.36)

| Particulars | ₹ | ₹ |
|---|---:|---:|
| **Profit as per cost accounts** | | xxx |
| Add: Overheads over-absorbed in cost accounts | xxx | |
| Add: Income in financial accounts only | xxx | |
| Add: Notional expenses charged in cost accounts | xxx | xxx |
| | | xxx |
| Less: Overheads under-absorbed in cost accounts | xxx | |
| Less: Expenses in financial accounts only | xxx | |
| Less: Over-valuation of closing stock in cost accounts | xxx | (xxx) |
| **Profit as per financial accounts** | | **xxx** |

**Memorandum Reconciliation Account**, starting from a costing profit: the costing profit and the additions are credited, the deductions debited, and the financial profit is the balancing figure on the debit side. Starting from a costing **loss**, the loss is debited and the financial result is the balancing figure on the credit side (SM 7.61):

**[cols] Memorandum Reconciliation Account (costing profit as base)**
| Dr. | ₹ |
|---|---:|
| To Deductions (under-absorption, financial-only expenses, over-valued closing stock) | xxx |
| To Net profit as per financial accounts (balancing figure) | xxx |
| **Total** | **xxx** |
---
| Cr. | ₹ |
|---|---:|
| By Net profit as per cost accounts | xxx |
| By Additions (over-absorption, financial-only income, notional costs) | xxx |
| **Total** | **xxx** |

**[formula] The identity behind every reconciliation**
$$\text{Financial profit} \qquad = \text{Cost profit} + \text{Additions} - \text{Deductions}$$
Additions are the items that make the financial profit higher than the cost profit; deductions, those that make it lower. A balancing item (one missing difference) is found by solving this identity for it (May 2025 Q4(a)).

**[exam] The traps**
- "Notional rent of own premises charged in cost accounts" is **added** to the costing profit to reach the financial profit (May 2024); starting from the financial profit it is deducted (September 2024).
- Stock "valued at ₹41,000 in cost accounts whereas market price ₹80 per unit" for 500 units (May 2025): the financial books take ₹40,000, so cost over-values by ₹1,000: deduct.
- Read the base: "taking net loss as per cost accounts as base" (May 2025), "to arrive at net profit/loss as per cost accounts" (September 2024: start from financial profit, reverse every sign).
- ICAI's MCQ: costing loss ₹5,600 with administrative overhead under-absorbed ₹600 gives a financial loss of ₹6,200 (SM 7.45).

**[trick] The "who got poorer" test**
For every item ask: which book shows the lower profit because of it? If it is the financial book (an expense only Dev records, an overhead Ritu under-charged, closing stock Ritu valued higher), the item is deducted from the cost profit. If it is the cost book (a notional charge, an over-absorption, closing stock Ritu valued lower, an income only Dev records), it is added. Conditions: the base is the profit as per cost accounts (from the financial profit, reverse it), and opening stock works the other way round: opening stock Ritu valued higher makes the cost book poorer.

### Answer frame

### Theory questions, in ICAI's order

- **Why is reconciliation necessary?** When cost and financial accounts are kept separately, they must be reconciled to make the cost accounts reliable; the financial accounts should analyse expenses as the cost accounts do, so that differences and their reasons can be located; the differences are collected in a Memorandum Reconciliation Account (SM 7.31).
- **Reasons for disagreement** (or "list the financial expenses not included in cost"): the four causes, with ICAI's sub-lists (SM 7.31–7.32).
- **Procedure and when it can be avoided** (January 2025, 3 + 2 marks): the three steps; then integrated accounts, where the same set of accounts fulfils both requirements (SM 7.33).

### A numerical answer

1. Heading: "Statement of Reconciliation of profit as per cost accounts and financial accounts" (or the account, if asked).
2. Start from the base the question names, with its sign.
3. "Add:" items with a working in brackets for every computed difference (₹19,15,000 − ₹18,40,000); subtotal.
4. "Less:" items, the same way; total in brackets.
5. End on the other profit, labelled, and check it against the figure given.

**[cols] **Loses marks****

A list of items with plus and minus signs and a final figure, no labels, no workings.

The examiner cannot see why the stock item was deducted.
---
**Passes**

The statement with Add and Less, but "Depreciation 1,60,000" and "1,45,000" both entered as separate items.

Half the depreciation logic is wrong.
---
**Full marks**

Base labelled; each item named with "(cost accounts only)", "under-absorbed", "over-valued in cost accounts"; computed differences shown; subtotals; final profit labelled and matching.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the notional rent that was deducted** **Fix:** it was charged only in the cost books, so the cost profit is the lower one; going to the financial profit, add it back.
- **Entering both depreciation figures** **Fix:** only the difference matters: the cost accounts charged ₹40,000 more, so the costing profit is ₹40,000 lower; from the financial profit, deduct ₹40,000 (from the costing profit, add it).
- **Closing stock and opening stock with the same sign** **Fix:** closing stock over-valued in cost accounts: deduct (it inflated the cost profit); opening stock over-valued in cost accounts: add (it inflated the cost of goods sold). The MTP January 2026 Series II answer debits "under valuation of opening stock in cost accounts", a deduction.
- **Losing the minus sign of a loss** **Fix:** a loss is a negative profit. With the costing profit as base, the balancing figure goes on whichever side is short: on the debit side it is a profit, on the credit side (deductions larger than the base and the additions) it is a loss. A costing loss as base sits on the debit side.

### Recap

- Reconciliation is needed when cost and financial accounts are kept separately (non-integrated); not when they are integrated.
- Procedure: profit as per financial accounts; profit as per cost accounts; reconcile the two.
- Causes, in ICAI's order: financial-only items (expenses and income); cost-only notional items; items treated differently; varying basis of stock valuation (financial: lower of cost or market; cost: at cost).
- From cost profit: add over-absorption, notional costs, financial-only income, cost over-charges, closing stock under-valued in cost; deduct the opposites.
- Opening stock works the other way round from closing stock.
- From financial profit, reverse every sign; a loss is a negative profit.
- Memorandum Reconciliation Account: base on its side, the other result as the balancing figure. (Miscellaneous expenses is not a reconciling item.)
