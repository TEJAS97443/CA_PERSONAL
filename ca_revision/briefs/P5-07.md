## Lesson 7.1: SA 560: subsequent events

*The auditor's duty for events after the balance sheet date in three windows: search until the report is signed, act on facts learnt before the accounts are issued, and act again, even to prevent reliance, after issue.*

### Key idea

Dates decide duties. Before the report date, the auditor must search for subsequent events; after it, the auditor need not search, but must act on any fact that would have changed the report.

### Formats and formulas

### Window 2 and Window 3, side by side

*Reproduce this from memory: "distinguish" questions are marked row by row.*

| Step | Window 2: before issue | Window 3: after issue |
|---|---|---|
| Duty to search | None | None |
| On learning a fact | Discuss; determine; inquire | Same three steps |
| Management amends | Procedures on amendment; new report | Same, plus review steps to inform recipients |
| New report adds | Only if restricted: additional date, or Emphasis or Other Matter paragraph | Always: Emphasis or Other Matter paragraph on the note and the earlier report |
| Management will not amend | Not provided: modify (SA 705). Provided: notify not to issue | Notify that the auditor will seek to prevent future reliance |
| Last resort | Issued anyway: act to prevent reliance | Still no steps: act to prevent reliance |

### Dating rules

- The original report is dated no earlier than the date on which the auditor has obtained sufficient appropriate audit evidence, including evidence that the statements have been prepared and that those with recognised authority have taken responsibility for them (SM 8.22).
- A new report on amended financial statements is dated **no earlier than the date of approval of the amended financial statements** (SM 7.8, 7.9).
- A restricted amendment gets an **additional date** in the report, restricted to that amendment (SM 7.8).

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where SA 560 appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
In the ICAI documents read for this lesson, SA 560 was asked 13 times in 12 new-scheme documents:

- **Procedures in Window 1**: MTP May 2026 Series II Q3(b) and MTP September 2026 Series I Q3(b) (5 marks each); RTP September 2025 Q16; RTP May 2024 Q16 (a fire before the report is signed: procedures and responsibilities).
- **Classifying the two types**: MTP January 2026 Series I Q6(c) (4 marks: an insolvent debtor and a planned merger). MCQs: September 2025 MCQ 12 (a legal claim settled for less is *not* a condition that arose after); MTP September 2024 Series II MCQ 5 (a fire after the year-end: disclose).
- **Window 2**: MTP January 2026 Series II Q6(c) (4 marks); RTP September 2025 MCQ 7 (first step: discuss and determine whether amendment is needed); September 2026 Q2(a) (5 marks: management refuses to amend, report provided or not, and the meaning of "date the financial statements are issued"; answers not yet published).
- **Window 3**: January 2026 Q5(a) (5 marks: "two months after the issue of auditor's report").
- **MCQs on the duty itself**: January 2025 MCQ 4 (reading minutes of meetings held only *up to* 31 March is the procedure not as per SA 560); MTP January 2025 Series II MCQ 10.

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
ICAI's suggested answers first fix the window ("after the date of the auditor's report but before the date the financial statements are issued"), state that there is no obligation to perform procedures after the report date, then list the steps in order: discuss, determine, inquire; if amended, procedures, (review of steps to inform recipients), new report and its date; if not, the SA 705 modification or the notification, and finally "appropriate action to seek to prevent reliance on the auditor's report".

**[trick] Discuss, Determine, Inquire: "DDI" opens Windows 2 and 3**
Both post-report windows start with the same three verbs in this order. Condition: only when a fact becomes known that, had it been known at the report date, may have caused the auditor to amend the report; there is no duty to go looking.

### Answer frame

### A case on a subsequent event

1. **Fix the window** from the dates: event or fact before the report date (Window 1), after the report but before issue (Window 2), or after issue (Window 3).
2. **Relevant provision** for that window, in SA 560's words.
3. **Facts**: type of event (existed at the year-end, or arose after), amount against materiality, management's response, whether the report has been provided.
4. **Analysis**: adjust or disclose; which branch of the window applies.
5. **Conclusion**: the steps in order, ending with the opinion, the new report's date and paragraph, or the action to prevent reliance.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: Bunty's minutes that stop on 31 March** **Fix:** SA 560 requires reading minutes of meetings held **after** the date of the financial statements, and inquiring about meetings whose minutes are not yet available (ICAI's January 2025 MCQ 4).
- **Adjusting for the fire** **Fix:** the fire is a condition that arose after the year-end: disclose it if significant; adjust only for events that give evidence of conditions existing at the year-end.
- **"Once signed, the auditor has no duty"** **Fix:** there is no duty to perform procedures after the report date, but a duty to respond to facts that become known and would have changed the report, in both Window 2 and Window 3.
- **Keeping the old date on the new report** **Fix:** a new report on amended financial statements is dated no earlier than the date of approval of the amended statements, after extending the subsequent-events procedures to that date (or carries an additional date restricted to a lawful restricted amendment).

### Recap

- Subsequent events: events between the date of the financial statements and the date of the auditor's report, and facts known to the auditor after the report date.
- Two types: conditions that existed at the year-end (insolvent debtor, claim settled for less: adjust) and conditions that arose after (fire, share issue, merger: disclose).
- Objectives: evidence that events up to the report date are appropriately reflected; respond to facts learnt after the report date that may have changed it.
- Window 1 procedures: understand management's procedures; inquire; read minutes of meetings after the year-end; read latest interim statements; written representation.
- Date the financial statements are issued: when the report and statements are made available to third parties; at or after the report date and the date the report is provided to the entity.
- Windows 2 and 3 open with discuss, determine, inquire. Amended: procedures on it; new report dated no earlier than approval (or an additional date for a restricted amendment).
- Window 2, not amended: report not provided, modify (SA 705); provided, notify not to issue; issued anyway, act to prevent reliance.
- Window 3: review steps to inform recipients; Emphasis or Other Matter paragraph; if no steps, notify that the auditor will seek to prevent future reliance, then act. (The lab phones everyone who got a copy.)

## Lesson 7.2: SA 570: going concern

*What the going concern basis means, who assesses it, the events that cast doubt on it, the procedures that follow, and the five ways the conclusion changes the auditor's report.*

### Key idea

Two questions decide the report: is the going concern basis appropriate, and does a material uncertainty exist? If it does, the third question is whether the financial statements disclose it adequately.

### Formats and formulas

### The reporting grid

*Reproduce this from memory: each row is a requirement of SA 570.*

| Situation | Opinion | Also in the report |
|---|---|---|
| Basis inappropriate | Adverse | – |
| Material uncertainty, adequately disclosed | Unmodified | "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" section |
| Material uncertainty, not adequately disclosed | Qualified or adverse | Statement in the Basis for Qualified (Adverse) Opinion section |
| Events or conditions, no material uncertainty | Unmodified | – (auditor evaluates their disclosure) |
| Management will not make or extend assessment | Qualified or disclaimer may be appropriate | – |

### The "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" section

Its two contents are listed in point 2 above: (a) draw attention to the note; (b) state that a material uncertainty exists and that the opinion is not modified in respect of it (SM 7.22). Neither an Emphasis of Matter paragraph ([[aud.report.sa706|SA 706]]) nor a key audit matter ([[aud.report.sa701|SA 701]]) is a substitute for this reporting (SM 8.50, 8.54).

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where SA 570 appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
In the ICAI documents read for this lesson, SA 570 was asked 20 times in 17 new-scheme documents:

- **Events or conditions**: May 2025 Q2(b) (5 marks, five operating examples); MTP May 2024 Series I Q2(a) (4 marks, four financial). MCQs: September 2024 MCQ 10 ("shortage of important supplies" is not *financial*); September 2025 MCQ 11 (sort eight events into the three categories).
- **Additional procedures**, 5 marks: MTP January 2025 Series II Q1(a); MTP September 2025 Series I Q3(b); RTP May 2024 Q17 (purpose of a twelve-month cash flow forecast, two procedures on it).
- **Evaluating management's assessment**: RTP September 2026 Q16 (no detailed analysis; a nine-month assessment).
- **Meaning, significance, responsibilities**: MTP January 2026 Series II Q3(b) (5 marks); MTP September 2024 Series I Q2(a) (5 marks: a loss-making start-up that meets its commitments: basis appropriate); MTP September 2026 Series I Q5(c) (4 marks: is an unmodified report a guarantee?); September 2026 Q1(c) (4 marks; answers not yet published).
- **Reporting**: RTP September 2024 Q16 and September 2024 MCQ 11 (adequately disclosed: unmodified with the section); MTP May 2024 Series I Q3(b) and January 2026 MCQ 11 (not adequately disclosed: qualified or adverse); MTP January 2026 Series I MCQ 15 (management refuses to assess: qualified or disclaimer); MTP May 2026 Series II MCQs 8–9 (a case on the auditor's primary focus and judgment over a "no rule requires it" argument); January 2025 MCQ 15.

**[exam] The traps**
- **The category trap.** Shortage of important supplies, labour difficulties and loss of key management are *operating*; arrears of dividends and cash-on-delivery suppliers are *financial*; non-compliance with regulatory requirements and pending litigation are *other*.
- **"Net loss means not a going concern"** is false (Study Material case study MCQ 2; MTP September 2024 Series I Q2(a)).
- **Its own section, never Key Audit Matters.** In January 2025 MCQ 15, management says the going concern assumption "will not be suitable" and that the uncertainty will be "suitably disclosed" in the notes. No option offered an adverse opinion; three options put the matter under Key Audit Matters, and ICAI's key is (A), a separate "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" section under SA 570. Keep the two rules of SM 7.22 apart in your own answers: an appropriate basis with an adequately disclosed material uncertainty gives that section; statements prepared on the going concern basis when its use is inappropriate get an adverse opinion.

**[trick] Three questions, five reports**
"Basis appropriate?" No: **adverse**. "Material uncertainty?" No: **unmodified**. "Adequately disclosed?" Yes: **unmodified + Material Uncertainty section**; No: **qualified or adverse**. And the odd one out: management will not assess: **qualified or disclaimer**. Condition: the first branch assumes the statements were prepared on the going concern basis.

### Answer frame

### A case on going concern

1. **Relevant provision**: the part of SA 570 the case turns on (events or conditions, additional procedures, the twelve-month rule, or the reporting requirement), in the Standard's words.
2. **Facts**: list the events or conditions with their category and figures; management's plans and assessment period; the disclosure made.
3. **Analysis**: is the basis appropriate? Do mitigating factors and feasible plans remove the doubt, or does a material uncertainty remain? Is it adequately disclosed?
4. **Conclusion**: the opinion and the report element ("Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" section, or the statement in the Basis section), or the procedure demanded.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: Bunty's "qualified, because of going concern doubts"** **Fix:** an adequately disclosed material uncertainty leaves the opinion **unmodified**, with a separate "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" section. Modification follows only inadequate disclosure, an inappropriate basis, or management's refusal to assess.
- **Using an Emphasis of Matter paragraph for a material uncertainty** **Fix:** SA 570 prescribes its own separate section; an Emphasis of Matter paragraph, or a key audit matter, is not a substitute (SM 8.50, 8.54).
- **Twelve months from the wrong date** **Fix:** management's assessment must cover at least twelve months from the **date of the financial statements**; if shorter, the auditor requests an extension.
- **"The clean report promised survival"** **Fix:** the absence of a reference to a material uncertainty cannot be viewed as a guarantee of the entity's ability to continue: inherent limitations are greater for future events.

### Recap

- Going concern: the entity continues in operation for the foreseeable future, with neither the intention nor the necessity of liquidation or of materially curtailing operations; otherwise, the liquidation basis.
- Management assesses; the auditor obtains evidence on the appropriateness of the basis, concludes on material uncertainty, and reports. No reference to a material uncertainty is not a guarantee.
- Events or conditions: financial (net current liabilities, maturing debt, negative cash flows, losses, dividend arrears, cash-on-delivery suppliers), operating (key management, major market or customer, labour, supplies, competitor), other (regulatory non-compliance, litigation, law changes, uninsured catastrophes).
- Management's assessment covers at least twelve months from the date of the financial statements, or the auditor requests an extension.
- Additional procedures: request an assessment; evaluate plans; test the forecast's data and assumptions; consider new information; written representations.
- Material uncertainty: potential impact and likelihood such that disclosure of its nature and implications is necessary.
- Basis inappropriate: adverse. Uncertainty adequately disclosed: unmodified + "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" section. Not adequately disclosed: qualified or adverse. Management will not assess: qualified or disclaimer may be appropriate.
- Kesar's "I will help" is a feeling, not an arrangement: confirm support is enforceable and affordable.

## Lesson 7.3: SA 450: evaluating misstatements

*How the auditor collects every misstatement above the clearly trivial, asks management to correct them, judges what stays uncorrected against reassessed materiality, and tells, asks and documents before forming the opinion.*

### Key idea

Accumulate every misstatement above the clearly trivial, ask management to correct all of them, and judge whatever stays uncorrected, individually and in aggregate, by size and nature, against materiality reassessed on the actual results. Then tell those charged with governance, obtain a written representation with the list attached, and document.

### Formats and formulas

*Reproduce both from memory: each is a frequent short question.*

### The audit documentation under SA 450 (SM 7.27)

1. The amount below which misstatements would be regarded as **clearly trivial**;
2. **All misstatements accumulated** during the audit and whether they have been corrected; and
3. The auditor's **conclusion** as to whether uncorrected misstatements are material, individually or in aggregate, and the **basis** for that conclusion.

### The SA 450 line in ICAI's illustrative representation letter (SM 7.36)

"The effects of uncorrected misstatements are immaterial, both individually and in the aggregate, to the financial statements as a whole. A list of the uncorrected misstatements is attached to the representation letter. (SA 450)"

The rest of that letter is in [[aud.complete.sa580|SA 580: written representations]].

**[note] The Study Material gives no percentage for "clearly trivial". The amount is the auditor's judgment, and it must be documented. It is not materiality: misstatements above it are accumulated even when each is far below materiality, because they may add up.**

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where SA 450 appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
In the documents read for this lesson, SA 450 was examined in 8, usually in a short descriptive part or one MCQ.

- **Documentation, 3 or 4 marks**: May 2024 Q6(c) (3 marks); MTP September 2025 Series II Q6(c) (4 marks, with the content of the written representation on uncorrected misstatements).
- **Communicating with management and a refusal to correct, 4 marks**: MTP May 2024 Series I Q1(b), a revenue understatement under AS 9: communicate, why timely communication is useful, and what to do if management refuses.
- **Multiple choice, 2 marks.** The written representation is on uncorrected (not corrected) misstatements: September 2026 MCQ 8 (answers not yet published). Uncorrected misstatements each below materiality but material together: MTP September 2024 Series II MCQ 6. Aggregate approaching materiality and management refusing to adjust: evaluate individually and in aggregate and consider the report, MTP May 2026 Series I MCQ 11 (repeated as MTP September 2026 Series I MCQ 11). Materiality applied in evaluating uncorrected misstatements, re-evaluated at completion: MTP May 2026 Series I MCQ 14 (repeated in MTP September 2026 Series I). The SA 450 line of the representation letter: January 2026 Q2(a).

**[exam] The traps**
MCQ distractors offer a representation on **corrected** misstatements, a threshold such as "5% of turnover" that SA 450 does not contain, "ignore it, it is below materiality", and "management refused, so automatically qualify". The Study Material's Test Your Knowledge MCQ 3 turns on one word: the representation is that the effects are **immaterial**, not material.

**[trick] ACCORD: the SA 450 sequence**
**A**ccumulate (above clearly trivial), **C**onsider revising the strategy and plan, **C**ommunicate to management and ask for correction, **O**btain the reasons for any refusal, then reassess materiality and evaluate the uncorrected items (individually and in aggregate, size and nature, prior periods), **R**eport to those charged with governance and request the representation, **D**ocument. Condition: the order is the Study Material's; the reassessment of materiality always comes before the evaluation of uncorrected misstatements, and the evaluation hides inside the "O": do not drop it.

### Answer frame

### A case question on misstatements

1. **Relevant provision**: "As per SA 450, …", only the requirements the facts need (accumulation, revision of the plan, communication, refusal, reassessment, evaluation, those charged with governance, representation, documentation).
2. **Facts of the case**: list the misstatements with amounts and say which are corrected.
3. **Analysis**: clearly trivial? Reassessed materiality? Individually, in aggregate (show the total), by nature, and the prior-period effect.
4. **Conclusion**: material or not, then what the auditor must do next, and the effect on the opinion if they stay uncorrected (SA 705).

### "State the documentation required under SA 450"

Three points, in order: the clearly trivial amount; all misstatements accumulated and whether corrected; the conclusion on whether uncorrected misstatements are material, individually or in aggregate, with its basis. Three marks are often exactly these three lines.

### "Discuss the auditor's responsibility to communicate with management"

| Point | What earns the mark |
|---|---|
| What and when | All accumulated misstatements, on a timely basis, to the appropriate level of management, unless prohibited by law or regulation |
| Request | Request management to correct those misstatements |
| Why timely | Lets management evaluate whether the items are misstatements, inform the auditor if it disagrees, and act |
| Why correct all | Accurate books and records; lower risk of material misstatement in future periods from the cumulative effect of immaterial uncorrected prior-period misstatements |
| If refused | Understand management's reasons and take them into account in evaluating the financial statements as a whole |

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: "each one is below materiality, so none matters"** **Fix:** SA 450 asks whether they are material "individually or in aggregate": add them up, and weigh their nature too.
- **The yardstick nobody re-read** **Fix:** reassess materiality against the actual financial results *before* evaluating uncorrected misstatements.
- **"We passed it last year"** **Fix:** consider the effect of prior-period uncorrected misstatements on this year's balances and the financial statements as a whole, and communicate it to those charged with governance.
- **Treating the representation as the evidence** **Fix:** the representation records management's belief with the list attached; the auditor still reaches and documents a conclusion, and a written representation does not on its own provide sufficient appropriate audit evidence.

### Recap

- Misstatement: a difference in amount, classification, presentation or disclosure from what the framework requires; from error or fraud.
- Objective: evaluate the effect of identified misstatements on the audit, and of uncorrected misstatements on the financial statements.
- Accumulate all misstatements except the clearly trivial; revise the strategy and plan if more may exist or the total approaches materiality.
- Tell management on a timely basis, request correction of all; if refused, understand the reasons.
- Reassess materiality on the actual results, then judge uncorrected items individually and in aggregate, by size and nature, with the prior-period effect.
- Tell those charged with governance (material items individually), request correction; obtain a written representation with the list attached.
- Document: the clearly trivial amount, every misstatement and whether corrected, the conclusion and its basis.
- Small ones add up, and old ones carry forward. (Mausi's name can stay; the wedding date cannot.)

## Lesson 7.4: SA 580: written representations

*What written representations are and why they are necessary but never sufficient evidence, the two management must give, the others the auditor may ask for, their date, period and form, and what follows a refusal.*

### Key idea

Written representations are necessary audit evidence but never sufficient on their own. The two about management's responsibilities (preparing the financial statements; providing all information and recording all transactions) are compulsory: if they are not provided, or are unreliable because of doubt about management's integrity, the auditor disclaims an opinion.

### Formats and formulas

### ICAI's illustrative representation letter (SM 7.35–7.37)

*Reproduce the headings and the bullets in this order: January 2026 asked for the "Financial Statements" part for 5 marks.*

*On the letterhead of the entity*

To Iyer & Associates, Chartered Accountants. Date: 28th May, 2026.

Dear Sir,

This representation letter is provided in connection with your audit of the financial statements of Tapri Tea Co. Ltd for the year ended March 31, 2026 for the purpose of expressing an opinion as to whether the financial statements give a true and fair view in accordance with the applicable accounting standards in India.

We confirm that (to the best of our knowledge and belief, having made such inquiries as we considered necessary for the purpose of appropriately informing ourselves):

**Financial Statements**

- We have fulfilled our responsibilities, as set out in the terms of the audit engagement dated [date], for the preparation of the financial statements in accordance with financial reporting Standards, in particular, the financial statements give a true and fair view in accordance with the applicable accounting standards in India.
- Significant assumptions used by us in making accounting estimates, including those measured at fair value, are reasonable.
- Related party relationships and transactions have been appropriately accounted for and disclosed in accordance with the requirements of applicable accounting standards in India. (SA 550)
- All events subsequent to the date of the financial statements and for which applicable accounting standards in India require adjustment or disclosure have been adjusted or disclosed. (SA 560)
- The effects of uncorrected misstatements are immaterial, both individually and in the aggregate, to the financial statements as a whole. A list of the uncorrected misstatements is attached to the representation letter. (SA 450)

**Information provided**

- We have provided you with:
  - Access to all information of which we are aware that is relevant to the preparation of the financial statements such as records, documentation and other matters;
  - Additional information that you have requested from us for the purpose of the audit; and
  - Unrestricted access to persons within the entity from whom you determined it necessary to obtain audit evidence.
- All transactions have been recorded in the accounting records and are reflected in the financial statements.
- We have disclosed to you the results of our assessment of the risk that the financial statements may be materially misstated as a result of fraud.
- We have disclosed to you all information in relation to fraud or suspected fraud that we are aware of and that affects the entity and involves:
  - Management;
  - Employees who have significant roles in internal control; or
  - Others where the fraud could have a material effect on the financial statements.
- We have disclosed to you all information in relation to allegations of fraud, or suspected fraud, affecting the entity's financial statements communicated by employees, former employees, analysts, regulators or others.
- We have disclosed to you all known instances of non-compliance or suspected non-compliance with laws and regulations whose effects should be considered when preparing financial statements.
- We have disclosed to you the identity of the entity's related parties and all the related party relationships and transactions of which we are aware. (SA 550)

Chief Financial Officer

**[tip] SA 580's own two representations are the first bullet under "Financial Statements" and the first two under "Information provided". The rest supports other evidence.**

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where SA 580 appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
In the documents read for this lesson, SA 580 was examined in 16, in every form ICAI uses.

- **Date and period with a change of management, 4 or 5 marks**: May 2024 Q2(b) (4 marks), MTP January 2025 Series II Q6(c) (4 marks), May 2026 Q1(c) (4 marks), MTP May 2026 Series II Q6(a) (5 marks; a letter 20 days early, refused for the half-year before new management): the most repeated SA 580 question in the documents read. The date alone: RTP September 2024 Q15 (a day before the report: appropriate).
- **The contents, 4 or 5 marks.** The letter's "Financial Statements" matters: January 2026 Q2(a) (5 marks). Other representations: May 2025 Q5(b) (5 marks). The responsibilities representations: MTP May 2024 Series II Q2(a) (4 marks); why they are necessary: RTP January 2026 Q16; when to reconfirm: MTP September 2025 Series I Q4(c) (4 marks). Judgments and intentions: MTP September 2026 Series II Q4(b) (5 marks).
- **Reliability**: a representation on non-compliance contradicted by evidence, MTP January 2025 Series I Q1(a) (5 marks). Specialists and "best of its knowledge and belief": MTP May 2026 Series I Q6(a) (5 marks).
- **Multiple choice, 2 marks.** Necessary but not sufficient evidence: May 2025 MCQ 14. Qualifying language acceptable: MTP September 2024 Series II MCQ 8, MTP May 2025 Series II MCQ 5. Representation not provided, the action **not** to take ("treat it as a disagreement and qualify"): May 2026 MCQ 4. Refusal of the responsibilities representations: September 2026 MCQ 6 (answers not yet published).

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
ICAI's suggested answers quote the requirement nearly word for word ("as near as practicable to, but not after", "does not diminish such persons' responsibilities") and apply it to the facts in a sentence or two. In change-of-management questions, the date and its reason, the period and its reason, and the conclusion on management's stand each earn marks. On a continued refusal, MTP May 2026 Series II Q6(a) ends "the auditor should consider appropriate modification in the audit report"; for the representations on management's responsibilities, SA 580 itself names that modification: a disclaimer of opinion (SM 7.38).

**[trick] Two must-haves, one must-not**
The **two must-haves** are the responsibilities representations: preparation, and information plus completeness. Refused or unreliable for integrity reasons: **disclaimer**, never "qualified". The **one must-not** is the date: never after the report. Condition: other representations that are refused lead to SA 705's judgment on the opinion, not automatically to a disclaimer.

### Answer frame

### A case question on written representations

1. **Relevant provision**: "As per SA 580, …", the requirement the facts turn on (date, period, form, qualifying language, the two required representations, reliability, refusal, disclaimer).
2. **Facts of the case**: who refused or wrote what, and on what date.
3. **Analysis**: is it one of the two responsibilities representations? Is the date within the rule? Does a change of management change anything (it does not)? Is there an inconsistency to resolve?
4. **Conclusion**: what management must provide, what the auditor must do (discuss, re-evaluate integrity), and the opinion if it is not provided (disclaimer, for the responsibilities representations).

### "Explain the date of and period covered by written representations"

| Point | What earns the mark |
|---|---|
| Date | As near as practicable to, but not after, the date of the auditor's report |
| Why | Necessary evidence: the opinion cannot be expressed, nor the report dated, before it; and events up to the report date matter |
| Period | All financial statements and period(s) referred to in the report |
| Why | Management reaffirms that earlier representations for prior periods remain appropriate |
| New management | Not being present does not diminish its responsibilities for the financial statements as a whole; the requirement still applies |

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the qualified opinion for a missing letter** **Fix:** for the two responsibilities representations, SA 580 requires a **disclaimer of opinion** in accordance with SA 705. ICAI's May 2026 MCQ 4, on a refused representation about receivables, lists Bunty's answer as the action not to take even there.
- **"Management has confirmed it, so no more work"** **Fix:** written representations are necessary audit evidence but do not provide sufficient appropriate audit evidence on their own; reliable ones do not reduce other evidence the auditor obtains.
- **"We were not here then"** **Fix:** representations cover all periods referred to in the report; a change of management does not diminish the responsibilities of current management for the financial statements as a whole.

### Recap

- Written representation: a written statement by management to the auditor to confirm certain matters or support other evidence; not the financial statements, assertions or books.
- Necessary audit evidence, like responses to inquiries; never sufficient on its own; reliable ones do not reduce other evidence.
- Objectives: obtain the responsibilities representations, support other evidence, respond appropriately to representations given or refused.
- The two required representations: responsibility for preparing the financial statements fulfilled; all information and access provided and all transactions recorded.
- "To the best of our knowledge and belief" is acceptable if made by those with appropriate responsibilities and knowledge.
- Date as near as practicable to, but not after, the report date; cover all periods in the report, even after a change of management; form: a letter addressed to the auditor.
- Inconsistent: resolve, then reconsider integrity. Refused: discuss, re-evaluate integrity, SA 705.
- Disclaimer when the responsibilities representations are refused, or unreliable because of sufficient doubt about management's integrity. (No signature, no surgery.)

## Lesson 7.5: SA 260 and SA 265: communicating with those charged with governance

*Who those charged with governance are, what SA 260 makes the auditor tell them and how, and which control deficiencies SA 265 sends in writing to them and to management, with what content.*

### Key idea

SA 260: two-way communication with those charged with governance, throughout the audit, on the auditor's responsibilities, the planned scope and timing, significant findings and, for listed entities, independence; in writing where oral would not be adequate, and always documented. SA 265: significant deficiencies in internal control go in writing, on a timely basis, to those charged with governance and to management, with a description, their potential effects and their context.

### Formats and formulas

### What the written communication of significant deficiencies must contain (SM 7.49)

*Reproduce the two contents and the three explanations, in this order.*

The auditor shall include:

- (a) a **description** of the deficiencies and an explanation of their **potential effects**; and
- (b) **sufficient information** to enable those charged with governance and management to understand the context of the communication.

In particular, the auditor shall explain that:

- (i) the **purpose** of the audit was for the auditor to express an opinion on the financial statements;
- (ii) the audit included consideration of internal control relevant to the preparation of the financial statements in order to design audit procedures that are appropriate in the circumstances, but **not for the purpose of expressing an opinion on the effectiveness of internal control**; and
- (iii) the matters being reported are **limited** to those deficiencies that the auditor has identified during the audit and has concluded are of sufficient importance to merit being reported to those charged with governance.

### SA 260 and SA 265 side by side

**[compare] | Point | SA 260 | SA 265 |**
|---|---|---|
| Deals with | Communication with those charged with governance in an audit | Communicating identified internal control deficiencies to those charged with governance and management |
| Direction | Two-way | Auditor to them |
| What | Responsibilities; scope and timing; significant findings; independence (listed) | Significant deficiencies; other deficiencies worth management's attention |
| Form | Written where oral is not adequate; independence (listed) in writing | Significant deficiencies always in writing |
| Timing | Timely | Timely |

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where SA 260 and SA 265 appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
In the documents read for this lesson, SA 260 was examined in 4 and SA 265 in 11, almost always as a descriptive part of 4 or 5 marks.

- **SA 260.** Who are those charged with governance: January 2025 Q5(b) (5 marks). Significant findings to communicate: MTP September 2025 Series II Q3(b) (5 marks; difficulties, and a practice acceptable but not most appropriate), MTP September 2026 Series II Q5(b) (5 marks). Independence for a listed entity with joint auditors: May 2026 Q5(c) (4 marks). The Study Material's case study MCQ 5: oral matters are documented with when and to whom.
- **SA 265, definitions and judgment.** Deficiency and significant deficiency: September 2025 Q2(a) (5 marks). Matters to consider for significance: MTP September 2024 Series I Q5(a) (5 marks). Indicators: MTP May 2025 Series I Q3(b) and Series II Q3(b) (5 marks each). Objective and communication: MTP January 2026 Series I Q3(b) (5 marks), RTP May 2026 Q16.
- **SA 265, the one-liner.** A significant deficiency reported in one sentence is not proper communication: September 2024 Q6(a) OR (4 marks, related party transactions), MTP May 2024 Series II Q6(b) (4 marks, sales), RTP May 2025 Q15 (inventory verification; the Study Material's Test Your Understanding 6).
- **Multiple choice.** Significant deficiencies go in writing to those charged with governance, not an oral briefing: MTP January 2026 Series II MCQ 15. Content of the communication, without an opinion on internal control: RTP May 2024 MCQ 2.4.

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
The one-liner questions are answered in ICAI's suggested answers in one pattern: the communication must describe the deficiency, explain its potential effects and give enough information for context; the one-liner does neither, so it "is not proper"; then the potential effect for the facts (misstatement of inventories, of related party transactions), ending with the suggestion that responsibility be fixed for adhering to the control.

**[trick] RSF + I, and the three Ps**
SA 260's matters: **R**esponsibilities, **S**cope and timing (with significant risks), **F**indings, plus **I**ndependence for listed entities. SA 265's three explanations: **P**urpose (an opinion on the financial statements), **P**artial look (internal control considered to design procedures, not to opine on it), **P**icked items (only the deficiencies identified and judged worth reporting). Condition: the three Ps belong to the written communication of significant deficiencies.

### Answer frame

### A one-liner question ("Is the communication proper?")

1. **Provision**: SA 265's definition of significant deficiency, and the required content: description, potential effects, sufficient information for context (and the three explanations).
2. **Facts**: quote the one-liner.
3. **Analysis**: it states the deficiency but explains no effect and gives no context.
4. **Conclusion**: "not proper"; then write the missing parts for the facts, and suggest fixing responsibility for the control.

### "What significant findings should be communicated?"

The five points in the Study Material's order: qualitative aspects (with "acceptable but not most appropriate"); significant difficulties; unless all of them manage the entity, matters discussed with management and written representations requested; circumstances affecting the report; other significant matters relevant to oversight. Apply each to the facts in a line.

### "Explain the independence communication for a listed entity"

The statement of compliance (engagement team, firm, network firms); all relationships and other matters bearing on independence, with total fees for audit and non-audit services by category; the safeguards applied. In writing.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: "controls are weak"** **Fix:** describe it, explain its potential effects, give enough information for context, and explain the purpose and limits of the audit.
- **"No misstatement found, so not significant"** **Fix:** significance depends on the likelihood and potential magnitude of misstatement, not only on whether one occurred: a significant deficiency may exist although the auditor found no misstatement.
- **Telling only the managing director** **Fix:** SA 260 asks the auditor to determine the appropriate persons within the governance structure; matters such as those discussed with management and the written representations requested go to those charged with governance unless all of them manage the entity, and significant deficiencies go to them in writing.
- **An opinion on internal control by accident** **Fix:** the communication must say the audit considered internal control to design procedures, **not** to express an opinion on its effectiveness.

### Recap

- Those charged with governance: the persons overseeing the entity's strategic direction and accountability, including the financial reporting process; they may include management (an owner-manager).
- SA 260 objectives: communicate responsibilities and the planned scope and timing; obtain relevant information; give timely significant observations; promote two-way communication.
- Communicate: responsibilities; scope and timing (with significant risks); significant findings (five points); independence and fees for listed entities.
- Process: form, timing and content agreed; in writing where oral is not adequate (independence always, for listed entities); timely; adequacy evaluated; oral matters documented with when and to whom.
- Deficiency: a control unable to prevent, or detect and correct, misstatements on time, or a necessary control missing. Significant: important enough to merit the attention of those charged with governance.
- Significance rests on likelihood and magnitude: it can exist with no misstatement found.
- Significant deficiencies: in writing, timely, to those charged with governance and (unless inappropriate) management; other deficiencies worth it, to management.
- Letter content: description and potential effects; context; the three Ps. (The engineer writes "cracked beam", never "building weak".)
