## Lesson 4.1: Growth strategies: integration and diversification

*How a firm grows: intensifying, diversifying (concentric through vertical or horizontal integration, or conglomerate), innovating, merging or acquiring, and forming strategic alliances.*

### Key idea

Classify a growth move by its link with the present business. Linked by process, technology or marketing: concentric, either vertical (an earlier or later step of the same chain) or horizontal (the same step). No link of product, market or technology: conglomerate.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where growth strategies appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
Of the 32 new-scheme ICAI documents searched (eight past papers, eight RTPs, sixteen MTPs), 24 ask about this lesson:

- **Distinguish concentric and conglomerate diversification, 5 marks**: RTP September 2024 Q14; MTP May 2024 Series I Q4(b); MTP May 2025 Series I Q3(b); MTP January 2026 Series I Q3(b); MTP September 2025 Series II Q4(a).
- **Vertically integrated diversification, forward versus backward, 5 marks**: May 2024 Q8(b); MTP January 2026 Series II Q4(a); as a case, RTP September 2025 Q13.
- **Expansion strategy and its characteristics, 1 + 4**: May 2025 Q8(b). **Identify the strategy from its definition and give the major reasons for it, 5 marks**: MTP September 2024 Series II Q3(b).
- **Innovation, 5 marks**: May 2024 Q6(a) (1 + 4: do you agree that innovation leads to unnecessary expenses? The answer disagrees, then gives the three things innovation offers); January 2026 Q7(b) (4 + 1: what innovation offers, and any risk involved). The MTPs repeat both: MTP May 2025 Series II Q3(b) the first, MTP September 2026 Series II Q3(b) the second.
- **Mergers and their types, 5 marks**: September 2025 Q7(b); MTP September 2024 Series II Q1(a).
- **Strategic alliance, 5 marks**: RTP January 2025 Q14; MTP January 2025 Series II Q4(b); MTP May 2024 Series II Q4(b); MTP September 2026 Series I Q1(c).
- **Identify the diversification**: RTP May 2025 Q13 and MTP May 2025 Series II Q2(a) (conglomerate); RTP May 2026 Q13.
- **MCQs, 2 marks**: conglomerate (May 2025 MCQ 11; RTP May 2026 MCQ 6; MTP January 2025 Series II (A)(iii)); concentric (January 2026 MCQ 11); forward integration (RTP September 2026 MCQ 1(ii)); strategic alliance (January 2025 MCQ 12; RTP May 2025 MCQ 3; RTP January 2026 MCQ 6; MTP January 2025 Series I (B)(ii)).

**[exam] The words that decide the answer**
"No linkage ... with specific reference to product or market or technology" (May 2025 MCQ 11): conglomerate. "Greater control over its supply chain" (Organo, SM 4.36): backward. "Connect directly with consumers and increase its control over the distribution process" (RTP September 2026 MCQ 1(ii)): forward. "Continue to exist as independent entities while sharing resources, expertise and risks" (MTP September 2026 Series I Q1(c)): strategic alliance.

**[exam] The risk in innovation**
The Study Material mentions only the argument that innovation "leads to unnecessary expenses that do not give as much returns" (SM 4.13). For the "risk involved" mark in January 2026 Q7(b), ICAI's suggested answer listed four risks of its own: high cost and financial risk, uncertain returns, technological uncertainty (rapid change may make an innovation obsolete) and implementation risk (poor execution or lack of capability).

**[trick] Up, down, sideways**
Draw the chain with raw materials at the top and the customer at the bottom. Moving **up** to suppliers is **backward**, **down** to customers is **forward**, staying at your own level is **horizontal**, leaving the chain is **conglomerate**. It works for any business with a production-marketing chain.

### Answer frame

### "Identify the growth strategy" (5 marks)

1. **Name it**: "The strategy adopted is backward integration, a form of vertically integrated (concentric) diversification."
2. **Define it** in the Study Material's words.
3. **Apply** the facts: which link exists, which stage of the chain is entered.
4. **Conclude**, then what else is asked (advantages, risks, the difference from another type).

### "Distinguish between concentric and conglomerate diversification"

ICAI's suggested answers (RTP September 2024 Q14 and four MTPs) give three points:

**[compare] | Point | Concentric diversification | Conglomerate diversification |**
|---|---|---|
| (i) What is added | Related products or markets | Areas unrelated to the current line of business |
| (ii) Linkage | Linked to the existing businesses through process, technology or marketing | No such linkages; disjointed from the existing businesses |
| (iii) Usual reason | Opportunities are available in the existing line of business | Opportunities in the current line are limited, or outside are highly lucrative |

Point (iii) is from ICAI's suggested answers; the Study Material gives (i) and (ii) (SM 4.10–4.12). For "Explain the expansion strategy and its characteristics" (1 + 4), write the definition, then the characteristics in the Study Material's order (May 2025 Q8(b)).

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the supermarket that "integrated horizontally" with a farm** **Fix:** horizontal is the *same* stage (another supermarket). Farms supply the supermarket, so it is **backward** integration (SM 4.11).
- **Forward and backward swapped** **Fix:** direction is about the chain: towards inputs is backward, towards distribution and the customer is forward.
- **Calling every partnership a merger** **Fix:** if both firms remain separate entities sharing resources, benefits and control, it is a **strategic alliance**; a merger creates one entity. (Where ICAI's facts said two firms "join forces" to design products, without saying they stay separate, the answer was a co-generic merger: MTP September 2024 Series II Q1(a).)
- **"Unrelated because it is a new product"** **Fix:** a new product linked by process, technology or marketing is concentric (clothes to shoes; a dairy launching vegan yogurt and organic milk, January 2026 MCQ 11). Conglomerate needs *no* link.

### Recap

- Corporate strategies: stability, expansion, retrenchment, combination (SM 4.4).
- Expansion: redefining the business by enlarging scope and substantially increasing investment; opposite of stability in risk and reward; routes: intensification and diversification.
- Internal growth: intensification (penetration, market development, product development), diversification, innovation; external: mergers and acquisitions, strategic alliance.
- Concentric = linked by process, technology or marketing: vertical (backward to inputs, forward to distribution) or horizontal (same stage).
- Conglomerate = no linkage of product, market or technology.
- Mergers: horizontal, vertical, co-generic, conglomerate.
- Strategic alliance: partners stay independent; advantages organisational, economic, strategic, political; the major disadvantage is sharing. (The cricket bats stay a dream.)

## Lesson 4.2: Stability and retrenchment: turnaround, divestment and liquidation

*When a firm holds its course (stability), and when it pulls back: turning a declining business around, divesting a part, or liquidating; plus combination strategy.*

### Key idea

Stability keeps the same business and improves it incrementally; it is not "do nothing". A declining business is first turned around (reverse the decline by improving internal efficiency); divestment of a part usually follows a turnaround that has failed; liquidation is the last resort, abandoning the activities totally.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where these strategies appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
Of the 32 new-scheme ICAI documents searched (eight past papers, eight RTPs, sixteen MTPs), 16 ask about this lesson:

- **Turnaround: identify it and state the stages of the action plan, 5 marks**: September 2024 Q5(b); January 2026 Q5(b); RTP September 2024 Q13 (Pizza Galleria); RTP January 2026 Q13; MTP May 2024 Series I Q3(b) and MTP September 2024 Series I Q2(a) (a loss-making textile mill).
- **Danger signals for a turnaround, 5 marks**: RTP September 2025 Q14; MTP September 2026 Series I Q2(b).
- **Divestment**: May 2025 Q5(c) (1 + 1 + 3: turnaround in one year, divestment the next, and reasons); September 2025 Q5(b) (a CFO with a loss-making unit and a better investment elsewhere); MTP September 2025 Series I Q1(c) (selling the rights to unviable content).
- **Stability**: September 2024 Q7(b) ("Start-ups rarely aim for stability", 5 marks); May 2026 Q5(c) (1 + 3 + 1); RTP May 2024 Q14 ("Stability is opposite of expansion").
- **All four corporate strategies**: May 2026 Q6(b) (1 + 4).
- **MCQs**: the initiative that supports stability (January 2025 MCQ 14: improving functional efficiency); which actions do not pertain to the turnaround plan (May 2026 MCQ 12: restructuring the organisational hierarchy, and a new transport service); the strategy after a failed turnaround (MTP May 2025 Series I (B)(iii): divestment); selling an arm of the business (MTP September 2024 Series I (A)(v): divestment); permanently closing a loss-making company and selling its assets (RTP September 2026 MCQ 5: liquidation).

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
In the turnaround answers read for this lesson ICAI names the strategy, ties it to the danger signals in the facts, then writes the five stages by name, in order, with one or two lines each. For divestment, the definition (including "a turnaround has been attempted but has proved to be unsuccessful") is followed by the reasons applied to the case.

**[trick] Fix, cut, close**
The exits go in the order of the Study Material: **fix** (turnaround), **cut** (divestment), **close** (liquidation). The turnaround stages: **A**ssess, **A**nalyse, **E**mergency, **R**estructure, **R**eturn: "two As, an E, two Rs".

### Answer frame

### Turnaround case (5 marks)

1. Strategy named, with the turnaround definition (internal retrenchment, improving internal efficiency).
2. The danger signals found in the facts.
3. "For turnaround strategies to be successful, it is imperative to focus on the short and long-term financing needs as well as on strategic issues."
4. The five stages, named in order, one or two lines each, applied to the case.
5. A closing line: the firm can regain financial stability and its competitive position.

### "What is meant by corporate level strategies? Identify the strategy in each decision" (May 2026 Q6(b), 1 + 4)

1. One line: corporate level strategies are formulated by the top management and give direction to the organisation as a whole; the four categories are stability, expansion, retrenchment and combination.
2. For each decision: name the strategy, write its **basic feature** from the table above, and point to the fact that shows it.
3. A decision that expands one part of a business and closes another (ICAI's case: expanding profit-making units abroad while closing loss-making units) was keyed as **combination**; a decision to sell out a whole loss-making division as **retrenchment**.

### "Justify: stability strategy is opposite of expansion strategy"

ICAI's suggested answer (RTP May 2024 Q14) runs on the first four points; the last row adds the Study Material's own contrast (SM 4.7):

**[compare] | Point | Stability strategy | Expansion strategy |**
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Safeguards existing interests and strengths | Redefines the business by substantially adding to its scope |
| Business | Same or similar markets and products; few functional changes | New products, markets, technology; diversifying, acquiring, merging |
| Character | Not a "do nothing" strategy, but a safe one | An aggressive strategy: dynamism, vigour, promise and success |
| Typical user | Mature organisations; some small organisations, to maintain a comfortable market or profit position | Firms with a mammoth growth ambition |
| Risk and reward | Less risk, limited rewards | Relatively unknown and risky paths, very high rewards |

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the stability strategy that bought more farms** **Fix:** stability keeps the same business and improves **functional efficiency** incrementally. Buying farms, new segments and new channels are growth moves (January 2025 MCQ 14).
- **"Restructure the organisation chart" as Stage Four** **Fix:** Stage Four is *restructuring the business*: the core business, cash forecasts, assets and debts, product mix, facilities, markets and morale. In May 2026 MCQ 12, "restructuring organizational hierarchy" (with "providing innovative transport service") was keyed as an action that did *not* pertain to the turnaround plan.
- **Divestment and liquidation used as synonyms** **Fix:** divestment sells or liquidates a **portion** (a division, profit centre or SBU) and the company carries on; liquidation **abandons the activities totally**.
- **Jumping straight to divestment** **Fix:** if the board wants to continue and improve internal efficiency, the answer is **turnaround**; divestment usually follows a turnaround that has proved unsuccessful, unless it is obvious that divestment is the only answer.

### Recap

- Four corporate strategies: stability, expansion, retrenchment, combination (SM 4.4).
- Stability: same business, product-market posture and level of effort; incremental efficiency; no redefinition; low risk; typical of mature products; not "do nothing".
- Stability reasons: maturity; staff comfort; stable environment; expansion seen as threatening; consolidation after rapid expansion.
- Strategic exits: turnaround (reverse the decline), divestment (cut off a unit), liquidation (abandon totally).
- Turnaround signals: negative cash flow, uncompetitive products, falling share, poor facilities, over-staffing and low morale, mismanagement.
- Turnaround stages: assessment; analysis and strategic plan; emergency action plan; restructuring; return to normal.
- Divestment: sale or liquidation of a portion, usually after a failed turnaround; reasons: mismatch, negative cash flows, competition, technology, better investment.
- Combination: mixing the alternatives, such as expanding one business while closing another. (The samosa gets one honest repair first.)

## Lesson 4.3: Ansoff's matrix and the ADL matrix

*Ansoff's grid sorts growth by two questions, new product and new market; the ADL matrix crosses the industry's stage of maturity with the firm's competitive position.*

### Key idea

Ansoff asks two questions, new product? new market?, and gives four growth strategies: penetration, market development, product development and diversification (the riskiest). ADL crosses the stage of industry maturity with the firm's competitive position, and the cell gives the strategies.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where Ansoff and ADL appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
Of the 32 new-scheme ICAI documents searched (eight past papers, eight RTPs, sixteen MTPs), 18 ask about this lesson:

- **"Explain the product market growth matrix as propagated by Igor Ansoff as a device for identifying growth opportunities for the future", 5 marks**: January 2025 Q8(b); RTP January 2026 Q14; MTP September 2025 Series I Q4(a); MTP May 2026 Series I Q4(a); MTP September 2026 Series II Q4(a). **Advise a company using the matrix**: MTP January 2026 Series II Q2(a).
- **Identify the Ansoff strategy, 5 marks**: MTP May 2025 Series I Q1(c) and MTP May 2026 Series I Q1(c) (products tailored to each region's tastes, in international markets: diversification); MTP September 2024 Series I Q1(c) (existing juices taken to new countries with adapted packaging: market development).
- **MCQs, 1–2 marks**: market development (RTP January 2026 MCQ 1(ii); MTP January 2025 Series I (A)(iv)); market penetration (RTP September 2025 MCQ 5: promotional discounts, more advertising and more retailers for existing phones in the home country; RTP September 2026 MCQ 1(i)); product development (MTP September 2024 Series II (B)(iii)); diversification (MTP September 2024 Series II (A)(iv); MTP September 2025 Series II (B)(iii); MTP January 2025 Series II (A)(v)); September 2026 MCQs 11 and 16 (answers not yet published).
- **ADL**: "Write a short note on the role of ADL matrix in assessing the competitive position of a firm", 5 marks (RTP May 2025 Q14; MTP May 2026 Series II Q3(b)); September 2025 MCQ 14 (a pioneer with a leading position in a maturing industry: strong position in a mature industry); MTP May 2026 Series II (A)(ii) (the BCG-ADL contradiction).

**[exam] The new-country trap, set in three MTPs**
New country plus *the same* product, even with adapted packaging or marketing: **market development** (MTP September 2024 Series I). New country plus products *created* for local tastes: **diversification** (MTP May 2025 and May 2026 Series I). Decide the product question honestly before the market question.

**[trick] Product first, then market**
Read every case twice: once for the product (same or new?), once for the market (same or new?). Same-same: penetration; same-new: market development; new-same: product development; new-new: diversification. For the ADL positions remember the order **D-S-F-T-W**: "Don't Stop For Tea, Waiter".

### Answer frame

### "Explain Ansoff's product market growth matrix" (5 marks)

1. One line on the tool: proposed by Igor Ansoff; helps businesses decide their product and market growth strategy; a device for identifying growth opportunities for the future.
2. The 2 × 2 matrix drawn (existing and new products across, existing and new markets down).
3. The four strategies, one definition each, in the order penetration, market development, product development, diversification (with "diversification is risky" and why).
4. Closing line: as market conditions change, a company may shift strategies; when the present market is saturated it may have to pursue a new market.

For "advise company X using the matrix" (MTP January 2026 Series II Q2(a); the chapter's own question on a pharmaceutical company, SM 4.47–4.48), add that the company can adopt the four strategies simultaneously for different products.

### "Write a short note on the ADL matrix" (5 marks)

ICAI's suggested answers (RTP May 2025 Q14; MTP May 2026 Series II Q3(b)): the name (Arthur D. Little); a portfolio analysis method based on product life cycle; a two-dimensional matrix of stage of industry maturity and the firm's competitive position; then the **five competitive positions, each with its description** in the Study Material's words. The 4 × 5 figure itself is not reproduced in those answers.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the new label that became a "new product"** **Fix:** packaging adapted for new customers is one of the Study Material's means of **market development**; the product is still the existing one (MTP September 2024 Series I Q1(c)).
- **"Increase usage" filed under product development** "Brush your teeth twice a day" or "a second cup before 8 a.m." is still the existing product to existing customers: **market penetration** (the chapter's own toothpaste case, SM 4.49).
- **ADL positions mixed with BCG names** **Fix:** ADL's five positions (dominant, strong, favourable, tenable, weak) measure business strength against the industry's life-cycle stage; BCG's quadrants come from market growth and relative market share. A business can be a Question Mark and Dominant at once.

### Recap

- Ansoff (Igor Ansoff): a product-market growth matrix for identifying growth opportunities; two questions, new product? new market?
- Penetration: existing products, existing markets (more sales to present customers; increase usage).
- Market development: existing products, new markets (new regions, segments, channels, packaging, pricing).
- Product development: new or modified products, existing markets; may need new competencies.
- Diversification: new products, new markets; risky; related or unrelated.
- ADL (Arthur D. Little): portfolio technique based on the product life cycle; stage of industry maturity (embryonic, growth, mature, ageing) × competitive position (dominant, strong, favourable, tenable, weak): a four by five matrix.
- Each ADL cell lists strategies: dominant-embryonic "fast grow, build barriers, act offensively"; weak-ageing "withdraw". (Don't Stop For Tea, Waiter.)

## Lesson 4.4: BCG and GE matrices

*Two portfolio tools for deciding which business gets money: BCG's growth-share matrix (stars, cash cows, question marks, dogs) and GE's nine-cell stop-light matrix.*

### Key idea

Portfolio matrices allocate money across businesses. BCG uses market growth rate and relative market share: stars (build), question marks (hold), cash cows (harvest), dogs (divest), in the pairing ICAI's suggested answers use. GE uses the broader market attractiveness and business strength, in a nine-cell green-amber-red grid.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where BCG and GE appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
Of the 32 new-scheme ICAI documents searched (eight past papers, eight RTPs, sixteen MTPs), 19 ask about this lesson:

- **BCG case, 1 + 2 + 2 marks**: January 2025 Q5(c), repeated as MTP January 2026 Series I Q1(c): a sugar-free drink with heavy R&D and marketing spend, a major share within five months and rapid growth: classify it (Star), the post-identification strategies, and the limitations of the technique.
- **"Explain the strategic implications of Stars, Question Marks, Cash Cows and Dogs"**: RTP May 2026 Q14; MTP January 2025 Series II Q4(b).
- **Identify the Star**: RTP May 2024 Q13 (the newly established software development unit).
- **Justify a contrasting allocation**: September 2026 Q6(b), a company's most profitable business "is not receiving the highest investment allocation" (answer not yet published).
- **BCG MCQs, 2 marks**: Build (September 2024 MCQ 15); harvest the cash cow and invest in the star (RTP May 2026 MCQ 1(iv)); the cash cow funds growth (MTP January 2026 Series II (A)(iii); MTP May 2026 Series II (A)(i)); Star (MTP May 2026 Series I (B)(i); MTP September 2026 Series II (B)(ii)); Cash cow (MTP September 2025 Series I (B)(ii)); a new product just launched in a rapidly growing market is a **Question mark** (MTP May 2024 Series I (B)(i); MTP September 2025 Series II (B)(i)); ice-cream flavours kept on despite an early market sense that they should be stopped: Dog (MTP May 2024 Series II (B)(ii)).
- **GE**: the two dimensions (May 2026 MCQ 16: business strength and market attractiveness); high attractiveness and strong business strength: Invest/Expand (September 2025 MCQ 16); identify the tool from "industry attractiveness and business unit strength" with "go ahead" and "be careful" ratings (RTP January 2025 Q13).

**[exam] The GE cell where an RTP and the Study Material differ**
RTP September 2026 Q14 (EcoBuild Materials) puts a division with **medium business strength in an industry with low market attractiveness** in the **yellow zone**, with selective investment and harvest or divest if prospects stay weak. In the Study Material's figure (SM 4.31) the low-attractiveness, average-strength cell is **Harvest/Divest, in red**. This lesson teaches the Study Material's figure. If you meet the case, name the cell from the figure; harvesting or divesting appears in both readings.

**[trick] Read the BCG axes in the right direction**
Vertical: market **growth** (up is high). Horizontal: relative **share**, with high on the **left**. "Stars shine high and to the left; dogs sit low and to the right." For GE, read the colour from the number of "good" ratings: two good (high, strong) or one good and one middle is green; the three diagonal cells from high-weak to low-strong are yellow; the rest are red. Check it against the figure, which it reproduces exactly.

### Answer frame

### "Describe the construction of the BCG matrix and its utility" (the chapter's own question)

1. What it is: developed by BCG in the early 1970s for companies organised into SBUs that must allocate resources among them.
2. The two axes and what each measures; the 2 × 2 figure with high share on the left.
3. The four types, one ICAI definition each.
4. The four strategies (build, hold, harvest, divest) and, if asked, the pairing with quadrants.
5. Conclusion: a powerful tool for strategic planning analysis and choice (SM 4.45–4.46). Add the limitations if the question asks for them.

### "Explain how the BCG matrix may justify a seemingly contrasting allocation" (September 2026 Q6(b))

1. The portfolio approach: resources can be channelised at the corporate level to the businesses with the greatest potential, diverting them from cash-rich businesses to more prospective ones that hold promise of faster growth (SM 4.23).
2. The two BCG axes, and that profit is not one of them.
3. The most profitable business is typically a **cash cow**: it generates cash and needs less investment to maintain its share, so the strategy is **harvest**.
4. The less profitable business receiving resources is typically a **star** (heavy investment to maintain its position and finance growth: **build**) or a **question mark** (a lot of cash to hold its share; to be turned into a star).
5. Conclusion: the cash cow's cash funds the businesses that hold the company's future growth; when their markets mature, stars become cash cows.

### "Distinguish between the BCG matrix and the GE matrix"

**[compare] | Point | BCG growth-share matrix | GE nine-cell matrix |**
|---|---|---|
| Vertical axis | Market growth rate | Market attractiveness (size, growth, profitability, competitive intensity and other factors) |
| Horizontal axis | Relative market share | Business strength (share, margin, brand, cost position and other drivers) |
| Cells | Four: stars, question marks, cash cows, dogs | Nine, in green, yellow and red zones |
| Strategies | Build, hold, harvest, divest | Invest/Expand, Select/Earn, Harvest/Divest |
| Origin | Boston Consulting Group | General Electric, with McKinsey and Company |

The two differences the Study Material itself states (SM 4.32): market attractiveness replaces market growth, with a broader range of factors; competitive strength replaces market share.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the new product that was "obviously a Star"** **Fix:** it does not yet have a high share: a new product in a rapidly growing market is a **Question mark** (MTP May 2024 Series I; MTP September 2025 Series II). It becomes a Star only once it wins a high share, as Coufix and "Say No to Sugar" did.
- **"Most profitable, so cash cow"** **Fix:** BCG classifies on growth and relative share, not profit. Coufix is profitable from day one and is a **Star**, because its market is growing rapidly and it needs heavy investment (SM 4.29–4.30).
- **Axes swapped or flipped** **Fix:** vertical: market growth rate; horizontal: relative market share, high on the left. For GE: vertical, market attractiveness; horizontal, business strength (May 2026 MCQ 16 asks for these two dimensions).
- **Writing "Divest" for every question mark** **Fix:** question marks need heavy investment, and the Study Material says it is for the organisation to turn them into stars; ICAI's answers pair them with **Hold**. Divest is the strategy for dogs.

### Recap

- Portfolio models channel resources at the corporate level to the businesses with the greatest potential; cash-rich businesses can fund more prospective ones (SM 4.23).
- BCG: vertical axis market growth rate (market attractiveness), horizontal axis relative market share (company strength), high share on the left.
- Stars: rapid growth, heavy investment, best opportunity for expansion. Cash cows: low growth, high share, generate cash, need less investment.
- Question marks: low share, high growth, need a lot of cash, can become cash traps; turn them into stars. Dogs: low growth, low share; minimise by divestment or liquidation.
- Strategies: build, hold, harvest, divest; ICAI's answers pair stars–build, question marks–hold, cash cows–harvest, dogs–divest.
- BCG limitations: difficult and costly; hard to define SBUs and measure share and growth; classifies the present, little advice on the future; over-emphasis on share growth.
- GE (stop-light, with McKinsey): market attractiveness (vertical) × business strength (horizontal); nine cells; green invest and grow, yellow caution, red retrenchment, divestment or liquidation.
- GE differs from BCG: attractiveness replaces growth; competitive strength replaces share. (Kesar still loves all four businesses; the budget does not.)
