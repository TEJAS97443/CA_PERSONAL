## Lesson 2.1: Cost sheet for a manufacturer

*The cost sheet stacks costs by function in ICAI's order: prime cost, works cost, cost of production, cost of goods sold and cost of sales, with stocks adjusted at the right step.*

### Key idea

A cost sheet classifies cost by function and builds it up in a fixed order: prime cost → works cost → cost of production → cost of goods sold → cost of sales. Each stock is adjusted at the tier where it sits: raw material in materials consumed, work-in-process in works cost, finished goods in cost of goods sold.

**[formula] Prime cost and works cost**
$$\text{Prime cost} = \text{Direct materials} \qquad + \text{Direct employee cost} \qquad + \text{Direct expenses}$$

$$\text{Works cost} = \text{Prime cost} \qquad + \text{Factory overheads} \qquad + \text{Opening WIP} - \text{Closing WIP}$$

**[formula] Cost of production, cost of goods sold, cost of sales**
$$\text{Cost of production} = \text{Works cost} \qquad + \text{QC} + \text{R\&D} \qquad + \text{Production admin} \qquad - \text{Recoveries} \qquad + \text{Primary packing}$$

$$\text{COGS} = \text{Cost of production} \qquad + \text{Opening FG} - \text{Closing FG}$$

$$\text{Cost of sales} = \text{COGS} + \text{General admin} \qquad + \text{Selling} + \text{Secondary packing} \qquad + \text{Distribution}$$
QC = quality control; R&D = research and development for the process; production admin = administrative overheads related to production; recoveries = realised or realisable value of scrap, waste and by-products; FG = finished goods.

**[formula] Profit as a percentage of sales or of cost**
$$\text{Profit} = x\% \text{ of sales} \qquad = \frac{x}{100 - x} \text{ of cost of sales}$$
20% on sales is 25% on cost; 25% on sales is 33⅓% on cost. Sales = cost of sales ÷ (1 − x%).

**[definition] Prime cost**
Prime cost represents the total of direct materials costs, direct employee (labour) costs and direct expenses. (SM 6.3)

**[definition] Primary and secondary packing cost**
Primary packing cost: cost incurred on packing materials which are essential to hold and preserve the product for further processing or its use by consumer. Secondary packing cost: cost incurred on packing materials which is used to store, transport, and promote the product. (SM 6.20)

**[definition] Cost of sales**
It is the total cost of a product incurred to make the product available to the customer or consumer. It is the aggregate of cost of goods sold, administrative costs, marketing costs and other separate line items of cost which could not form part of cost of production. (SM 6.20)

**[exam] The traps ICAI set**
- "Inclusive of GST @ 18% (ineligible for ITC)" stays in material cost (January 2025); creditable GST comes out.

- "Profit is 20% on sales": add 25% of cost of sales, or divide it by 80%.

**[trick] FIFO stock in one line**
Units produced = units sold + closing stock − opening stock; closing stock = closing units × (cost of production ÷ units produced). Condition: FIFO, or closing stock stated to be from this period's output.

### Answer frame

The chapter's own theory questions (SM 6.22) and the MTP favourites. Write the points in this order.

**Describe how costs are classified on the basis of function.** Costs are divided according to the function for which they have been incurred: (i) production / manufacturing cost; (ii) administration cost; (iii) selling cost; (iv) distribution cost; (v) research and development costs. In a cost sheet they are grouped into prime cost, cost of production, cost of goods sold and cost of sales.

**Explain the treatment of administration overheads.** (1) Only administrative overheads related to production are included in cost of production. (2) General administration overheads are not included in production cost; they are added to cost of goods sold to arrive at cost of sales. (3) General administration includes (SM 6.7): (a) depreciation and maintenance of building, furniture etc. of corporate or general management; (b) salary of administrative employees, accountants, directors, secretaries etc.; (c) rent, rates and taxes, insurance, lighting, office expenses etc.; (d) indirect materials: printing and stationery, office supplies etc.; (e) legal charges, audit fees, corporate office expenses like directors' sitting fees, remuneration and commission, meeting expenses etc.

**State the advantages of a cost sheet.** The five points of SM 6.10, in that order.

**Explain the treatment of the following items.** One line per item, in the words of the table in How it works.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the GST that sneaked into material cost** **Fix:** GST is material cost only if input tax credit is not available or not availed.
- **The managing director's salary in cost of production** **Fix:** only administration related to production goes into cost of production; the rest comes after cost of goods sold.
- **The two kinds of packing swapped** **Fix:** packing that holds and preserves the product is primary (cost of production); packing to store, transport or promote it is secondary (selling and distribution).
- **Closing stock valued at the wrong rate** **Fix:** this period's cost of production per unit (FIFO), ₹58.

### Recap

- Functional classification: production, administration, selling, distribution, R&D (SM 6.2).
- Prime cost = direct materials consumed + direct employee cost + direct expenses.
- Works cost = prime cost + factory overheads + opening WIP − closing WIP.
- Cost of production = works cost + QC + R&D (process) + production administration − scrap and by-product credit + primary packing.
- Cost of goods sold = cost of production + opening − closing finished goods (closing at this period's cost of production per unit, FIFO).
- Cost of sales = cost of goods sold + general administration + selling + secondary packing + distribution; interest as a separate item.
- Out of cost: abnormal costs, penalties and fines, creditable GST, income tax; subsidies reduce the cost they relate to.
- x% profit on sales = x ÷ (100 − x) on cost. (The tiffin is built from the bottom up.)

## Lesson 2.2: Estimated cost sheets, tenders and quotations

*A quotation is a cost sheet for the future: the order's estimated direct costs, overheads at rates taken from past cost sheets, order-specific costs, then the profit margin converted correctly.*

### Key idea

Price to be quoted = the order's estimated direct costs + overheads at pre-determined recovery rates (from past cost sheets, on ICAI's bases) + costs specific to the order + profit. A margin "on selling price" must be converted before it is added to cost: 20% on price is 25% on cost.

### Formats and formulas

The estimated cost sheet follows the cost sheet's own order ([[cma.sheet.mfg|cost sheet for a manufacturer]]), with each overhead line showing its rate and base. *Reproduce it from memory.*

| Statement of estimated cost and price to be quoted | Amount (₹) |
|---|---:|
| Direct materials (estimated quantity × expected price) | xxx |
| Direct wages (estimated hours × expected rate) | xxx |
| Direct expenses specific to the order | xxx |
| **Prime cost** | **xxx** |
| Add: Factory overheads (F% of direct wages) | xxx |
| **Works / factory cost** | **xxx** |
| Add: Administration overheads (A% of works cost) | xxx |
| **Cost of production** | **xxx** |
| Add: Selling and distribution overheads (S% of cost of production) | xxx |
| Add: Costs specific to the order (delivery, special packing) | xxx |
| **Total cost** | **xxx** |
| Add: Profit (x% on price = x ÷ (100 − x) on cost) | xxx |
| **Price to be quoted** | **xxx** |

**[formula] Overhead recovery rates from a past cost sheet**
$$F = \frac{\text{Factory overheads}}{\text{Direct wages}} \times 100 \qquad A = \frac{\text{Admin overheads}}{\text{Works cost}} \times 100$$

$$S = \frac{\text{S\&D overheads}}{\text{Cost of production}} \times 100$$
S&D = selling and distribution. The base in each denominator is the one the question names; the same base is used for the order.

**[formula] Pre-determined overhead rate**
$$\text{Pre-determined rate} = \frac{\text{Estimated overheads}}{\text{Budgeted units}}$$
Estimated (normal) overheads for the period ÷ budgeted number of units during the period (SM 4.41); with hours or wages as the denominator for an hour rate or a percentage rate.

**[formula] Price from a margin, and cost from a price**
$$\text{Price} = \frac{\text{Total cost}}{1 - x\%} \qquad \text{Total cost} = \frac{\text{Selling price}}{100\% + p\%}$$
x = profit as a percentage of selling price; p = profit as a percentage of total cost (SM 4.51).

**[definition] Estimated cost**
Kohler defines estimated cost as "the expected cost of manufacture, or acquisition, often in terms of a unit of product computed on the basis of information available in advance of actual production or purchase". Estimated costs are prospective costs since they refer to prediction of costs. (SM 1.34)

**[definition] Pre-determined cost**
A cost which is computed in advance before production or operations start, on the basis of specification of all the factors affecting cost, is known as a pre-determined cost. (SM 1.34)

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where quotations appeared, May 2024 to September 2026 (and the old scheme)**
In the documents read for this lesson:

- **Bulk order priced from last year's rates, 6 marks:** January 2025 Q4(b): production overhead as a percentage of labour, marketing and distribution as a percentage of production cost, packaging and transport added, "Sales required for achieving a profit margin of 20% on the final sales amount".
- **Cost sheet at two activity levels, 8 marks:** September 2024 Q2(a): January at 40% and February at 100% capacity, with TV sponsorship, a hi-tea, a gift per dozen and lucky draw prizes.
- **Job cost sheet and price to be quoted:** MTP Series I September 2026 Q2(a) (7 marks, 20% on selling price, normal rectification); RTP January 2026 Q13 (revised overhead structure, same profit % on sales).
- **Old scheme:** May 2022 Q2(a) (10 marks: two jobs, find both rates, price a third job at 15% on selling price); RTPs May 2021 Q7, May 2022 Q8 and November 2023 Q7 (overhead rates from last year's actuals, price at 25% on sales); November 2019 Q5(a) (10 marks, lowest price for an extra order).
- **Multiple choice:** MTP Series I September 2026 MCQ 11 (2 marks), the relevant cost of material in a quotation.

**[exam] The traps ICAI set**
- "Profit 20% on sales" or "on the final sales amount": divide total cost by 80%; never add 20% of cost.
- "Profit percentage on total cost" for the old jobs, then "on selling price" for the new one (May 2022): two different conversions in one question.
- Price changes: apply the new material price and wage rate before the overhead rates.
- Promotional costs (gifts, prizes, sponsorship) belong only to the level that needs them (September 2024).

**[trick] Margin conversions you will use every paper**
On price → on cost: 10% → 11.11%, 12.5% → 14.29%, 20% → 25%, 25% → 33.33%, 33⅓% → 50%. Rule: x on price = x ÷ (100 − x) on cost; p on cost = p ÷ (100 + p) on price. Always true, for any x below 100.

### Answer frame

For a quotation question, ICAI's suggested answers earn marks in this order:

1. **Working note: recovery rates**, each with its formula and base ("Normal factory overheads ÷ direct labour hours = ₹150 an hour"; "Administration ÷ works cost × 100 = 10%").
2. **Working note: revised figures** (new material price, wage rate, revised overheads).
3. **Statement of estimated cost**, in cost sheet order, each overhead line showing "% of base".
4. **Profit line with the conversion stated** ("20% on price = 25% on cost").
5. **The final line in the question's words**: "Price to be quoted" or "Sales value", and a one-line conclusion ("Hence the price to be quoted is ₹42,23,340").

For theory, the point to write is ICAI's: pre-determined overhead rates are used because "production overheads are to be determined in advance for fixing selling price, quote tender price and to formulate budgets" (SM 4.41), and a cost sheet "facilitates the preparation of cost estimates required for submitting tenders" (SM 6.10).

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the 20% that was added to cost** **Fix:** 20% on price = 25% on cost; price = ₹33,78,672 ÷ 80% = ₹42,23,340.
- **Last year's material price in this year's quotation** **Fix:** revise direct costs first (quantity with its normal loss, then the new price), then apply the rates.
- **The right rate on the wrong base** **Fix:** a rate is only valid on the base it was computed on; write the base on every overhead line.
- **Fixed cost per unit carried to a new volume** **Fix:** at a new level, take fixed costs in total and divide by the new volume.

### Recap

- An estimated cost sheet is a cost sheet for the future; its bottom line is the price to be quoted (SM 6.10, advantage iii).
- Recovery rates from last period: factory overheads ÷ direct wages; administration ÷ works cost; selling and distribution ÷ cost of production (or the bases the question names).
- Revise direct costs to expected prices first, then apply each rate to the order's own base.
- Add order-specific costs (delivery, special dies or packing) at their own amounts.
- Price = total cost ÷ (1 − margin on price); x% on price = x ÷ (100 − x) on cost; total cost = price ÷ (100% + p% on cost).
- Two jobs with known prices give two equations in F and A; eliminate, then price the third job (SM 4.49–4.52). A common unknown mark-up cancels in the ratio of the two prices.
- At a new volume, fixed costs stay in total and change per unit; split semi-variable costs first.
- The halwai prices the wedding before the wedding.

## Lesson 2.3: Cost sheet for the service sector

*A service's cost statement has no stocks to adjust; it is built by function or by variability (standing, running and maintenance charges), divided by the service unit, and priced on takings.*

### Key idea

A service cost statement has no stock adjustments. It is prepared on functional classification or on variability (fixed or standing charges, variable or running charges, semi-variable or maintenance charges), and the total is divided by the service's cost unit (per meal, passenger-km, patient-day, project) to get cost per unit, then the price.

### Formats and formulas

*Reproduce the operating cost sheet from memory.* Each head (A standing charges, B running charges, C maintenance charges) is followed by its own total and a per-unit figure. A commission or tax on takings is shown either inside running charges (SM Illustration 3, 12.16) or on its own line after the A + B + C total (January 2025 Q4(a): "total cost before commission", then commission); either way its amount is solved from takings.

On functional classification the heads are those of [[cma.sheet.mfg|the manufacturer's cost sheet]] (direct material, direct employee cost, direct expenses, overheads), without the work-in-process and finished goods lines.

**[formula] Cost per unit of service**
$$\text{Cost per unit} = \frac{\text{Total cost of the period}}{\text{Total service units of the period}}$$
Units: per meal, per passenger-km, per patient-day, per room-day, per project, per application (SM 12.3–12.4).

**[formula] Takings when charges are a percentage of takings**
$$\text{Takings} = \frac{\text{Total cost}}{1 - c\% - p\%}$$
c = commission, licence fee or tax as a percentage of takings; p = profit as a percentage of takings. The equation behind it: T = cost + cT + pT (SM 12.16).

**[definition] Statement of costs for service sectors (SM 12.10)**
For preparing a statement of cost or a cost sheet for service sector, costs are usually collected and accumulated for a specified period viz. a month, quarter or a year. The cost statement may be prepared either on the basis of functional classification or on the basis of variability: (1) fixed costs or standing charges; (2) variable costs or operating expenses; (3) semi-variable costs or maintenance expenses.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where service cost statements appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
In the documents read for this lesson:

- **Training batch, 5 to 6 marks:** cost per batch, minimum fee for a full batch, then the fee or the fill needed for a margin on the fee: September 2024 Q1(a) (5 marks, TOEFL institute, 80% filled, 25% margin); January 2026 Q3(b) (6 marks, air-hostess institute, ₹14,800 fee, 20% margin, minimum 62.5% filled).
- **Cost per application, 8 marks:** January 2026 Q3(a), a bank's gold-loan and car-loan applications with overheads allocated by percentage.
- **Statement of cost and price on takings, 6 to 10 marks:** toll plaza, total monthly cost including interest, then toll rates at 20% profit on takings: September 2025 Q2(a) (10 marks). Bus fare with 10% commission on takings and 15% profit: January 2025 Q4(a) (8 marks). Operating cost statement with a passenger tax of 22% and profit of 25% on takings: MTP Series I September 2024 Q3(b) (8 marks); monthly operating cost sheet and fare: MTP Series II September 2026 Q2(b) (6 marks).
- **Theory:** "Differentiate between service costing and product costing": RTP September 2024 Q19(b). "What do you understand by operating costs? Describe its essential features": RTP May 2025 Q20(b).

**[exam] The traps ICAI set**
- Charges and profit stated "on takings": solve T = cost + cT + pT; never add them to cost.
- Mixed periods (per month, per quarter, per annum) in one list.
- Depreciation on a time basis (standing charge) against an activity basis (running charge).
- A full batch versus a partly filled one: the fixed cost does not shrink with the batch.

**[trick] The takings divisor**
If commission and profit are c% and p% of takings, takings = cost ÷ (1 − c − p). For 10% and 15%: cost ÷ 0.75. Condition: both are percentages of takings, not of cost; a percentage of cost is added to cost first.

### Answer frame

**Differentiate between service costing and product costing.** ICAI's four points, in ICAI's order (SM 12.3), as a table. ICAI's own answer (RTP September 2024 Q19(b)) states each point for services only; the product column is the contrast that makes it a "differentiate" answer.

| Basis | Service costing | Product costing |
|---|---|---|
| Tangibility | Services are intangible and cannot be stored: no inventory | Products can be stored: inventories of WIP and finished goods |
| Cost units | Composite cost units (passenger-km, patient-day) | Simple units (per unit, per batch, per job) |
| Material vs employee cost | Employee cost is the major element | Material cost is usually the major element |
| Traceability | Indirect costs such as administration are significant; tracing may not be economically feasible | Larger share of costs traced directly |

**What do you understand by operating costs? Describe their features.** ICAI's answer (RTP May 2025 Q20(b)): (1) operating costs are the costs incurred by undertakings which do not manufacture any product but provide a service, such as transport concerns, electricity undertakings, hospitals and theatres; service costing is also known as operating costing (SM 12.2). (2) They are classified under three categories, with examples: operating and running charges (variable: petrol, diesel, lubricants), maintenance charges (semi-variable: tyres, repairs, spares) and fixed or standing charges (garage rent, insurance, road licence, depreciation, interest on capital, salary of the operating manager) (SM 12.10, 12.12). (3) The cost unit is composite, such as passenger-km or kWh (SM 12.3). Add internal and external applications (SM 12.2) if the marks allow.

**Numerical answer, in order:** working notes (units of service, period conversions, depreciation basis); the statement with A, B, C heads, totals and per-unit column; the takings equation; the final rate in the question's words ("fare per passenger-km", "fee per trainee").

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the licence fee charged on cost** **Fix:** percentages of takings are solved for: ₹66 ÷ 0.80 = ₹82.50.
- **Closing stock in a service statement** **Fix:** a service cannot be stored (SM 12.3); the statement runs straight from costs of the period to cost per unit.
- **Depreciation always fixed** **Fix:** time basis = fixed; activity or usage basis = variable (SM 12.10).
- **The drop-outs forgotten** **Fix:** cost what actually happens (54 completers, 6 half-used kits, 54 exam fees) and choose the divisor the question asks for: per enrolled trainee for the fee, per certified trainee for the cost of a certificate.

### Recap

- No inventory in services: no WIP or finished goods lines (SM 12.3).
