## Lesson 5.1: Formulation versus implementation: matrix, linkages and issues

*The five stages of strategic management, why a sound strategy still fails without good implementation, the A-B-C-D and thrive-or-die matrices, forward and backward linkages, and the issues in implementing strategy.*

### Key idea

Organisational success is a function of good strategy **and** proper implementation. Successful formulation does not guarantee successful implementation: it is always more difficult to do something than to say you are going to do it.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where this lesson appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
Of the 32 new-scheme ICAI documents searched (eight past papers, eight RTPs, sixteen MTPs), 13 ask about this lesson:

- **Formulation versus implementation, 5 marks**: September 2025 Q8(b) ("Many managers fail to distinguish…"); MTP May 2025 Series II Q4(a).
- **Efficiency and effectiveness**: RTP September 2025 Q15 and MTP May 2026 Series II Q4(b) (OR), the same Merako Appliances case: how to balance them, and the four situations; ICAI's answer draws the 2 × 2 grid.
- **Principal aspects of the strategy-execution process**: RTP September 2025 Q16; MTP September 2024 Series II Q4(a) (5 marks).
- **Forward and backward linkages, with examples**: MTP May 2024 Series II Q3(b) (5 marks).
- **Strategic uncertainty and how to manage it**: May 2026 Q8(a) (5 marks); RTP January 2026 Q16.
- **Strategic planning versus operational planning**: RTP May 2025 Q16; RTP September 2026 Q16; MTP January 2025 Series I Q4(b) (OR); MTP September 2025 Series I Q3(b) (5 marks).
- **MCQ, 2 marks**: January 2026 MCQ 15 (a realty firm allocates budgets, assigns projects, sets up monitoring and builds culture: **strategy implementation**).

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
"Distinguish" answers in the Suggested Answers reproduce the Study Material's two-column table, row for row. Case answers name the concept first (the cell, the square, the linkage), quote its definition, apply it to the facts, and end with the advice. For strategic versus operational planning, ICAI's RTP answers add one row the Study Material's figure lacks: operational planning "is concerned with the short-term success of the organisation".

**[trick] A-B on top, C-D below; "sound" before "flawed", "weak" before "excellent"**
Draw the grid as ICAI does: formulation on the side (sound above, flawed below), implementation along the bottom (weak, then excellent). Then A = sound/weak, B = sound/excellent, C = flawed/weak, D = flawed/excellent, read like a page. For the second grid: effective survives, efficient alone dies slowly.

### Answer frame

### A case on the matrix or the efficiency grid (5 marks)

1. Name the tool and the concept: "the strategy formulation and implementation matrix" or "the principal combinations of efficiency and effectiveness".
2. Define the two dimensions in ICAI's words (sound or flawed formulation, weak or excellent implementation; efficiency as input-output, effectiveness as attainment of goals).
3. Describe all four squares or cells, one line each.
4. Place the company, with the facts that put it there.
5. Advise the path: A to B; D redesigns strategy first; C redesigns both; cell 2 needs strategic direction.

### Three answers to "Distinguish between strategy formulation and implementation"

**[cols] **Loses marks****

"Formulation is planning, implementation is doing."

True, but one point for a five-mark question.
---
**Passes**

Four rows of the table: meaning, entrepreneurial versus administrative, effectiveness versus efficiency, precedes versus follows.

About half the points.
---
**Full marks**

All eight rows of ICAI's table in its order, as a two-column table, each row a contrast.

Every point the Suggested Answer gives.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: Bunty's square D** **Fix:** square A is sound strategy, weak implementation. Brilliant execution of a flawed strategy is **square D**, and the first step there is to redesign the strategy.
- **"Efficient is safer than effective"** **Fix:** the Study Material says the opposite: cell 2 is a worse place than cell 3, because effectiveness (the right strategic direction) is what ensures survival.
- **The linkages written backwards** **Fix:** forward is formulation's impact on implementation (new strategy, so new structure and leadership style); backward is implementation's impact on formulation (strategies chosen that the present structure of resources can implement).
- **Tactics on the wrong side** **Fix:** operational planning develops tactics rather than strategy; strategic planning develops overall objectives and strategies.

### Recap

- Strategic management has five stages, from vision, mission and objectives through analysis, formulation and implementation to evaluation and control, and the process never really ends; it is dynamic and continuous.
- Implementation is operations-oriented, the most demanding and time-consuming stage; eight principal aspects from budgets to internal leadership, building "fits" with capabilities, rewards, systems and culture.
- Matrix: A sound/weak (move to B), B sound/excellent (ideal), C flawed/weak (redesign both), D flawed/excellent (redesign strategy first).
- Efficiency grid: 1 thrive, 2 die slowly, 3 survive, 4 die quickly; cell 2 is worse than cell 3. Effective = right thing; efficient = thing right.
- Formulation: entrepreneurial, intellectual, effectiveness, few people at the top, precedes. Implementation: administrative, operational, efficiency, many people at middle and lower levels, follows.
- Forward linkage: formulation changes structure and leadership style. Backward linkage: the present structure of resources shapes the choice.
- Issues, in order: project, procedural, resource allocation, structural, functional, behavioural (they can overlap). Strategy, then programmes, then projects.
- Strategic uncertainty: flexibility, diversification, monitoring and scenario planning, resilience, collaboration. (Kulhad Express needed a cook, not a new recipe.)

## Lesson 5.2: Strategic change and digital transformation

*Why strategy needs change, the three steps to initiate it, Lewin's unfreeze-change-refreeze with Kellman's three methods, and how change management carries a digital transformation: four elements, five best practices, five pointers.*

### Key idea

Strategy changes only when behaviour changes. Lewin's unfreezing, changing and refreezing is a continuous cycle, not a one-time event, and a digital transformation succeeds only when change management carries it at every level of the organisation.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where this lesson appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
Of the 32 new-scheme ICAI documents searched, 18 ask about this lesson:

- **Lewin's model, 1 + 3 + 1 marks**: May 2026 Q5(a) (Eagle Manufacturing: identify the model, explain the phases, is it a one-time process?). MCQs: September 2024 MCQ 16 (sequence: unfreezing, changing, refreezing); MTP May 2026 Series I MCQ 1(B)(iii) (the final step: refreeze).
- **Steps to initiate strategic change, 5 marks**: MTP January 2025 Series I Q2(a) and MTP January 2026 Series I Q2(a) (a handset maker that did not adapt); MTP September 2025 Series II Q2(b).
- **Change management strategy and the five best practices (SMEs)**: May 2025 Q5(b) (the Twaran case, 5 marks), repeated word for word as MTP September 2026 Series II Q1(b), and with a renewable-energy firm in RTP January 2026 Q15; MTP May 2024 Series I Q1(c); MTP May 2025 Series II Q1(c) (a firm whose staff resist a cloud system: "key change management strategies").
- **Pointers for navigating change during digital transformation, 5 marks**: September 2024 Q8(b) (OR); MTP May 2025 Series I Q3(a); MTP September 2025 Series I Q4(b); MTP January 2026 Series II Q3(a); MTP September 2026 Series I Q3(b); also RTP May 2024 Q16 and MTP May 2026 Series I Q4(b) (OR) ("Why is change management crucial…, and what are some key strategies for navigating change?"), both answered with the five pointers.
- **The four elements**: RTP May 2026 Q15 (a firm defines objectives, assesses gaps, prepares a phased roadmap and implements: "which concept?").
- **Definitions and the five ways change management helps**: September 2026 Q8(b) (1 + 3 + 1; answers not yet published).

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
Case answers name the concept in the first line ("Kurt Lewin's Three Phase Model of Change"; "a Change Management Strategy"), then list the phases or practices by their Study Material names, each tied to the facts. In the Lewin case, the "one-time process?" mark goes to: no, it is cyclical and remains continuously in action.

**[trick] Which list does the question want?**
"Best practices", "change management strategies", "small and medium-sized business": the **five best practices** (Begin, Ensure, Reduce, Encourage, Recognise). "Pointers", "navigating", "how to manage change during": the **five pointers** (Specify, Communicate, Resistance, Gradually, Training). "Elements of change management in the digital transition": the **four elements** (Define, Assess, Roadmap, Implement). "How a change management strategy can help": the **five ways** (parameters and goals, procedures and tools, plan, involve, track). When "navigating" appears next to "strategies" (RTP May 2024 Q16), ICAI's answer gives the pointers; "change management strategies" to "minimise disruption" in a firm whose staff resist (MTP May 2025 Series II Q1(c)) gets the best practices.

### Answer frame

### Lewin case (1 + 3 + 1 = 5 marks)

1. **Identify** (1 mark): "The model being applied is Kurt Lewin's three-phase model of change: unfreezing, changing and refreezing."
2. **Explain** (3 marks): each phase in the Study Material's words, followed by "In the given case, …".
3. **One-time?** (1 mark): "No. Change is a continuous process due to the ever-changing environment; the cycle of unfreezing, changing and refreezing remains continuously in action."

### Five lists that look alike

**[compare] | List | How many | Items in order |**
|---|---|---|
| Steps to initiate strategic change | 3 | Recognise the need; create a shared vision; institutionalise the change |
| Lewin's phases | 3 | Unfreezing; changing (compliance, identification, internalisation); refreezing |
| Elements of change management in the digital transition | 4 | Define goals; assess current state and gaps; roadmap; implement and manage at every level |
| Best practices for SMEs | 5 | Begin at the top; necessary and desired; reduce disruption; encourage communication; change is the norm |
| Pointers during digital transformation | 5 | Aims and objectives; always communicate; ready for resistance; gradually; assistance and training |

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: Kesar's Monday launch** **Fix:** in any digital-transformation case with a big-bang launch, say so: sudden and unannounced change is socially destructive and morale lowering.
- **Writing the pointers when the question wants the best practices** **Fix:** ICAI's answers to those questions give the five best practices (begin at the top … change is the norm). The pointers answer "how to manage" or "navigate" change during digital transformation.
- **Compliance, identification and internalisation as Lewin's phases** **Fix:** they are H.C. Kellman's three methods inside Lewin's **changing** phase. The phases are unfreezing, changing and refreezing.

### Recap

- Strategic change: modifying existing strategies or bringing out new ones as the environment changes; a corporate strategy focused on new markets, products, services and new ways of doing business.
- Strategic change is initiated in three steps: recognise the need for change, create a shared vision to manage change, and institutionalise the change.
- Lewin's three phases are unfreezing (no surprise: announcements, meetings, new ideas), changing (Kellman: compliance, identification, internalisation) and refreezing (new behaviour the normal way of life, continuously reinforced), in a continuous cycle.
- Digital transformation: digital technologies to develop fresh, improved or entirely new procedures, goods or services.
- Four elements: define goals; assess current state and gaps; roadmap; implement and manage at every level.
- Five best practices (SMEs): begin at the top; necessary and desired; reduce disruption; encourage communication; change is the norm.
- Five pointers: aims and objectives; always communicate; ready for resistance; gradually; assistance and training.
- Digital transformation efforts are more likely to fail without change management. (Melt, mould, freeze, and do not forget the freezer.)

## Lesson 5.3: Organisation structure and culture

*The McKinsey 7S model's hard and soft elements, why structure follows strategy, the eight structures from simple to hourglass with their uses and limits, and how culture helps or blocks strategy execution.*

### Key idea

Structure should be designed to fit the strategy (Chandler: structure follows strategy), and the culture must fit it too. Changing structure cannot make a bad strategy good, and a strategy-culture conflict can defeat the best plan.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where this lesson appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
Seven of the eight past papers from May 2024 to September 2026 (all but September 2026) ask about it, as do many RTPs and MTPs:

- **Identify the structure, with merits and demerits, 5 marks**: matrix (January 2025 Q5(a), 1 + 2 + 1 + 1); network (September 2024 Q5(a); MTP September 2025 Series I Q4(b) OR).
- **SBU, 5 marks**: the "PQR Ltd" case (identify, four attributes and benefits) in RTP September 2024 Q15 and Q1(b) of MTP May 2024 Series II, MTP May 2025 Series I and MTP May 2026 Series I (it is also the chapter's own scenario question 2, SM 5.66); "how the SBU structure becomes imperative" (MTP January 2025 Series II Q2(b)).
- **Short notes, 5 marks**: hourglass structure (September 2025 Q8(b) OR); matrix structure (RTP May 2026 Q16; MTP September 2024 Series I Q2(b)).
- **McKinsey 7S**: the model and its limitations (May 2024 Q5(a), 2 + 3; RTP January 2025 Q16); identify the model (RTP May 2024 Q15; MTP May 2026 Series II Q4(b)). MCQs: a knowledge-sharing platform and teamwork-linked appraisals strengthen **systems** (September 2025 MCQ 15); the soft elements are shared values, style and staff, **not** strategy (May 2026 MCQ 10).
- **Corporate culture**: "What is corporate culture? Where does it come from?" (January 2026 Q6(a); MTP September 2026 Series I Q4(a), 5 marks each).
- **Structure MCQs**: constricted middle: **hourglass** (January 2025 MCQ 15); "independent product centres": **SBU** (three documents, see Under the hood), as is a semi-autonomous segment with its own legal and finance heads (RTP May 2026 MCQ 4); a dispersed team linked by technology and contracts: **network** (RTP January 2026 case MCQ (iii)); multiple leaders per project: **matrix** (RTP May 2024 MCQ 6).

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
Identification carries one mark; the rest go to the definition in the Study Material's words and numbered merits and demerits with short headings ("Loss of synergies"). The chapter's own "draw the structure" answer includes the chart: draw a small labelled tree.

**[trick] Hard Ss are the three that start "Str" or "Sy"**
**Str**ategy, **Str**ucture, **Sy**stems: hard, directly controlled by management. The other four (shared values, style, staff, skills) are soft. Holds for the McKinsey 7S model as the Study Material gives it.

### Answer frame

### "Identify the structure" case (5 marks)

1. **Name it** (1 mark): "The structure best suited is the … structure."
2. **Explain it** in the Study Material's words, tied to two or three facts.
3. **Advantages and disadvantages** (for an SBU: attributes and benefits), four to six numbered points.
4. A closing line on what it needs to work (matrix: planning, training, clear roles, communication, trust).

### "Write a short note on …" (5 marks)

Definition and when used; how it works (draw it); benefits; drawbacks; remedies. Hourglass: constricted middle, IT links top and bottom, generalist managers, lower cost, faster decisions, fewer promotions, three remedies.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: Bunty's "multidivisional" answer** **Fix:** ICAI keys this as an **SBU** in every MCQ read for this lesson: independent planning, own competitors, a manager responsible for strategic planning and profit.
- **Strategy filed under the soft Ss** **Fix:** strategy, structure and systems are the hard elements; shared values, style, staff and skills are soft (May 2026 MCQ 10 tested exactly this).

### Recap

- McKinsey 7S has three hard elements (strategy, structure, systems, directly controlled by management) and four soft ones (shared values, style, staff, skills). Limits: ignores the environment, does not explain effectiveness, static, misses execution gaps.
- Chandler: structure follows strategy; new strategy, new problems, performance falls, new structure, performance improves.
- Small firms functional (centralised), medium divisional (decentralised), large SBU or matrix. Structure cannot make a bad strategy good.
- Simple: owner decides. Functional: by function, cheap, myopic. Divisional: geography, product, customer, process; accountable but costly. M-form: separate businesses, small corporate office.
- SBU: related businesses, composite planning, own competitors, a manager responsible for profit; at least three levels.
- Matrix: two bosses, most complex, violates unity of command; conditions: cross-fertilisation, scarce resources, better information processing; phases: task forces, product/brand management, mature matrix.
- Network: outsourced "non-structure", broker headquarters, unstable environments. Hourglass: constricted middle, IT links top and bottom; cheaper and faster, fewer promotions.
- Culture: values, beliefs, principles, traditions, ways of operating, work environment; ally or obstacle; change it by diagnosis, talk, visible action and CEO commitment, over two to five years. (Rebuild the kitchen and the cooks still need new habits.)

## Lesson 5.4: Strategic leadership

*What a strategic leader does, the five leadership roles in pushing strategy execution, the leader's responsibilities and frame of reference, and when a transformational or a transactional style fits.*

### Key idea

Strategic leadership sets direction by developing and communicating a vision, formulates strategies, brings about the changes needed to implement them and inspires people to execute. Transformational leadership inspires change; transactional leadership exchanges rewards for results and improves the current situation.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where this lesson appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
Of the 32 new-scheme ICAI documents searched, 9 ask about this lesson:

- **Leadership roles in strategy execution, 1 + 4 marks**: May 2025 Q8(b) (OR: "A manager as a strategic leader has to play many leadership roles… delineate the leadership roles"); MTP January 2026 Series I Q3(a) (the same wording, 5 marks).
- **Frame of reference and responsibilities, 2 + 3 marks**: September 2026 Q7(b) (define a managerial frame of reference and its significance; list any six responsibilities; answers not yet published).
- **Identify and distinguish the styles**: RTP September 2026 Q15 (a plant director with fixed targets, rewards and salary deductions: transactional, distinguished from transformational on organisational change and employee motivation); MTP May 2026 Series II Q1(b) (two brothers: authority with explicit rewards and punishment, against participative involvement; 5 marks).
- **MCQs, 2 marks**: a CEO who monitors metrics, sets clear expectations and uses rewards and improvement plans: **transactional** (MTP January 2025 Series I (B)(i)); a leader promoting collaboration, continuous learning and innovation: **transformational** (MTP September 2024 Series II (A)(ii)); a CEO who wants to inspire innovation amid uncertainty: **shifting from transactional to transformational** (MTP January 2026 Series II (A)(iv)); an authoritative CEO who invests in leadership development to build a collaborative environment (RTP January 2025 case MCQ (iii)).

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
In the "roles" question, ICAI's answer opens with one or two lines on strategic leadership and the many roles (the 1 mark), then the five roles by name, each with a line (the 4 marks). In style cases, the answer names the style of each person, quotes its features from the Study Material, and, where asked to distinguish, gives the contrast point by point.

**[trick] Five roles: "Watch, Energise, Respond, Ethics, Correct"**
Watch what is happening; energise (esprit de corps); keep the organisation responsive; be ethical; push corrective actions. The order is the Study Material's.

### Answer frame

### "Discuss the difference in leadership style" (5 marks)

1. Name the style of each person in the case, with the facts that show it.
2. Transformational: charisma and enthusiasm; turbulent environments, start or end of life-cycle, poor performers, major change; excitement, vision, intellectual stimulation; a "dream" of a higher calling; innovation.
3. Transactional: designing systems and controlling; authority of the office to exchange rewards such as pay and status; clear goals with explicit rewards or penalties; builds on the existing culture; static environments, mature industries, organisations performing well.
4. If asked, a two-column table on means of influence, when appropriate, and motivation.

### Three answers to the "many leadership roles" question

**[cols] **Loses marks****

"A leader must motivate, communicate and lead by example."

Generic management words; none of the five roles by name.
---
**Passes**

The five roles listed by name only.

Four marks' worth of headings with no content, and no opening on strategic leadership.
---
**Full marks**

Two lines on strategic leadership and the many roles (visionary … head cheerleader), then the five roles in order, each explained in a line.

The structure of ICAI's own answer.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: Bunty's "transformational" plant director** **Fix:** the style is judged by how the leader influences people. Authority of office, explicit rewards and penalties, and monitoring deviations make it **transactional**.
- **"Transformational is always better"** **Fix:** each style has its setting. Transactional leadership may be appropriate in static environments, mature industries and organisations that are performing well; transformational in turbulent environments, at the start or end of the life-cycle, and in poorly performing organisations needing major change.
- **Writing the seven responsibilities when the question asks for the five roles** **Fix:** "leadership roles in pushing for good strategy execution" = the five roles (staying on top … pushing corrective actions). "Responsibilities of a strategic leader" = the seven (making strategic decisions … sustaining high performance).

### Recap

- Strategic leadership: sets direction by developing and communicating a vision, formulates strategies, brings about the changes required to implement them, inspires staff to execute.
- Many roles: visionary, chief entrepreneur and strategist, chief administrator, culture builder … crisis manager, spokesperson, negotiator, motivator … head cheerleader; authoritarian, listener, participative or coach as the situation needs.
- The strategic leader is a change agent; major change is top-down and vision-driven, starting with diagnosis.
- Five roles in execution: stay on top; esprit de corps; keep responsive; ethical leadership; corrective actions.
- Seven responsibilities: strategic decisions; policies and action plans; communication; human capital (most critical); change; strong culture; high performance over time.
- Frame of reference: the assumptions, premises and accepted wisdom bounding a manager's understanding; battles are between mindsets.
- Transformational: charisma, vision, innovation; turbulent, start or end of life-cycle, poor performers. Transactional: authority, rewards and penalties, systems and control; static, mature, performing well. (Pick the coach for the season.)

## Lesson 5.5: Strategic control and performance measures

*The process of control and its three types, Schendel and Hofer's two questions, the four strategic controls with implementation control's two forms, and strategic performance measures: types, importance and how to choose them.*

### Key idea

Strategic control focuses on the dual questions of whether the strategy is being implemented as planned and whether the results produced are those intended. It watches the premises, the environment, sudden events and the steps of implementation, so the strategy can be adjusted in time.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where this lesson appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
Of the 32 new-scheme ICAI documents searched, 23 ask about this lesson:

- **The process of control, 5 marks**: September 2025 Q5(c) (a company whose expansion is missing projections, with improper use of resources and non-conformance to norms: "which function … what elements?"; ICAI's answer: strategic control, and the four elements).
- **Types of strategic control**: MTP January 2026 Series II Q1(c) (5 marks: continuous monitoring with flexibility; all four types); RTP January 2025 Q15 (regularly reviewing business assumptions: premise control).
- **Strategic control MCQs, 2 marks**: a competitor's unexpected merger and an intense review: **special alert** (May 2025 MCQ 15); owners who read business magazines and attend conclaves: **strategic surveillance** (MTP September 2024 Series I); **premise control** for re-evaluating economic assumptions (MTP September 2025 Series I), for staying on track while making proactive adjustments (MTP September 2026 Series I), for a firm that relied on outdated assumptions (RTP September 2025) and for a hospital chain's regular risk assessment (RTP May 2024); sudden regulation: **special alert** (MTP May 2026 Series I); regular evaluation of goals and performance: **implementation control, milestone reviews** (RTP September 2024 MCQ 6); a 15-year plan resting on one interest-rate assumption (September 2026 MCQ 14, answers not yet published); integrated activities of a complete department: **management control** (MTP May 2025 Series I; RTP September 2026 MCQ 6).
- **Strategic performance measures, 5 marks**: why they are essential (MTP May 2024 Series I Q4(a); RTP September 2024 Q16; MTP May 2026 Series I Q4(b)); meaning and importance (January 2025 Q7(b), 1 + 4); types (September 2024 Q8(b); MTP May 2024 Series II Q4(a); MTP January 2025 Series I Q1(c) and MTP September 2025 Series II Q1(c), the same case); a case on goal alignment and accountability (RTP May 2025 Q15); choosing the right measures (January 2026 Q7(a); MTP September 2026 Series II Q3(a); MTP September 2024 Series I Q4(b) (OR)); SPM and silos (September 2026 Q8(b) (OR), answers not yet published).

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
ICAI's control answers name the function first ("strategic control"), give its purpose in the Study Material's words (curbs on undesirable tendencies, conformance to norms, proper use of resources, plans translated into results), then the four elements (a) to (d). SPM answers use one of three lists, and the question's verb picks it: "types" (six), "why essential" or "importance" (four), "factors to consider" (four).

**[trick] "Premise is focused, surveillance is not"**
Premise control watches **chosen assumptions**; strategic surveillance browses **everything**, unfocussed; special alert reacts to **one sudden event**; implementation control checks **the steps**. If the case says "assumption", it is premise control; "newspapers, magazines, conferences", surveillance; "sudden", "unexpected", "crisis team", special alert; "milestones", "stages", "complete reassessment", implementation control.

### Answer frame

### "Which function, and which elements?" (5 marks)

1. "The company should perform the strategic control function of the strategic management process."
2. Purpose of control in the Study Material's words, tied to the problems in the case.
3. The four elements (a) to (d), each with one line applied to the facts.
4. Closing: the elements ensure a check on improper use of resources, undesirable tendencies and non-conformance, and a result-oriented implementation.

### "Explain the types of strategic control" (5 marks)

Open with Schendel and Hofer's dual questions; then premise control (environmental and industry factors; selective), strategic surveillance (unfocussed, casual browsing), special alert control (sudden events, crisis teams) and implementation control (incremental steps; monitoring strategic thrusts and milestone reviews); close with "these four controls steer the organisation and its sub-systems to the right track".

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: Bunty's "premise surveillance"** **Fix:** surveillance is unfocussed browsing for the unanticipated; checking a specific premise of the strategy is **premise control**.
- **Budgetary control as a type of strategic control** **Fix:** stock, production, quality, cost and budgetary control are **operational** controls. The four strategic controls are premise, surveillance, special alert and implementation control.
- **The wrong SPM list** **Fix:** that is the list of **types**. "Essential" or "importance" wants goal alignment, resource allocation, continuous improvement and external accountability; "factors" wants relevance, data availability, data quality and data timeliness.

### Recap

- The process of control has four elements: (a) objectives as measurable, controllable standards, (b) monitoring and measuring, (c) comparing, detecting deviations and learning new insights on standards, and (d) feeding back corrective and adaptive information.
- There are three types of organisational control: operational (individual tasks, mechanistic: stock, production, quality, cost and budgetary control), management (a whole department, division or organisation, as Robert Anthony defines it) and strategic.
- Strategic control (Schendel and Hofer): is the strategy implemented as planned, and are the results those intended?
- Premise control: focused check of assumptions (environmental and industry factors), selectively. Strategic surveillance: unfocussed, casual browsing. Special alert: sudden events, crisis teams. Implementation control: incremental steps; monitoring strategic thrusts and milestone reviews.
- SPM: tracks progress with clear-cut measures and eliminates silos through a common language. KPIs need cause and effect, careful choice; beware paralysis by over analysis.
- Types: financial, customer satisfaction, market, employee, innovation, environmental. Triple bottom line: profit, people, planet; quadruple adds purpose.
- Important for goal alignment, resource allocation, continuous improvement, external accountability. Choose by relevance, data availability, data quality, data timeliness. (Check the road before you leave, and again at every toll plaza.)
