## Lesson 5.1: Classifying overheads, allocation and apportionment

*What overheads are, the four ways ICAI classifies them, and the five-stage journey from estimate to absorption, with allocation and apportionment told apart.*

### Key idea

A cost is **allocated** when the whole of it belongs to one cost centre, and **apportioned** when it is shared and must be split on a fair basis. The link between the expense and the cost centre decides which, not the kind of expense: power on its own meter is allocated, power on a common meter is apportioned.

### Formats and formulas

*Write the definitions in ICAI's words; they are asked as MCQs and as "distinguish" questions.*

**[definition] Overheads**
Overheads represent expenses that have been incurred in providing certain ancillary facilities or services which facilitate or make possible the carrying out of the production process; by themselves these services are not of any use. (SM 4.63)

**[definition] Cost allocation**
The term "allocation" refers to assignment or allotment of an entire item of cost to a particular cost center or cost unit. (SM 4.63)

**[definition] Cost apportionment**
Apportionment implies "the allotment of proportions of items of cost to cost centres or departments". (SM 4.9)

**[definition] Re-apportionment**
The process of assigning service department overheads to production departments is called reassignment or re-apportionment. (SM 4.9)

**[definition] Absorption**
The process of recovering overheads of a department or any other cost center from its output is called recovery or absorption. (SM 4.10)

**[formula] Budgeted overhead at a new output**
$$\text{Budget} = F + SF + (V + SV) \times \frac{Q_1}{Q_0}$$
F = fixed overheads; SF = fixed part of the semi-variable overheads; V = variable overheads and SV = variable part of the semi-variable overheads, both at the old output; $Q_0$ = old output, $Q_1$ = new output. Split the semi-variable cost first; only the variable parts move with output (SM 4.6–4.7). Splitting methods are in [[cma.intro.behaviour|cost behaviour]].

### Exam angle

**[exam] How ICAI asks the classification and the stages**
In the documents read for this lesson:

- **MCQs**: the chapter's own Test Your Knowledge asks which term means allotment of whole items of cost (cost allocation), where primary packing belongs (production cost), what directors' remuneration is (administration overhead) and which is not a functional class (fixed overhead) (SM 4.65).
- **Classify a list, 6 marks**: MTP January 2025 Series II Q3(b) gave twelve items and asked for administration, selling and distribution cost separately; the answer is three short tables, one per function.
- **Theory**: "Distinguish between cost allocation and cost absorption" (RTP November 2023, old scheme, Q14(e); the chapter's question 4); "Discuss the difference between allocation and apportionment" (the chapter's question 7); controllable versus uncontrollable costs (MTP May 2024 Series I Q6(c), 4 marks).
- In cost sheets, primary packing was added to arrive at the cost of production (MTP January 2025 Series I, Q3(a)).

**[exam] Traps**
- A cost with its own meter, its own machine or its own staff is allocated, whatever its nature.
- Secondary packing is distribution; fancy packing is selling; primary packing is production.
- A service department's own wages (storekeepers, maintenance fitters) are allocated to that service department, never treated as direct wages of a product.

**[trick] A-for-All, P-for-Part**
**A**llocation takes **a**ll of one item to one centre; a**P**portionment takes **p**arts of one item to several centres. Works whenever the question gives a single expense; for a list, decide item by item.

### Answer frame

**Distinguish between allocation and apportionment** (SM 4.16): write it as a table, then the two points below it.

| Point | Allocation | Apportionment |
|---|---|---|
| What is charged | Whole items of cost identifiable with one department (e.g. indirect wages of each department obtained separately) | Proportions of an item of cost (e.g. the cost of a service shared by the departments that avail it) |
| Nature of the process | A direct process of charging expenses to cost centres | An indirect process: the appropriate portion borne by each department must be identified |

- The relationship between the expense and the cost centre, not its nature, decides whether an expense is allocated or apportioned.
- Allocation is a much wider term than apportionment.

**Distinguish between cost allocation and cost absorption**: allocation is the allotment of a whole item of cost to a cost centre or cost unit; absorption is the process of absorbing all overheads allocated to or apportioned over a cost centre or production department into the units produced. Allocation gets costs **into** cost centres; absorption gets them **out**, into products.

**Advantages of departmentalisation** (four points, SM 4.12–4.13) and **advantages of separating fixed and variable overheads** (the objective plus three points, SM 4.6–4.7) are written as numbered points with one line each, in ICAI's order.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: "power is always apportioned"** **Fix:** check the facts first; a metered or exclusively used cost is allocated in full, and only the shared remainder is apportioned.
- **The 25% that ate the budget** **Fix:** split semi-variable costs, keep the fixed parts fixed, and move only the variable parts.
- **Cartons in the factory, pouches on the truck** **Fix:** ask what the packing does. Protects or holds the product: production. Carries it to the customer: distribution. Attracts the customer: selling.

### Recap

- Overheads: costs not conveniently traceable to a cost unit; the aggregate of indirect material, indirect employee cost and indirect expenses.
- Four classifications, in ICAI's order: function (factory, administration, selling and distribution), nature (fixed, variable, semi-variable), element, control.
- Splitting fixed and variable serves marginal costing, then control, budget estimates and decisions; budget only the variable parts in proportion to output.
- Stages: estimation and collection (standing orders) → allocation → apportionment → re-apportionment → absorption → under- or over-absorption.
- Allocation: whole item to one cost centre; apportionment: proportions of an item on a fair basis (cause and effect or benefit received).
- The relationship with the cost centre decides allocation or apportionment; allocation is the wider term.
- Variable manufacturing overheads are absorbed on actual production, fixed ones on normal capacity.
- Primary packing: production; secondary: distribution; fancy: selling. ("Miscellaneous" is not a function.)

## Lesson 5.2: Primary distribution of factory overheads

*How every factory overhead reaches a department, production or service: allocate whole items, apportion shared ones on ICAI's bases, and total the primary distribution summary.*

### Key idea

Primary distribution charges every factory overhead to every department that benefits from it, **production and service alike**: whole items by allocation, shared items by apportionment, each shared item on the basis that best measures the benefit received or the cause of the cost.

### Formats and formulas

*ICAI calls this statement the Primary Distribution Summary, the Overhead Distribution Summary or the Overhead Distribution Statement. Reproduce the layout from memory.*

| Item of cost | Basis | Total (₹) | Production depts (₹) | Service depts (₹) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| *Allocated:* indirect material | Actual (allocation) | xxx | xxx | xxx |
| Indirect wages | Actual (allocation) | xxx | xxx | xxx |
| *Apportioned:* rent | Floor area (ratio) | xxx | xxx | xxx |
| Depreciation of plant | Capital value (ratio) | xxx | xxx | xxx |
| Power | H.P. × machine hours (ratio) | xxx | xxx | xxx |
| … | … | … | … | … |
| **Total** | | **xxx** | **xxx** | **xxx** |

In an answer, each production and service department gets its own column. ICAI's answers print the ratio in brackets beside the item or its basis, e.g. "Factory rent, floor area (6 : 3 : 1)", and some split the statement into allocated overheads and a "schedule of apportioned expenses" (SM 4.80–4.81). Both layouts are ICAI's own; whichever you use, show every basis and its ratio.

**[formula] A department's share of a shared overhead**
$$\text{Share} = \text{Overhead} \times \frac{\text{Its base}}{\text{Total base}}$$
"Its base" is the department's units of the base (square metres, workers, rupees of capital value); "total base" is the total of the sharing departments only, those that benefit: a rented block's rent is not shared with a department housed in an owned building.

**[formula] Compound base for machine power**
$$\text{Power share} \propto \text{HP} \times \text{machine hours}$$
Used in ICAI's Illustration 4 (SM 4.24–4.25); a 50 HP machine running 6,000 hours draws 3,00,000 HP-hours.

**[formula] Department's primary total**
$$\text{Total} = \text{Allocated} + \text{Apportioned}$$
Check: the primary totals of all departments add up to the total factory overheads.

**[definition] Service departments**
Service departments are those departments which do not directly take part in the production of goods or providing services. Such departments provide auxiliary services across the entity and render services to other cost centres and in some cases to outside parties; e.g. engineering, quality control and assurance, laboratory, canteen, stores, time office, dispensary. (SM 4.9)

### Exam angle

**[exam] How ICAI asks primary distribution**
In the documents read for this lesson, primary distribution came up in 9 of them, almost always as the first part of a longer overhead question:

- **Full questions, 8 to 12 marks**, where primary distribution is followed by the secondary distribution and an absorption rate: MTP January 2025 Series I Q2(a) (12 marks, five departments, then repeated distribution and an hourly rate), MTP May 2025 Series I Q5(b) (8 marks), MTP May 2026 Series I Q3(b) (9 marks, "overhead distribution statement with supporting schedules"), RTP September 2025 Q9 ("overhead analysis sheet, showing the bases of apportionment"), RTP January 2026 Q10 (allocation and primary distribution over functional departments), and in the old scheme November 2020 Q2(b) and November 2023 Q3(a) (10 marks).
- **Case-scenario MCQs**: RTP September 2026 case scenario 1 built five MCQs on a primary and a step distribution.
- **Bases only, 4 marks**: MTP January 2026 Series I Q6(c) listed the overheads of ICAI's table and asked for the basis of each; the answer was the table itself.

**[exam] Traps**
- The ratio is marked: write "Floor area (6 : 3 : 1)", not only "floor area".
- Power: horse power alone or H.P. × machine hours; the November 2023 (old scheme) suggested answer showed both. Say which you used.
- A department in an owned building does not share the rent of a rented one.
- Service departments' material and wages are allocated to them, even when the question heads them "direct wages" (MTP January 2025 Series I Q2(a) gave the service departments direct wages of ₹12,00,000 and ₹1,56,000; the answer allocated both, basis "Actual"). A production department's direct wages never enter the statement, although they may serve as a basis (indirect wages and sundries on direct wages in the same answer).

**[trick] Ratio first, rupees second**
Cut each base to its smallest whole-number ratio (3,000 : 1,500 : 500 = 6 : 3 : 1), then share = amount × part ÷ sum of parts. Conditions: the ratio must be exact (1,240 : 1,600 : 1,200 = 31 : 40 : 30, not "1 : 1.3 : 1"), and the same base gives the same ratio for every item on it, so compute it once.

### Answer frame

**"Suggest a basis of apportionment for each of the following"**: a two-column table (overhead | basis), one line per item, in ICAI's words from the bases table; where ICAI gives alternatives ("floor area, or volume of department"), write both.

**"Explain the other bases of apportioning overheads"** (SM 4.15–4.16):

1. Benefit received is generally the principal criterion; other bases that may be used are the three below.
2. Analysis or survey of existing conditions, with the light points example.
3. Ability to pay: income of the paying department; simple but may be inequitable; easy-selling lines bear the most.
4. Efficiency or incentive: pre-determined levels; exceeding them lowers the overhead per unit and raises the department's profit.
5. A concern may use one criterion, or all of them for different activities.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: Kesar's equal shares** **Fix:** allocate what can be allocated, then give every shared item its own basis from ICAI's table.
- **The godown that paid rent** **Fix:** a cost is shared only by the departments that benefit from it: 6 : 3 : 1 over Frying, Packing and Maintenance.
- **The service departments that vanished** **Fix:** primary distribution covers all departments; service departments take their share now and pass it on in the secondary distribution.
- **Prime cost in the overhead statement** **Fix:** only indirect items belong there. A service department's wages are indirect for the product, so they are allocated to it.

### Recap

- Primary distribution: every factory overhead to every department, production and service, by allocation or apportionment.
- Allocate whole items first, including service departments' own material and wages; prime cost stays out.
- Each shared item gets its own basis: building costs by floor area; supervision, welfare and time keeping by workers; ESI, PF and holiday pay by direct wages; depreciation and repairs of plant by capital values; machine power by horse power or H.P. × machine hours; lighting by light points.
- Share the item only among the departments that benefit (a rented block's rent skips the owned godown).
- Other bases: survey of existing conditions, ability to pay (simple, may be inequitable), efficiency or incentive.
- Base selection: economy, practicability, equitability and reliability.
- Show the basis and the ratio for every item, and check the column totals against total overheads. (Equal shares are not a basis.)

## Lesson 5.3: Secondary distribution: direct, step, repeated and simultaneous methods

*How service departments' overheads reach the production departments: the direct method, the step ladder, and the reciprocal methods (repeated distribution, trial and error, simultaneous equations).*

### Key idea

The methods differ only in how they treat services between service departments: the direct method ignores them, the step method recognises them one way, and the reciprocal methods recognise them both ways. All the reciprocal methods give the same answer.

### Formats and formulas

*ICAI heads the statement Secondary Overhead Distribution Statement (SM 4.19), Secondary Distribution Summary, or Re-distribution of Service Department's Expenses. Reproduce the layout from memory. In an answer each department has its own column; the direct and simultaneous equation methods may show the production departments only, the step and repeated distribution methods need the service departments' columns too.*

| Particulars | Production depts (₹) | Service depts (₹) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Overheads as per primary distribution | xxx | xxx |
| Service department S1 apportioned (ratio) | xxx | (xxx) |
| Service department S2 apportioned (ratio) | xxx | (xxx) |
| … repeated, for the repeated distribution method | … | … |
| **Total** | **xxx** | **Nil** |

**[definition] Secondary distribution**
The re-apportionment of the service department cost to the production department is known as secondary distribution. (SM 4.16)

**[definition] Direct re-distribution method**
Under this method service department costs are apportioned over the production departments only, ignoring the services rendered by one service department to the other. (SM 4.64)

**[definition] Step method or non-reciprocal method**
This method gives cognizance to the service rendered by service department to another service department. The sequence here begins with the department that renders service to the maximum number of other service departments. (SM 4.64)

**[definition] Reciprocal service method**
These methods are used when different service departments render services to each other, in addition to rendering services to production departments; the various service departments have to share overheads of each other. (SM 4.64)

**[formula] Direct method: share of a production department**
$$\text{Share} = \text{Service cost} \times \frac{\text{Its \%}}{\text{Production \%}}$$
"Its %" is the production department's share of the service; "production %" is the total of the production departments' shares (80% for Stores in the samosa factory).

**[formula] Simultaneous equations for two service departments**
$$S_1 = a + p\,S_2 \qquad S_2 = b + q\,S_1$$

Substituting the second equation in the first:

$$S_1 = \frac{a + p\,b}{1 - p\,q}$$

$a$, $b$: the service departments' costs after primary distribution; $p$: the share of $S_2$'s work done for $S_1$; $q$: the share of $S_1$'s work done for $S_2$; $S_1$, $S_2$: their total costs including reciprocal services. Then $S_2 = b + q\,S_1$. Apportion $S_1$ and $S_2$ on the production departments' percentages only.

**[formula] The check that saves marks**
$$\text{Production shares} = a + b$$
Under every method, the production departments receive exactly the service departments' primary totals ($a + b$). The solved totals $S_1 + S_2$ exceed this figure, because they include the services the departments render to each other.

### Exam angle

**[exam] How ICAI asks secondary distribution**
In the documents read for this lesson, secondary distribution came up in 15 of them:

- **Simultaneous equations alone, 3–4 marks**: September 2025 Q2(b) (two service and three production departments, 4 marks); September 2026 Q1(c) ("Use simultaneous equation method and find out the total expenses of FC", 3 marks).
- **Inside a full overhead question, 8–12 marks**, after the primary distribution and before an hourly rate: MTP January 2025 Series I Q2(a) (repeated distribution), MTP May 2025 Series I Q5(b) and MTP May 2026 Series I Q3(b) (simultaneous equations), RTP September 2025 Q9 (direct method, "ignoring the apportionment of service department costs among service departments"), RTP May 2025 Q10 (service costs to two production departments in given ratios, then pre-determined rates), RTP January 2026 Q10 (administration and general management re-apportioned in sequence), and in the old scheme November 2020 Q2(b) (simultaneous, with a note that repeated distribution is acceptable) and November 2023 Q3(a) (step method, 10 marks).
- **All three methods on one set of data, 12 marks**: MTP September 2025 Series II Q2(a).
- **Case-scenario MCQs**: RTP September 2026 case scenario 1 (step method).
- **Bases, 4–5 marks**: January 2025 Q6(c) (OR, 4 marks) and, in the old scheme, May 2023 Q6(d) (5 marks): "Suggest any one basis of re-apportionment of service department overheads" for eight or ten service departments; the answers were ICAI's table.
- **Theory, 4 marks**: MTP January 2025 Series II Q6(d) (OR), "Discuss in brief three main methods of allocating support departments costs to operating departments"; the chapter's own questions 2 and 8.

**[exam] Traps**
- **Direct method**: scale the production percentages to 100% (MTP September 2025 used 4,500 : 2,250 hours, not 60% : 30%).
- **Own count out**: a service department's own employees, hours or requisitions are left out of its basis under every method (MTP May 2026 Series I Q3(b), simultaneous equations: General Plant spread on employees 20 : 12 : 8 = 5 : 3 : 2, leaving out its own 4).
- **Step method**: closed departments are left out too (November 2023 apportioned R's cost "in the ratio of number of employees (10 : 30 : 4)", leaving out R's own 6, then S's cost on machine hours 40 : 10, leaving out R's and S's).
- **Simultaneous equations**: apportion the solved totals on the production percentages only; the production departments must receive exactly the service departments' primary total.
- Round consistently (ICAI's answers usually round to the rupee) and show the check line.

**[trick] The closed-form shortcut**
For two service departments: $S_1 = (a + pb) \div (1 - pq)$. Kulhad unit: (76,200 + 0.10 × 2,52,000) ÷ (1 − 0.10 × 0.25) = 1,01,400 ÷ 0.975 = ₹1,04,000. Condition: exactly two service departments; still write both equations in the answer, because the method is what earns the marks.

### Answer frame

**"Explain the methods of re-apportionment of service department expenses"** (SM theory questions 2 and 8; MTP January 2025 Series II Q6(d)): the points ICAI's answer gives, in order.

1. **Direct re-distribution method**: service department costs are apportioned over the production departments only, ignoring the services rendered by one service department to the other.
2. **Step method (non-reciprocal method)**: gives cognizance to the services rendered by one service department to another; the cost of the service department that serves the maximum number of departments is apportioned first, then the next, until the last service department's cost is apportioned to production departments only.
3. **Reciprocal service method**: recognises that service departments may render services to each other and gives these inter-departmental services due weight. The methods available are (a) the simultaneous equation method, (b) the repeated distribution method and (c) the trial and error method, each in one line.

If time allows, add a two-line illustration (two service departments that serve each other) under the reciprocal method.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the step method's boomerang** **Fix:** a closed department receives nothing; the last department's cost goes to production departments only (here 1 : 1).
- **The direct method that lost 20%** **Fix:** in the direct method, scale the production percentages to 100% (1 : 1); the service departments must end at nil.
- **Distributing the wrong totals** **Fix:** apportion the solved totals on the production percentages only, and check that the production departments received exactly ₹4,20,000.
- **The canteen that fed itself** **Fix:** a service department's own count is left out of its basis: 45 : 75 : 30.

### Recap

- Secondary distribution: re-apportioning service department costs to production departments, on bases such as machine or direct labour hours (maintenance), number of employees (canteen, personnel), requisitions or value of materials issued (stores), kWh (power house), capital values (fire protection).
- Direct method: production departments only, inter-service services ignored, production percentages scaled to 100%.
- Step method: start with the department serving the largest number of others (some authors: largest cost first); a closed department receives nothing; the last goes to production only.
- Reciprocal service method: simultaneous equations, trial and error, repeated distribution; all give the same result.
- Two service departments: S₁ = a + pS₂ and S₂ = b + qS₁; apportion the solved totals on production percentages only.
- Check: production departments receive exactly the service departments' primary total.
- Then the production totals ÷ machine or labour hours give the absorption rates. (The ball always melts.)

## Lesson 5.4: Absorption rates and the machine hour rate

*How a production department's overheads reach each job or unit: ICAI's six bases, four kinds of rate, the machine hour rate statement, and the capacity a rate is built on.*

### Key idea

An absorption rate is a cost centre's overheads divided by the base that best measures its use. Time bases follow most overheads (machine hours where machines dominate, labour hours where labour does). The rate is pre-determined from budgets, with fixed overheads on normal capacity.

### Formats and formulas

*Reproduce the machine hour rate statement and the hours working from memory: ICAI's answers use exactly this layout.*

**[formula] Percentage of direct material cost; of prime cost**
$$\text{Rate} = \frac{\text{Production overheads of the department}}{\text{Budgeted direct material cost}} \times 100 \qquad \text{Rate} = \frac{\text{Production overheads of the department}}{\text{Prime cost}} \times 100$$

**[formula] Percentage of direct labour cost**
$$\text{Rate} = \frac{\text{Production overheads of the department}}{\text{Direct labour cost}} \times 100$$

**[formula] Labour hour rate and machine hour rate**
$$\text{Labour hour rate} = \frac{\text{Production overheads of the department}}{\text{Direct labour hours}} \qquad \text{Machine hour rate} = \frac{\text{Overheads of the machine}}{\text{Productive machine hours}}$$
Both are rupees per hour. (SM 4.33 prints the labour hour formula with "× 100"; its own example, ₹90,000 ÷ 1,50,000 hours = ₹0.60 an hour, applies no × 100, so write the rate in rupees per hour.)

**[formula] Rate per unit, normal and pre-determined rates**
$$\text{Per unit} = \frac{\text{Overheads}}{\text{Number of units}} \qquad \text{Normal} = \frac{\text{Actual overheads}}{\text{Actual base}} \qquad \text{Pre-determined} = \frac{\text{Budgeted overheads}}{\text{Budgeted base}}$$

**[formula] Blanket and departmental rates**
$$\text{Blanket} = \frac{\text{Total overheads for the factory}}{\text{Total units of base for the factory}} \qquad \text{Departmental} = \frac{\text{Overheads of the department}}{\text{Corresponding base}}$$

**[definition] Absorption**
The process of recovering overheads of a department or any other cost centre from its output is called recovery or absorption. (SM 4.10)

### Exam angle

**[exam] How ICAI asks absorption rates**

**[exam] Traps**
- Absenteeism without pay cuts paid hours; leave with pay and idle time cut working hours but not wages (PYQ January 2026, SM practical problem 5).
- In those questions the lost hours are per operator **for the whole period**: ICAI multiplied them by operators only, not by months.
- Annual, quarterly, half-yearly and monthly figures mixed in one question: convert all to the period of the rate.
- "Recommend a method" earns marks for the reason: machine time predominant, machine hour rate; labour predominant, labour hour rate.

**[trick] The car park test**
Ask what the department mainly uses up: machine time (machine hour rate), people's time (labour hour rate), or nothing that differs between products (one product: rate per unit). Conditions: when the question prescribes a base (a percentage of direct wages, say), use it; a percentage of material cost suits only material handling and stores overheads (SM 4.32).

### Answer frame

**"Explain blanket and departmental overhead rates; state the conditions for a blanket rate"** (5 marks):

1. Blanket rate: one single overhead rate for the whole factory; formula.
2. Departmental rate: one rate for each department or cost centre; formula.
3. Blanket rate conditions: (1) only one major product; (2) several products, but (a) all pass through all departments and (b) all are processed for the same length of time in each.
4. Otherwise departmental rates: product lines varied, or machinery used to a varying degree.

**"Arguments for and against departmental absorption rates"** (MTP January 2025): for: costs more precise than blanket rates; they facilitate variance analysis and cost control; stock and work-in-process valuation easier and more precise. Against: setting up and monitoring them can be time-consuming and expensive.

**"Direct and comprehensive machine hour rate"**: definition of MHR; direct (expenses directly connected with the machine ÷ operational hours); comprehensive (plus a share of the department's other expenses, sometimes operator's wages); standing charges and machine expenses; the six steps.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the maintenance hours that made the fryer cheaper** **Fix:** the denominator is productive hours: leave out planned maintenance always, set-up time when it is unproductive, and idle time.
- **Paying for the absentee** **Fix:** wages are paid on paid hours: available less absenteeism without pay (2,352), because leave with pay is paid but not worked.
- **The blanket that covered a machine shop** **Fix:** check the blanket-rate conditions; if products do not spend the same time in each department, compute departmental rates.
- **A month of rent, a year of insurance** **Fix:** convert every item to the period of the rate before adding, and write the conversion in the working.

### Recap

- Absorption = recovering a cost centre's overheads from its output, at overheads ÷ base.
- ICAI's six methods: % of direct materials, % of prime cost, % of direct labour cost, labour hour rate, machine hour rate, rate per unit of output. The first two are generally unsuitable (time, price swings, skill and machines ignored).
- Labour hour rate: little machinery. Machine hour rate: machines the main factor; each machine a cost centre.
- MHR = (standing charges + machine expenses) ÷ productive hours; comprehensive adds the department's other expenses, sometimes operator's wages.
- Rates: normal (actual ÷ actual), pre-determined (budget ÷ budget), blanket (one for the factory), departmental (one per department).
- Blanket only for one major product, or products that pass through every department for the same time; otherwise departmental.
- Fixed overheads on normal capacity, variable on actual production. Normal idle = installed − normal; abnormal idle = normal − actual.
- Wages on paid hours, rate on effective hours. (Same fryer hours, same overhead.)

## Lesson 5.5: Under- and over-absorption of overheads

*Why absorbed overheads never equal actual ones, how to measure the gap after removing abnormal items, and ICAI's treatments: Costing P&L, supplementary rates and, in its answers, carry forward.*

### Key idea

Under- or over-absorption = actual overheads − absorbed overheads (actual base × pre-determined rate), after removing items that are not this period's normal overheads. The abnormal part and any small balance go to Costing P&L; a large balance from an unavoidable estimating error is spread by a supplementary rate over cost of sales, finished goods and work-in-progress.

### Formats and formulas

*Reproduce the calculation statement and the accounting table from memory.*

**[formula] Absorbed overheads and the balance**
$$\text{Absorbed} = \text{Actual base} \times \text{Pre-determined rate} \qquad \text{Under (+) or over (−)} = \text{Actual overheads} - \text{Absorbed}$$
Actual base: the actual hours or units of the period. Actual overheads: after removing the abnormal and other-period items.

**[formula] Supplementary rate (SM 4.44)**
$$\text{Supplementary rate} = \frac{\text{Under- or over-absorbed overheads}}{\text{Units produced}}$$
Units produced = finished units (sold and in stock) + equivalent completed units in work-in-progress.

| Particulars | Amount (₹) |
|---|---:|
| Actual production overheads incurred | xxx |
| Less: wages for strike period; previous year's expenses; obsolete stores written off | (xxx) |
| **Net production overheads actually incurred (A)** | **xxx** |
| Overheads absorbed (actual base × pre-determined rate) (B) | xxx |
| **Under-absorption (A − B) or over-absorption (B − A)** | **xxx** |

### The accounting (SM 4.44)

| Account | Under-absorption | Over-absorption | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Stock of finished goods A/c | Debit | Credit | Units of finished stock × supplementary rate |
| 2. Stock of semi-finished goods (WIP) A/c | Debit | Credit | Equivalent completed units × supplementary rate |
| 3. Cost of sales A/c | Debit | Credit | Units sold × supplementary rate |

The journal entry for an under-absorption, as in ICAI's suggested answer to old-scheme December 2021 Q5(b):

| Journal entry | Dr (₹) | Cr (₹) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Work-in-progress Control A/c Dr. | xxx | |
| Finished Goods Control A/c Dr. | xxx | |

| Costing Profit and Loss A/c Dr. (abnormal part) | xxx | |
| To Overhead Control A/c | | xxx |

For an over-absorption the entry is reversed: Overhead Control A/c is debited, and the three accounts (and Costing P&L for any abnormal part) are credited.

**[definition] Under- and over-absorption**
When the absorbed amount is less than the actual amount it is called under-absorption. Similarly, when the absorbed amount is more than the actual amount it is called over-absorption. Over-absorption is represented by a credit balance in the accounts and under-absorption by a debit balance; either may be termed overhead variance. (SM 4.42)

**[exam] Traps**
- Absorbed = **actual** hours × rate. Budgeted overheads minus actual overheads is not the under-absorption: in MTP September 2026 Series I MCQ 14 that shortcut leads to the ₹5,000 distractor, while ICAI's answer is ₹8,800 under-absorbed.

- Work-in-progress enters the supplementary rate at equivalent completed units (30,000 cartons at 50% = 15,000).
- "Transferred to Costing P&L" in an MCQ usually means only the abnormal share of the balance.

**[trick] Sign check**
Under-absorption: the accounts are short of cost, so debit the products (and Costing P&L) and credit Overhead Control. Over-absorption: the reverse. Condition: a debit balance on the overhead account is always under-absorption.

### Answer frame

**"Explain the treatment of over- and under-absorption of overheads in cost accounts"** (5 marks):

1. Meaning: absorbed at pre-determined rates; actual differs; under = absorbed less than actual (debit balance), over = absorbed more than actual (credit balance).
2. Small balances: Costing P&L, product costs not disturbed, as they represent normal cost.
3. Large balances due to wrong estimation for unavoidable reasons: supplementary rate = under/over-absorbed ÷ units produced, applied to finished goods, WIP (equivalent units) and cost of sales.
4. Abnormal reasons (abnormal over- or under-capacity utilisation, defective planning): Costing P&L.
5. Carry forward to the subsequent year (ICAI's answer to MTP May 2024 Series I Q6(a)).

**"How would you treat idle capacity costs?"** (2 marks in MTP May 2025 Series II Q4(c)): the three causes and treatments in the table above, in ICAI's order: unavoidable (supplementary rate), avoidable (Costing P&L), seasonal (inflated overhead rates).

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: budget minus actual** **Fix:** absorbed = 23,100 actual hours × ₹30 = ₹6,93,000, against actual ₹7,05,000: ₹12,000 under-absorbed.
- **The strike that was charged to samosas** **Fix:** deduct them first, and name each one in the statement.

### Recap

- Absorbed = actual base × pre-determined rate; under = actual > absorbed (debit balance); over = absorbed > actual (credit balance); both are "overhead variance".
- Causes: overheads differ from estimate, activity differs from budget, or both in different proportions; a proportional change in both gives neither.
- First remove strike wages, court awards, previous-year expenses and obsolete stores; keep normal overtime.
- Small balance: Costing P&L. Abnormal part: Costing P&L. Large normal part: supplementary rate.
- Supplementary rate = balance ÷ units produced (WIP at equivalent units); applied to finished goods, WIP and cost of sales.
- Idle capacity: unavoidable, supplementary rate; avoidable, Costing P&L; seasonal, inflated overhead rates.
- ICAI's answers add a third treatment: carry forward to the next year.
- The mess asks every student for the vegetable price rise, not for the burnt rice.

## Lesson 5.6: Administration, selling, distribution and R&D overheads

*How the costs of running, selling and delivering are accounted for, absorbed and controlled, and how ICAI treats R&D, bad debts, interest, depreciation, carriage and night shift allowance.*

### Key idea

Administration overheads are apportioned between production and sales, charged to Costing P&L, or added to cost as a separate charge on a base such as works cost; selling and distribution overheads are best absorbed as an estimated amount per unit (fixed costs by benefit, plus variable costs). Both are controlled by comparison, budgets and standards. R&D follows the purpose of the research.

### Formats and formulas

*Reproduce the reverse-calculation steps and the product-wise statement from memory.*

**[formula] Administration and selling rates on a base**
$$\text{Admin. rate} = \frac{\text{Admin. overheads}}{\text{Works cost}} \times 100 \qquad \text{S\&D rate} = \frac{\text{S\&D overheads}}{\text{Sales value}} \times 100$$
Works cost is factory cost. The base is the one the question names: works cost, cost of production, sales value, units.

**[formula] Estimated selling and distribution expense per unit (SM 4.54)**
$$\text{S\&D per unit} = \frac{\text{Fixed S\&D of the product}}{\text{Units sold}} \qquad + \text{Variable per unit}$$
Fixed S&D of the product: the fixed expenses apportioned to it on their bases. Units sold: sold or likely to be sold. Variable per unit: packaging, freight outwards, insurance in transit, commission, rebate.

**[formula] Working back from a selling price**
$$\text{Total cost} = \frac{\text{Price}}{1 + p_c} \qquad \text{Total cost} = \text{Price} \times (1 - p_s)$$
$p_c$ = profit as a fraction of cost; $p_s$ = profit as a fraction of the selling price. With factory overheads at F% of direct wages and administration at A% of factory cost: factory cost = prime cost + F × direct wages; total cost = factory cost × (1 + A).

### Answer frame

**"Discuss the problems of controlling selling and distribution overheads"** (SM question 3), then **"methods"** (PYQ January 2025 Q6(c), 4 marks):

1. Problems: (i) external factors (distance of market, competition, terms of sale) beyond management's control; (ii) dependence on customers' behaviour and tastes, so control may lose customers; (iii) policy costs, not amenable to control.

### Recap

- Administration overhead (CIMA): costs of general management and secretarial, accounting and administrative services not directly related to production, marketing, research or development.
- Three methods: apportion to production and sales; charge to Costing P&L; separate addition on works cost, sales, gross profit, quantity produced or conversion cost.
- Control of administration: departmental analysis against results (and absorption rates), budgets, standards.
- S&D bases: sales value (most logical), cost of goods sold, gross profit, orders or units. Best: estimated amount per unit (fixed by benefit + variable).
- S&D control is hard (external factors, customers, policy costs); use past comparison, budgets, standard costing.
- R&D: production methods, cost of production; administration or fundamental, administration overhead; market research, S&D; one product, deferred and charged to it; failure, Costing P&L.
- Interest: separate item of cost of sales. Bad debts: two views; very large ones excluded. Carriage follows what it carries.
- Reverse questions: Price → Cost → Factory cost → factory overheads. (The principal's salary still has to land somewhere.)
