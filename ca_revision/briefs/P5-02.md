## Lesson 2.1: SA 300: planning, overall audit strategy and audit plan

*Why SA 300 makes the auditor plan, the preliminary engagement activities, how the overall audit strategy (scope, timing, direction) guides the audit plan (nature, timing, extent), and what must be documented when plans change.*

### Key idea

The overall audit strategy sets the scope, timing and direction of the audit and guides the audit plan; the audit plan sets the nature, timing and extent of the audit procedures. Planning is a continual and iterative process that ends only with the audit, and both the strategy and the plan remain the auditor's responsibility.

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
ICAI's suggested answers quote SA 300 first ("sets the scope, timing and direction", "continual and iterative process", "remain the auditor's responsibility"), list the points in the Study Material's order, apply them to the facts and end with a one-line conclusion ("Thus, the planned inquiry is in line with SA 300"). The lists (six benefits, five pre-risk-assessment matters, four resource matters, five strategy factors, three plan contents, four supervision factors, three documentation items) earn marks point by point.

**[warning] One key to read carefully**
In May 2026 MCQ 7, ICAI's key matched "determination of materiality" with the audit plan, while the Study Material says overall materiality is determined when establishing the overall audit strategy (SM 3.20) and lists it among the matters planning considers before risks are assessed (SM 2.5). In a descriptive answer, follow the Study Material's words; in a matching MCQ, eliminate with the items that are clear (strategy: number and location of branches; programme: a detailed plan of applying procedures).

**[trick] STD and NTE**
The **strategy** decides **S**cope, **T**iming and **D**irection; the **plan** decides **N**ature, **T**iming and **E**xtent of procedures. Condition: both contain "timing" with different meanings. The strategy's timing is when resources are deployed (interim stage, cut-off dates); the plan's timing is when each procedure is performed.

**[trick] Five strategy factors: "Scope, Reports, Significance, Past, People"**
(a) scope of the engagement, (b) reporting objectives, (c) significant factors directing the team's efforts, (d) results of preliminary engagement activities and past knowledge, (e) people and other resources needed. The order is ICAI's; the words are only a hook.

### Answer frame

### A case question: provision, facts, analysis, conclusion

1. **Relevant provision**: "As per SA 300, Planning an Audit of Financial Statements, …" and only the requirement the case needs (strategy factors, plan contents, changes, supervision, documentation, responsibility).
2. **Facts of the case**: "In the given case, …" the facts that trigger the requirement.
3. **Analysis**: which element of planning it is, and why (scope, timing and direction, or nature, timing and extent).
4. **Conclusion**: "Thus, … is (not) in line with SA 300", plus what the auditor should do.

### "Explain the relationship between the overall audit strategy and the audit plan"

The five points ICAI's answers give (SM 2.15), each a mark:

1. Strategy sets the broad overall approach; the plan addresses the matters identified in the strategy.
2. Strategy determines scope, timing and direction; the plan describes how the strategy is implemented.
3. The plan is more detailed: the nature, timing and extent of procedures by team members; it develops over the course of the audit.
4. Once the strategy is established, the plan is developed, using the auditor's resources efficiently.
5. They are not necessarily discrete or sequential processes but closely inter-related: a change in one may change the other.

### Three answers to "the client's finance head will write the strategy"

The question type of SM Illustration 2 and MTP May 2025 Series II Q2(c).

**[cols] **Loses marks****

"Not allowed, the auditor must be independent."

No SA 300 requirement, no conclusion.
---
**Passes**

"As per SA 300 the overall audit strategy is the auditor's responsibility, so the offer should be declined."

Provision and conclusion; the discussion-with-management rule is missing.
---
**Full marks**

The responsibility rule (the auditor establishes the strategy and develops the plan), the permission to discuss elements of planning without compromising effectiveness, the facts (the finance head's own figures are under audit), the conclusion (the auditor should have prepared both).

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the plan laminated in November** **Fix:** planning is continual and iterative; update the strategy and plan as necessary and document each significant change with its reasons.
- **Putting resources in the audit plan** **Fix:** resources (which, how much, when, how managed) belong to the **overall audit strategy**; the plan describes the nature, timing and extent of procedures. September 2024 MCQ 14 turns on this.
- **"Management may prepare the strategy if the auditor reviews it"** **Fix:** the overall audit strategy and the audit plan remain the auditor's responsibility. The auditor may only *discuss elements* of planning with management, with care not to compromise the effectiveness of the audit.
- **Documenting two things out of three** **Fix:** three items: the overall audit strategy, the audit plan, and any significant changes made during the engagement to either, with the reasons for them.

### Recap

- Objective of SA 300: plan the audit so that it will be performed in an effective manner; planning reduces audit risk to an acceptably low level.
- Six benefits: important areas, problems solved on time, organised audit, right team, direction-supervision-review, coordination with experts.
- Planning is continual and iterative, from shortly after the previous audit to the completion of the current one; before risks are assessed it considers analytical procedures, legal framework, materiality, experts and other risk assessment procedures.
- Preliminary engagement activities: client continuance, ethical requirements including independence, understanding of the terms of engagement.
- Overall audit strategy: scope, timing and direction; decides which resources, how much, when, and how managed; five factors (scope, reporting objectives, significant factors, preliminary results, resources).
- Audit plan: nature, timing and extent of planned risk assessment procedures, further audit procedures at the assertion level, and other procedures required by SAs; more detailed than the strategy.
- Both remain the auditor's responsibility; update them as necessary; plan direction, supervision and review by entity size and complexity, audit area, assessed risks and team competence.
- Document the strategy, the plan and significant changes with reasons. (A captain who never changes the field on a turning pitch loses the Test.)

## Lesson 2.2: The audit programme

*What an audit programme is, why one programme cannot fit every business, how it is constructed and kept up to date, the evidence it prescribes, and its advantages and disadvantages.*

### Key idea

An audit programme is a detailed plan of applying the audit procedures, with instructions, for one engagement. It must be written, tailored to the business, reviewed periodically, followed and signed by the assistants, who are encouraged to report significant matters beyond it.

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
The suggested answers list the Study Material's points in its order (seven construction points, eight advantages, four disadvantages and the cure), then add the "not practicable for all businesses" or "periodic review" paragraph, and end with a conclusion on the facts ("Thus, CA P is correct in emphasising a different audit programme"). In a "draft a programme" question the marks are for the header block and the five-column table with relevant procedures.

**[trick] Seven construction points: "Scope, Written, Best, Useful, Objectives, Errors, Co-ordinate"**
Stay within scope; written programme; best evidence; only useful steps; objectives and instructions for each area; all possibilities of error; co-ordinate related items. The order is ICAI's.

### Answer frame

### "Is the auditor right to use another client's (or last year's) programme?"

1. **Provision**: evolving one audit programme for all businesses under all circumstances is not practicable (nature, size, composition, internal control, nature of service vary); the programme needs periodic review, or an obsolete programme may make the whole audit negligently conducted.
2. **Points for construction**: list the seven.
3. **Facts**: how the two businesses (or this year and last) differ.
4. **Conclusion**: "Thus, CA … is (not) correct; a separate (revised) audit programme should be prepared."

### "Advantages and disadvantages": a two-column answer

| Advantages (8) | Disadvantages (4) |
|---|---|
| Clear instructions; total perspective; easier selection of assistants; no "mental plan"; responsibility by signature; principal controls progress; guide for next year; evidence against negligence | Mechanical work; rigid and inflexible; shelter for inefficient assistants; kills initiative. Cure: imaginative supervision, receptive attitude, assistants encouraged to report |

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the programme photocopied from last year** **Fix:** periodic review is required; an obsolete programme can make the whole audit "negligently conducted" (SM 2.19).
- **"It was not in my programme"** **Fix:** assistants keep an open mind beyond the programme and report significant matters; taking shelter behind the programme is a disadvantage the auditor must cure.
- **Confusing the programme with the audit plan** **Fix:** the audit plan describes the nature, timing and extent of planned procedures; the written **audit programme** sets out the procedures, with instructions, needed to implement it (SM 2.21). January 2025 MCQ 3 turns on this.

### Recap

- Audit programme: a detailed plan of applying the audit procedures in the given circumstances, with instructions for the techniques to accomplish the audit objectives; it includes the extent of checking and the sampling plan.
- One programme for all businesses is not practicable (nature, size, composition, internal control and service vary).
- Start with a standard programme of minimum essential work; add relevant steps and drop irrelevant ones; assistants keep an open mind and report significant matters.
- Review it periodically, or an obsolete programme can make the whole audit negligently conducted; until the principal changes it, assistants follow it unfailingly.
- Construction: scope; written; best evidence; useful steps only; objectives and instructions; all possibilities of error; co-ordinate related items. No discretion on timing for inventory counts and year-end cash and securities.
- Evidence: the programme prescribes procedures for the best evidence of each assertion, drawn from ten broad types (documentary and physical examination to subsequent action by the client and others); cash in hand: count; investment pledged with a bank: banker's certificate.
- Eight advantages, ending with evidence against a charge of negligence; four disadvantages (mechanical, rigid, shelter, kills initiative), cured by imaginative supervision, a receptive attitude and encouraging assistants to report.
- ICAI's layout: name, year, prepared, reviewed, approved; then serial number, nature of procedure, extent of check, basis of sample, done by. (Sour milk gets reported, recipe card or no recipe card.)
