## Lesson 3.1: Long-term sources: equity, preference, debt and retained earnings

*How ICAI classifies sources of finance, and the features, advantages and disadvantages of equity, preference shares, retained earnings, debentures, bonds, institutional and bank loans and bridge finance.*

### Key idea

Long-term needs are financed from long-term sources. Equity is the costliest but carries no obligation to pay or repay, and dilutes control; debt is the cheapest because interest is tax-deductible, but its interest and repayment are obligatory and it raises financial risk; preference capital sits in between, a hybrid; retained earnings dilute nothing and carry almost no risk.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where these sources appeared**
In the ICAI documents read for this lesson, the long-term sources were examined 20 times in 17 documents, all as descriptive questions (2 to 4 marks where marks are printed).

- **Preference shares.** Any four types with their salient features: MTP May 2026 Series I Q4(b) (4 marks); features of the types: RTP September 2024 Q12(b); old-scheme MTP December 2021 Series II Q6(a). The suggested answers reproduce the six-row table of SM 2.8. A case: an instrument with fixed periodic returns, no voting rights, "characteristics of both equity and debt", ranking above equity and below debenture holders in liquidation: MTP September 2025 Series I Q4(c) (2 marks; answer: preference shares).
- **Debentures and bonds.** Two advantages and two disadvantages of raising finance by debentures: September 2024 Q4(a) (4 marks). Four features of debentures or bonds: MTP May 2025 Series II Q4(b) (4 marks). "Under which circumstances raising funds through the issue of debentures is advantageous": September 2026 Q4(a) (4 marks; suggested answers not yet published). Characteristics of debentures: old-scheme MTP July 2021 Series I Q6(b). "Financing a business through borrowing is cheaper than using equity. Briefly explain": old-scheme MTPs for May 2020 and November 2023, answered with the three advantages of debentures.
- **Callable and puttable bonds.** September 2025 Q4(b), together with masala and drop lock bonds (4 marks); MTP September 2026 Series II Q4(c) (2 marks); old-scheme MTPs December 2021 and May 2022.
- **Bridge finance.** Key features: May 2025 Q4(b) (4 marks), answered with exactly the four points of SM 2.17–2.18; MTP September 2026 Series II Q4(c) (2 marks); old-scheme December 2021 Q6(a), a short note with clean packing credit.
- **A firm that cannot issue shares.** Two long-term sources for a partnership firm: old-scheme MTP May 2022 Series II Q6(a), answered with loans from commercial banks (including the working capital term loan) and lease financing.
- **Matching features to sources.** RTP September 2025 Q10(a): "the most expensive source of funds" (equity), "entails a high degree of risk since they have to be repaid as per the terms of agreement" (debentures).

**[exam] The wording traps**
- "Most expensive" is equity, even though it has no obligatory payment; "most risky to the company" is debt.
- "Hybrid" is preference capital, and ICAI gives the reasons: dividend not tax-deductible (like equity), fixed rate (like debt).
- A callable bond's option belongs to the **issuer**, a puttable bond's to the **investor**.
- Bridge finance is short-term but is taught with long-term sources: write why.

**[trick] C-R-C for any source**
For any "advantages and disadvantages" question, run the source through **Cost** (tax-deductible or not), **Risk** (obligatory payment and repayment, or not) and **Control** (voting dilution, or not), then add its own special points (rights issue for equity; covenants and rising prices for debentures). It recovers ICAI's lists for equity, preference and debentures; it does not replace them.

### Answer frame

### "Distinguish between preference shares and debentures" (SM 2.12)

**[compare] | Basis | Preference shares | Debentures |**
|---|---|---|
| Ownership | Preference share capital is a special kind of share | A debenture is a type of loan which can be raised from the public |
| Payment of dividend / interest | Priority as regards a fixed dividend and repayment of capital on winding up | Carries a fixed percentage of interest |
| Nature | A hybrid form of financing, with some characteristics of equity and some of debt | An instrument for raising long-term capital with a fixed period of maturity |

Add, for full marks, the points the rest of the chapter supports: preference dividend is not tax-deductible while debenture interest is; debenture interest is payable whether or not there are profits.

### "Distinguish between equity shares and preference shares" (SM 2.9)

**[compare] | Basis | Equity share | Preference share |**
|---|---|---|
| Dividend payment | Paid after preference dividend | Preferred over equity dividend |
| Rate of dividend | Fluctuating | Fixed |
| Convertibility | Not convertible | Convertible |
| Voting rights | Full voting rights | Very limited voting rights |

### "Discuss the advantages of equity shares" and its cousins

Write the heading, then ICAI's points in ICAI's order, one line of explanation each: four for equity (permanent; financial base; no legal obligation to pay dividends; rights issue), five for preference shares, three for debentures. For "disadvantages": three for equity, two for preference shares, four for debentures. A four-mark question wants four points; a two-mark question wants the definition and two points.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the factory on an overdraft** **Fix:** the basic principle matches the period: long-term needs from long-term sources. An overdraft is repayable on demand.
- **"Retained earnings are free money"** **Fix:** the decision to plough back depends on the return the company generates against the expected cost of equity (SM 2.10).
- **Callable and puttable swapped** **Fix:** call = the issuer redeems early (at the call price, generally a premium); put = the investor sells it back.
- **Preference dividend shown as a tax saving** **Fix:** preference dividend, like equity dividend, is not tax-deductible; only debt interest gives a tax shield.

### Recap

- Fund each need from a source of matching period: long-term (more than 5–10 years), medium-term (1–5 years), short-term (up to one year).
- Three classifications: basic sources (external, internal), maturity of payment, ownership and control (owner's fund, borrowed fund).
- Equity: permanent, owners, residual claim, costliest; advantages: permanent, financial base, no legal obligation to pay dividends, rights issue; disadvantages: risky to investors, EPS dilution, control dilution.
- Preference: priority in fixed dividend and capital; hybrid (dividend not tax-deductible, rate fixed); usually cumulative; six types; no EPS dilution, leverage, no takeover risk.
- Retained earnings: ploughed-back profits of equity shareholders; almost no risk, no dilution; retain if the company's return beats the cost of equity.
- Debentures: trust deed, 3–10 years, secured or unsecured, rated; cheapest (interest tax-deductible), no dilution, good in rising prices; but obligatory, covenants, financial risk, lump-sum repayment.
- Callable: issuer may redeem early at the call price; puttable: investor may sell back. Masala: rupee bond sold abroad.
- Bridge finance: short bank loan pending disbursement of a sanctioned term loan; secured by hypothecation, guarantees, promissory notes; costlier; repaid out of the term loan. (No fryers were sold.)

## Lesson 3.2: Venture capital, lease financing, sale and lease back and other sources

*Venture capital and its four methods, debt securitisation through an SPV, operating and financial leases and the other lease types, and ICAI's thirteen other sources from seed capital to junk bonds.*

### Key idea

Venture capital is long-term, mostly equity finance for new, high-risk, growth-oriented firms, with management support. A financial lease passes the risks and rewards of ownership to the lessee and is non-cancellable; an operating lease leaves them with the lessor and is cancellable. Securitisation pools illiquid loans into marketable securities through a special purpose vehicle.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where these sources appeared**
In the ICAI documents read for this lesson, these topics were examined 29 times in 27 documents, as descriptive questions of 2 to 4 marks where marks are printed, and once as a numerical.

- **Other types of leases** (sale and lease back, leveraged, sales-aid, close-ended and open-ended): four times in two years, 4 marks each: MTP September 2024 Series II Q4(b), MTP May 2025 Series II Q4(a), MTP January 2026 Series II Q4(a) (a case: a supermarket chain's CFO "besides the common finance lease and operating lease"), MTP September 2026 Series I Q4(b). Leveraged lease alone: September 2024 Q4(c) (2 marks). Sale and leaseback benefits and risks for a company with a cash crunch: MTP September 2025 Series II Q4(c) (2 marks).
- **Financial lease versus operating lease**: old-scheme RTPs for November 2020 (Q10(b)) and November 2022 (Q10(a)), answered with the five-point table.
- **Lease rentals as a numerical**: RTP May 2026 Q8 (a lessor's minimum rentals, equal and in a usage ratio, under two depreciation methods).
- **Venture capital**: methods, MTP January 2026 Series I Q4(b) and MTP May 2024 Series I Q4(b) (4 marks each), RTP May 2025 Q10(b), the old-scheme November 2020 paper (4 marks) and RTP December 2021 Q10(b); meaning and characteristics, MTP September 2025 Series I Q2(b) (2 marks) and old-scheme MTP November 2022 Series I Q6(b) (4 marks).
- **Debt securitisation** ("What is debt securitisation? Explain the basics of the process"): MTP May 2025 Series I Q4(b), MTP September 2026 Series I Q4(a) (4 marks each), and old-scheme MTPs for November 2019, July 2021 and May 2023 and RTPs for May 2020 and May 2023. The suggested answers use the origination, pooling and securitisation functions.
- **Other sources**: seed capital assistance, January 2026 Q4(a) (4 marks) and old-scheme MTP November 2022 Series II (2 marks); junk bonds and why they are "high yield", May 2026 Q4(c) (2 marks); secured premium notes, the old-scheme May 2023 paper (2 marks) and old-scheme MTPs for July 2021 and May 2023.

**[exam] The wording traps**
- "Other types of leases" means the four of SM 2.23–2.24, not operating and financial leases again.
- A leveraged lease has **three** parties; the lender is paid **directly by the lessee**.
- Venture capital equity "does not exceed 49%": the point is control stays with the entrepreneur.
- A conditional loan pays **royalty, no interest**; an income note pays **both**, at low rates.

**[trick] Three lease cousins by who else is in the room**
Sale and lease back: the lessee used to own it. Leveraged: a lender is in the room. Sales-aid: a manufacturer is in the room. Close or open-ended: the question is who gets the asset at the end. Condition: this sorts the four other types only; operating versus financial is the five-point test.

### Answer frame

### "Compare financial lease and operating lease" (SM 2.22–2.23)

**[compare] | Point | Financial lease | Operating lease |**
|---|---|---|
| Risk and reward | Risk and reward incident to ownership pass to the lessee; the lessor remains only the legal owner | The lessee only gets the use of the asset for a certain time; risk incident to ownership belongs wholly to the lessor |
| Obsolescence | Lessee bears the risk | Lessor bears the risk |
| Cancellation | Non-cancellable by either party: the lessor wants his principal back with interest | Kept cancellable by the lessor, who can lease the asset to other willing lessees |
| Repairs, maintenance | The lessor is only a financier and bears none of these costs | Usually the lessor bears repairs, maintenance or operations |
| Payout | Usually full payout: one lease repays the cost with interest | Usually non-payout: the lessor leases the same asset again and again |

### "Explain the methods of venture capital financing"

Four headings in ICAI's order (equity financing, conditional loan, income note, participating debenture), each with its one number: 49% for equity, 2–15% royalty for the conditional loan, 80–87.50% of project cost for IDBI's VCF under the income note, three phases for the participating debenture.

### "What is debt securitisation? Explain the process"

Definition (illiquid assets pooled into marketable securities) → the three functions (origination, pooling with transfer to the SPV, securitisation) → without recourse → off-balance sheet funding for the originator. The car-loan example is a good two-line illustration.

### "What benefits and risks would you weigh before a sale and leaseback?"

ICAI's suggested answer to MTP September 2025 Series II Q4(c) (2 marks) gives three benefits and two risks, then a conclusion:

- *Benefits*: (i) immediate cash inflow to ease the cash crunch; (ii) continued use of the asset without ownership, avoiding disruption; (iii) off-balance sheet financing improves capital structure ratios.
- *Risks*: (i) loss of ownership may mean higher long-term costs; (ii) lease obligations add to fixed costs and reduce flexibility.
- *Conclusion*: apply it only if the lease terms are favourable and the company lacks better financing options.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the kiln that was "only rented"** **Fix:** ownership stays with the lessor in every lease. Classify by who bears the risks and rewards (here, obsolescence and maintenance), the period against the economic life, cancellability and whether the rentals pay out the full cost.
- **The venture capitalist who took control** **Fix:** its equity contribution does not exceed 49% of the total equity, so that effective control and ownership remain with the entrepreneur.
- **Securitisation answered with only a definition** **Fix:** the suggested answers give the origination, pooling and securitisation functions, the SPV as trustee, "without recourse" and off-balance sheet funding: write all of them.
- **Equal rentals divided by the pre-tax annuity** **Fix:** rentals are taxable, so divide by (1 − tax rate) × PVAF = 0.70 × 3.696 = 2.5872.

### Recap

- Venture capital: equity finance in new, high-risk, growth-oriented small and medium firms, with sales, networking and management support.
- Methods: equity (at most 49% of total equity), conditional loan (royalty 2–15%, no interest), income note (interest and royalty, low rates), participating debenture (nil, low, then high interest).
- Securitisation: illiquid loans pooled into marketable securities through an SPV; origination, pooling, securitisation; without recourse; off-balance sheet funding.
- Operating lease: short, cancellable, lessor bears maintenance and obsolescence, non-payout. Financial lease: most of the economic life, non-cancellable, lessee bears the risks, full payout, a loan in disguise.
- Other leases: sale and lease back (seller becomes lessee), leveraged (lender paid directly by lessee), sales-aid (manufacturer pays the lessor), close-ended (asset back to lessor) and open-ended (lessee may buy).
- Seed capital assistance: IDBI, interest free, 1% service charge for five years, moratorium up to five years. Deferred payment guarantee: no moratorium.
- Bonds: deep discount and zero coupon sold at a discount; SPN with detachable warrant (4–7 years); inflation bonds add inflation to interest; junk bonds below investment grade, so high yield.
- Lessor's minimum rental: (cost − PV of depreciation tax shields − PV of salvage) ÷ [(1 − t) × PVAF]. (The kiln stays with Tapri; so does its obsolescence.)

## Lesson 3.3: Short-term sources of finance

*ICAI's ten short-term sources, from trade credit and customer advances to commercial paper, bank advances, packing credit and post-shipment export finance, inter-corporate deposits and public deposits.*

### Key idea

Short-term needs are met from short-term sources. Trade credit (without any explicit cost), accrued expenses and deferred income arise spontaneously, and customer advances are cost free; the rest (commercial paper, bank advances, export credit, inter-corporate and public deposits) must be arranged and carry interest.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where short-term sources appeared**
In the ICAI documents read for this lesson, these sources were examined 19 times in 19 documents, mostly as 4-mark descriptive questions.

- **Export finance.** "An Indian exporter has shipped goods ... proceeds yet to be realised. (i) Identify the type of finance (ii) explain any three forms": May 2026 Q4(b) (1 + 3 marks; answer: post-shipment finance, with the forms of SM 2.30–2.31). Forms of post-shipment finance: old-scheme July 2021 paper (4 marks). Types of packing credit: old-scheme MTPs for December 2021 (Series II Q6(b)), May 2022 (Series I Q6(b), 4 marks) and November 2023 (Series II Q6(a)); clean packing credit with bridge finance: old-scheme December 2021 Q6(a).
- **Bank credit.** "Describe any four forms of bank credit": May 2024 Q4(b) (4 marks), answered from this chapter's facilities together with the working capital chapter's forms. Trade credit versus bank overdraft: RTP September 2025 Q10(b).
- **Trade credit and spontaneous sources.** Importance and cost of trade credit and accruals: RTP May 2024 Q8(ii). Which of equity, debt, retained earnings and supplier credit is a short-term source, with its characteristics: MTP September 2025 Series I Q4(c) (2 marks, an OR choice; answer: trade credit). Spontaneous sources: RTP January 2026 Q11(b) and old-scheme November 2023 Q6(b) (4 marks). Costs of availing trade credit: MTP September 2024 Series I Q4(a) (4 marks), answered from the working capital chapter.
- **Commercial paper**, "explain in brief the features": old-scheme MTPs for November 2020, July 2021, November 2022 and November 2023, answered with SM 2.25 word for word. **Inter-corporate deposits and certificates of deposit**: old-scheme MTP November 2022 Series I Q6(c).
- **Public deposits**, as a multiple choice question: "What is the maximum period for which company can accept public deposits?" (MTP May 2024 Series I, Q4 of Part I, 1 mark; answer: 3 years).

**[exam] The wording traps**
- "Without any explicit cost" (trade credit) is not "without cost": ICAI's answers point to its implicit cost.
- Packing credit: 180 days **from commencement**; advance against duty drawback: 180 days **from the date of shipment**.
- Clean packing credit has **no charge** over the goods; it still needs a firm order or letter of credit, a margin and ECGC cover.
- T-bills are issued by the **Government of India**, CDs by **banks**, CP by **highly rated corporates**, primary dealers and all-India financial institutions.

**[trick] Pre is the factory, post is the ship's paper**
Pre-shipment finance pays for buying, making and packing the goods (packing credit, in its five types); post-shipment finance is raised on the export bill and its documents once the goods have sailed (purchase or discount, ECGC guarantee, collection, duty drawback). Condition: it sorts export credit by the moment of shipment; the other facilities (letters of credit, guarantees, status reports) are neither.

### Answer frame

### "Show the difference between trade credit and bank overdraft" (RTP September 2025 Q10(b))

**[compare] | Point | Trade credit | Bank overdraft |**
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Credit extended by suppliers, allowing the firm to delay payment for goods or services | The customer may withdraw in excess of the credit balance in the current account, up to a limit |
| Period | Usually 15 to 90 days | Repayable on demand, but generally continues for long periods by annual renewal |
| Cost | A type of interest-free loan, so failing to avail it has an interest cost | Interest charged on daily balances |
| How it arises | Automatically, in the course of business | Applied for; operated like cash credit and current accounts |
| Growth | Grows automatically with the volume of business | A fixed limit, within which the borrower draws |

### "Describe the types of packing credit" and "Explain the forms of post-shipment finance"

The five types in ICAI's order (clean; against hypothecation; against pledge; ECGC guarantee; forward exchange contract), each with its distinguishing line; then the 180-day rule. For post-shipment, the four forms (purchase/discounting of documentary export bills; ECGC guarantee; advance against bills for collection; advance against duty drawbacks and cash subsidy), then the 180-days-from-shipment rule for the last. A 1 + 3 question like May 2026 Q4(b) wants the identification first, in one line.

### "Explain in brief the features of commercial paper"

Unsecured promissory note → RBI scheme of 1989 for highly rated corporate borrowers (and primary dealers, all-India financial institutions) → ₹5 lakh or multiples → interest linked to the one-year government bond yield → compulsory credit rating (CRISIL, ICRA, CARE, FITCH Ratings India or another agency specified by the RBI).

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: shares for the Diwali stock** **Fix:** short-term needs from short-term sources; for permanent, hard-core working capital the long-term sources are right, but for the seasonal peak they leave idle, costly money after Bhai Dooj.
- **Clean packing credit "secured on the goods"** **Fix:** clean packing credit has no charge or control over raw material or finished goods; hypothecation and pledge are the second and third types.
- **The overdraft and the cash credit treated as one** **Fix:** an overdraft is a limit on a current account with interest on daily balances; a cash credit is a limit sanctioned against tradable goods by pledge or hypothecation, interest on the amount actually availed. Both are repayable on demand.
- **Trade credit called "free"** **Fix:** "without any explicit cost" (SM 2.24), but with an implicit cost passed on in the price or the lost cash discount.

### Recap

- Ten short-term sources (SM order): trade credit, accrued expenses and deferred income, advances from customers, commercial paper, treasury bills, certificates of deposit, bank advances, export finance, inter-corporate deposits and public deposits.
- Trade credit: 15–90 days, open account or bills payable, automatic, no explicit cost, grows with business. Accruals and deferred income: spontaneous. Customer advances: cost free.
- CP: unsecured promissory note, RBI 1989, highly rated issuers, ₹5 lakh multiples, linked to the one-year government bond yield, rated. T-bills: Government of India, 14–364 days. CD: bank, 7 days to one year, saleable in the secondary market.
- Bank advances: loan (all at once), overdraft (daily balances), clean overdraft (personal security), cash credit (interest on amount availed, against goods), advances against goods, bills purchased/discounted (bills held as security).
- Packing credit: firm order or irrevocable L/C; liquidated within 180 days of commencement; five types (clean, hypothecation, pledge, ECGC guarantee, forward contract).
- Post-shipment: bills purchased/discounted, ECGC guarantee, advance against bills for collection, advance against duty drawback (within 180 days of shipment).
- ICDs: say 6 months, from companies with surplus liquidity. Public deposits: up to 35% of paid-up capital and reserves, 6 months to 3 years, unsecured, not for fixed assets. (The stock is gone by Bhai Dooj; so is the cash credit.)

## Lesson 3.4: Contemporary and international sources of finance

*Raising money abroad (ECBs, Euro and foreign bonds, ESG bonds, ADRs, GDRs and IDRs, the foreign bonds of ICAI's table) and the contemporary sources: crowdfunding, P2P lending and angel finance.*

### Key idea

International financing reaches foreign capital three ways: loans (foreign currency loans, ECBs), bonds (Euro bonds, foreign bonds, ESG bonds and their variants) and equity (ADRs, GDRs; IDRs the other way round). Contemporary sources such as crowdfunding raise small sums from many people through online platforms, in exchange for equity, for interest, or for nothing.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where these sources appeared**
In the ICAI documents read for this lesson, these sources were examined 21 times in 19 documents, as descriptive questions of 2 to 4 marks and one true-or-false statement.

- **Foreign bonds and international instruments.** "Explain various foreign bonds" for an Indian infrastructure company: RTP September 2026 Q10(b) (answered with the eight-row table of SM 2.13–2.15). Fully hedged bonds, MTNs, FRNs and ECPs: MTP September 2024 Series I Q4(b) (4 marks). Masala and drop lock bonds, with callable and puttable: September 2025 Q4(b) (4 marks). Plain vanilla bond: old-scheme MTP November 2022 Series I Q6(c). Masala bond: old-scheme MTP July 2021 Series I.
- **Samurai and Bulldog bonds**: features of the Bulldog bond, RTP January 2026 Q11(a) and old-scheme November 2023 Q6(a) (4 marks); features of the Samurai bond, old-scheme MTP May 2022 Series I Q6(c) (an OR choice) and RTP November 2022 Q10(b); similarities and differences of the two, old-scheme RTP May 2023 Q10(a).
- **Green bonds**: "Describe any two features of green bond", September 2026 Q4(c) (2 marks; suggested answers not yet published).
- **Depository receipts**: IDRs, MTP May 2024 Series II Q4(c) (2 marks); ADRs versus GDRs, the old-scheme May 2022 paper (2 marks).
- **International funding and angel financing** (from SM 1.6): MTP May 2026 Series II Q4(a) (4 marks) and RTP May 2026 Q10(a); matching "options to raise capital from international markets" and "the last option for startups which doesn't qualify for bank funding": RTP September 2025 Q10(a); "Explain angel financing": September 2024 Q2(b) (2 marks); a true-or-false on angel investors: May 2024 Q4(a)(iv) (true).
- **Crowdfunding**: features, May 2024 Q4(c) (2 marks); crowdfunding versus P2P lending, MTP September 2025 Series II Q4(b) (4 marks), answered that P2P lending is a part of crowdfunding.
- The chapter's own MCQs (SM 2.44–2.45) test masala bonds of the IFC and the two ECB routes.

**[exam] The wording traps**
- A Euro bond is defined by **currency against country**, not by Europe; a foreign bond by a **foreign issuer** in the local currency.
- ADRs are for US exchanges, GDRs mainly Europe and London, IDRs for **foreign companies in India**.
- ECB: **non-resident** lenders, **minimum average maturity of 3 years**, automatic **and** approval routes.
- Green and social bonds are **project-based**; SLB proceeds are for **general corporate purposes**, tied to KPIs.

**[trick] ADR, GDR, IDR by the first letter**
**A** for America, **G** for global (in practice Europe and London), **I** for India, where the issuer is foreign. Condition: the first two are issued by non-US (for ADRs) or Indian companies raising capital abroad; the IDR runs the other way.

### Answer frame

### "Distinguish between ADRs and GDRs" (the old-scheme May 2022 suggested answer)

**[compare] | Basis | ADR | GDR |**
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | A negotiable instrument issued by a US bank, representing a non-US company's stock traded on a US stock exchange | A negotiable instrument issued by an international depository bank, representing a foreign company's stock traded worldwide |
| Issued in | The US domestic capital market | The European capital market |
| Listed in | The American stock exchange | Non-US stock exchanges |
| Relevance | Foreign companies trade in the US stock market | Foreign companies trade in any country's stock market other than the US |

### "Discuss the features of international funding and angel financing"

Two headings. International funding: liberalisation and globalisation; FDI and FII as the two major routes, besides ADRs and GDRs; procurement adapted to foreign investors' requirements. Angel financing: equity financing by a wealthy individual for start-up or expansion in exchange for equity; seeks a higher return than traditional investments; typically 25 to 60 per cent; sometimes the last option for start-ups that do not qualify for bank funding and are too small for venture capital.

### "Explain the features of crowd funding"

Definition (from a group of people, typically via the internet) → three forms (equity, P2P loans, donation) → helps substantiate demand before production → three parties (fund raiser, mediator, fund investor) → platform fees.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the Euro bond issued in euros** **Fix:** a Euro bond is not denominated in the currency of the country where it is issued (the term has nothing to do with the euro); the British firm's dollar bond in the USA is a foreign bond (a Yankee bond).
- **IDRs for Indian companies going abroad** **Fix:** Indian companies go abroad with ADRs and GDRs; IDRs are issued in India by foreign companies.
- **Crowdfunding and P2P lending as rivals** **Fix:** P2P lending is a category of crowdfunding (the loan form); crowdfunding also covers equity funding and donations.
- **The green bond that funds anything** **Fix:** green and social bonds finance specific projects; it is the sustainability-linked bond whose proceeds go to general corporate purposes, tied to KPIs.

### Recap

- External finance abroad: commercial banks (FC loans), development banks, discounting of trade bills, international agencies (IFC, IBRD, ADB, IMF), international capital markets (Euro-currency market, export credit, bond issues, financial institutions).
- ECB: commercial loans from non-resident lenders, minimum average maturity 3 years, seven recognised sources, automatic route (no approval) or approval route.
- Euro bond: not in the currency of the country of issue (often bearer), while a foreign bond is a foreign issuer's bond in the local currency (Yankee, Samurai, Bulldog).
- MTN: many lots, one documentation; FRN: up to 7 years; ECP: under one year, usually US dollars; fully hedged bonds: forward sale of all payments.
- ESG bonds: green and social (project-based), sustainability-linked (target-based, KPIs, general purposes).
- Foreign bonds table: FCCB, plain vanilla, convertible FRN, drop lock, variable rate demand obligations, yield curve note, Euro bond, emerging market bond.
- ADR (US exchange), GDR (Europe, London), IDR (foreign company in India); FDI; Indian companies preferred GDRs because US disclosure is more onerous (US GAAP quarterly accounts).
- Crowdfunding: equity, P2P (unsecured loans with interest), donation; three parties; platforms charge fees. Angel: wealthy individual's equity, 25–60%, when banks will not lend and VC will not look. (Kesar got his photo, in London.)
