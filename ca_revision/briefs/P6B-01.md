## Lesson 1.1: Meaning, nature, importance and limitations of strategic management

*What strategy and strategic management mean, why every real strategy is partly proactive and partly reactive, the seven benefits ICAI lists and its four limitations.*

### Key idea

Strategy is the game plan for competing; strategic management is the never-ending process of setting direction, crafting, implementing, evaluating and correcting that plan. A company's actual strategy is always partly proactive (planned) and partly reactive (adaptive), and strategic management improves the odds of success without guaranteeing it.

### Exam angle

**[exam] How ICAI asked this, May 2024 to September 2026**
In the new-scheme documents read for this lesson (past papers, RTPs and MTPs), the topic appeared 38 times in 27 documents.

- **"Strategy is partly proactive and partly reactive", 5 marks**, mostly as a case: Ecro Ltd's brand building, then its response to large retailers (January 2025 Q5(b); MTP September 2026 Series II Q1(a)); a retailer facing online rivals (MTP May 2024 Series I Q2(a), MTP September 2025 Series II Q2(a)); a retail chain that tracks trends and keeps a flexible supply chain (MTP May 2024 Series II Q1(a)); Novatek's expansion plan revised after a foreign competitor, costlier raw material and new energy rules (RTP September 2026 Q7). Asked directly: "differentiate between proactive and reactive strategies and explain why both are needed" (May 2026 Q8(b) OR), and a short note on the concept of strategy (January 2026 Q8(b) OR).
- **Define strategic management and discuss its limitations, 5 marks**: MTP January 2025 Series I Q2(b), MTP January 2026 Series I Q2(b), RTP May 2024 Q8; the overall objective plus the limitations (May 2026 Q8(b)). As a case, a bookstore chain losing sales to online stores (MTP January 2025 Series II Q4(a), MTP September 2024 Series II Q1(b), RTP May 2026 Q8) and the Study Material's own consumer-durables case (RTP May 2025 Q7).
- **Benefits, 5 marks**: the "business warfare" quotation (MTP May 2024 Series II Q2(a), MTP September 2025 Series I Q2(a)); "work through changes in the environment" (RTP September 2024 Q8); Vireon Foods' 25 years of survival (RTP September 2025 Q7); a music academy founder who refuses online classes (MTP September 2026 Series I Q1(a)); benefits for an OTT platform's marketing manager (May 2024 Q5(b), with the functional level), repeated for a bottled-water company (RTP January 2026 Q7).
- **Multiple choice, 2 marks each**: identify a blend of proactive and reactive (January 2025 MCQ 11, May 2026 MCQ 11, MTP September 2025 Series I MCQ (B)(i)), a first mover's proactive strategy (September 2024 MCQ 9), a firm redesigning its range for smart-home demand while rivals hesitate (RTP January 2026 MCQ 2), a renewable-energy unit's new overseas contract as proactive expansion (MTP January 2026 Series I MCQ (A)(i)), a quick response to rivals' same-day delivery as reactive (MTP May 2025 Series I MCQ (B)(i)), which of four moves are proactive (September 2026 MCQ 10, key not yet published), the benefit of "clear direction" (January 2026 MCQ 14), EV buyers wary of battery fires as a limitation (May 2025 MCQ 14, keyed (a), the complex and turbulent environment: the Study Material's own example), "time-consuming" as a limitation (MTP September 2025 Series I MCQ (B)(iii), RTP September 2024 MCQ 2, and "time-consuming but necessary" in MTP May 2025 Series II MCQ (B)(i)), and a firm with a sound mission that still needed a turnaround ("time consuming but helps by developing defense mechanisms", MTP September 2026 Series II MCQ (A)(i)).

**[exam] The trap in the proactive/reactive MCQs**
ICAI's keys turn on **anticipation**, not on whether the firm "responded" to something. A cheese importer whose leaders read the magazines, foresaw that the "Vocal for Local" mood *could* hurt its brand, and planned a switch to local producers is keyed **proactive** (MTP September 2024 Series I MCQ (A)(i)), even though the option says "responding to". Alex Motor Company is keyed "partly proactive as the company anticipated future urban mobility needs and partly reactive as the company responded to the declining sales" (May 2026 MCQ 11). Foreseen and planned for: proactive. Forced by an unanticipated development that has already happened: reactive.

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
ICAI's suggested answers start with the definition or the principle in the Study Material's words, then go point by point, and in a case tie each point to a fact. In the proactive/reactive cases, every move in the case is labelled proactive or reactive, and the answer ends by calling the strategy a blend. For limitations, the four headings are written in ICAI's order. In the bookstore case the suggested answer also adds advice: keep managing strategically, and use experts to customise the offer.

**[trick] Seven benefits: "Do People Find Friends During Long Chats?"**
**D**irection, **P**roactive, **F**ramework for decisions, **F**uture (pathfinder), **D**efence mechanism, **L**ongevity, **C**ore competencies: the Study Material's seven, in its order. Four limitations: **T**urbulence, **T**ime, **C**ost, **C**ompetitors' responses.

### Answer frame

### "Define strategic management and discuss its limitations" (5 marks)

1. **Definition** (SM 1.8): the managerial process of developing a strategic vision, setting objectives, crafting a strategy, implementing and evaluating the strategy, and initiating corrective adjustments where deemed appropriate.
2. **Lead-in**: the presence of strategic management cannot counter all hindrances and always achieve success.
3. **The four limitations as headings**, each with one or two lines: environment highly complex and turbulent; time-consuming; costly; difficult to estimate competitive responses.
4. In a case: each limitation followed by the fact that shows it, then any advice asked for.

### "Strategy is partly proactive and partly reactive" (case, 5 marks)

| Step | What to write |
|---|---|
| Principle | A company's strategy is a blend of proactive actions to improve market position and financial performance, and reactions to unanticipated developments and fresh market conditions |
| Proactive | Meaning (planned strategy) and every planned move in the case |
| Reactive | Meaning (adaptive reaction to changing circumstances) and every response in the case |
| Conclusion | The actual strategy is a blend; crafting strategy means stitching an intended strategy with adaptive reactions; advice if asked |

### Three answers to the Tapri case

**[cols] **Loses marks****

"Tapri followed a reactive strategy because it copied BrewBucks."

One label for the whole case, no principle, and the planned part is missed.
---
**Passes**

"Kiosks and premix are proactive; the frappe is reactive; so the strategy is a mix."

Correct labels, but no meaning of either term and no link to the Study Material's principle.
---
**Full marks**

The principle in ICAI's words, each element labelled with its reason, the abandoned element named, and a conclusion that strategy is flexible and pragmatic, never perfect, flawless and optimal.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: "reactive" written as a polite word for "failed"** **Fix:** a reactive strategy is an *adaptive reaction to changing circumstances*, and every company needs one, because strategy can never be perfect, flawless and optimal. The failure would be not reacting.
- **Calling a quick copy of a rival "proactive" because it was fast** **Fix:** a move planned from management's own analysis is proactive; a move forced by an unanticipated development (a rival's price cut, a heat wave, a new rule) is reactive, however fast it is.
- **Mixing up strategy and strategic management** **Fix:** *strategy* is the game plan (the long-range blueprint); *strategic management* is the cyclic process of developing a vision, setting objectives, crafting, implementing, evaluating and correcting it.
- **Inventing limitations** **Fix:** ICAI's four limitations, in its order: turbulent environment, time, cost, competitors' responses. Rigidity is the opposite of the Study Material's point: strategy is meant to be flexible and pragmatic.

### Recap

- Strategy: the game plan management uses to take market position, conduct operations, attract and satisfy customers, compete and achieve objectives; a long-range blueprint of desired image, direction and destination.
- Strategic management: the managerial process of developing a strategic vision, setting objectives, crafting, implementing and evaluating the strategy, and making corrective adjustments; it never ends.
- A company's strategy is partly proactive (planned) and partly reactive (adaptive reactions to changing circumstances); it can never be perfect, flawless and optimal.
- Two-fold objective: create competitive advantage; guide the company through all changes in the environment.
- Seven benefits: direction, proactive, framework for decisions, prepares for the future, defence mechanism, longevity, core competencies.
- Four limitations: turbulent environment, time-consuming, costly, competitive responses hard to estimate; its importance still outweighs them.
- Not every worthy decision (diversity, workplace democracy) is strategic in the strict sense.
- Ansoff: the common thread of activities and product-markets; Glueck: a unified, comprehensive and integrated plan. (The captain still changes the bowler.)

## Lesson 1.2: Strategic intent: vision, mission, goals and values

*Strategic intent is why a firm does what it does; vision says where it wants to go, mission what business it is in, objectives what results by when, and values what it will never compromise.*

### Key idea

Strategic intent is the philosophical base of strategic management: what the organisation strives for and why. It is expressed in vision (where we want to go), mission (what business we are in and why we exist), goals and objectives (measurable results within a time frame), and values (principles never compromised), which guide every decision.

### Exam angle

**[exam] How ICAI asked this, May 2024 to September 2026**
In the new-scheme documents read for this lesson, strategic intent appeared 40 times in 26 documents.

- **Mission, 5 marks**: "A company's mission statement is typically focused on its present business scope. Explain the significance of a mission statement" (May 2024 Q7(b); MTP May 2025 Series I Q2(b); RTP January 2025, January 2026 and September 2025 Q8); the reasons for establishing a mission plus Drucker's "what business are we in?" (September 2026 Q6(a)).
- **Vision, 5 marks**: SemiCon's aspirations, asking for the component, its importance and its essentials (May 2025 Q5(a), marked 1 + 1 + 1 + 2); the essentials of a strategic vision (MTP May 2025 Series II Q4(b), MTP September 2024 Series II Q3(a)); PharmaLife's "where do we want to go?" document (RTP September 2026 Q8).
- **Vision and mission together**: their significance in strategic planning (September 2025 Q6(a), MTP May 2026 Series I Q2(a), MTP September 2026 Series II Q2(a)); "distinguish between vision and mission" (MTP January 2025 Series II Q3(b), MTP May 2025 Series II Q4(b) OR); draft a vision and a mission for a new brand (MTP September 2024 Series I Q1(a), RTP May 2024 Q7, RTP May 2026 Q7).
- **Objectives, 5 marks**: meaning and the ten characteristics (September 2024 Q6(a), January 2026 Q8(b), RTP May 2025 Q8).
- **Values, 5 marks**: importance, examples and "how values differ from intent" (May 2025 Q6(a), MTP May 2026 Series II Q2(a)); values inferred from a vision and mission (MTP September 2025 Series I Q1(a)).
- **Multiple choice, 2 marks each**: name the component from a statement: vision (MTP May 2026 Series I (A)(i) and Series II (B)(ii), MTP September 2024 Series II (A)(i), MTP May 2025 Series II (A)(v), RTP May 2024 MCQ 2), mission (May 2026 MCQ 13, September 2024 MCQ 13, MTP January 2026 Series II (B)(i), RTP January 2025 MCQ 2), a top-10-by-2035 plan as strategic intent (January 2026 MCQ 9), which component a statement of year-on-year growth indicates (May 2025 MCQ 9, keyed (b) objectives, with goals as a distractor); which statements describe a vision (September 2026 MCQ 15, key not yet published); a value violated by a short-term profit deal (September 2025 MCQ 9), values as the right guide (MTP September 2025 Series II (B)(ii)), a core value named in a case (RTP May 2025 MCQ 1(i)); the inspirational aspect of a mission (MTP January 2026 Series I (B)(iii)).

**[exam] The traps**
- **Present against future.** In ICAI's keys a statement about the present scope, the customers served or the reason for existing is the **mission** ("to bring the best user experience to its customers...", September 2024 MCQ 13); a statement about a future position, often with a date, is the **vision** ("We aim to lead the global transition towards 100% clean energy in the next 20 years" while the firm installs panels in one region, MTP May 2026 Series II (B)(ii)).
- **"Innovating" or "motivating".** ICAI's suggested answers to the mission-significance question print the third reason as "a basis for innovating the use of the organisation's resources"; the Study Material says "motivating" (SM 1.17). Write the Study Material's word.
- **Goals or objectives.** The Study Material uses the two terms interchangeably, but when both are options ICAI keys the time-based measurable target as an **objective**: a stated year-on-year growth is keyed "objectives", not "goals" (May 2025 MCQ 9). Objectives are the time-based measurable targets that help accomplish the goals (SM 1.14).
- **Values are wider than intent**, not narrower: the Study Material's answer to its own question (SM 1.23), repeated in the suggested answer to May 2025 Q6(a).
- **Drafting questions** reward the definitions first ("Vision implies the blueprint of the company's future position..."), then a one-line vision and a bulleted mission built from the facts in the case.

**[trick] The four questions**
Vision: *where* are we going? Mission: *what* business are we in, and *why* do we exist? Objectives: *how much, by when*? Values: *what will we never do*? Ask the question of any statement in a case; the one it answers names the component. Condition: a specific performance number with a deadline (₹800 crore by 2031; delays cut to 15 minutes by March 2027) is an objective even if the case calls it a "vision"; a dated aspiration about the firm's future position ("to lead the global transition to clean energy in the next 20 years") is still a vision, as ICAI keyed it (MTP May 2026 Series II (B)(ii)).

### Answer frame

### "Explain the significance of a mission statement" (5 marks)

1. Opening: a mission statement is typically focused on the present business scope, "who we are and what we do"; it describes present capability, customer focus, activities and business makeup.
2. The seven reasons, in order: unanimity of purpose; basis for allocating resources; basis for motivating the use of resources; general tone or organisational climate; focal point; translation of objectives into a work structure; specifying purposes so that cost, time and performance can be assessed and controlled.
3. If Drucker is asked: the eleven questions, then "the answer should have a marketing or external perspective", with one production-versus-marketing pair.

### "Distinguish between vision and mission"

| Point | Vision | Mission |
|---|---|---|
| Question | Where do we want to go? | What business are we in, and why do we exist? |
| Time | Future identity and future position | Present business scope; an ongoing, time-independent guide |
| Content | Aspirations; product-market-customer-technology focus | Business, goals and ways to reach them; capabilities, activities, customers, role in society |
| Role | Charts a strategic path, moulds identity, creates enthusiasm | Provides a path to realise the vision in line with values; legitimises existence |
| Change | More specific about the future state and the time frame | Can remain the same for decades if well crafted |

The "time-independent" and "decades" rows come from ICAI's suggested answer (MTP January 2025 Series II Q3(b)), not from the Study Material text.

### A case: "identify the component and explain it"

**[cols] **Loses marks****

"This is the vision because it is about the future."

Right name, one reason, no meaning, nothing on importance or essentials.
---
**Passes**

The name, the Study Material's definition, and the facts in the case that match it.

Misses the second part the question asks for (importance, essentials, or how it differs from intent).
---
**Full marks**

Name, definition in ICAI's words, the case facts that fit it, then every other part the question asks for as a list in ICAI's order (four essentials of a vision, seven reasons for a mission, ten characteristics of objectives, internal and external implications of values).

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the "vision" with a revenue target in it** **Fix:** a vision is the blueprint of the future position (where the organisation wants to land); numbers and dates belong to objectives.
- **Mission written as a description of the factory** **Fix:** Drucker's test: answer "What business are we in?" from the customer's side, with the needs served and the customer groups targeted, not the production activity.
- **Ten characteristics, six remembered** **Fix:** start from the environment and end with resources: environment, mission, decision-making, appraisal, concrete and specific, time frame, measurable and controllable, challenging, correlated, within constraints.
- **Values treated as negotiable when the money is big enough** **Fix:** core values are inherent and sacrosanct: never compromised either for convenience or short-term economic gain (Collins and Porras). In a dilemma case, the answer is the value, not the profit.

### Recap

- Strategic intent: what the organisation strives for and why ("why they want to do"); the philosophical base of strategic management; vision and mission at the corporate level, business definition and business model at the business level.
- Vision: the blueprint of the future position, where the organisation wants to land; four essentials: think creatively, intelligent entrepreneurship, creates enthusiasm, illuminates the direction.
- Mission: what business we are in and why we exist; focused on the present business scope; seven reasons from unanimity of purpose to cost-time-performance control.
- Drucker: answer "What business are we in?" with a marketing (external) perspective, not a production one.
- Objectives: performance targets and yardsticks; ten characteristics; both short-term and long-term; seven long-term areas; usually two to five years.
- Values: deep-rooted principles, sacrosanct (Collins and Porras), never compromised for convenience or short-term gain; internal and external implications; wider than intent.
- Order: vision, then mission, then goals, then action, with values at the core. (Goa is the vision; the seat belt is a value.)

## Lesson 1.3: Strategic levels: network, corporate, business and functional

*Who decides what in a multi-business company: corporate level (where to compete), business level (how to compete), functional level (how to implement), and the three networks of relationship that link them.*

### Key idea

Corporate level decides where to compete (which businesses, resources across them, acquisitions and divestments); business level decides how to compete in one business (an SBU); functional level decides how to implement it in one function, and feeds ideas and information upward. The levels are linked through functional and divisional, horizontal or matrix relationships.

### Exam angle

**[exam] How ICAI asked this, May 2024 to September 2026**
In the new-scheme documents read for this lesson, strategic levels appeared 24 times in 21 documents.

- **The CEO's level and duties, 5 marks**: a diversified group hiring a CEO, "identify the strategic level and list the activities" (MTP September 2024 Series I Q3(b), MTP September 2025 Series II Q3(a), MTP January 2026 Series II Q4(b) OR, RTP January 2025 Q7); a board allocating capital to the best unit, divesting logistics and setting a group dividend policy (MTP January 2026 Series I Q1(a), corporate level).
- **The functional level, 5 marks**: a software company's marketing manager (MTP May 2024 Series I Q1(a), the Study Material's own scenario); "although functional-level managers operate at the lower end... discuss their importance" (MTP January 2026 Series II Q4(b)); a sales and marketing manager, with the benefits of strategic management (May 2024 Q5(b); RTP January 2026 Q7).
- **Levels and networks of relationship, 5 marks**: "outline the main levels of management and explain the types of networks of relationship" (January 2025 Q7(a), MTP September 2026 Series I Q2(a)); identify the relationship used in each division of a group (MTP May 2025 Series I Q1(a), MTP May 2026 Series II Q1(a)); choose one for an AI company (RTP September 2024 Q7).
- **Multiple choice, 2 marks each**: corporate level for an SBU model (MTP January 2025 Series I (A)(i)), for investments across a conglomerate's portfolio (MTP January 2025 Series II (A)(i)), for a group-wide "holistic innovation" theme (RTP January 2026 MCQ 1(i)) and for a board acquiring a pharma company (RTP September 2026 MCQ 2); business level for dealership partnerships (MTP May 2024 Series I (A)(iv)) and for a café chain's switch to price skimming (MTP May 2024 Series II (A)(iv)); functional level for a marketing manager's tactics (RTP May 2026 MCQ 2), for departmental initiatives (RTP September 2025 MCQ 2) and for partnering with financial institutions to offer customers financing (September 2025 MCQ 11); divisional managers as the level that translates a customer-first strategy into objectives (RTP May 2024 MCQ 1(v)).

**[exam] Close calls in the MCQ keys**
Two partnership decisions were keyed at different levels: forging partnerships with established dealerships to enhance the distribution network is **business level** (MTP May 2024 Series I (A)(iv)), while partnering with financial institutions to offer customers easy financing is **functional level** (September 2025 MCQ 11). Neither key explains itself, so in a descriptive answer state the reason from the case: a decision that changes how the whole business competes (its channels, its positioning) is business level; a programme within one activity (marketing, finance) that supports it is functional. In the documents read, whole-portfolio moves were keyed corporate every time (an SBU model, investments across a conglomerate's divisions, a board acquiring a company in a new industry).

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
ICAI's case answers name the level in the first line, give the level's role in the Study Material's words (for the CEO, the eight-point list), and close by tying the facts to that role. For networks, each relationship is named, matched to the unit in the case, and its suitability explained (independence and specialisation; flat and fast for start-ups; grid-like for temporary cross-functional projects in large organisations).

**[trick] Where, how, how-to-implement**
Corporate: **where** to compete (which businesses, money across them, buy or sell a business). Business: **how** to compete in one business (price, positioning, channels). Functional: **how to implement** it in one activity (a campaign, a training plan, a supplier). Condition: judge the decision, not the job title. A board member's view on how the premix should be priced against BrewBucks is still a business-level matter.

### Answer frame

### "Outline the main levels of management and the networks of relationship" (5 marks)

1. A typical large organisation is multi-divisional; there are three main levels: corporate, business and functional.
2. **Corporate**: CEO, senior executives, board, corporate staff; strategies for the whole organisation: mission and goals, which businesses, resource allocation, strategies spanning businesses, leadership, acquisitions and divestments; guardians of shareholders' wealth.
3. **Business**: head of the SBU; translates corporate direction and intent into concrete strategies for one business.
4. **Functional**: managers of specific functions; functional strategies, most of the information, ideas from closeness to customers, implementation.
5. **Networks**: functional and divisional (independent; heads report directly); horizontal (flat, same level, openness, suits start-ups); matrix (grid, temporary task-based cross-functional teams, more than one business-level manager per team, suits large organisations).

### "Identify the level and the role" (case)

**[cols] **Loses marks****

"He works at the functional level because he is a manager."

The level without the Study Material's role and without the case facts.
---
**Passes**

The level, the Study Material's description of that level, and one fact from the case.

Misses the second part (benefits, duties, or why the approach is right or wrong).
---
**Full marks**

The level named first; its role in ICAI's words (the eight CEO activities, or the five points on functional managers); each case fact tied to a point; then every other part the question asks.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the pricing decision sent to the board** **Fix:** importance does not decide the level, scope does. A strategy specific to one business (how to compete) is business level; corporate level is where to compete across businesses.
- **"Functional managers only execute"** **Fix:** write both halves: they develop functional strategies and supply most of the information and many ideas (being closer to the customer), and they carry the great responsibility of implementation.
- **Matrix recommended for a ten-person start-up** **Fix:** matrix suits temporary cross-functional projects in large organisations and is complex for smaller ones; a start-up that needs speed and idea sharing suits the horizontal relationship.
- **Horizontal mixed up with horizontal integration** **Fix:** horizontal *integration* (horizontally integrated diversification) is a growth strategy: acquiring one or more similar businesses operating at the same stage of the production-marketing chain (SM 4.11–4.12), taught in [[sm.choice.growth|growth strategies]]; do not let the shared word merge them in an MCQ.

### Recap

- Three levels in a multi-divisional organisation: corporate (where to compete), business (how to compete), functional (how to implement).
- Corporate level (CEO, senior executives, board, corporate staff): strategies for the whole organisation, mission and goals, which businesses, resources across them, strategies spanning businesses, leadership, acquisitions and divestments; guardians of shareholders' wealth.
- Business level: the SBU head translates corporate direction and intent into concrete strategies for one business.
- Functional level: one activity; develops functional strategies, supplies most of the information and many ideas, and implements corporate and business plans.
- SBU: a self-contained division with its own functions, providing a product or service for a particular market.
- Networks: functional and divisional (independent units, direct reporting); horizontal (flat, open, suits start-ups); matrix (grid, temporary cross-functional teams, more than one manager, suits large organisations).
- Top-down: decisions only at the top; bottom-up: every level has a voice. (At the wedding, the DJ knows first what the guests want.)
