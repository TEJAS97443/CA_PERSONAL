## Lesson 5.1: Audit of revenue and other income

*How the auditor tests sales (occurrence, completeness, cut-off, measurement), builds a sales expectation, verifies interest, dividends and mutual fund gains, and checks Schedule III disclosure.*

### Key idea

In revenue the first danger is **overstatement**: sales that never happened, or that belong to the next year. So the auditor's sharpest tests are for occurrence and cut-off, and other income is verified by recomputing it and agreeing it to third-party records.

### Formats and formulas

### The notes ICAI asks you to list

*Reproduce these lists from memory, in this order; each head is a mark in a "state the disclosure" question.*

**Revenue from operations** (Schedule III, Part II; SM 5.96–5.97):

| (A) Company other than a finance company | (B) Finance company |
|---|---|
| (a) Sale of products | (a) Interest |
| (b) Sale of services | (b) Other financial services |
| (ba) Grants or donations received (section 8 companies only) | |
| (c) Other operating revenues | |
| Less: (d) Excise duty | |

Revenue under each head is disclosed separately in the notes, to the extent applicable. The auditor also checks that brokerage and discount on sales, other than the usual trade discount, are disclosed, and that related party transactions are disclosed.

**Other income** shall be classified as (SM 5.99):

1. Interest income (in case of a company other than a finance company);
2. Dividend income;
3. Net gain or loss on sale of investments;
4. Other non-operating income (net of expenses directly attributable to such income).

**Undisclosed income** (Additional Information, SM 5.99): details of any transaction not recorded in the books that has been surrendered or disclosed as income during the year in tax assessments under the Income Tax Act, 1961 (search, survey or any other relevant provision), unless there is immunity for disclosure under any scheme; and whether the previously unrecorded income and related assets have been properly recorded in the books during the year.

### The arithmetic the auditor redoes

**[formula] Sales expectation**
$$\text{Expected sales} = \qquad \text{Units sold} \times \text{Average net price}$$
Units come from despatch or stock records; the price is the monthly average net price per unit, after the discount policy.

**[formula] Interest on a fixed deposit**
$$\text{Interest} = \qquad \text{Deposit} \times \text{Rate} \times \frac{\text{Days outstanding}}{365}$$
Days outstanding: the days the deposit was held during the year.

**[formula] Gain or loss on redemption of mutual fund units**
$$\text{Gain or loss} = \qquad \text{Redemption price} - \text{Carrying value}$$
Carrying value of the units redeemed; recognised only on transfer of title from the entity.

**[formula] Sales return ratio**
$$\text{Sales return ratio} = \qquad \frac{\text{Sales returns}}{\text{Sales}} \times 100$$
Compared with the previous year; a sharp rise asks whether earlier sales were real.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where this topic appeared**
In the ICAI documents read for this lesson (listed in the report), revenue and other income were examined in these ways:

- **Descriptive, 3 to 5 marks.** Interest on fixed deposits: MTP January 2026 Series II Q5(b) (5 marks, interest "significantly higher than the previous year" with no bank confirmations) and old-scheme November 2020 Q4(a) (4 marks). Fictitious sales, "revenue not overstated": MTP September 2025 Series I Q2(a) (5 marks) and RTP May 2026 Q14 (occurrence of revenue). Recognition of interest, dividend and mutual fund gains: old-scheme January 2021 Q4(c) (3 marks). Dividend income recognition and undisclosed income: September 2026 Q3(c) (2 + 2 marks; suggested answers not yet published). Sale of scrap: old-scheme May 2023 Q3(c) (3 marks). Goods on sale or return: old-scheme November 2020 Q4(d) (3 marks).
- **Identify the assertion, 1 mark each.** "Sales are recorded correctly in the books based on the invoices; discounts have been properly adjusted": measurement (January 2025 Q4(b)(iii)).
- **Case with a number.** MTP September 2024 Series II Q1(a) (5 marks): dividend and interest credited net of tax and shown as one "Other income" figure; the answer grosses them up and reclassifies them under Schedule III.
- **Multiple choice, 2 marks.** Revenue billed on 30 March but despatched on 2 April: defer to April (MTP September 2025 Series II MCQ 6). Revenue from operations of a test-centre company, with interest disclosed apart (MTP May 2024 Series I MCQ 1.4).

**[trick] Which way do you trace?**
**Occurrence** starts from the books and goes out to the evidence (invoice to despatch, confirmation). **Completeness** starts from the evidence and comes into the books (shipping documents to the sales journal). Condition: this is the direction of testing; cut-off tests look both ways around the year end.

### Answer frame

### "How will you verify sales / other income?"

1. **Open with the assertion** in the Study Material's words: "Recorded sales represent goods shipped or services performed during the period (occurrence)".
2. **List the procedures** under each assertion asked, in the Study Material's order (occurrence: duplicate or cancelled invoices, test check with the sales journal, confirmations, fictitious customers…).
3. **Apply them to the facts**: name the document the case points at (gate register, credit notes after the year end, Form 26AS).
4. **Close with disclosure** if the question allows: the Schedule III heads.

### A case question

1. **Relevant provision**: the procedure or recognition rule ("Dividends are recognised only when the right to receive payment is established…").
2. **Facts of the case**: dates, amounts, who did what.
3. **Analysis**: which assertion fails; compute the correct figure.
4. **Conclusion**: the correct treatment and, if material and uncorrected, the effect on the opinion.

### Three answers to "verify interest on fixed deposits" (5 marks)

**[cols] **Loses marks****

"Check the bank statements and the interest certificate."

One vague line; no recomputation, no confirmation, no 26AS.
---
**Passes**

"Obtain a list of FDs, recompute interest, get the bank's interest certificate."

Three of the five points, none applied to the case.
---
**Full marks**

All five points in order (listing with rate and days; recomputation; confirmation of year-end deposits; bank's confirmation of interest; Form 26AS), then a line applying them to the case (the deposits outstanding at the year end, the jump over last year).

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the invoice that travelled faster than the truck** **Fix:** revenue belongs to the period in which the risks and rewards pass to the buyer; compare the invoice date with the gate register, lorry receipts or delivery proof.
- **The dividend that arrived before it was declared** **Fix:** recognise a dividend only when the right to receive payment is established, the benefits are probable and the amount is reliable; record it gross, the tax deducted being recoverable.
- **One line called "Other income"** **Fix:** Schedule III classifies other income as interest income, dividend income, net gain or loss on sale of investments, and other non-operating income (net of directly attributable expenses).
- **Stopping at the analytical procedure** **Fix:** an analytical procedure points to where to look; the conclusion comes from the tests of details that follow.

### Recap

- Revenue: the risk of material misstatement is generally high (SA 315); the main fear is overstatement.
- Approach: understand the entity and revenue centres → understand and test controls → substantive analytical procedures (build a sales expectation: units × average price) → tests of details.
- Occurrence: duplicate or cancelled invoices, customer confirmations, fictitious customers, year-end shipments without consent, invoice sequence, unusual journals, sales return ratio.
- Completeness and cut-off: invoice date is not the sale date; check post-year-end credit notes; trace shipping documents to the sales journal; reconcile with stock records and GST returns. (Kesar's best March is only as good as the gate register.)
- Other income: FD interest on time proportion (recompute; bank confirmation; Form 26AS); dividends only when the right to receive is established; mutual fund gain on transfer of title.
- Schedule III: revenue from operations (products, services, grants for section 8 companies, other operating revenues, less excise duty); other income (interest, dividend, net gain on investments, other non-operating income net of direct expenses).

## Lesson 5.2: Audit of expenses

*How the auditor tests purchases, employee benefits, depreciation and other expenses: the controls, the assertion-wise procedures, the six vouching attributes and the Schedule III disclosures.*

### Key idea

An expense can be wrong in both directions: recorded but not real (**occurrence**), or real but missing (**completeness** and **cut-off**), besides wrongly measured or shown under the wrong head. Purchases and payroll lean on controls and an expectation the auditor builds; depreciation is recomputed; other expenses are vouched against six attributes.

### Formats and formulas

### The disclosures to verify

*Reproduce these lists in this order: "state the disclosures" questions give a mark a head.*

| Caption | Disclosure the auditor ensures (SM 5.104, 5.107, 5.110, 5.113) |
|---|---|
| Purchases (Schedule III, Part II) | Purchases of stock-in-trade specifically disclosed; changes in inventories of finished goods, stock-in-trade and work-in-progress specifically disclosed; related party transactions disclosed in the notes |
| Employee benefits expense (Schedule III, Part II) | Showing separately (i) salaries and wages, (ii) contribution to provident and other funds, (iii) expense on Employee Stock Option Scheme (ESOP) and Employee Stock Purchase Plan (ESPP), (iv) staff welfare expenses |
| Depreciation and amortisation | Accounting policy; useful lives of assets as per Schedule II to the Companies Act, 2013; residual value; depreciation method |
| Other expenses | Classified under rent; insurance; power and fuel; repairs and maintenance (building, plant and machinery, others); legal and professional; printing and stationery; travel expenses; miscellaneous expenses |

**Corporate Social Responsibility** (Additional Information, for a company covered under section 135; SM 5.113–5.114):

- (a) amount required to be spent by the company during the year;
- (b) amount of expenditure incurred;
- (c) shortfall at the end of the year;
- (d) total of previous years' shortfall;
- (e) reason for shortfall;
- (f) nature of CSR activities;
- (g) details of related party transactions, for example a contribution to a trust controlled by the company in relation to CSR expenditure;
- (h) where a provision is made with respect to a liability incurred by entering into a contractual obligation, the movements in the provision during the year shown separately.

**Crypto currency or virtual currency** (Additional Information; SM 5.114): (a) profit or loss on transactions involving it; (b) amount held at the reporting date; (c) deposits or advances from any person for trading or investing in it.

**[warning] Version-sensitive**
CSR and Schedule III requirements are stated as the Study Material gives them. Verify against the Study Material and Statutory Update for your attempt.

### The arithmetic the auditor redoes

**[formula] Payroll reasonability**
$$\text{Average salary} = \qquad \frac{\text{Salary expense for the month}}{\text{Employees on the payroll}}$$
Per employee per month, compared with the previous year and the preceding month.

**[formula] PF (or ESI) reasonability**
$$\text{Expected contribution} = \qquad \text{Basic wages} \times \text{Applicable rate}$$
The rate is the one applicable to the entity, as given in the question.

**[formula] Depreciation, straight-line method**
$$\text{Annual depreciation} = \qquad \frac{\text{Cost} - \text{Residual value}}{\text{Useful life}}$$
Charged from the date the asset is available for use.

**[formula] Insurance expense for the period**
$$\text{Expense} = \qquad \text{Premium} \times \frac{\text{Days of cover in the period}}{\text{Days of cover in the policy}}$$
The rest of the premium is prepaid.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where this topic appeared**
In the ICAI documents read for this lesson (listed in the report):

- **Descriptive, 3 to 5 marks.** Occurrence of purchases, "any five points": MTP May 2026 Series I Q2(a) (5 marks). Employee benefit expense procedures, "any five points": MTP May 2026 Series I Q5(b) (5 marks; the answer adds a detailed variance analysis where the expense has risen). Analytical procedures for purchase quantity and price: May 2024 Q4(d) (3 marks) and MTP September 2025 Series II Q5(c) (4 marks). Rent expense: MTP September 2025 Series II Q6(b) (5 marks). Depreciation attributes for an aircraft with a single rate and an undisclosed change in amortisation method: MTP January 2026 Series I Q6(b) (5 marks). CSR disclosures, "any four such matters": May 2025 Q6(c) (4 marks, an internal choice).
- **Capital charged to revenue as a misstatement**: machinery parts that extend useful life charged to repairs, January 2026 Q4(a) (1 + 4 marks).
- **Multiple choice, 2 marks.** Trend analysis of a 43% salary rise, fictitious employees, and occurrence against completeness of employee benefits: MTP January 2026 Series II MCQs 1–5. Bifurcating employee benefits into salaries, PF, ESOP and staff welfare: the **disclosure** assertion (September 2024 MCQ 2). Depreciation "from the date of actual usage" as the incorrect statement (May 2025 MCQ 8). An air conditioner under repairs: the revenue-not-capital attribute (MTP May 2026 Series I MCQ 7). A director's foreign travel: bills in the company's name and business purpose (MTP January 2026 Series I MCQ 5).
- **Identify the assertion, 1 mark.** "Employee benefit expenses do not include the cost of any unauthorised personnel": occurrence (January 2025 Q4(b)(i)).

**[trick] The vouching mantra: "Period, Capital, Paper, Head, Boss, Business"**
This period? Revenue, not capital? Valid paper? Right head? Authorised by the right boss (delegation of authority)? For the business, not personal? Condition: it covers the Study Material's six attributes for other expenses; purchases and payroll have their own procedure lists.

### Answer frame

### "Explain the audit procedures for [purchases / employee benefits / rent]"

1. **Name the assertion** where the question does ("to verify the occurrence of purchases").
2. **List the Study Material's procedures in its order**, one line each, with the document named (gate inward register, GRN, appointment letter, rent agreement).
3. **Apply to the case**: the weak controls, the increase, the escalation clause.
4. **Conclude**: if the evidence is satisfactory the assertion is addressed; otherwise the reporting consequences.

### A "comment on the treatment" case

1. **Provision**: the attribute or rule (six attributes; ready for use; straight-line rent under the policy).
2. **Facts**: the item and its amount.
3. **Analysis**: which attribute fails; compute the correct figure.
4. **Conclusion**: the correct treatment and the misstatement.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: Bunty's tick on the paint bill** **Fix:** a valid document is one attribute of six; "revenue, not capital" (in both directions) and "for the business" are separate questions.
- **Depreciation from the wrong day** **Fix:** depreciation begins when the asset is available for use (in the location and condition to operate as management intends): not before installation and trials are complete, and not later just because orders arrive late.
- **Reading the payroll reasonability one way only** **Fix:** occurrence asks whether recorded expense is real and relates to the entity's personnel (overstatement); completeness and cut-off ask whether all the period's expense, accruals included, has been recorded (understatement). A shortfall against the expectation is as much a finding as an excess.
- **Legal fees vouched and forgotten** **Fix:** legal expenses may reveal a dispute with no provision; follow it into provisions and contingent liabilities.

### Recap

- Purchases: control points (quotes, PO, GRN, 2-way or 3-way match, invoice approval); occurrence: fictitious vendors, gate inward register, quality check, signed GRN, approval and match, stock record; original invoice, risk and reward, input tax, related parties at arm's length, capitalise or expense.
- Purchases completeness: cut-off on the last 5 inward transactions, goods in transit, written representation; analytics: consumption, stock composition, ratios, quantitative reconciliation.
- Employee benefits are tested against fictitious employees (meet a sample, bank details or identity proofs) and against omissions; monthly movement; new hires and leavers; average salary per employee per month, read both ways; accruals for bonus, gratuity, leave encashment; PF and ESI reasonability and challans; production per employee.
- Depreciation: residual value deducted; significant components separately; from the date available for use (not delivery, not first use); on revalued amount from revaluation reserve; change in useful life prospectively.
- Other expenses, six attributes: current period, revenue not capital, valid documents, correct head, authorised as per DoA, business not personal. (Kesar's personal will fails the sixth.)
- Disclosures: employee benefits in four parts; other expenses under eight heads; CSR (a) to (h); crypto (a) to (c).

## Lesson 5.3: Audit of share capital, reserves and borrowings

*How the auditor verifies share capital (sections 52, 53, 54, 66 and 68), reserves and surplus, and borrowings, with the procedures by assertion and the Schedule III disclosures.*

### Key idea

For share capital and reserves the auditor's question is "was it authorised and lawful?" (resolutions, Memorandum, sections 52 to 54, 66 and 68). For borrowings it is "is every claim recorded, and correctly classified?" (board minutes, loan agreements, confirmations from lenders, covenants).

### Formats and formulas

*These lists are what "state the disclosure requirements of Schedule III" questions ask for. Reproduce them in this order.*

### Share capital (Schedule III, Part I; SM 5.20–5.22)

For each class of share capital (different classes of preference shares treated separately):

- (a) the number and amount of shares authorised;
- (b) the number of shares issued, subscribed and fully paid, and subscribed but not fully paid;
- (c) par value per share;
- (d) a reconciliation of the number of shares outstanding at the beginning and at the end of the reporting period;
- (e) the rights, preferences and restrictions attaching to each class, including restrictions on the distribution of dividends and the repayment of capital;
- (f) shares held by the holding company or ultimate holding company, including shares held by their subsidiaries or associates, in aggregate;
- (g) shares held by each shareholder holding **more than 5 per cent** shares, specifying the number;
- (h) shares reserved for issue under options and contracts or commitments for the sale of shares or disinvestment, with terms and amounts;
- (i) for the five years immediately preceding the balance sheet date: (A) shares allotted as fully paid-up under contracts without payment in cash; (B) shares allotted as fully paid bonus shares; (C) shares bought back, each by aggregate number and class;
- (j) terms of securities convertible into equity or preference shares, with the earliest date of conversion, in descending order from the farthest date;
- (k) calls unpaid (showing the aggregate value of calls unpaid by directors and officers);
- (l) forfeited shares (amount originally paid-up);
- (m) **shareholding of promoters**:

*Shares held by promoters at the end of the year:*

| S. No. | Promoter name | No. of shares | % of total shares | % change in year |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | **Total** | | | |

Details are given separately for each class; the percentage change is on the number at the beginning of the year (or at the date of a first issue during the year). If an issue for a specific purpose is not fully used at the balance sheet date, a note shows how the unutilised amount is used or invested.

### Reserves and surplus (Schedule III, Part I; SM 5.26–5.27)

- (i) Classified as: (a) capital reserves; (b) capital redemption reserve; (c) securities premium; (d) debenture redemption reserve; (e) revaluation reserve; (f) share options outstanding account; (g) other reserves (nature, purpose and amount of each); (h) surplus, that is, the balance in the statement of profit and loss showing allocations and appropriations such as dividend, bonus shares and transfers to and from reserves (additions and deductions since the last balance sheet shown under each head).
- (ii) A reserve specifically represented by earmarked investments is termed a "**fund**".
- (iii) A debit balance in the statement of profit and loss is shown as a **negative figure under "Surplus"**; the balance of reserves and surplus, after adjusting it, is shown under "Reserves and Surplus" even if the result is negative.

### Borrowings (Schedule III, Part I; SM 5.32–5.34)

| Long-term borrowings | Short-term borrowings |
|---|---|
| (a) Bonds or debentures | (a) Loans repayable on demand: from banks; from other parties |
| (b) Term loans: from banks; from other parties | (b) Loans and advances from related parties |
| (c) Deferred payment liabilities | (c) Deposits |
| (d) Deposits | (d) Other loans and advances (specify nature) |
| (e) Loans and advances from related parties | |
| (f) Long-term maturities of finance lease obligations | |
| (g) Other loans and advances (specify nature) | |

For both: **secured and unsecured**, with the nature of security; the aggregate of loans **guaranteed by directors or others** under each head; the period and amount of **default** in repaying loans and interest. Long-term only: bonds or debentures (rate, redemption or conversion particulars) in descending order of maturity from the farthest date (for instalments, the first instalment's date); redeemed bonds or debentures that can be reissued; terms of repayment of term loans. Short-term only: **current maturities of long-term borrowings** separately. Other long-term liabilities: trade payables and others. Bank borrowings not used for their specific purpose: where they were used.

**Additional regulatory information** (SM 5.35–5.37): (1) for borrowings on the security of current assets, whether the **quarterly returns or statements** of current assets filed with the banks agree with the books, and if not, a summary of the reconciliation and reasons for material discrepancies; (2) if the company is a declared **wilful defaulter**, the date of declaration and details of defaults; (3) charges or satisfaction **yet to be registered with the Registrar** beyond the statutory period, with details and reasons; (4) **utilisation of borrowed funds and share premium** passed through intermediaries to ultimate beneficiaries (or received from funding parties): dates, amounts, details, and a declaration of compliance with FEMA, 1999 and the Companies Act and of no violation of the Prevention of Money-Laundering Act, 2002.

**[warning] Version-sensitive**
Sections, penalties and Schedule III requirements here are stated as the Study Material gives them. Verify against the Study Material and Statutory Update for your attempt.

### The arithmetic

**[formula] Securities premium on an issue**
$$\text{Premium} = \qquad \text{Shares} \times (\text{Price} - \text{Face value})$$

**[formula] Current maturities of a term loan**
$$\text{Current maturities} = \qquad \text{Instalments due in 12 months}$$
Shown under short-term borrowings; the rest stays long-term.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where this topic appeared**
In the ICAI documents read for this lesson (listed in the report):

- **Descriptive, 4 or 5 marks.** Uses of securities premium: MTP May 2025 Series I Q5(b) (5 marks). Section 53, shares at ₹95 on a face value of ₹100: May 2024 Q5(a) (4 marks); at a discount to creditors: old-scheme May 2023 Q3(b) (4 marks). Sweat equity: May 2026 Q5(a) (1 + 4 marks). Share capital procedures after a fresh issue: MTP September 2026 Series I Q6(a) (5 marks). Borrowings as valid claims: September 2025 Q5(b) (5 marks). Bank borrowings with a confirmation differing from the ledger: September 2024 Q2(b) (4 marks). A cash credit guaranteed by directors: RTP May 2024 Q12.
- **Multiple choice, 2 marks.** Premium cannot write off a debit balance of profit and loss (September 2024 MCQ 7). Sweat equity to employees and directors, at a discount, of a class already issued: all correct (May 2025 MCQ 5). Securities premium is a capital reserve; a debit balance is negative "Surplus" (May 2025 MCQ 6). Special resolution for sweat equity and for reduction (MTP May 2025 Series I MCQ 3). No change in capital: the Company Secretary's written confirmation (MTP May 2025 Series II MCQ 4). Uses of revenue and capital reserves (September 2026 MCQ 5; answers not yet published).

**[trick] "Bonus, Preliminary, Expenses, Redemption, Buy-back"**
Section 52's five uses in order: **B**onus shares, **P**reliminary expenses, **E**xpenses/commission/discount on issue, **R**edemption premium, **B**uy-back. A prescribed class keeps only the first B (equity bonus), E (equity issues) and the last B. Condition: dividends and losses are never on the list.

### Answer frame

### A section-based case (premium, discount, sweat equity)

1. **Relevant provision**: the section, in the Study Material's words (the list of uses; "void"; the penalty and refund; the conditions).
2. **Facts of the case**: price, recipients, resolution, amounts.
3. **Analysis**: is the use or issue within the section? Compute premium, refund, interest, penalty where asked.
4. **Conclusion**: valid or not, and what the auditor verifies or reports.

### "What procedures to verify borrowings?"

Answer by assertion, as the Study Material does: existence, completeness, valuation, then the Schedule III disclosure.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the dividend paid from premium** **Fix:** securities premium is applied only for the section 52 purposes; dividends and losses are not among them.
- **"Discount is allowed if the shareholders agree"** **Fix:** shares at a discount are void, whatever the shareholders resolve, except sweat equity shares and the RBI-scheme conversion of debt into shares for creditors.
- **The whole term loan as long-term** **Fix:** instalments falling due within twelve months are current maturities, shown separately under short-term borrowings; a covenant breach without the lender's written waiver may make the loan current.

### Recap

- Share capital is tallied to last year's audited statements, with the Company Secretary's written confirmation when nothing changed; after a change: within authorised capital (MOA), resolutions, fresh-issue compliances, no discount, cash or non-cash consideration, SEBI, MCA forms (SH-7, PAS-3), fees and stamp duty.
- Section 52: premium goes to a securities premium account and is used only for bonus shares, preliminary expenses, issue expenses or commission or discount, redemption premium and buy-back; a prescribed class keeps only equity bonus, equity issue expenses and buy-back.
- Section 53: shares at a discount are void, except sweat equity and RBI-scheme debt conversion for creditors; penalty up to the amount raised or ₹5 lakh, whichever is less; refund with 12% interest.
- Section 54: sweat equity to employees or directors, at a discount or for non-cash consideration; special resolution with particulars; class already issued; pari passu. Section 66 reduction: special resolution and Tribunal's order.
- Reserves: revenue reserves distributable; capital reserves not free (premium: section 52; CRR: section 55); a debit balance of profit and loss is negative "Surplus"; dividends proposed after the year end are disclosed, not a liability.
- Borrowings: existence (minutes, agreements, confirmations, trust deed, discharge, representation); completeness (schedule, later transactions); valuation (interest, closing rate, covenants and written waivers, current maturities, charges, section 180). (The sahukar always comes back.)

## Lesson 5.4: Audit of trade payables, provisions and other liabilities

*How the auditor searches for unrecorded liabilities (confirmations, cut-off, subsequent payments, statutory dues), values payables and provisions, and checks the MSME, ageing and AS 29 disclosures.*

### Key idea

For liabilities, the auditor hunts for what is **not** in the books: confirmations from creditors (even those with small balances but large purchases), cut-off between goods received and invoices recorded, and payments and bills after the year end. For provisions, the auditor tests the **estimate**.

### Formats and formulas

- Commitments: (a) estimated amount of contracts remaining to be executed on capital account and not provided for; (b) uncalled liability on shares and other investments partly paid; (c) other commitments (specify nature).

**AS 29, for each class of provision**:

- the carrying amount at the beginning and end of the period;
- additional provisions made in the period, including increases to existing provisions;
- amounts used (incurred and charged against the provision) during the period;
- unused amounts reversed during the period;
- a brief description of the nature of the obligation and the expected timing of outflows;
- an indication of the uncertainties about the amount or timing (with the major assumptions about future events, where necessary);
- the amount of any expected reimbursement, stating any asset recognised for it.

### Answer frame

### "Explain the procedures for [existence / completeness / valuation] of trade payables"

1. **State the assertion** in the Study Material's words ("Trade payables and liability balances that were supposed to be recorded have been recognised": completeness).
2. **List that assertion's procedures** in ICAI's order (for completeness: cut-off on the last 5 invoices, sample from ledgers, invoice dates against gate entry, subsequent vouchers, customer advances, statutory dues reasonability and challans, CARO list).
3. **Apply them to the facts**: invoices near the year end, the creditor who did not reply, the dispute.
4. **Close** with the conclusion and, where asked, the disclosure (MSME, ageing).

### "Management refuses a confirmation request"

1. **Provision**: consent of the entity and valid grounds (SM 5.72–5.73); SA 505's three steps: reasons and their validity, effect on risk (including fraud), alternative procedures.
2. **Facts**: the reason given (a dispute, a negotiation) and the size of the balance or purchases.
3. **Analysis**: is the reason supported by evidence; which alternative procedures (subsequent payments, correspondence, goods received notes).
4. **Conclusion**: if the refusal is unreasonable or evidence cannot be obtained, communicate with those charged with governance ([[aud.complete.sa260|SA 260]]) and consider the opinion ([[aud.report.sa705|SA 705]]).

### A provision case

1. **Provision**: the three recognition conditions; the expert and representation procedures.
2. **Facts**: the obligation, the probability, the estimate.
3. **Analysis**: provision, contingent liability disclosure, or nothing; compute the movement.
4. **Conclusion**: the treatment and the AS 29 disclosure.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: celebrating a fall in payables** **Fix:** for liabilities the risk is understatement; test cut-off, invoices and payments after the year end, and confirm with creditors from whom material purchases were made.
- **Confirming only the big balances** **Fix:** confirmations go to all significant balances **and** to parties with material purchases during the year, even if their year-end balance is small: a small balance after large purchases is itself a warning.
- **Hall of Shame: "They cancel out, Ma'am"** **Fix:** each is a separate misstatement of purchases and trade payables (and usually of profit); ask for both to be corrected, extend the cut-off test, and accumulate whatever remains uncorrected.
- **A representation in place of a reply** **Fix:** perform the alternative procedures: subsequent payments, vendor invoices, detailed analysis of the balance.
- **Providing for everything the lawyer mentions** **Fix:** provide only when there is a present obligation, a probable outflow and a reliable estimate; disclose a contingent liability when the outflow is possible; do nothing when it is remote.

### Recap

- The main risk for liabilities is understatement, and verifying them is as important as verifying assets; an omitted liability makes the balance sheet look stronger and profit higher.
- Existence rests on direct confirmation of all significant balances and of parties with material purchases, even if their balance is small; also controls against double recording, the ageing report traced to the ledger, related party payables at arm's length, and the purchases-to-payables trend.
- The confirmation form with no balance is preferable, and replies come to the auditor, not the client; the entity consents; a "do not confirm" request needs valid grounds (SA 505: reasons, risk, alternative procedures); the date is at or near the year end, set in consultation; selection is not revealed; discrepancies are reconciled; non-replies: subsequent payments, invoices, detailed analysis.
- Completeness: last 5 invoices (goods received or risks and rewards passed) and goods received before the year end; invoice dates against gate entries; subsequent vouchers; customer advances; statutory dues reasonability and later challans; CARO list. (Kesar's April bills are March's liability.)
- Valuation: write-back process and approval by senior management (CEO/MD); ageing and disputes; forex restatement (AS 11); MSME principal and interest process.
- Provisions: present obligation + probable outflow + reliable estimate; working, expert (actuary, legal: probable, possible, remote), SA 500 evaluation of a management's expert, written representation; AS 29 movement and narrative disclosures.
- Disclosures: MSME (five items), payables ageing (four rows, from due date, unbilled separately), other current liabilities (nine heads), share application money refundable under other current liabilities; ARI: benami property, struck off companies, eleven ratios with reasons for changes over 25%.

## Lesson 5.5: Audit of property, plant, equipment and intangibles

*How the auditor proves that PPE and intangible assets exist, are completely recorded, belong to the entity, are carried at the right cost less depreciation, and are disclosed as Schedule III requires.*

### Key idea

For PPE and intangible assets the auditor proves five things: the asset **exists** (physically verified, or for an intangible, in active use); every addition and deletion is **completely** recorded; the entity has **rights** to it (invoices and title deeds in its name, charges known); it is **valued** at cost as AS 10 or AS 26 allows, less depreciation or amortisation and impairment; and it is **disclosed** as Schedule III requires.

**[trick] "Price, Path, Pull-down": what goes into PPE**
**Price**: purchase price with import duty and non-refundable taxes, less trade discounts. **Path**: every cost directly attributable to getting the asset to its location and working condition (site, delivery, installation, testing net of sale proceeds, professional fees, the employees who built it). **Pull-down**: the initial estimate of dismantling and restoring the site. Anything that is not one of the three (inauguration, advertising, training, general overheads, regular repairs) is an expense. Condition: it applies to the cost at recognition under AS 10; later costs are tested against the same recognition criteria.

### "How should the auditor verify [assertion] for PPE?"

1. **Name the assertion** and what it means here ("the entity has valid legal ownership rights over the PPE recorded").
2. **List the procedures** of SM 5.58–5.63 for it, in the Study Material's order and words.
3. **Apply them**: the mortgaged property gets the lender's confirmation and the register of charges; the new plant the AS 10 cost test.
4. **Conclude**: what the auditor obtains, and what follows if it cannot.

### PPE against intangibles, assertion by assertion

| Assertion | PPE | Intangible assets |
|---|---|---|
| Existence | Physical verification, tagging, reconciliation with the register | Active use: related sales or production data |
| Completeness | Movement schedule; AS 10 test of additions | Movement schedule; AS 26 test; no research |
| Valuation | Depreciation on all but freehold land; AS 28 | Amortisation on all; AS 28 |
| Rights | Invoices, title deeds, lender confirmation, charges | Invoices and contracts in the entity's name |

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the singer capitalised with the kiln** **Fix:** only the purchase price, costs directly attributable to bringing the asset to its location and working condition, and the dismantling estimate join PPE. Inauguration, advertising, training and overheads are expenses however close to the asset they sit.
- **Proving an app exists by looking at the server** **Fix:** existence of an intangible is shown by active use: the sales of the goods or services in which the software was used, or production data for a design.
- **Accepting the photocopy of a mortgaged deed** **Fix:** insist on the original title deeds; where the property is given as security and the bank holds them, ask management to obtain the lender's confirmation, and check the register of charges.
- **Treating all software as intangible** **Fix:** software that is an integral part of its hardware (the machine cannot operate without it, or an operating system) goes with the hardware as a fixed asset; only software that is not integral is an intangible asset.

### Recap

- PPE cost = purchase price (import duties and non-refundable taxes in, trade discounts out) + directly attributable costs + initial dismantling and restoration estimate.
- Not PPE: inauguration, launch advertising, staff training for a new location, general overheads, regular repairs.
- Existence: verification plan, supervision, tagging, reconciliation with the fixed asset register; shortages deleted after approval, depreciation stops.
- Completeness: opening + additions − deletions = closing, tallied both ways; additions tested for AS 10, date, approval, quotations; deletions for approval, discard note, sale process, gain or loss.
- Rights: invoices and deeds in the entity's name, original title deeds, lender's confirmation for pledged deeds, register of charges.
- Valuation: depreciation on all PPE except freehold land, method matching the pattern of benefits, AS 28 impairment.
- Intangibles: existence by active use; nothing from research; no internally generated goodwill; integral software is a fixed asset.
- Schedule III adds title deeds not in the company's name, registered-valuer revaluation and CWIP ageing. (The singer still cannot fire a kulhad.)

## Lesson 5.6: Audit of inventories, receivables, cash and loans and advances

*How the auditor tests the assets that move every day: receivables confirmed and aged, cash counted by surprise and banks reconciled, inventories counted and valued, loans and advances confirmed, and each disclosed as Schedule III requires.*

### Key idea

Current assets are proved at the balance sheet date: **receivables and loans** by direct confirmation and a recoverability review, **cash** by a surprise count of all balances together and by bank reconciliations and bank confirmations, **inventories** by attending the count and valuing at the lower of cost and net realisable value, and each is **disclosed** as Schedule III requires.

### Formats and formulas

**[formula] Inventory turnover ratio**
$$\text{Inventory turnover ratio} = \qquad \frac{\text{Cost of goods sold}}{\text{Average inventory}}$$
A significantly lower ratio than before may reveal obsolete inventory (SM 5.52, 5.55).

### Schedule III disclosures to verify

*Each caption's list in this order; "state the disclosures" questions mark each point.*

**Trade receivables** (SM 5.44–5.45): (i) the ageing schedule below; (ii) sub-classified as secured, considered good; unsecured, considered good; doubtful; (iii) allowance for bad and doubtful debts disclosed under the relevant heads; (iv) debts due by directors or other officers, severally or jointly with others, or by firms or private companies in which a director is a partner, director or member, stated separately.

**Cash and cash equivalents** (SM 5.49–5.50): (i) classified as balances with banks; cheques, drafts on hand; cash on hand; others; (ii) earmarked balances (for example, unpaid dividend) stated separately; (iii) balances held as margin money or security against borrowings, guarantees or other commitments disclosed separately; (iv) repatriation restrictions stated; (v) bank deposits with more than 12 months' maturity disclosed separately.

**Inventories** (SM 5.56): classified as raw materials; work-in-progress; finished goods; stock-in-trade (goods acquired for trading); stores and spares; loose tools; others (specify nature); **goods-in-transit disclosed under each sub-head**; mode of valuation stated.

- Cash: count by surprise on the last day and again after the close, all balances simultaneously, cashier signing; verify every BRS; confirm 100% of bank balances.
- Inventories: attend the count, test cut-off, confirm third-party stock by a signed declaration, value at the lower of cost and NRV.
- Receivables also: test sales controls; cut-off on the last 5 days; allowance reviewed with the ageing and litigation lists; write-offs approved by the board.
- Ageing schedule: undisputed or disputed, good or doubtful, from the due date (else the transaction date); unbilled dues separately.
- Stale cheque (3 months or more) leaves the BRS and goes back to liabilities; unrecorded bank items adjusted or the report qualified.
- Loans and advances: confirmations with interest, agreements, MOA/AOA authority, board minutes; GST credit by reasonability and returns. Loans on demand or without terms to promoters, directors, KMPs and related parties: amount and percentage to total loans. (Never phone the cashier first.)

## Lesson 5.7: Audit of contingent liabilities

*How the auditor hunts for liabilities that are not in the ledger, grades each claim, guarantee and dispute as probable, possible or remote with expert evidence, and checks the Schedule III and AS 29 disclosure.*

### Key idea

Contingent liabilities are found, not vouched. The auditor searches for every claim, guarantee and dispute, obtains evidence (often a legal expert's assessment) of whether an outflow is probable, possible or remote, and checks that probable obligations are provided for, possible ones are disclosed as Schedule III and AS 29 require, and remote ones are left out.

### Formats and formulas

### Schedule III: "Contingent liabilities and commitments (to the extent not provided for)" (SM 5.88)

*Reproduce the heads in this order; the class decides where each item goes.*

| (i) Contingent liabilities | (ii) Commitments |
|---|---|
| (a) Claims against the company not acknowledged as debt | (a) Estimated amount of contracts remaining to be executed on capital account and not provided for |
| (b) Guarantees | (b) Uncalled liability on shares and other investments partly paid |
| (c) Other money for which the company is contingently liable | (c) Other commitments (specify nature) |

### AS 29: what each class of contingent liability must say (SM 5.89)

Unless the possibility of any outflow in settlement is remote, for **each class** of contingent liability at the balance sheet date, a brief **description of its nature** and, where practicable:

1. an **estimate of its financial effect**;
2. an **indication of the uncertainties** relating to any outflow; and
3. the **possibility of any reimbursement**.

Where any of this is not disclosed because it is not practicable, ==that fact should be stated==.

**[warning] Schedule III and AS 29 requirements are stated as the Study Material gives them. Verify against the Study Material and Statutory Update for your attempt.**

**[trick] "Probable provides, possible prints, remote rests"**
A **probable** outflow with a present obligation and a reliable estimate is **provided** in the books; a **possible** one is **printed** in the notes as a contingent liability; a **remote** one **rests**, with no disclosure. Condition: "probable" also needs the present obligation and the reliable estimate; a present obligation that cannot be measured reliably is printed, not provided.

### Answer frame

### "How will you verify contingent liabilities?"

1. One line: contingent liabilities are not recorded, so the audit is a search plus an assessment.
2. The Study Material's joint list in order: list of provisions against the ledger; agreements, warranty commitments and legal claims; management's working; the expert's report (actuary, legal expert: probable, possible or remote); evaluation of the management's expert under SA 500; the written representation.
3. The SA 501 search: inquiry, minutes and correspondence with counsel, legal expense accounts; direct communication with external counsel.
4. Disclosure: the Schedule III heads and the AS 29 particulars.

### A case on an undisclosed claim

| Step | What to write |
|---|---|
| Relevant provision | AS 29 (provision or contingent liability, disclosed unless remote); SA 501 (search, direct communication with counsel, refusal leads to SA 705) |
| Facts of the case | The claim, its amount, materiality, what management has done or refused |
| Analysis | Probable, possible or remote, on what evidence; material or not; pervasive or not |
| Conclusion | The treatment management must adopt and, if it does not or evidence is denied, the modification |

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: vouching the litigation list and stopping there** **Fix:** the list is where the search starts, not ends. Inquiry, minutes, correspondence with counsel and the legal expense accounts are how unlisted claims are found.
- **Calling every claim a contingent liability** **Fix:** ask the three recognition questions first; only what fails them is disclosed, and then only if not remote.
- **Disclosing the remote ones "to be safe"** **Fix:** AS 29 requires disclosure unless the possibility of outflow is remote. A remote claim is left out; the auditor's work is to check that "remote" is supported by the expert's assessment.
- **Putting capital commitments under contingent liabilities** **Fix:** contracts remaining to be executed on capital account and uncalled liability on partly paid shares are **commitments**, a separate head in the same Schedule III note.

### Recap

- Contingent liability: a possible obligation that uncertain future events outside the entity's control will confirm, or a present obligation not recognised (outflow not probable, or not reliably measurable).
- Present obligation, probable outflow, reliable estimate: provision. Possible: disclose. Remote: nothing.
- Procedures: list against the ledger, agreements and claims, management's working, the expert's report (probable, possible, remote), SA 500 evaluation, written representation.
- SA 501 search: inquiry (including in-house counsel), minutes and correspondence with external counsel, legal expense accounts; then a letter of inquiry to external counsel, with management's permission.
- Refusal of permission and no alternative evidence: modify the opinion under SA 705.
- Schedule III: claims not acknowledged as debt; guarantees; other money contingently liable. Commitments: capital contracts, uncalled liability on partly paid shares, others.
- AS 29, each class: nature, and where practicable the financial effect, uncertainties and reimbursement; if impracticable, say so. ("Nothing has happened yet" is exactly when the note is needed.)
