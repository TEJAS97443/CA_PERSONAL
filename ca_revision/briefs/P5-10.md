## Lesson 10.1: Bank accounting system and audit approach

*Why a bank audit is different, what the auditor must check in a core banking system, the four bases of the audit plan, the six control questions, the team discussion and the stages of conducting a bank audit.*

### Key idea

In a bank, the system produces the numbers that matter most: asset classification, provisions and interest income. The auditor must obtain evidence that the RBI's norms are built into that system, never assume that system-generated information is correct, and plan the audit around the bank's risks and controls.

### Exam angle

**[exam] What ICAI asked, May 2024 to September 2026**
In the ICAI documents read for this lesson, the bank audit approach was asked in five papers, each time as a 4–5 mark descriptive question built on a short bank scenario:

- **CBS and reliance on controls**: MTP January 2026 Series I Q1(a) (5 marks): a bank whose CBS processes deposits, interest and loans; when tests of controls are needed and what evidence supports reliance.
- **Risk management process**: MTP January 2025 Series II Q3(a) (5 marks): how to evaluate the adequacy of a bank's risk management controls (the five requirements (a) to (e)).
- **Engagement team discussion**: MTP January 2026 Series II Q2(b) (5 marks): a branch audit meeting with a valuation expert; purpose, three matters, two advantages.
- **KYC and going concern**: MTP May 2024 Series I Q3(b) (4 marks): a payments bank barred from new deposits for KYC and money-laundering failures, with an undisclosed material uncertainty.
- **Other reports on advances**: MTP September 2024 Series II Q3(a) (5 marks): a branch auditor whose sample of advances showed nothing unusual asks which documents carry comments on advances; ICAI's answer is the seven reports of stage 18, in the Study Material's order.

The chapter's own Test Your Knowledge asks for four peculiarities of banking, the RBI's role, the importance of KYC norms and the engagement team discussion, and has correct/incorrect statements on relying on system-generated information.

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
Suggested answers reproduce the Study Material's lists in its order and words ("nature and level of operations", "segregation of duties (particularly between front and back offices)", "susceptibility of the bank's branch financial statements to material misstatements"), then apply them in one line each to the scenario. For an "Explain … engagement team discussion" question, the marks sit in three blocks: purpose, matters, advantages.

**[trick] Plan on N-A-C-R**
The four bases of the audit plan: **N**ature and level of operations, **A**dverse features, **C**ompliance in previous reports, **R**isks from weak or breached internal controls. It works for the audit plan of a bank or a branch; it is not the list for the overall audit strategy under SA 300.

### Answer frame

### "Explain the auditor's approach in a CBS environment"

1. The duty: be familiar with and satisfied that the latest RBI norms and parameters are built into the system generating classification, provisioning and income recognition data.
2. The prohibition: do not assume system-generated information is correct without evidence.
3. The method: professional skepticism and prudence; manual checks of authenticity and consistency; documentation of the results.
4. Applied to the facts, then the conclusion (rely, or do not rely and re-work).

### "Discuss the engagement team discussion in a bank audit"

| Block | Points that earn marks |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Understand the bank and its environment, including internal control; assess the potential for material misstatement; documented; at the planning stage |
| Who | All personnel on the engagement, including experts contracted by the firm, with the engagement partner |
| Matters | (a) to (f): likely errors; prior-year errors; how fraud might be perpetrated; responses to engagement, pervasive and specific risks; professional skepticism; alertness to signs of misstatement |
| Advantages | Emphasis on fraud risk and the response; delegation to experienced members and procedures when fraud is found; need for specialists reviewed |

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: "the system is RBI-compliant, so I ticked it"** **Fix:** the Study Material says the opposite: the auditor should not assume system-generated information is correct without evidence that it is based on the required parameters; check the parameters, re-work a sample by hand, document it.
- **Leaving the expert out of the team** **Fix:** the engagement team is all personnel performing the engagement, including any experts contracted by the firm in connection with it.
- **Mixing up the plan's bases with SA 300's strategy factors** **Fix:** the bank answer is the Study Material's four bases: nature and level of operations, adverse features, compliance in previous reports, internal control risks.

### Recap

- A bank audit is shaped by huge volumes, a wide network, many products, heavy technology and strict RBI vigilance.
- In a CBS environment the auditor must be satisfied that the latest RBI norms are built into the system that generates classification, provisioning and income data; never assume system information is correct; check manually where needed and document.
- Banking operations happen only at branches; controlling offices set policies, delegation and supervision.
- Audit plan bases: nature and level of operations, adverse features, compliance in previous reports, internal control risks.
- Six control questions: who, what, when, where, why, how.
- Engagement team (experts included) discusses six matters at planning: likely errors, prior errors, fraud methods, responses to risks, skepticism, alertness; documented.
- Review the adverse comments on advances in seven reports, including the RBI's inspection report, concurrent audit reports and the manager's charge-handing-over report.
- Financial statements: section 29 of the Banking Regulation Act; Third Schedule Form A (Balance Sheet) and Form B (Profit and Loss Account). (Read the recipe before you taste the chai.)

## Lesson 10.2: Appointment, powers and report of a bank's auditor

*Who may audit a bank, who appoints and pays the auditor of a banking company, a nationalised bank and an RRB, the auditor's powers, the report to the Central Government, the LFAR and reporting frauds to the RBI.*

### Key idea

A bank's auditor must be qualified to audit companies and has a company auditor's powers of access, but the appointing body, the approving authority and the fee-fixing authority differ for a banking company, a nationalised bank and a Regional Rural Bank; a nationalised bank's auditor reports to the Central Government; and suspected frauds are reported to the RBI as well as to the bank's chief executive.

### Exam angle

**[exam] What ICAI asked, May 2024 to September 2026**
In the ICAI documents read for this lesson, the bank auditor's appointment and reports were asked mostly as 2-mark MCQs:

- **LFAR**: January 2026 MCQ 13 (four statements: who must furnish it, who specifies its matters, the submission date, whether an executive summary is mandatory); the old-scheme MTP May 2023 Series II asked the submission date directly (30th June).
- **SCAs' other reports**: MTP September 2024 Series I MCQ 5. Option (v) read "adverse credit-**lending** ratio in the rural areas", and ICAI's key, (b), leaves it out. The Study Material's report is on adverse **credit-deposit** ratio, which is the likely reason (the key gives only the letter). Learn the Study Material's words.
- **CARO and banks**: MTP September 2024 Series I Q3(a) and MTP January 2025 Series II Q2(b) asked which companies CARO 2020 exempts (5 marks); a banking company is the first on the list.
- **Accepting a branch audit**: MTP September 2024 Series II MCQs 9 and 10 (an undertaking that the firm is not disqualified under section 141(3)(d)(ii) for indebtedness: in ICAI's answer the provision addresses self-interest threats and the firm can give the undertaking after verifying that it complies; and a non-disclosure declaration, which is about confidentiality). The old-scheme MTP November 2023 Series II Q6(a) (3 marks) asked whether a bank's concurrent auditor could take its statutory branch audit in the same year.
- **Pressure from the branch**: MTP September 2026 Series I MCQ 12: a branch manager suggesting that irregularities not be reported calls on integrity and objectivity.

The chapter's Test Your Knowledge adds an MCQ on who appoints a nationalised bank's branch auditor, correct/incorrect statements on the AGM appointment and on fraud reporting, and "Is the statutory auditor of a bank required to report under CARO 2020?".

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
For appointment questions, the marks are in three words per bank: who appoints, whose approval, who fixes the fee. For the report of a nationalised bank's auditor, ICAI's points are the four matters (a) to (d) in order, addressed to the Central Government.

**[trick] RBI for the two, Centre for the rural**
Banking company and nationalised bank: **RBI** approval before appointment. Regional Rural Bank: **Central Government** approval. Condition: this is the approval for appointment only; the remuneration rules are separate (general meeting under section 142 for a banking company; RBI with the Central Government for nationalised banks and the State Bank of India).

### Answer frame

### "Discuss the provisions on the appointment and remuneration of a bank's auditor"

| Point | Banking company | Nationalised bank | Regional Rural Bank |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qualification | Qualified to audit companies (section 30(1)) | Same | Same |
| Appointed by | Shareholders at the AGM | Bank, through its Board | The bank concerned |
| Prior approval | RBI | RBI | Central Government |
| Remuneration | Section 142 (general meeting) | RBI, with the Central Government | Not given |
| Powers | As a company auditor (access to books, accounts, documents, vouchers) | Same | Same |

### A reporting case

1. **Provision**: whom the report goes to and what it must state, in the Study Material's order; or the RBI circular, SA 250 and SA 240 for a fraud case.
2. **Facts**: the kind of bank, the gap in the draft or the suspicious transaction.
3. **Analysis**: why the provision applies (confidentiality is over-ridden; CARO excluded for a banking company; unaudited branches need figures).
4. **Conclusion**: the corrected report or the report to the RBI and the Chairman/MD/CEO.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the nationalised bank's AGM** **Fix:** it is appointed by the bank acting through its Board of Directors, with the RBI's prior approval; the AGM appointment is for a banking company.
- **The CARO annexure that follows every report** **Fix:** CARO 2020 does not apply to a banking company as defined in section 5(c) of the Banking Regulation Act; the section 143 matters are still stated.
- **"The vigilance department will handle it"** **Fix:** anything susceptible to be fraud goes to the RBI, in addition to the Chairman/MD/CEO; confidentiality is over-ridden by statute, law or courts (SA 250).

### Recap

- A bank is audited by a person duly qualified to be an auditor of companies (section 30(1), Banking Regulation Act); eligibility and disqualifications are as for a company auditor.
- Appointment: banking company at the AGM, nationalised bank by its Board, both with RBI approval; Regional Rural Bank by the bank with Central Government approval.
- Remuneration: banking company under section 142 (general meeting); nationalised banks and SBI by the RBI in consultation with the Central Government.
- Powers: the same access to books, accounts, documents and vouchers as a company's auditor.
- A nationalised bank's auditor reports to the Central Government: true and fair view and explanations; transactions within powers; adequacy of branch returns; any other matter.
- Report the number of unaudited branches and their advances, deposits, interest income and interest expense; banking company: section 143 matters yes, CARO 2020 no.
- LFAR: matters specified by the RBI; SCAs submit it latest by 30 June; executive summary optional.
- Anything susceptible to be fraud: report to the RBI in addition to the Chairman/MD/CEO; confidentiality is over-ridden by law (SA 250). (The invigilator tells the board, not just the principal.)

## Lesson 10.3: Audit of advances, NPAs and drawing power

*What bank advances are and how they are secured, when an advance becomes an NPA, how it ages from sub-standard to loss and what provision each stage needs, how drawing power is computed, and what the auditor checks.*

### Key idea

Classification as an NPA rests on the record of recovery, borrower by borrower, never on the security or the borrower's net worth. The security comes in afterwards, to size the provision, and to push an account straight to doubtful or loss when it has eroded.

### Formats and formulas

### Computation of drawing power

*Reproduce this layout from memory (SM 10.35): unpaid stocks come off before the margin, ineligible debtors come off before the margin, and the total is compared with the sanctioned limit.*

| Particulars of current assets | Amount | DP |
|---|---:|---:|
| **(A) Stocks** | | |
| Stocks at realisable value | 1,000 | |
| Less: Unpaid stocks: sundry creditors 300, acceptances/LCs etc. 300 | 600 | |
| Paid-for stocks | 400 | |
| Less: Margin @ 25% | 100 | 300 |
| **(B) Debtors** | | |
| Total debtors | 1,000 | |
| Less: Ineligible debtors | 200 | |
| Eligible debtors | 800 | |
| Less: Margin @ 40% | 320 | 480 |
| **Total drawing power** | | **780** |

The margins and the eligibility of debtors (for example, debtors up to 90 days) come from the sanction terms. ICAI's suggested answers then restrict the amount the borrower can draw to the **sanctioned limit** when the computed DP is higher.

**[formula] Provision on a doubtful asset**
$$\text{Provision} = r \times \text{Secured portion} \qquad + 100\% \times \text{Unsecured portion}$$

Here $r$ is 25% (D1), 40% (D2) or 100% (D3); the secured portion is the realisable value of the security, up to the outstanding, and the unsecured portion is the rest of the outstanding. Sub-standard: 15% of the outstanding; loss: 100% of the outstanding (SM 10.28).

**[formula] Large advance (SM 10.21)**
$$\text{Large advance} > \min\left(\text{₹10 crore},\ 10\%\ \text{of}\ A\right)$$

Here $A$ is the aggregate year-end advances of the branch, and the test is on the advance's year-end balance.

### Exam angle

**[exam] What ICAI asked, May 2024 to September 2026**
This is the most examined part of the chapter. In the ICAI documents read for this lesson it appeared in 26 documents:

- **Out of order** (3–5 marks): September 2024 Q3(c) (no credits for two quarters); MTP September 2024 Series I Q6(a) (a CBS account extract with no credit after 31 December); MTP September 2025 Series II Q3(c) (credits not covering interest); MTP May 2026 Series II Q2(b); MTP September 2026 Series II Q4(a).
- **Drawing power computations** (4–5 marks): May 2025 Q5(c); RTP September 2024 Q19; MTP September 2025 Series I Q2(b); MTP September 2026 Series I Q2(b); RTP January 2026 MCQ 5. Three of these end with ICAI's twist: the computed DP exceeds the sanctioned limit, so drawings are restricted to the limit.
- **Provisioning** (usually 5 marks): RTP January 2025 Q19; RTP September 2026 Q19; RTP May 2024 Q23 (a D1 provision); January 2026 Q6(a) (D1 provision, plus a loan secured at under 10%); MTP January 2025 Series II MCQs 6 and 8.
- **Erosion of security**: MTP January 2026 Series I Q2(b) (5 marks); January 2025 MCQ 12; MTP September 2024 Series I MCQ 4.
- **Special rules in MCQs**: Government guarantees (May 2024 Q6(b), 4 marks; January 2025 MCQ 10; MTP September 2024 Series I MCQ 2); consortium advances (January 2025 MCQ 9; May 2026 MCQ 11); SMA (May 2026 MCQ 12; MTP May 2024 Series I MCQ 3.3); borrower-wise classification (MTP January 2025 Series II MCQ 7); crop loans (September 2025 MCQ 13; MTP May 2025 Series II MCQ 10; MTP January 2025 Series II MCQ 9; RTP September 2025 Q19); primary security (MTP September 2026 Series II MCQ 8); advances against NSCs (RTP September 2025 MCQ 8); regularisation before year-end (MTP January 2025 Series I Q3(a), 5 marks; MTP September 2026 Series II MCQ 9); a stale stock statement and stock at a third-party warehouse (MTP May 2026 and September 2026 Series I, the same Case Scenario 3).
- **Audit of advances**: the matters on which the auditor obtains evidence (MTP May 2024 Series II Q1(b), 4 marks).

**[exam] A published answer that differs from the Study Material**
MTP January 2026 Series I Q2(b) gives a loan of ₹10 crore whose security is now worth ₹80 lakh against ₹6 crore at the last RBI inspection. ICAI's suggested answer applies the 50% erosion test and classifies it as doubtful. The realisable value is 8% of the outstanding, and the Study Material says that when it is less than 10% of the outstanding the security is ignored and the asset is straight away a loss asset (SM 10.32). This lesson follows the Study Material: test 10% of the outstanding first. ICAI's own January 2026 Q6(a) and January 2025 MCQ 12 apply the 10% rule.

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
Suggested answers quote the norm in the Study Material's words ("record of recovery", "remains continuously in excess of the sanctioned limit/drawing power", "straight-away classified as loss asset"), apply it to the figures, and end with the classification and the provision on the secured and unsecured portions. Drawing power answers are a statement in the format above plus a one-line conclusion.

**[trick] 15, 25, 40, 100**
Sub-standard 15; D1 25; D2 40; D3 100; loss 100. For D1, D2 and D3 the rate is on the secured portion, and the unsecured portion is always 100%. Condition: these are the Study Material's rates; sub-standard and loss rates apply to the whole outstanding.

### Answer frame

### A classification and provisioning case

1. **Provision**: the NPA definition for the facility (term loan, CC, bill, crop loan), the categories and rates, and any special rule the facts touch (borrower-wise, guarantee, erosion, deposits).
2. **Facts**: dates or periods, outstanding, security, what the branch did.
3. **Analysis**: when it became an NPA; its age; any rule that moves it; secured and unsecured portions; the provision.
4. **Conclusion**: classification, provision required, short or excess, and the effect (advances and profit overstated).

### "State the matters on which the auditor obtains evidence about advances"

The seven points (a) to (g) of section 7 above, in the Study Material's order and words, each applied in a line if a case is given.

### A drawing power question

The statement in the format above, then one line comparing the total with the sanctioned limit and the outstanding, and one on the date of the stock statement.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: "fully secured, so not an NPA"** **Fix:** classification as NPA is based on the record of recovery; the security and the borrower's net worth are not taken into account.
- **The good loan of a bad borrower** **Fix:** classification is borrower-wise, not facility-wise.
- **The doubtful provision on the whole outstanding** **Fix:** the rate goes on the secured portion only; the unsecured portion needs 100%.
- **Testing erosion on the wrong base** **Fix:** 50% is measured against the value assessed by the bank or accepted by the RBI at the last inspection; 10% against the outstanding. Check the 10% test first: if it is met, the asset is loss.
- **DP on paper the borrower never paid for** **Fix:** deduct unpaid stocks first, then the margin; use a current statement; restrict drawings to the lower of DP and the sanctioned limit.

### Recap

- An NPA is an advance that has ceased to generate income: term loan or bill overdue for more than 90 days, cash credit or overdraft out of order.
- A cash credit is out of order in any of three cases: balance continuously above the limit or DP, no credits for 90 days, or credits below the interest debited.
- Classification rests on the record of recovery, borrower-wise; security and net worth are ignored.
- Sub-standard (NPA up to 12 months) 15%; doubtful D1/D2/D3 25%/40%/100% of the secured portion plus 100% of the unsecured; loss 100%.
- Security below 10% of the outstanding: loss straight away; below 50% of the last inspection value: doubtful straight away.
- Central Government guarantee intact: standard, but NPA for income; State Government: NPA for both. Advances against deposits with adequate margin need not be NPAs.
- Crop loans: NPA after two crop seasons overdue (short duration) or one (long duration, season over a year, fixed by the State Level Bankers' Committee).
- DP = paid-for stocks less margin + eligible debtors less margin, on a statement not older than three months; drawings within both DP and the sanctioned limit; stock audit above ₹5 crore funded exposure. (Rent, not the house.)

## Lesson 10.4: Audit of revenue items in banks

*A bank's Profit and Loss Account in Form B, which income it may accrue and which it books only on receipt, how income on NPAs is reversed, and how the auditor checks interest expense, operating expenses and provisions.*

### Key idea

A bank recognises income on accrual, but income from non-performing assets only when actually realised: when an advance becomes an NPA, unrealised interest, fees and commission already credited are reversed or provided for, and further interest is kept in a memorandum account outside income and outside gross advances.

### Formats and formulas

### Form B: Profit and Loss Account for the year ended 31st March

|---|---:|

**[formula] Weighted average interest rate on deposits**
$$\bar r = \frac{\sum (B \times r)}{\sum B}$$

Here $B$ is the average balance of each type of deposit, worked out from its quarter-end balances, and $r$ is that type's rate. Compare $\bar r$ with the actual average rate (interest on deposits as per the accounts ÷ total average deposits) and with the previous year's rate (SM 10.46–10.47).

**[formula] Discount on a bill purchased, at the year-end**
$$\text{Earned} = \text{Discount} \times \frac{\text{Days to year-end}}{\text{Days of the bill}}$$

Earned: the discount income of the year; the rest is unexpired discount, shown under Other Liabilities (SM 10.42). The Study Material gives no day-count rule; this lesson counts from the day after the purchase up to the year-end (15 February to 31 March is 44 days). State the convention in an answer.

### Exam angle

**[exam] What ICAI asked, May 2024 to September 2026**
In the ICAI documents read for this lesson, revenue items appeared in 10 documents:

- **Provisions and contingencies, procedures** (4 marks): September 2024 Q6(a) (four provisions of a bank listed); MTP September 2026 Series II Q6(c). The old-scheme MTP November 2020 Series I Q4(a) asked the same for a nationalised bank.
- **Operating expenses**: MTP May 2026 Series I Q2(b) (5 marks): a 28% rise in operating expenses at a 350-branch bank.
- **Income on NPAs**: RTP May 2025 Q18 (procedures to verify reversal of income) and Q19 (partial recoveries without an agreement); RTP May 2024 Q22 (income recognition once a term loan is sub-standard); September 2025 Q3(c) (4 marks: take-out finance and income on an NPA); RTP September 2025 Q19 (interest on a crop loan and on a guaranteed advance).
- **Accrual of particular items**: May 2026 MCQ 10 (interest on advances against deposits, discount on bills purchased spanning the year-end, rediscounting charges); MTP September 2024 Series II MCQ 12 (a letter of credit is non-funded and its commission is not interest earned).
- **CBS and interest**: MTP January 2026 Series I Q1(a) (5 marks): reliance on CBS controls over interest calculations.

The chapter's Test Your Knowledge asks what is included in "Interest earned" (discount on bills), the audit of provisions and contingencies, a short note on reversal of income, and two analytical procedures for interest on deposits.

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
Lists are marked in the Study Material's words: "reversed or provided for", "not out of fresh/additional credit facilities", "unexpired discount … Other Liabilities", "commission … due only when the bill has been collected", "weighted average interest rate". For a case, each item earns marks for the rule, the amount and the treatment.

**[trick] The NPA income checklist: R-S-M-R**
**R**everse or provide the unrealised; **S**top applying interest; **M**emorandum account, outside gross advances; **R**ecognise only realised amounts, not funded by a fresh facility. It applies to every NPA, including Government guaranteed accounts treated as NPA for income recognition.

### Answer frame

### "Write a short note on reversal of income"

1. NPA as at the close of any year: entire unrealised interest credited in past periods reversed or provided for; also for Government guaranteed accounts.
2. Fees, commission and similar income cease to accrue and are reversed or provided for, if uncollected.
3. Wrongly recognised income: reverse if in the current year; provide an equivalent amount if in previous years.
4. Enquire into large unexplained debits to interest income and borrowers' complaints about interest charged.
5. Add, where relevant: leased assets, take-out finance, partial recoveries, memorandum account.

### "Discuss two analytical procedures to verify interest on deposits"

| Procedure | What the auditor does |
|---|---|
| Weighted average rate | From quarter-end deposit analysis, compute the weighted average rate; compare with the actual average rate as per the accounts; enquire into a material difference |
| Trend | Compare the average rate paid with previous years; analyse material differences |
| Period analysis | Month-on-month or quarter-on-quarter cost analysis from the ledger break-up; document reasons for variances |

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: "the system charged it, so it is income"** **Fix:** on an NPA, interest is income only when actually realised; unrealised interest already credited is reversed (current year) or provided for (previous years).
- **The Central Government guarantee that saves the income** **Fix:** it is standard for classification but an NPA for income recognition; the reversal rules apply to Government guaranteed accounts also.
- **Netting the rediscount** **Fix:** the rediscount interest is not to be netted off; and only the discount for the period up to the year-end is income, the unexpired part going to Other Liabilities.
- **Commission on a bill nobody has paid yet** **Fix:** the commission becomes due only when the bill has been collected.

### Recap

- Form B has two income heads (interest earned, other income) and three expenditure heads (interest expended, operating expenses, provisions and contingencies).
- Income is accrued when collection is reasonably expected, but income on NPAs only when actually realised; this also covers Government guaranteed accounts.
- On an NPA: reverse or provide for unrealised interest, fees and commission; reverse current-year wrong income, provide for earlier years'; memorandum account outside gross advances.
- Partial recoveries: uniform, consistent appropriation policy; never income if paid out of a fresh facility to the same borrower.
- Advances against deposits, NSCs, KVPs and life policies with adequate margin: interest on the due date.
- Bills purchased: apportion the discount; unexpired part to Other Liabilities; rediscount interest not netted. Bills for collection: commission due when collected.
- RBI 1% norm: accrue items above 1% of total income (gross) or of net profit before tax (net); smaller ones may be on receipt without qualification.
- Interest expense: weighted average rate from quarter-end deposits against the actual average rate and last year's; test checks against the rate card. Provisions: RBI circulars, classification sample, ledger agreement, tax computation. (Cross out the unpaid cups.)
