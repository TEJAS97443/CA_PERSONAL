## Lesson 12.1: Service costing and composite cost units

*A service has no physical unit to count, so its cost unit often joins two measures (tonne-km, patient-day); absolute and commercial bases count them, and weights put different grades on one scale.*

### Key idea

When one measure cannot capture what a service does, the cost unit joins two (a composite unit: tonne-km, passenger-km, patient-day). Absolute basis: add up load × distance leg by leg. Commercial basis: average load × total distance. Different grades of a service are put on one scale by weights (equivalent service units).

### Formats and formulas

*Reproduce the working-note layout from memory.* ICAI's suggested answers lay the absolute tonne-km out leg by leg (MTP Series I for May 2024 Q2(a)):

| Leg | Distance (km) | Load (tonnes) | Tonne-km |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A to B | d₁ | W₁ | d₁ × W₁ |
| B to C | d₂ | W₂ | d₂ × W₂ |
| C to A | d₃ | W₃ | d₃ × W₃ |
| **Total** | **Σd** | | **Absolute tonne-km** |

**[formula] Absolute (weighted average) tonne-km**
$$\text{Absolute} = (W_1 \times D_1) + (W_2 \times D_2) \qquad + \dots + (W_n \times D_n)$$
W = weight carried on a leg, D = the distance of that leg (SM 12.7).

**[formula] Commercial (simple average) tonne-km**
$$\text{Commercial} = (D_1 + D_2 + \dots + D_n) \qquad \times \frac{W_1 + W_2 + \dots + W_n}{n}$$
n = the number of legs. Total distance × the simple average of the loads (SM 12.7).

**[formula] Cost per composite unit**
$$\text{Cost per tonne-km} = \frac{\text{Total cost}}{\text{Total tonne-km}}$$
Total cost and total tonne-km of the same period. The same for passenger-km, patient-days or room-days.

**[formula] Equivalent service units**
$$\text{Equivalent units} = \sum (\text{units} \times \text{weight}) \qquad \text{Grade rate} = \text{Base rate} \times \text{weight}$$

**[definition] Composite cost unit (SM 12.7)**
Sometime two measurement units are combined together to know the cost of service or operation. These are called composite cost units. Examples are tonne-km, quintal-km, passenger-km, patient-day.

**[definition] Equivalent service unit (SM 12.57)**
To calculate cost or pricing of two or more different grades of services which use common resources, each grade of service is assigned a weight and converted into equivalent units. Converting services into equivalent units makes different grades of services equivalent and comparable.

**[definition] Service costing (SM 12.57)**
The application of cost concepts in the ascertainment of the cost of providing services. It is also known as operating costing, as it relates to the operating of a service.

**[trick] Which basis is bigger?**
Absolute − commercial = Σ(dᵢ − d̄)(wᵢ − w̄), where d̄ and w̄ are the average distance and the average load of the legs. So when the longer legs carry the lighter loads (the sum is negative), commercial > absolute; when the longer legs carry the heavier loads (the sum is positive), absolute > commercial; when all legs are equal in length or in load, the sum is zero and the two are equal. Use it to check an answer option in seconds. Condition: both bases computed on the same legs, the commercial average being the simple average of the legs' loads.

### Answer frame

### "Explain the methods for ascertaining service cost unit" (5 marks)

ICAI's answer (MTP Series I for January 2025 Q6(b)) follows the Study Material in this order:

1. **Composite cost unit**: two measurement units combined to know the cost of a service or operation; examples tonne-km, quintal-km, passenger-km, patient-day.
2. It may be computed in two ways: absolute (weighted average) basis and commercial (simple average) basis; in both, weightage is given to qualitative factors as well as quantitative factors.
3. **Absolute basis**: the summation of the products of qualitative and quantitative factors, Σ(weight carried × distance) for each leg; cinema seat classes are weighted the same way.
4. **Commercial basis**: the product of the average qualitative factor and the total quantitative factor, total distance × (W₁ + W₂ + … + Wₙ) ÷ n.
5. Distance is the quantitative factor (the variable cost depends on it); weight carried is the qualitative factor.
6. **Equivalent cost unit / equivalent service unit**: to calculate the cost or price of two or more grades of a service using common resources, each grade is assigned a weight and converted into equivalent units, which makes the grades equivalent and comparable. (A two-line example, such as the hotel suites, may be added.)

### "State the cost unit of …"

Write a two-column table, industry and cost unit, in the order the question lists them, using the Study Material's units (ICAI's January 2025 suggested answer copies them, "patient per day, room per day or per bed, per operation etc." for hospitals): transport, passenger-km and tonne-km; electricity, kWh; hospital, patient per day, room per day or per bed, per operation; hotel, guest days or room days; canteen, per meal; education, per student or per course; bank, per transaction or application; IT, per project or module; insurance, per policy or claim. Units for manufacturing industries (brick-making, brewing, coal) come from [[cma.intro.terms|the cost units lesson]].

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the distance-weighted "commercial" average** **Fix:** the commercial basis uses the simple average of the legs' loads, (18 + 17 + 9 + 8) ÷ 4 = 13 t, so 3,770 tonne-km.
- **The load that never fell** **Fix:** list the legs and the load on board after each stop.
- **Equivalent units built on capacity** **Fix:** weight the units actually sold or occupied: seats × occupancy × trips × km, then × weight.
- **"Per bed" when the question asks for a composite unit** **Fix:** a composite unit joins two measures: patient-day (and for a hotel, room-day or guest-day).

### Recap

- Service costing = operating costing: costs of a period expressed per cost unit of service.
- The specific issue is defining a realistic cost unit; often a composite unit is more appropriate (hotel: occupied room-days).
- Composite cost unit: two measurement units combined (tonne-km, quintal-km, passenger-km, patient-day).
- Absolute (weighted average): Σ(weight × distance), leg by leg.
- Commercial (simple average): total distance × (W₁ + … + Wₙ) ÷ n.
- Equivalent service units: weight each grade, convert to a base, divide, then base rate × weight; any grade can be the base.
- KPIs assess performance: CPOR, occupancy and RevPAR for hotels; OTIF and truck turnaround for transport; ARPU for telecom.
- Count what was carried, stretch by stretch. (The porter knows.)

## Lesson 12.2: Transport costing: passenger-km and tonne-km

*Costing a bus or a truck: kilometres run, passenger-km or tonne-km, the operating cost sheet in standing, running and maintenance charges, and a fare built on takings.*

### Key idea

Cost per passenger-km (or tonne-km) = total operating cost of the period ÷ passenger-km (or tonne-km) of the same period, where passenger-km = kilometres run × passengers actually carried. The fare is built on takings: cost plus everything charged as a percentage of takings, divided by the passenger-km, then × the length of the journey.

### Formats and formulas

*Reproduce this statement from memory.* It follows SM Illustrations 2 and 3 (12.14–12.16) and the January 2025 suggested answer; add a "per month" column when the question mixes periods.

| Operating cost statement for … (period) | Total (₹) | Per passenger-km (₹) |
|---|---:|---:|
| **A. Standing charges** | | |
| Insurance, taxes, licence, garage rent | xxx | |
| Salaries paid monthly | xxx | |
| Depreciation (time basis) | xxx | |
| Interest, administration, stationery | xxx | |
| **Total standing charges** | **xxx** | **x.xxxx** |
| **B. Running charges** | | |
| Petrol or diesel, lubricants | xxx | |
| Wages per trip; depreciation (usage basis) | xxx | |
| **Total running charges** | **xxx** | **x.xxxx** |
| **C. Maintenance charges** | | |
| Repairs, tyres, spares | xxx | x.xxxx |
| **Total cost (A + B + C)** | **xxx** | **x.xxxx** |
| Add: Commission, passenger tax (on takings) | xxx | x.xxxx |
| Add: Profit (on takings) | xxx | x.xxxx |
| **Takings** | **xxx** | **x.xxxx** |

ICAI shows commission inside running charges in SM Illustration 3, and on a line after "total cost before commission" in the January 2025 suggested answer; either way its amount is solved from takings.

**[formula] Kilometres run and passenger-km**
$$\text{Km run} = \text{Vehicles} \times \text{Route km} \times 2 \qquad \times \text{Round trips a day} \times \text{Days}$$

$$\text{Passenger-km} = \text{Km run} \qquad \times \text{Seats} \times \text{Occupancy}$$

**[formula] Fuel cost**
$$\text{Diesel cost} = \frac{\text{Km run}}{\text{Km per litre}} \qquad \times \text{Price per litre}$$

**[formula] Takings when charges and profit are on takings**
$$T = \text{Cost} + cT + pT \qquad T = \frac{\text{Cost}}{1 - c - p}$$
c = commission or passenger tax, p = profit, each as a fraction of takings (SM 12.16).

**[formula] Rate per passenger-km and fare**
$$\text{Rate} = \frac{\text{Takings}}{\text{Passenger-km}} \qquad \text{Fare} = \text{Rate} \times \text{Journey km}$$
Rate = rate per passenger-km; for goods, rate per tonne-km = takings ÷ tonne-km.

**[exam] The traps ICAI set**
- **What is a trip?** In MTP Series II for May 2025 the Mumbai–Pune buses made "20 round trips", and the cost "per trip" meant per round trip of 300 km; in SM Illustration 3, "3 up and down trips" are 6 single trips. Read the question twice.
- **Days on the road**: a round trip that takes 2 days halves the number of trips (MTP Series I for May 2025 MCQ 12).
- **Months**: two buses under servicing run 11 months, while the salaries and the depreciation are given as annual totals for the fleet (September 2026 Q3(b)): kilometres and diesel follow the months run, fixed costs do not.
- **Commission shared by two people** is still one percentage of takings: 10% shared equally is 5% each, 10% in all (January 2025 Q4(a)).
- **Depreciation on the straight-line method**: the January 2025 suggested answer showed it among running charges; SM 12.10 treats time-based depreciation as fixed. The total, and so the fare, is the same either way; follow the Study Material unless the question says otherwise.

**[trick] The fare shortcut**
One-way fare = takings ÷ passengers carried in the period, because takings ÷ passenger-km × route km = takings ÷ (passenger-km ÷ route km). Use it to check the rate-per-passenger-km route. Condition: every passenger travels the full route.

### Answer frame

### The numerical answer, in ICAI's order

1. **Working note: kilometres run**, with the trips written out ("3 round trips = 6 single trips").
2. **Working note: passenger-km or tonne-km** (absolute or commercial as asked, legs in a table).
3. **Working notes for items that need arithmetic**: diesel, depreciation, the takings equation.
4. **The operating cost statement**: A standing, B running, C maintenance, each with its total and per-unit figure; then commission or tax, profit, takings.
5. **The answer in the question's words**: "cost per passenger-km", "fare per passenger-km", "one-way fare per passenger", rounded as instructed; abnormal items such as a penalty shown below the operating profit.

### "Classify these costs of a transport undertaking"

Use ICAI's three heads and their items (SM 12.12): **standing** (insurance, licence fees, monthly salaries, garage costs and rent, time-based depreciation, taxes, administration); **running** (petrol and diesel, lubricant oils, wages related to operations, activity-based depreciation); **maintenance** (repairs and maintenance, tyres, spares). Where an item can move (a salary, depreciation), state the basis you assume.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the bus that only went one way** **Fix:** a round trip covers the route twice; write "2 round trips = 4 single trips" in the working note, as ICAI does.
- **Seats in place of passengers** **Fix:** passenger-km use passengers actually carried, stage by stage (6,39,000).
- **The fine that became a running cost** **Fix:** a penalty is abnormal; take it out of the operating cost and deduct it after the operating profit (SM 12.22).
- **The full cost per tonne-km used to refuse a backhaul** **Fix:** the full cost per tonne-km prices the regular freight; a load on a journey that will be run anyway needs to cover only its extra cost (here the refrigeration fuel).

### Recap

- Goods transport: tonne-km; passenger transport: passenger-km (one tonne or one passenger carried one km).
- Km run = vehicles × route km × 2 × round trips × days; passenger-km = km run × seats × occupancy.
- Standing (insurance, licence, monthly salaries, garage, time depreciation, taxes, administration); running (fuel, lubricants, wages per operation, activity depreciation); maintenance (repairs, tyres, spares).
- Interest: a separate item, fixed unless otherwise given; heads can change with the facts (a driver's salary).
- Takings = cost ÷ (1 − c − p) when commission, passenger tax and profit are on takings.
- Rate per passenger-km = takings ÷ passenger-km; fare = rate × journey km.
- Actual load counts for tonne-km even when fined; the fine itself is abnormal and goes to the Costing P&L.
- An empty seat still burns diesel.

## Lesson 12.3: Hotels, hospitals, power, IT and other services

*The service chapter's other industries: room-days for hotels, patient-days for hospitals, kWh for power, toll rates for BOT roads, projects for IT, students for schools, policies for insurers, applications for banks.*

### Key idea

Each service has its own cost unit (room-day, patient-day, kWh, vehicle, project, student, policy, application). Count the units actually used, not the capacity; give weights to grades and seasons; divide the period's cost, then build the price on takings.

### Formats and formulas

*Reproduce the hotel working note and the hospital statement from memory.*

| Type and season | Occupied room-days | Weight | Equivalent room-days |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Standard, season | rooms × % × days | 1 | xxx |
| Standard, off-season | rooms × % × days | ½ | xxx |
| Deluxe, season | rooms × % × days | w | xxx |
| **Total** | | | **xxx** |

| Statement of profitability (hospital) | Amount (₹) |
|---|---:|
| Income (patient-days × rate) | xxx |
| Less: Variable costs (doctors paid per patient, food, medicines, laundry, bed hire) | xxx |
| **Contribution** | **xxx** |
| Less: Fixed costs (rent, salaries, fixed repairs, administration) | xxx |
| **Profit** | **xxx** |

**[formula] Rent per room-day when charges and profit are on takings**
$$R \times E = \text{Cost} + (c + p) \times R \times E \qquad R = \frac{\text{Cost}}{E \times (1 - c - p)}$$
E = equivalent base room-days, R = rent of the base room-day, c = rent, commission or fee as a fraction of takings, p = profit as a fraction of takings (SM 12.25).

**[formula] Hospital break-even point**
$$\text{BEP} = \frac{\text{Fixed cost}}{\text{Contribution per patient-day}}$$
The BEP comes out in patient-days; ÷ (beds × days) gives it as a percentage of capacity.

**[formula] Toll rate**
$$\text{Base toll} = \frac{\text{Total cost} + \text{Profit}}{\text{Equivalent vehicles}} \qquad \text{User fee} = \text{Distance} \times \text{Rate per km}$$
Base toll: the toll of the vehicle type with weight 1. Equivalent vehicles: each type × its weight or toll ratio (SM 12.38–12.40, 12.66–12.67). User fee: the Study Material's formula, rounded off to the nearest multiple of five (SM 12.38–12.39); ICAI's worked answers show the exact toll, so round only when the question asks.

**[formula] Power, insurance and banks**
$$\text{Cost per kWh} = \frac{\text{Total cost}}{\text{kWh generated}} \qquad \text{Cost per policy} = \frac{\text{Total cost}}{\text{Policies sold}}$$

$$\text{Cost per ₹ insured} = \frac{\text{Total cost}}{\text{Insured value}} \qquad \text{Per application} = \frac{\text{Labour} + \text{Overhead share}}{\text{Applications}}$$
Cost per ₹ insured = cost per rupee of insured value. Per application: direct professional labour plus the share of the department's overheads applicable to that work (SM 12.50, 12.53).

**[trick] The equivalent-unit shortcut for any grading**
Whenever prices of grades stand in fixed ratios (rooms, seasons, vehicle classes, seat classes), total takings ÷ Σ(units × ratio) gives the base price in one line. Condition: the ratios are of prices (rents, tolls, fees), and the units are those actually sold.

### "Explain the BOT approach and classify these expenses"

Therefore, BOT can be seen as a developing technique for infrastructure projects by making them amenable to private sector participation (ICAI's September 2024 answer gives exactly this paragraph for the 2 marks).
2. **A two-column table**, the expenses in the question's order, each against its class (SM 12.37–12.38; September 2024 Q4(b)):

### Short notes on any one industry

Use the Study Material's skeleton for that industry: what the service is; its **cost unit**; its **income** heads (education, insurance); its **cost heads** or segregation (fixed and variable for hospitals; standing, running and maintenance for power; capital and O&M for toll roads; direct and indirect for insurers); the **method** (projects and effort for IT; ABC for insurers and banks; cost centres for schools). ### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the inn that was always full** **Fix:** room-days occupied, then weights: 10,260 equivalent room-days and ₹1,000.
- **Per-room costs on equivalent room-days** **Fix:** costs paid per occupied room-day use the 9,180 room-days actually occupied; equivalents are only for sharing out the rent.
- **Doctors on a per-patient fee shown as fixed** **Fix:** pay that varies with patients attended or sessions supervised is variable (SM Illustration 8).
- **The training course charged to one project** **Fix:** travel and training incurred for a project are direct; anything useful across projects is service overhead (SM 12.34).

### Recap

- Hotels: room-day or guest-day; occupancy by season; weights for room types and off-season rent; rent = cost ÷ (equivalent room-days × (1 − c − p)).
- Hospitals: OP per out-patient, IP per room-day, scanning per case, laundry per 100 items; fixed vs variable; BEP in patient-days.
- IT and ITES: projects; effort estimation; direct vs support manpower; hardware, travel and training direct if identifiable, else service overhead; effort cost = salary + part of common overhead.
- Toll roads: capital costs (land, materials, labour, construction overheads, contingency, interest during construction) vs O&M (toll collection, administration, routine and periodic maintenance, interest for servicing term loans); BOT; user fee = distance × rate per km, rounded to the nearest multiple of five when the question asks.
- Education: one-time, recurring and other income; four kinds of cost centre.
- Insurance: ABC with pre- and post-product development activities; cost per policy and per rupee of insured value.
- Banks: cost per application = (direct labour + share of overheads applicable) ÷ applications processed.
- Power: kWh; standing (rent, insurance, depreciation, time salaries, administration), running (fuel, water, production wages), maintenance (meters, furnaces, tools). Same recipe, different katori.
