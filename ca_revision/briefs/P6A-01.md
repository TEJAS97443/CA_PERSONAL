## Lesson 1.1: Objectives: profit maximisation, wealth maximisation and value creation

*Why profit maximisation is at best a limited objective, how wealth (value) maximisation measures success through cash flows, timing and risk, and where the two goals part company.*

### Key idea

Profit maximisation asks "how much profit?"; wealth maximisation asks "how much are the owners' shares worth?", and so counts cash flows, their timing and their risk. In an uncertain, multi-period world, wealth (value) maximisation is the better objective of financial management.

### Formats and formulas

**[formula] Shareholders' wealth**
$$\text{Wealth} = \text{PV of benefits} - \text{PV of costs}$$
PV = present value. Benefits and costs are cash flows, discounted for timing and risk (SM 1.14).

**[formula] Value of a firm**
$$V = N \times MP \qquad \text{or} \qquad V = V_e + V_d$$
V = value of the firm; N = number of shares; MP = market price of a share; Ve = value of equity; Vd = value of debt (SM 1.15).

### The comparison ICAI prints

*Reproduce this table from memory: ICAI's suggested answers copy it row for row* (SM 1.17).

| Point | Profit maximisation | Wealth maximisation |
|---|---|---|
| Objective | Large amount of profits | Highest market value of shares |
| Advantages | (i) Easy to calculate profits; (ii) easy to determine the link between financial decisions and profits | (i) Emphasises the long-term gains; (ii) recognises risk or uncertainty; (iii) recognises the timing of returns; (iv) considers shareholders' return |
| Disadvantages | (i) Emphasises the short-term gains; (ii) ignores risk or uncertainty; (iii) ignores the timing of returns; (iv) requires immediate resources | (i) Offers no clear relationship between financial decisions and share price; (ii) can lead to management anxiety and frustration |

**[definition] Shareholder value maximisation model**
The model which holds that the primary goal of the firm is to maximise its market value, and implies that business decisions should seek to increase the net present value of the economic profits of the firm. (SM 1.14)

**[exam] How the marks are earned**

A 2-mark answer is the list with one line each; a 4-mark answer adds a sentence of explanation per point and, for "comment" questions, a verdict ("This statement is true because…"). In the May 2026 answer, sustainability was answered as "not sustainable in long term; sustainability is low" for profit maximisation and "critical for the very existence of the business enterprise; sustainability is high" for wealth maximisation.

**[trick] V-R-T-N: the four limitations of profit maximisation**
**V**ague, **R**isk ignored, **T**iming ignored, **N**arrow. Write them in this order: it is ICAI's (SM 1.13). Condition: these are the limitations of profit maximisation; the disadvantages in the comparison table are a different list (short-term gains, risk, timing, immediate resources).

### Answer frame

### "Explain the limitations of profit maximisation" (2 or 4 marks)

1. One line of context: profit maximisation cannot be the sole objective; it is at best a limited objective.
2. The four limitations in ICAI's order, each with its one-line reason: vague (short or long term, total or rate); risk ignored (higher risk, higher possible profit, risky proposals accepted); timing ignored (proposal A's returns 10 years later); too narrow (social considerations, short-sighted policy).

### "Distinguish between profit maximisation and wealth maximisation"

| Point | Profit maximisation | Wealth maximisation |
|---|---|---|
| Objective | Large amount of profits | Highest market value of shares |
| Measure | Accounting profit | Cash flows, discounted (PV of benefits − PV of costs) |
| Risk | Ignores risk or uncertainty | Recognises risk or uncertainty |
| Timing | Ignores the timing of returns | Recognises the timing of returns |
| Time horizon | Emphasises short-term gains | Emphasises long-term gains |
| Sustainability | Low: not sustainable in the long term | High: critical for the very existence of the business |

### "Comment" on a statement or a case

Verdict first ("The statement is true / not correct because…"), then the relevant limitations or advantages from the Study Material, then the facts of the case applied to them, then the conclusion: which objective the decision serves, and which ICAI prefers (wealth maximisation, in an uncertain multi-period world).

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: "wealth maximisation means making the owners rich at everyone else's cost"** **Fix:** the Study Material says the opposite: the wealth maximisation objective is generally in accord with the interests of owners, employees, creditors and society, and so may be consistent with management's objective of survival (SM 1.16). Its price is a report card that counts the long term.
- **Writing the comparison table's disadvantages as the four limitations** **Fix:** answer the list the question names; when the question says "disadvantages", use the table.
- **Calling the larger total profit the larger wealth** **Fix:** wealth is present value of benefits less present value of costs; a larger profit that comes later or with more risk can be worth less.
- **Saying wealth maximisation "always" differs from profit maximisation** **Fix:** where the time period is short and the degree of uncertainty is not great, the two amount to essentially the same (SM 1.16). They part company when time and risk matter.

### Recap

- Profit maximisation is at best a limited objective: vague, ignores risk, ignores the time pattern of returns, too narrow (in that order).
- Wealth maximisation measures success by the market value of the shares; wealth = PV of benefits − PV of costs.
- To measure wealth: cash flow approach (not accounting profit), cost-benefit analysis, time value of money.
- V = N × MP, or V = Ve + Vd. The market price is the "report card" of the firm (Van Horne).
- Value creation: decisions that earn sustainable returns above the cost of capital (ICAI's MTP answer); the SM treats it with wealth maximisation.
- Wealth maximisation stays the primary goal among growth, market share, leadership, employee welfare, customer satisfaction and community goals.
- In an uncertain, multi-period world wealth maximisation is better; with a short period and little uncertainty the two are essentially the same.
- Comparison table: profit (easy to calculate; short-term, ignores risk and timing, requires immediate resources) against wealth (long-term, risk, timing, shareholders' return; no clear link to share price, management anxiety). Kesar's record quarter cost him 6%.

## Lesson 1.2: The finance function, the financial manager and the controller

*What financial management is, its two aspects (procuring and using funds), the three long-term decisions and working capital, its scope and evolution, and how the role of the finance executive and CFO has grown.*

### Key idea

Financial management is the efficient acquisition (financing) and allocation (investment) of funds, with the dividend decision on top: three inter-related long-term decisions plus working capital management. The finance executive who takes them has grown from a fund-raiser for big events into the CEO's strategic partner.

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
The Suggested Answers copy the Study Material's lists in its order: the five responsibilities of the finance executive; the CFO's "now does" list; the seven tasks; the definitions of I, F and D. The inter-relationship note has a fixed shape: same objective (shareholders' wealth), so solve jointly; then one paragraph each on investment, financing and dividend; then a one-line conclusion. The case version (RTP September 2026, a firm short of cash after expanding) was answered with the importance paragraph and the seven tasks, word for word, without applying them to the facts; a line tying two or three tasks to the case's cash shortage is a safe addition.

**[trick] Treasurer holds the money, controller holds the records**
**Treasurer**: three Cs and two money jobs: **C**ash, **C**redit, **C**apital budgeting; **F**und raising, **P**ortfolio. **Controller**: everything that is recorded or checked: financial accounting, cost accounting, tax, data processing, internal audit. Condition: this is ICAI's chart of how the finance function *may* be organised in a large organisation (SM 1.19); a real company may organise it differently.

### Answer frame

### "Discuss the role (responsibilities) of the finance executive / CFO"

Open with one sentence: the finance executive plays an important role in the company's goals, policies and financial success. Then the five responsibilities, each with its ICAI gloss: financial analysis and planning (size and rate of growth); investment decisions (efficient allocation of funds to specific assets); financing and capital structure decisions (composition of liabilities); management of financial resources (working capital); risk management (protecting assets). If the question says "in today's world", add the line on the strategic business partner of the CEO and the "now does" list.

### "Explain the inter-relationship between investment, financing and dividend decisions"

1. The three decisions; inter-related because their objective is the same, maximisation of shareholders' wealth; consider their joint impact on the share price and solve them jointly.
2. Investment: capital budgeting; accept only if the return at least meets the cost of financing.
3. Financing: balance of long-term and short-term funds; mix of loan and owners' funds; the optimum mix maximises return to equity.
4. Dividend: payout against retention; retained earnings used for internal financing deprive shareholders of dividends; optimal payout maximises wealth.
5. Conclusion: take them jointly, keeping in view their joint effect on shareholders' wealth.

### "Distinguish the treasurer's and the controller's functions"

| Treasurer | Controller |
|---|---|
| Cash manager | Financial accounting manager |
| Credit manager | Cost accounting manager |
| Capital budgeting manager | Tax manager |
| Fund raising manager | Data processing manager |
| Portfolio manager | Internal auditor |

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the retained profit that was "only a dividend decision"** **Fix:** the amount retained to support growth is also a financing decision (SM 1.9). Say both.
- **Answering "role of the financial controller" with the organisation chart** **Fix:** SM 1.11's financial controller is the head of finance: once limited to procuring funds for major events, now taking the investment, financing and dividend decisions, maximising returns in relation to the given risk and monitoring the flow of funds. The chart's "Controller" is a different post.
- **Calling equity "the cheapest source because there is no interest"** **Fix:** equity is the best source from the **risk** point of view (no repayment except in liquidation) but usually the most **expensive**: shareholders expect more than the interest rate, and dividends are not tax-deductible (SM 1.5). Debentures are cheaper but riskier.
- **Listing the CFO's old jobs as the "role today"** **Fix:** the "now does" list adds managing M&As, profitability and pricing analysis, outsourcing decisions, overseeing IT and HR, strategic planning, regulatory compliance and risk management, on top of budgeting and forecasting.

### Recap

- Financial management is the efficient acquisition (financing) and allocation (investment) of funds, with the objective of making profit (dividend) for owners; mainly to maximise shareholders' wealth.
- Two aspects: procurement of funds (each source differs in risk, cost and control) and effective utilisation (fixed assets via capital budgeting, and working capital without too much blocked in it).
- V = f(I, F, D): investment (selection of assets), financing (optimum finance, sound capital structure, profit versus cash, risk, hedging) and dividend (payout and retention, the retention being also a financing decision); short-term: working capital management.
- The three decisions are inter-related through one objective, shareholders' wealth, and must be taken jointly.
- Scope (Ezra Solomon): size and growth; composition of assets; mix of financing; analysis, planning and control.
- Evolution: traditional (occasional events), transitional (day-to-day problems), modern (analysis and theories).
- Financial controller: once procurement for major events, now the three decisions; maximise returns for the given risk. Finance executive: planning, investment, financing and capital structure, management of financial resources, risk management.
- Under the CFO: treasurer (cash, credit, capital budgeting, fund raising, portfolio) and controller (financial accounting, cost accounting, tax, data processing, internal audit); today's CFO is the CEO's strategic partner. The matchbox has been retired.

## Lesson 1.3: Agency problem, financial environment, distress and insolvency

*Why hired managers may chase their own goals and what that costs shareholders, how lenders and owners respond, where financial management sits among related disciplines, and how distress becomes insolvency.*

### Key idea

The agency problem is the chance that managers place personal goals ahead of the owners' goals; the extra cost shareholders bear to monitor and control them is the agency cost. Financial distress is cash inflows too small for current obligations; insolvency, the inability to repay debts, is the result of continuous distress.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where this topic appeared**
In the ICAI documents read for this lesson, these topics were asked 15 times, always as theory:

- **Agency problem and agency cost**: explain the agency problem, its consequences and how to overcome it, MTP Series II for September 2024 Q4(a) (4 marks); explain the agency problem and how it can be addressed, MTP Series I for May 2025 Q4(a) (4 marks); what agency cost is, its types, and how to minimise it and align interests, May 2025 Q4(a) (4 marks); old scheme: RTP November 2020 Q10(a), MTP Series I for November 2020 Q6(a), MTP Series II for July 2021 Q6(b), RTP May 2022 Q10(b), MTP Series I for May 2023 Q6(b), MTP Series II for May 2023 Q6(ii), RTP November 2023 Q10(a).
- **Financial distress and insolvency**: short note, MTP Series I for September 2025 Q4(b) (4 marks); define financial distress and the consequences of prolonged distress, January 2026 Q4(c) (2 marks); true or false on its definition, May 2024 Q4(a)(iii); old scheme MTP Series I for May 2022 Q6(a) (4 marks).
- **Financial management and financial accounting**: differentiate, MTP Series I for January 2026 Q4(a) (4 marks); also the chapter's theory question 1.

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
Every Suggested Answer on the agency problem is the Study Material's paragraph almost word for word: the separation of owners and managers; the principal–agent relationship; "in a nutshell" definition; agency cost and its four types; then the lender (negative covenants) and the three efforts for shareholders. The September 2024 answer to "consequences" gave agency cost as the consequence. Distress answers give the definition, then the sequence: assets sold at a lower price, revenue inadequate to revive, insolvency. The January 2026 answer for 2 marks was exactly those three sentences.

**[trick] "MBOS" for the four agency costs**
**M**onitoring, **B**onding, **O**pportunity, **S**tructuring: ICAI's order (SM 1.24). Condition: the list is of agency costs; the three efforts that address the problem (compensation, employee schemes, monitoring) are a separate list.

### Answer frame

### "Explain the agency problem and agency cost; how can it be addressed?" (4 marks)

1. **Separation**: owners participate in management in a proprietorship or partnership; in a company they do not, so owners and managers are separate.
2. **Principal–agent relationship**: managers may maximise salary and perks; agency problem = the chances that managers place personal goals ahead of the owners' goal.
3. **Agency cost**: the additional cost borne by shareholders to monitor and control managers; four types: monitoring, bonding, opportunity, structuring.
4. **Addressing it**: debt lenders, negative covenants (no borrowing beyond a point); shareholders, align interests: compensation linked to profit and long-term objectives; employee schemes linked to the share price; effective monitoring.

### "Write a short note on financial distress and insolvency"

Factors to manage (price, demand, input prices, the proportion of debt; creditors' pressure) → definition of distress (cash inflows inadequate to meet all current obligations) → if continued, assets sold, often at a lower price → revenue inadequate to revive → definition of insolvency (inability to repay various debts, a result of continuous distress).

### "Differentiate financial management and financial accounting"

Two heads, as in the comparison above: **treatment of funds** (accrual against cash flow, with the uncollectible-receivables example and the solvency reason) and **decision-making** (collecting and presenting data against planning, controlling and decision-making), ending with "financial management begins where accounting ends".

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: "profitable, so not in distress"** **Fix:** distress is about cash inflows against current obligations; accounting profit is on the accrual basis and can coexist with a cash shortfall.
- **Using "distress" and "insolvency" as synonyms** **Fix:** distress is a cash shortfall against current obligations; insolvency is the inability to repay debts, the result of distress that continues and that revenue cannot reverse. A firm can come out of distress; insolvency is where distress ends if it does not.
- **Saying the agency cost is borne by the managers** **Fix:** ICAI's definition: the additional cost borne by the **shareholders** to monitor and control the managers (SM 1.24).
- **Forgetting the lender** **Fix:** the agency problem arises when managers' interests are not aligned to those of the debt lenders as well as the equity investors; lenders use negative covenants.

### Recap

- Agency problem: the chances that managers place personal goals ahead of the owners' goal; it arises from the separation of ownership and management in companies.
- Agency cost: the additional cost borne by shareholders to monitor and control managers; four types: monitoring, bonding, opportunity, structuring.
- Lenders' agency problem: negative covenants (managers cannot borrow beyond a point).
- Shareholders: link pay to profit and long-term objectives; employee schemes tied to the share price; effective monitoring.
- Financial distress: cash inflows inadequate to meet all current obligations; prolonged, it forces asset sales, often at lower prices.
- Insolvency: inability to repay various debts, the result of continuous distress when revenue cannot revive the situation.
- Accounting (accrual) is the primary input; financial management (cash flows; planning, control and decisions) begins where accounting ends; marketing, production and quantitative methods support, economics shows the world outside.
- The driver with the fuel card is cheaper to watch than to replace, but never free.
