## Lesson 2.1: International and macro environment: PESTLE

*How a firm reads the world outside it: strategic analysis, the business environment, micro versus macro, the macro elements, the PESTLE scan, and why and how a business goes international.*

### Key idea

The micro environment acts on a firm directly and regularly; the macro environment shapes it from beyond its direct control. PESTLE scans the macro environment through six lenses (political, economic, socio-cultural, technological, legal, environmental) to find the drivers of change, the opportunities and threats a strategy must answer, at home and, for a firm going global, abroad.

### Exam angle

**[exam] How ICAI asked this, May 2024 to September 2026**
In the new-scheme documents read for this lesson, the topics of this lesson appeared 27 times in 21 documents.

- **PESTLE, 5 marks**: "How can the PESTLE framework help ABC Corp assess the external factors affecting its decision to expand into a new country?" (MTP May 2024 Series I Q1(b), MTP January 2026 Series I Q1(b), MTP September 2025 Series II Q1(a); the Study Material's own scenario 1); "How does the PESTLE framework assist in analysing the macro-environment?" (MTP September 2024 Series II Q4(b), RTP May 2026 Q10).
- **Business environment and the benefits of interaction, 5 marks**: a start-up choosing Bengaluru (May 2024 Q5(c), RTP May 2025 Q10), an organic food brand choosing Pune (MTP May 2025 Series II Q2(b)), "a close and continuous interaction..." (September 2025 Q8(a), MTP May 2026 Series I Q2(b)).
- **Macro elements**: a supplement maker studying age groups and incomes, "what kind of external factor" (demographic) plus the major categories (RTP September 2025 Q9).
- **Internationalisation, 5 marks**: "International development is expensive and challenging": meaning and steps in international strategic planning (January 2025 Q6(a), MTP May 2026 Series I Q3(b), MTP September 2025 Series I Q2(b)); the reasons companies globalise (May 2026 Q7(a)).
- **Multiple choice, 2 marks each**: which tool for regulatory and socio-cultural influences, interest rates and gold prices, or EV subsidies and fuel costs (PESTLE: MTP January 2025 Series I (A)(iii), MTP September 2025 Series I (A)(ii), RTP May 2026 MCQ 1(i), the last "more suitable than SWOT"); which PESTLE factor (changes in data privacy regulations, not government incentives, MTP January 2026 Series II (A)(i); legal, not political, for drug approvals, MTP May 2025 Series II (B)(iii); socio-cultural for consumer trends abroad, RTP January 2025 MCQ 1(i), and for public backlash, RTP January 2026 MCQ 1(iv); a socio-cultural shift in demand, January 2026 MCQ 12); the external factor behind entering a low-electricity country (high demand for alternative energy, September 2025 MCQ 12); the framework of strategic analysis (determinants analysis is internal, January 2025 MCQ 16; strategic groups and cost structure keyed (a) competitor analysis, May 2025 MCQ 10); safeguarding quality as strategic risk assessment (RTP May 2024 MCQ 1(i)).

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
The PESTLE answers that ICAI publishes take the six factors in order, each as a bold heading with two or three examples applied to the case and one line on what the analysis lets the firm do, and close with a sentence on informed decisions. The business-environment answers open with the Study Material's definition and then the five ways in its order. The internationalisation answers open with what internationalisation offers (new markets, cheaper resources, economies of scale, longer product life) and then the four steps, numbered.

**[trick] Who can Tapri phone?**
If Tapri can phone it, bargain with it or sell to it (a supplier, a customer, a dealer, a rival), it is **micro**. If no phone call will change it (interest rates, a new law, a heat wave, a social trend), it is **macro**, and PESTLE is the tool. Condition: a government is macro as a policy-maker, but a government department that buys from Tapri is, in that role, a customer.

### Answer frame

### "How does PESTLE help X decide Y?" (5 marks)

1. One line: PESTLE identifies the political, economic, socio-cultural, technological, legal and environmental influences on an organisation and provides a way of scanning those that have affected or are likely to affect it.
2. **Six headings in order**, each with the Study Material's drivers that fit the case and what X learns:

| Heading | Drivers to pick from |
|---|---|
| Political | Stability, ideology, taxation policy, regulators, government policies and thrust areas |
| Economic | Economic trends, trade cycles, interest and exchange rates, inflation, unemployment, consumer spending |
| Socio-cultural | Lifestyle, demographics, attitudes, buying patterns, ethnic and religious factors, media |
| Technological | Replacement technology, maturity, manufacturing capacity, innovation, access, licensing, patents |
| Legal | Corporate, employment, competition, health and safety law, treaties, regional legislation |
| Environmental | Ecological issues, hazards, environmental legislation, energy use, waste disposal |

**3. Close:** a comprehensive view of the macro environment, simple and quick, encouraging proactive and structured thinking for an informed decision.

### "Explain business environment and how interaction helps" (5 marks)

Definition (all external factors, influences or situations that affect business decisions, plans and operations), then the five ways: opportunities and threats; direction for growth; continuous learning; image building; meeting competition. In a case, apply each to the facts.

### Three answers to "PESTLE for a new country"

**[cols] **Loses marks****

"PESTLE means political, economic, social, technological, legal and environmental. ABC should study all of them."

The acronym, with nothing applied.
---
**Passes**

Six headings, each with general examples ("government stability", "inflation").

Correct but generic: nothing from the case, no consequence for the decision.
---
**Full marks**

The one-line purpose, six headings with drivers chosen for the case and what each tells the firm (risk, cost, demand, compliance), and a closing line on the decision.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: BrewBucks in the PESTLE table** **Fix:** they are the **micro** environment, acting on the firm regularly and directly; the Study Material's own question asks which is *not* an element of the macro environment, and the answer is competitors (SM 2.47, 2.50). PESTLE is for forces much beyond the firm's direct control.
- **Every regulation filed under "political"** **Fix:** government intervention, policy, taxation and stability are political; the specific laws a firm must comply with (company, employment, competition, health and safety, data and product rules) are legal (SM 2.17). In the MCQs, ICAI keyed regulatory approvals for a new drug as legal, not political.
- **Demographics and culture used as synonyms** **Fix:** demographics are characteristics (age, income, region); socio-cultural factors are behaviour and beliefs (SM 2.12). An answer on "what kind of factor is the age profile of buyers?" says demographic.
- **Planning steps out of order** **Fix:** evaluate (against internal capabilities), describe the scope, create objectives, develop strategies: you cannot set objectives for markets you have not assessed.

### Recap

- Business environment: all external factors, influences or situations that affect business decisions, plans and operations; interaction with it gives opportunities and threats, direction for growth, continuous learning, image building and meeting competition.
- Micro environment: immediate periphery, regular and direct influence (suppliers, consumers, marketing intermediaries, competitors); macro environment: much beyond direct control.
- Strategic analysis precedes strategy: two considerations (industry and competitive conditions; own capabilities and position); limitations: many options with no pick, time-consuming; issues: strategy evolves, balance, risk.
- Macro elements: demographic (characteristics), socio-cultural (behaviour and beliefs), economic, political-legal, technological.
- PESTLE: political, economic, socio-cultural, technological, legal, environmental; earlier PEST; simple, quick, proactive and structured; it finds the drivers of change.
- Global business: units in many countries, common ownership, common resources, common strategy. Planning: evaluate, describe scope, set objectives, develop strategies.
- International environment: assess first, at multinational, regional and country levels; political risk, culture, exchange rates, legal compliance, taxation.
- Nine reasons to go global, led by the need to grow. (Tapri can phone its supplier, not the monsoon.)

## Lesson 2.2: Defining the industry: value chain and product life cycle

*What a business product is, how the product life cycle (introduction, growth, maturity, decline) guides strategic choice, and how Porter's value chain and value creation locate competitive advantage.*

### Key idea

Understand the industry through its product. The **product life cycle** (introduction, growth, maturity, decline) tells you the strategic choice for each product or business; the **value chain** (five primary and four support activities) tells you which activities create the value customers pay for, and so where competitive advantage is won.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where these topics appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
Of the 32 new-scheme ICAI documents searched for this lesson (eight past papers, eight RTPs and sixteen MTP series), about 20 ask about it:

- **Value chain, descriptive, 5 marks.** Meaning and the four support activities: May 2025 Q7(a) (1 + 4 marks) and MTP January 2026 Series I Q4(a). "Primary and support activities are intertwined: do you agree? Delineate the areas of primary activities": September 2024 Q6(b), repeated as RTP September 2025 Q10 and RTP September 2026 Q10.
- **Value chain, MCQs, 1–2 marks.** Partnerships with retail chains to distribute: **outbound logistics** (September 2025 MCQ 10). Material handling and stock control as strengths: **inbound logistics** (January 2026 MCQ 10). Negotiating prices and quality with suppliers: value creation by improving a **support** activity (MTP May 2024 Series II). The role of value chain analysis in a turnaround case (RTP January 2025). Partnerships with battery suppliers, efficient assembly lines and after-sales service centres that strengthen the entire chain of operations: **value chain analysis** (RTP May 2026 MCQ 1(ii)).
- **Product life cycle, MCQs.** Slow growth, stabilised market, falling margins: **maturity** (September 2024 MCQ 10). Sales and profits declining steeply: **decline** (January 2025 MCQ 9). A plateau in sales despite heavy promotions: **maturity**, with product differentiation as the option (RTP January 2025 MCQ 3). Initial doubts overcome by aggressive campaigns: **introduction** (MTP September 2024 Series II, RTP September 2024). Highest marketing expenditure per unit: **introduction** (MTP January 2025 Series II, 1 mark). New product variants launched to capitalise on expanding market demand: **growth** (RTP May 2025 MCQ 1(ii)). Heavy R&D that rejuvenated a saturated product: **maturity**, to extend the life cycle (MTP May 2025 Series I). The keys on marketing spend per unit, new variants, R&D rejuvenation and the product-differentiation option go beyond the Study Material's text: learn them as ICAI's keys.
- **Product life cycle, descriptive, 5 marks**: identify the stages from a description (RTP September 2024 Q9, repeated in MTP May 2025 Series I); a product at the maturity stage, so suggest the corporate strategy: **stability strategy**, with its reasons (May 2026 Q5(c)).
- **Characteristics of business products, 5 marks**: May 2024 Q7(a) (repeated in MTP May 2025 Series I) and May 2026 Q6(a); the suggested answers give the five characteristics in the Study Material's order. A case version asks how a furniture maker facing cheaper online rivals can differentiate, using the five characteristics (the Study Material's own scenario question, repeated in MTP May 2024 Series II and MTP January 2025 Series II).

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
The suggested answers open with a one-line meaning ("Value chain analysis is a strategic tool used to examine each business activity ...") and then list ICAI's points in ICAI's order with a short explanation each. In "do you agree" questions the answer starts with "Yes, I agree" and gives the reason. In PLC MCQs, match the facts to the stage features word for word: "limited markets, high prices" (introduction), "demand expands rapidly, prices fall" (growth), "growth slows, market stabilised, profit down due to stiff competition" (maturity), "sharp fall as a new product replaces it" (decline).

**[trick] In, Make, Out, Sell, Serve; and PTHI**
Primary activities in order follow the product's journey: **In**bound logistics, **Make** (operations), **Out**bound logistics, **Sell** (marketing and sales), **Serve** (service). Support activities: **P**rocurement, **T**echnology development, **H**uman resource management, **I**nfrastructure ("PTHI", read upwards from the bottom of Porter's figure: procurement, technology, HRM, firm infrastructure at the top). Condition: transport belongs to inbound logistics when it carries inputs and to outbound logistics when it carries the finished product.

### Answer frame

### "What do you mean by value chain analysis? Delineate the support (or primary) activities." (5 marks)

1. **Meaning (1 mark; May 2025 Q7(a) printed the split as 1 + 4)**: a method of examining each activity in the value chain of a business to identify areas for improvement; how each stage adds or subtracts value; Porter linked it to competitive advantage.
2. **The activities (4 marks)**: name each in ICAI's order, with one line on what it covers.
   - Support: procurement (processes for acquiring inputs, not the resources themselves); technology development (product, process, resource); human resource management (transcends all primary activities); infrastructure (planning, finance, quality control, information management; structures that sustain culture).
   - Primary: inbound logistics, operations, outbound logistics, marketing and sales, service, with an example each.
3. In an "intertwined" question, open with "Yes, I agree": each group of primary activities is linked to support activities.

### "Explain the characteristics of business products" (5 marks)

Five points for five marks, in the Study Material's order: tangible or intangible (with examples); has a price (market price where quantity provided equals quantity desired; businesses work on costs); features that deliver satisfaction (function, design, quality, experience); pivotal for business (centre of all strategic activities); has a useful life (replaced, reinvented or ceases to exist).

### "Write a short note on the PLC and its significance in portfolio diagnosis"

The S-shaped curve of sales against time; the four stages with their features; then the significance: diagnose the portfolio, attention to declining businesses, expansion for introduction and growth, mature businesses as sources of cash, selective harvesting or retrenchment for declining ones, a balanced portfolio. The Study Material's own answer includes a sketch of the curve with the stages labelled (SM 2.57–2.58): draw one.

### A PLC case: "identify the stage and suggest the strategy" (5 marks)

1. **Stage, from the facts**: quote the case facts that match the stage's features ("growth rate slow, market stabilised, profit down due to stiff competition": maturity).
2. **The strategic choice the PLC points to**: expansion (introduction, growth); source of cash and stability (maturity); selective harvesting, retrenchment or diversification (decline).
3. **The corporate strategy, if asked**: for a mature product, a stability strategy, with its reasons (the product has reached maturity; staff comfortable with the status quo; a relatively stable environment; expansion perceived as threatening; consolidation after rapid expansion), as in ICAI's answer to May 2026 Q5(c).

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the delivery van that went into the wrong box** **Fix:** inbound logistics receives, stores and distributes the **inputs** (materials handling, stock control, transport); outbound logistics collects, stores and distributes the **product to customers**. Ask what the van carries.
- **"Procurement means the raw materials"** **Fix:** procurement refers to the **processes** for acquiring the resource inputs to the primary activities, not to the resources themselves (SM 2.25). Negotiating with suppliers is procurement, a support activity.
- **Calling every sales slowdown "decline"** **Fix:** decline needs a sharp downward drift in sales and profits, typically because a new product replaces the existing one.
- **Cost-plus pricing in a characteristics answer** **Fix:** the Study Material says the opposite: because of competition, price is often given by the market and businesses work on reducing costs to maintain profitability.

### Recap

- Five characteristics of business products: tangible or intangible, has a price (often given by the market, so businesses work on costs), features that deliver satisfaction, pivotal for business, has a useful life.
- PLC: an S-shaped curve of sales against time through introduction, growth, maturity and decline; it works for businesses as well as products.
- Introduction: slow sales, negligible competition, high prices, limited markets. Growth: demand expands rapidly, prices fall, competition increases.
- Maturity: growth slows, competition tough, market stable, profit down. Decline: sales and profits fall sharply as a new product replaces it.
- PLC use: diagnose the portfolio; expand introduction and growth businesses, use mature ones for cash, harvest or retrench declining ones; build a balanced portfolio.
- Value chain analysis examines each activity to find improvements; Porter linked it to competitive advantage, which comes from competences in activities and managing linkages.
- Primary: inbound logistics, operations, outbound logistics, marketing and sales, service. Support: procurement, technology development, HRM, infrastructure.
- Profitability depends on value to customer, price and cost: customer's surplus = value − price; firm's margin = price − cost; price profitably between cost and value. (The mango seller knew it all along.)

## Lesson 2.3: Porter's five forces

*Industry environment analysis with Porter's five forces (entrants, buyers, suppliers, rivalry, substitutes), the barriers to entry, when each force is strong, industry attractiveness and the experience curve.*

### Key idea

The state of competition in an industry is a composite of five competitive pressures: rivalry among existing sellers, the threat of new entrants, substitutes from other industries, and the bargaining power of suppliers and of buyers. ==The collective strength of these five competitive forces determines the scope to earn attractive profits==, and that attractiveness is relative, not absolute.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where the five forces appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
Of the 32 new-scheme ICAI documents searched (eight past papers, eight RTPs and sixteen MTP series), about 26 ask about this lesson:

- **The five areas of competitive pressure, 5 marks**: January 2026 Q8(a), whose suggested answer reproduces the Study Material's five bullet points word for word. RTP January 2025 Q10 asks for "the five competitive forces" and answers with each force and two lines from its explanation (entrants add capacity and limit prices; buyers form groups; specialised, few suppliers; rivalry in prices and advertising; substitutes with a price or performance advantage).
- **When rivalry is cut-throat, 5 marks**: January 2025 Q8(a); RTP January 2026 Q10. The six conditions, each with a line of explanation.
- **Barriers to entry, 5 marks**: September 2024 Q8(a) ("the common barriers which may cause restrain for the keenness of new entrepreneurs"), repeated in MTP January 2026 Series II and MTP September 2026 Series II. A case: RTP May 2025 Q9, repeated in MTP January 2025 Series I Q3(a) (a government registration process that raises barriers to entry for the incumbents' benefit).
- **When buyers have power**: MTP September 2024 Series I Q4(a).
- **Five forces case analysis, 5 marks**: RTP September 2024 Q10 (four similar rivals, patents, many suppliers), RTP September 2026 Q9 (one force per fact, in a table), MTP May 2026 Series II Q1(c) (organic restaurants), MTP September 2026 Series I Q4(b) (electric vehicles), MTP May 2024 Series II Q1(c) (new sustainable fashion brands: threat of new entrants, the Study Material's own scenario question); RTP May 2026 Q9 asks which aspect of external analysis a crowded market reflects (the competitive or industry environment).
- **MCQs, 2 marks each in the MTPs**: identify the force from the facts in MTP January 2025 Series I and II, MTP January 2026 Series I (numerous players offering functionally similar, low-priced smartphones: **rivalry**), MTP May 2025 Series I and II, MTP September 2025 Series II, MTP May 2026 Series I and II, MTP September 2024 Series I and II, and in RTP January 2026, RTP May 2026 (few makers of a specialised screen with no alternative: **suppliers**) and RTP May 2024; when the threat of substitutes is high (low switching costs: MTP May 2024 Series I, 1 mark).
- **Experience curve**: descriptive, 5 marks, September 2025 Q6(b), RTP May 2024 Q10, MTP September 2025 Series II Q3(b), MTP May 2026 Series II Q3(a), MTP September 2026 Series II Q4(b); MCQs: September 2024 MCQ 11, RTP May 2025 MCQ 5, MTP May 2026 Series I.
- **Attractiveness of industry**: only the Study Material's own MCQ (attractiveness is seen in relative terms, SM 2.47).

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
Descriptive answers reproduce ICAI's list in ICAI's order with one line each. Case answers name the force, quote the condition from the Study Material that the facts satisfy ("buyers are more concentrated than the firms supplying the product"), and end with a conclusion on the industry's attractiveness or the firm's position. Where the case says nothing about a force, ICAI writes so ("There is no information relating to substitutes ...") and may add a reasonable inference (RTP September 2024 Q10).

**[trick] "Can Every Person Stand By Distributors' Retaliation?"**
The seven barriers to entry in ICAI's order: **C**apital requirements, **E**conomies of scale, **P**roduct differentiation, **S**witching costs, **B**rand identity, **D**istribution channels (access to), aggressive **R**etaliation. Condition: these are the Study Material's "common" barriers; a case can add others (a licence, patents), which you name as barriers in the same way.

### Answer frame

### A five forces case (5 marks)

1. **Model (1 line)**: "Porter's five forces model holds that the state of competition in an industry is a composite of competitive pressures operating in five areas ..."
2. **Force by force**: name the force, the fact from the case, the Study Material's condition it meets, and its strength (fierce, strong, moderate, weak). A table works well.
3. **Forces the case is silent on**: say so; add an inference only if the facts support it.
4. **Conclusion**: the collective strength of the forces decides the scope to earn attractive profits; is the industry attractive for this firm (relative, not absolute), and what should it do?

### "Identify the areas in which competitive pressure operates" or "What are the five competitive forces?" (5 marks)

Five forces for five marks. For "areas", write the Study Material's five bullet points as they stand (rival sellers' jockeying for buyer patronage; threat of new entrants; substitutes from companies in other industries; supplier bargaining power and supplier-seller collaboration; buyer bargaining power and seller-buyer collaboration). For "forces", name each force and add its key line: new entrants add capacity and limit prices; buyers gain power by forming groups; specialised and few suppliers have clout; rivalry shows in prices, advertising and pressure on costs; substitutes with a price or performance advantage limit prices and profits. Close with: their collective strength determines the scope to earn attractive profits.

### "Explain the conditions in which rivalry is cut-throat" (5 marks)

Open with "the more intensive the rivalry, the less attractive the industry", then the six conditions in order: industry leader, number of competitors, fixed costs, exit barriers, product differentiation, slow growth, each with its one-line reason.

### "Explain the experience curve and its relevance" (5 marks)

Concept (learning curve; unit costs decline with cumulative volume; "we learn as we grow"); implication (larger firms, lower unit costs, cost advantage); causes (learning effects, economies of scale, product redesign, technological improvements); the three features; a sketch of cost per unit against cumulative production; relevance (barrier to entry, builds market share and discourages competition; Maruti Suzuki; rivals choose a niche or segmentation by demography or geography).

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: "Bargaining power of unions"** **Fix:** the five are the threat of new entrants, the bargaining power of buyers, the bargaining power of suppliers, rivalry among existing firms and the threat of substitutes. The Study Material's own MCQ uses "bargaining power of unions" as the wrong option (SM 2.47).
- **Calling a rival's similar product a "substitute"** **Fix:** substitutes come from **companies in other industries** and perform the same, or nearly the same, function; products of rivals in the same industry belong to rivalry.
- **Mixing up who has the power** **Fix:** suppliers are powerful when they are few and more concentrated than their buyers, their product is crucial with no substitutes, and they can erect high switching costs; buyers are powerful when they are big, concentrated, fully informed and able to switch.
- **"This industry is attractive", full stop** **Fix:** attractiveness is relative, not absolute. State for whom: an industry unattractive to weak competitors may be attractive to strong ones, depending on the firm's competitive position and its ability to counteract the unattractive factors.

### Recap

- Industry analysis estimates the competitive pressures the business faces now and will face in the near future; the industry is where competitive advantage is won or lost.
- Five areas of pressure: rivalry among sellers, threat of new entrants, substitutes from other industries, supplier power and supplier-seller collaboration, buyer power and seller-buyer collaboration.
- Three steps: identify the pressures, evaluate their strength (fierce, strong, moderate to normal, weak), determine whether their collective strength permits attractive profits.
- Barriers to entry: capital requirements, economies of scale, product differentiation, switching costs, brand identity, access to distribution channels, aggressive retaliation.
- Buyers are powerful when fully informed, big, concentrated and able to switch; suppliers when their product is crucial with no substitutes, switching costs are high and they are more concentrated than their buyers.
- Rivalry is cut-throat with no strong leader to discipline it, many competitors, high fixed costs, high exit barriers, little differentiation and slow growth.
- Substitutes perform the same function from another industry and limit prices and profits. Attractiveness is relative, not absolute.
- Experience curve: unit costs decline with cumulative volume ("we learn as we grow"); a barrier to entry; rivals respond with a niche or segmentation by demography or geography. (The twentieth-Diwali pani puri stall wins on cost.)

## Lesson 2.4: Understanding customers, markets and competition

*Markets and marketing orientations, customers and consumers, customer analysis and customer behaviour, competitive strategy, the five steps to understand the competitive landscape, and key success factors.*

### Key idea

Understand the market from both sides. On the customer side: who buys (the customer) and who uses (the consumer), what they need (customer analysis), and how they buy (external and internal influences, decision making, post-decision processes). On the competition side: who the competitors are and what they do well and badly (the competitive landscape, in five steps), and the few **key success factors** that decide success in the industry.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where this lesson appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
Of the 32 new-scheme ICAI documents searched (eight past papers, eight RTPs and sixteen MTP series), 16 ask about it:

- **Steps to understand the competitive landscape, 5 marks**, mostly as a case of a business with falling sales or margins: RTP May 2024 Q9 and RTP January 2026 Q9 (a confectionery business), RTP January 2025 Q9 (ceramic décor), January 2026 Q5(c) (organic grocery stores), MTP September 2024 Series I Q3(a), MTP September 2025 Series I (smart home devices). MCQ: which is **not** a step ("understanding the customer", MTP January 2026 Series II).
- **Key success factors, 5 marks**: meaning, the questions that identify them and how they help gain sustainable competitive advantage, May 2025 Q8(a) (1 + 2 + 2 marks); the role of KSFs in competitive success, MTP September 2024 Series II Q2(a) and MTP January 2025 Series II Q2(a); KSFs from a case, MTP May 2025 Series II Q1(a). In an MCQ, "key success factor" is a distractor: partnerships that enhance a travel firm's competitive advantage are keyed **competitive advantage** (MTP September 2026 Series I 1(A)(iv)); the same series' case lists the firm's KSFs in its facts.
- **Customer behaviour**: a marketing strategy built on the factors that influence consumer behaviour (a new smartphone in the Study Material's own question, repeated in MTP May 2024 Series I and MTP January 2025 Series I; an AI-enabled smartphone in MTP September 2026 Series I; celebrity endorsements of a skincare brand in MTP January 2026 Series II Q1(a)). MCQ: preferences, attitudes, motivation and perception shaping behaviour before any advertisement: **internal influences** (May 2026 MCQ 15).
- **Customer analysis**: an assertion-reason MCQ (September 2026 MCQ 9; ICAI's answers for that paper were not yet published).
- Markets, marketing orientations and competitive strategy have not been asked on their own in these documents; they appear inside case scenarios.

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
The suggested answers to competitive landscape cases give a one-line meaning ("a business analysis which identifies competitors, either direct or indirect"), then the five steps in order, each with its Study Material sentence, then a line applying it to the case. KSF answers give the definition, the three questions as bullets, and the "distinctively better than rivals on one or more KSFs" point for sustainable competitive advantage. Behaviour answers use the four headings, each with a marketing action from the case.

**[trick] "I Understand Strong, Weak, Together"**
The five steps of the competitive landscape in order: **I**dentify the competitor, **U**nderstand the competitors, **S**trengths, **W**eaknesses, put it all **T**ogether. Condition: the steps study competitors; studying customers is customer analysis, a different tool.

### Answer frame

### "Explain the steps to understand the competitive landscape" (case, 5 marks)

1. **Meaning (1 line)**: a business analysis which identifies competitors, direct or indirect; requires competitive intelligence.
2. **The five steps, in order**: identify the competitor (market share data); understand the competitors (sources, their products and services); determine strengths (what they do well, why customers buy, finances, cost and price advantage, distribution, people); determine weaknesses (consumer reports, reviews, annual reports); put all the information together (gaps the firm can fill, areas it must strengthen).
3. **Apply each step** to the case in one sentence, and close with what the firm should do.

### "What are KSFs? How do they help gain sustainable competitive advantage?" (5 marks)

Definition (1 mark); the three questions (2 marks); then (2 marks, the split printed on May 2025 Q8(a)): training strategy on KSFs and being distinctively better than rivals on one or more of them gives sustainable competitive advantage; misdiagnosing them risks a misdirected strategy; rarely more than three or four, one or two outrank the others (2 marks).

### "Develop a marketing strategy using customer behaviour" (5 marks)

Meaning of customer behaviour (how customers purchase), then four headings, each with an action from the case: external influences (advertising, influencers, peers); internal influences (motivation, attitudes); decision making (information and comparisons for a significant purchase); post-decision processes (after-sales service and feedback to turn satisfaction into repeat purchases and recommendations).

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: Bunty studies the customers and calls it the competitive landscape** **Fix:** those belong to customer analysis. The competitive landscape's five steps are about **competitors**: identify them, understand them, their strengths, their weaknesses, then put it all together.
- **"Customer and consumer mean the same thing"** **Fix:** they are often used interchangeably, but there is a thin distinction: the customer purchases, the consumer consumes or uses. A parent buying groceries for the family is the customer; every family member is a consumer.
- **The KSF list that runs to a page** **Fix:** only rarely does an industry have more than three or four KSFs at any one time. Listing every factor that matters a little defeats the purpose of concentrating management attention on what is truly critical.
- **Writing "competitive strategy" as "beating competitors on price"** **Fix:** competitive strategy is how a firm competes in its business areas: how it expects to **create and sustain** a competitive advantage. It is analysed on two criteria: the creation and the protection of competitive advantage.

### Recap

- Market: a place where buyers and sellers exchange items and services for a price; physical or virtual. Marketing: research, design, pricing, promotion, transport, distribution; the four Ps (product, place, pricing, promotion).
- Orientations: product (best quality and features), production (low price), sales (advertising and promotion persuade), customer or market (gather and use customer and competitive information); customer-centric firms keep learning.
- Customer: buys. Consumer: consumes or uses. A parent buys the groceries; the family consumes them.
- Customer analysis identifies target clients, their wants and how the product meets them: surveys, consumer data, positioning, customer profiles, segmentation.
- Customer behaviour: external influences (marketing efforts, environmental elements), internal influences (motivation, attitudes), decision making (problem recognition, search, weigh, choose), post-decision processes (satisfaction or dissonance).
- Competitive strategy: how a firm creates and sustains competitive advantage; judged on its creation and protection.
- Competitive landscape: identify competitors, understand them, their strengths, their weaknesses, put it all together; requires competitive intelligence.
- KSFs: what most affects the ability to prosper; three questions (basis of customer choice, resources and capabilities needed, sustainable advantage); rarely more than three or four. (The caterer never lists the napkins.)
