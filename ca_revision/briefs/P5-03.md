## Lesson 3.1: Audit risk: inherent, control and detection risk

*Why an auditor can sign a wrong opinion, the three risks behind it, the one risk the auditor controls, and how ICAI asks you to spot each in a case.*

### Key idea

Audit risk is a function of the risk of material misstatement (inherent risk and control risk, the entity's risks) and detection risk (the auditor's). The auditor can influence only detection risk, so the higher the assessed risk of material misstatement, the lower detection risk must be kept, through more and better audit evidence.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where these risks appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
In the 32 new-scheme Paper 5 documents searched (past papers, RTPs and MTPs), a question on audit risk or its components appeared in 19.

- **Descriptive, 4 or 5 marks.** Inherent risk applied to a case: MTP May 2026 Series I Q1(a), MTP September 2026 Series II Q1(b). Control risk with examples: MTP September 2026 Series I Q1(a). Detection risk: RTP May 2024 Q5; MTP September 2024 Series I Q1(b); MTP September 2025 Series II Q4(c) (4 marks); September 2026 exam Q4(a) (answers not yet published). What audit risk includes and excludes: MTP January 2026 Series II Q1(a), MTP September 2024 Series II Q4(b). Examples of misstatement: RTP January 2026 Q11.
- **Multiple choice, 2 marks each**, mostly "identify the type of risk": control risk (MTP May 2025 Series I MCQ 5, RTP January 2026 MCQ 6, MTP January 2026 Series II MCQ 11, September 2026 MCQ 7); inherent risk (MTP May 2026 Series II MCQ 2); sampling and non-sampling risk (May 2026 MCQ 2, September 2025 MCQ 3); audit risk and the financial statement level (MTP January 2026 Series I MCQs 10–12); misstatement (MTP May 2024 Series II MCQ 3); first audits, where audit risk or detection risk is high (MTP May 2024 Series I MCQ 1, RTP May 2024 MCQ 1.1); which risks are not audit risks (January 2026 MCQ 15, and the Study Material's case study in MTP May 2025 Series II MCQ 12).

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
Suggested answers give the SA 200 definition word for word, the Study Material's explanation and examples, then the facts and a conclusion ("the view of the audit assistant is not correct"); for detection risk, also its two parts and the three ways to reduce it.

**[trick] Item, client, checker**
**I**nherent risk lives in the **item** (before any control), **C**ontrol risk in the **client's** controls, **D**etection risk in what the auditor **does**. Ask "whose failure is it?". Condition: the misstatement could be material; the auditor's business risks are not audit risk.

### Answer frame

### "Identify and explain the risk" in a case

1. **Name the risk** and give its SA 200 definition in ICAI's words.
2. **Explain it** with the Study Material's points (inherent risk: higher for complex calculations, external circumstances; control risk: inverse relation with efficiency of internal control; detection risk: sampling and non-sampling).
3. **Apply it to the facts**: "In the given case, ..."
4. **Conclude**, and add the consequence: "Hence detection risk must be kept low by increasing the area of checking, testing larger samples and using competent and experienced staff."

### "Distinguish between inherent, control and detection risk"

| Point | Inherent risk | Control risk | Detection risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Key words | Susceptibility of an assertion, "before consideration of any related controls" | Not "prevented, or detected and corrected, on a timely basis" by internal control | Auditor's procedures "will not detect" a material misstatement |
| Owner | Entity | Entity | Auditor |
| Auditor can change it? | No, only assess it | No, only assess it | Yes |
| Moves with | Complexity, external circumstances | Inversely with efficiency of internal control | Area of checking, sample size, staff competence |
| Example | Complex calculation; failing industry | Locked-safe rule not followed | Unrepresentative sample |

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the definition swap** **Fix:** "susceptibility ... before consideration of controls" is inherent risk; "not prevented, or detected and corrected, on a timely basis by internal control" is control risk.
- **"The risk is high, so keep detection risk high"** **Fix:** they move in opposite directions. High risk of material misstatement means detection risk must be kept low, which means more and better evidence (MTP September 2024 Series I).
- **The auditor who promises to cut control risk** **Fix:** those are the entity's risks, influenced by the client and not by the auditor (SM 3.5); the auditor assesses them and can reduce only detection risk.
- **Counting the lawsuit** **Fix:** litigation, adverse publicity, and calling correct accounts wrong are not audit risk for the SAs.

### Recap

- Audit risk: the risk that the auditor gives an inappropriate opinion when the financial statements are materially misstated. It is a function of the risk of material misstatement and detection risk.
- Risk of material misstatement: misstated before audit; at the financial statement level and the assertion level; components inherent risk and control risk, both the entity's.
- Inherent risk: susceptibility to material misstatement before considering controls; higher for complex calculations and in failing industries.
- Control risk: internal control will not prevent, or detect and correct, a material misstatement on time; inverse to the efficiency of internal control.
- Detection risk: the auditor's procedures will not detect it; sampling and non-sampling risk; the only one the auditor can influence.
- Reduce detection risk: wider checking, larger samples, competent and experienced staff.
- Not audit risk: the auditor's business risks; calling correct accounts wrong. Assessment is judgment, not measurement.
- Audit risk = inherent risk × control risk × detection risk. (Sour chai needs all three strainers to fail.)

## Lesson 3.2: SA 320: materiality and performance materiality

*What makes a misstatement material, how the auditor sets materiality from a benchmark and a percentage, why performance materiality sits lower, and when both are revised and documented.*

### Key idea

Materiality is the size (or nature) of misstatement that could influence users' economic decisions. The auditor sets it by judgment from a benchmark and a percentage, sets performance materiality lower to cover misstatements that add up, applies both from planning to the opinion, revises them when circumstances change, and documents them.

### Formats and formulas

**[formula] Materiality for the financial statements as a whole**
$$\text{Materiality} = \text{Benchmark} \times \text{Percentage}$$
A starting point, not a mechanical rule: a percentage is often applied to a chosen benchmark as a starting point (SM 3.20). Benchmark: the figure users focus on, from relevant financial data, normalised if exceptional. Percentage: professional judgment (for example 5% of profit before tax from continuing operations, 1% of total revenue or expenses for a not-for-profit entity) (SM 3.20–3.21).

### The materiality working paper

The audit documentation shall include these amounts **and the factors considered in their determination** (SM 3.23). *Reproduce the four items from memory, in this order.*

| Item to document | What it records |
|---|---|
| (a) Materiality for the financial statements as a whole | Benchmark, percentage, amount, reasons |
| (b) Materiality level(s) for particular classes, balances or disclosures, if applicable | The items, amounts, reasons |
| (c) Performance materiality | Amount(s), reasons |
| (d) Any revision of (a) to (c) as the audit progressed | What changed, revised amounts |

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where SA 320 appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
In the 32 new-scheme Paper 5 documents searched, a question on materiality appeared in 10.

- **Descriptive, 4 or 5 marks.** Benchmark factors, often with examples: MTP May 2026 Series II Q1(a) (5 marks), MTP January 2025 Series II Q4(c) (4 marks; also old-scheme November 2020 Q2(b)). The auditor's responsibility under SA 320 and the framework's three points, with aggregation and "users as a group": MTP September 2026 Series II Q2(b) (5 marks). Assumptions about users: MTP September 2024 Series II Q2(c) (4 marks). "Materiality is considered only at planning": MTP September 2024 Series II Q4(b).
- **Multiple choice, 2 marks.** Normalised profit before tax for an exceptional year: September 2026 MCQ 3. Profit before tax for a company with steady profits: MTP September 2024 Series I MCQ 7. Misstatements below materiality individually but material together: MTP September 2024 Series II MCQ 6, MTP September 2026 Series I MCQ 11. Materiality applied in planning, performing and evaluating: MTP May 2026 Series I MCQ 14, MTP September 2026 Series I MCQ 14. Documenting performance materiality "set at less than" materiality: RTP January 2026 MCQ 3. Branch materiality: MTP May 2024 Series I MCQ 2.2.

**[exam] The traps**
Benchmark factors were the descriptive question asked most often in the documents read (two MTPs and an old-scheme paper): write all four, in order, with examples. ICAI's suggested answer in MTP May 2026 Series II splits the third factor into two bullets (nature, life cycle, industry and economy; ownership structure and financing); either layout covers the same points. MCQ distractors put performance materiality at or above materiality, let management choose the benchmark, or confine materiality to planning, or ignore an exceptional year.

**[trick] EFNV for the benchmark factors**
**E**lements, **F**ocus of users, **N**ature of the entity (life cycle, industry, ownership, financing), **V**olatility. "Every Firm Needs Value." Condition: these are factors for the benchmark for the financial statements as a whole, not for the lower levels for particular items, which have their own three factors.

### Answer frame

### A case on materiality

1. **Provision**: the SA 320 definition, plus the rule the case needs (benchmark factors, nature over size, statutory disclosure, revision, performance materiality).
2. **Facts**: the amounts and their comparison with materiality, or the nature of the item.
3. **Analysis**: size, nature, aggregate, statutory requirement.
4. **Conclusion**: "material / not material", and the consequence (evaluate under SA 450, revise performance materiality, document).

### "Distinguish materiality from performance materiality"

| Point | Materiality | Performance materiality |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Misstatements that could reasonably influence users' economic decisions | Amount set below materiality |
| Purpose | Frame for the whole audit and the opinion | Reduces to an appropriately low level the probability that uncorrected and undetected misstatements together exceed materiality |
| Level | Benchmark × percentage | Less than materiality (or than a lower level) |
| Documented | Yes, with factors | Yes, with factors |

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: "below materiality, so ignore it"** **Fix:** materiality is "individually or in the aggregate"; add them up. And a small fraud or a missing statutory disclosure is material by nature.
- **Materiality set once and forgotten** **Fix:** it is applied in planning, performing and evaluating, and revised when circumstances or the auditor's understanding change (SM 3.22–3.23).
- **Using this year's profit in a freak year** **Fix:** an exceptional decrease or increase calls for a normalised profit before tax from continuing operations based on past results (SM 3.21).
- **Performance materiality above materiality** **Fix:** by definition it is set **at less than** materiality for the financial statements as a whole. An amount at or above it is not performance materiality.

### Recap

- Material: misstatements, including omissions, that individually or in the aggregate could reasonably be expected to influence users' economic decisions. Size or nature, or both; users as a group.
- Planning materiality guides risk assessment procedures, the assessment of risks and further audit procedures; it is not a bright line.
- Statutory disclosure items are material irrespective of amount; a small fraud can be material by nature.
- Benchmark factors: elements, users' focus, nature of the entity (life cycle, industry, ownership, financing), volatility. Profit before tax from continuing operations is common; normalise an exceptional year.
- Example percentages: 5% of profit before tax (profit-oriented manufacturer); 1% of revenue or expenses (not-for-profit).
- Performance materiality: set below materiality, to reduce to an appropriately low level the probability that uncorrected plus undetected misstatements exceed materiality.
- Revise for changed circumstances, new information or better understanding; if lower, revisit performance materiality and further procedures.
- Document materiality, item levels, performance materiality and revisions, with factors. (Mind the weevil, not the grain.)

## Lesson 3.3: SA 315: risk assessment procedures and understanding the entity

*How the auditor learns the business, uses inquiries, analytical procedures, observation and inspection to find what could go wrong, and spots the significant risks that need special attention.*

### Key idea

SA 315: understand the entity and its environment, including internal control, through risk assessment procedures (inquiry, analytical procedures, observation and inspection), so as to identify and assess the risks of material misstatement at the financial statement and assertion levels. These procedures are the basis for the responses, but by themselves they never give sufficient appropriate audit evidence for the opinion.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where SA 315 appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
In the 32 new-scheme Paper 5 documents searched, a question on SA 315 appeared in 8.

- **Descriptive, 4 or 5 marks.** How the auditor identifies and assesses risks (the objective and the four "shall" steps): September 2025 exam Q1(b) (5 marks), MTP May 2024 Series I Q5(b) (4 marks). Inquiries of internal audit, legal counsel, marketing and IS personnel: May 2024 exam Q2(a) (4 marks). Analytical procedures as risk assessment procedures, and whether the opinion can rest on them: MTP January 2025 Series I Q1(c). Factors for a significant risk: MTP September 2025 Series I Q6(b) (5 marks). Why risks are greater for non-routine transactions: MTP May 2026 Series I Q6(c) (4 marks).
- **Multiple choice, 2 marks.** Inquiries of internal audit, legal counsel and IT heads are risk assessment procedures: MTP September 2025 Series I MCQ 1. What is not part of understanding the entity: RTP May 2024 MCQ 5. Evaluating contradictory information and corroborating inquiry: MTP September 2026 Series II MCQs 2 and 3. Ratio changes as risk indicators: RTP May 2024 MCQ 1.3, and the Study Material's Test Your Understanding 4 and 5 (a listing next year).

**[exam] The marks**
Lists carry the marks: the three procedures, the five areas of understanding, the six significant-risk factors, the two "always significant" risks, the four documentation items. Write them in ICAI's order and add one line applying each to the facts.

**[trick] FRCRSU for the significant-risk factors**
**F**raud, **R**ecent developments, **C**omplexity, **R**elated parties, **S**ubjectivity in measurement, **U**nusual or outside the normal course. "Fraud Ruins Clean Reports, So Understand." Condition: "at least" these; the decision is the auditor's judgment, except that fraud risks and significant related party transactions outside the normal course are always significant.

### Answer frame

### "How will the auditor identify and assess the risks?"

1. Objective of SA 315, in its words.
2. Identify and assess at two levels: financial statement and assertion.
3. The four steps: identify, assess (pervasive?), relate to assertions, consider likelihood and magnitude.
4. Through risk assessment procedures (inquiry, analytical procedures, observation and inspection) and the understanding of the entity.
5. Conclusion: this provides the basis for further audit procedures under SA 330.

### "Is this a significant risk?"

Provision (the six factors and the two "always" cases) → facts → the factor(s) the facts trigger → conclusion, with "special audit consideration" as the consequence.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the opinion built on ratios** **Fix:** risk assessment procedures by themselves do not provide sufficient appropriate audit evidence for the opinion; they are the basis for further procedures.
- **Understanding the entity "done" at planning** **Fix:** it is a continuous, dynamic process throughout the audit (SM 3.28).
- **Listing every business risk** **Fix:** the auditor considers business risks that may result in risks of material misstatement; there is no responsibility to identify or assess all business risks (SM 3.26).
- **Calling every large item a significant risk** **Fix:** size alone is not the test; apply the six factors. Significant risks often relate to significant non-routine transactions or judgmental matters (SM 3.42), and fraud risks and significant related party transactions outside the normal course always are significant.

### Recap

- SA 315 objective: identify and assess the risks of material misstatement (fraud or error) at the financial statement and assertion levels, through understanding the entity and its environment, including internal control, as a basis for responses.
- Steps: identify; assess (pervasive?); relate to what can go wrong at the assertion level; consider likelihood and magnitude.
- Risk assessment procedures: inquiries, analytical procedures, observation and inspection. Never sufficient evidence on their own.
- Understand: (a) industry, regulatory, external factors; (b) nature of the entity; (c) accounting policies; (d) objectives, strategies, related business risks; (e) measurement and review of performance; plus internal control.
- Understanding is continuous and dynamic, a frame of reference for the whole audit.
- Significant risk factors: fraud, recent developments, complexity, related parties, subjectivity, unusual or outside the normal course. Always significant: fraud risks; significant related party transactions outside the normal course.
- Document: team discussion, key elements of the understanding and sources, assessed risks at both levels, risks and related controls. (No prescription before the consultation.)

## Lesson 3.4: Internal control: components, limitations and evaluation

*What internal control is under SA 315, its five components, why it gives only reasonable assurance, which controls matter to the auditor, and the four ways to record and evaluate it.*

### Key idea

Internal control is designed, implemented and maintained by those charged with governance, management and other personnel, and gives only reasonable assurance. The auditor understands and evaluates the internal control relevant to the audit, to design the nature, timing and extent of further audit procedures. Maintaining it is never the auditor's job.

### Formats and formulas

### The questionnaire and the check list

*Reproduce these layouts from memory. When ICAI asks you to draft questions, frame each so that "Yes" is the satisfactory answer.*

| No. | Question | Answer | Remarks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Are purchase orders pre-numbered? | Yes / No / N.A. | Explain every "No" |

| No. | Instruction or question | Answer | Initials |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Are tenders called before placing orders? | Yes / No / N.A. | |

The questionnaire is answered by the client's staff; the check list is followed and initialled by the auditor's own staff (SM 3.47–3.48). The Study Material's check list examples: tenders called before orders; purchases on written orders; standardised and pre-numbered purchase order forms; inventory control accounts kept by persons with nothing to do with custody, receipt, inspection or purchase of inventory (SM 3.47).

### The Study Material's questionnaire extracts, in brief

ICAI has asked students to match questions to their area, so know what each area covers (SM 3.48–3.50):

- **Purchases**: centralised purchase department; approved suppliers, with more than one source for important materials; requisitions signed by authorised persons; competitive quotations; pre-numbered orders signed by authorised staff; materials received only in the Receiving Department, by people who cannot issue orders or approve invoices; materials inspected and counted, weighed or measured on receipt; pre-numbered Goods Received Notes.
- **Creditors**: invoices routed direct to Accounts and entered in a bill register; all invoices processed; quantities and values per invoices reconciled with receipts into stock records; invoices matched with GRNs and purchase orders and checked for prices, freight and terms; advance payments authorised; duplicate invoices marked; suppliers' statements compared with ledgers and differences followed up; unpaid creditors listed and reconciled.
- **Inventories**: stocks in assigned areas; comprehensive insurance (an uninsured risk only by a senior official's decision); a record of policies, reviewed periodically; an official deciding the value insured and reviewing adequacy; perpetual records for raw materials, work-in-progress, finished goods and stores, reconciled with accounts; shortages and excesses reported, investigated and adjusted with written approval; norms for stock levels.
- **Fixed assets**: capital budgets approved; authority to spend restricted to specified officials; the same controls as other purchases; spending kept within the amount authorised; periodic verification under a written procedure, with reports showing damaged or obsolete items; discrepancies investigated and records corrected with appropriate authority.

**[exam] What ICAI asked, May 2024 to September 2026**

What the control environment includes, and its elements: RTP January 2025 Q11. The entity's risk assessment process: MTP September 2024 Series I Q5(b). The information system areas, with the risk assessment process accepted as an alternate answer: May 2026 Q2(a).
- **Limitations.** Explain the limitations behind five lapses: MTP September 2025 Series II Q1(a), 5 marks; collusion as a limitation inside an inherent-limitations-of-audit case: RTP January 2025 Q9.
- **Methods.** Name the method and draft questions on creditors: MTP September 2025 Series I Q1(a), 5 marks. Draft a questionnaire for an insurance department: MTP May 2024 Series II Q2(d), 3 marks.
- **MCQs, 2 marks each.** Four on one case (evaluation is part of the audit programme; check list; narrative record; questionnaire): May 2025 MCQs 9–12. **[exam] How the marks are earned**
The suggested answers name the component or method, describe it in the Study Material's words ("culture of honesty and ethical behaviour", "operating as intended", "most widely used", "bird's eye view") and tie it to the facts. Learn the seven control environment elements, four risk assessment steps and six limitations in the Study Material's order.

**[trick] Five components: "Every Rupee Is Carefully Monitored"**
**E**nvironment (control), **R**isk assessment process, **I**nformation system and communication, **C**ontrol activities, **M**onitoring of controls: ICAI's order, A to E.

### Answer frame

### "Identify the component of internal control"

1. **Name it**: "CA X is gaining an understanding of the [component] component of internal control."
2. **Describe it** in the Study Material's words: what the auditor shall understand or evaluate for that component.
3. **Tie it to the facts**: which fact shows it.
4. **Why it helps**: identifying potential misstatements and designing the nature, timing and extent of further procedures.

### "Can a satisfactory control environment prevent fraud?"

The points, from SM 3.35:

| Point | What to write |
|---|---|
| Positive factor | In assessing the risks of material misstatement |
| But | May reduce the risk of fraud; not an absolute deterrent |
| Conversely | Deficiencies undermine other controls (IT security example) |
| By itself | Does not prevent, or detect and correct, a misstatement |
| Conclusion | "Fraud cannot happen" is not a correct assurance |

### "Explain the limitations of internal control"

Name and explain each limitation in the Study Material's words, match each lapse to one, and conclude that internal control gives only reasonable assurance. A bare list of names does not get full marks.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the auditor who maintains internal control** **Fix:** management is, because internal control is designed, implemented and maintained by those charged with governance and management (SM 3.82). The auditor understands and evaluates it.
- **"Strong culture, so no fraud"** **Fix:** a satisfactory control environment is "not an absolute deterrent to fraud", and by itself it does not prevent, or detect and correct, a misstatement (SM 3.35).
- **Mixing up control activities and monitoring** **Fix:** control activities are the checks themselves; monitoring asks whether those checks are operating as intended over time and are modified for changes (SM 3.37–3.38).
- **A narrative record for a listed giant** **Fix:** the narrative record suits small businesses and cases with no formal control system, and makes weaknesses hard to identify (SM 3.46–3.47). A large entity's formal systems call for the questionnaire (the most widely used method) or a flow chart (the most concise record).
- **The questionnaire where "Yes" means trouble** **Fix:** frame each question so that "Yes" denotes a satisfactory position and "No" suggests a weakness, with space to explain every "No" (SM 3.48).

### Recap

- Internal control (SA 315): a process by those charged with governance, management and others; reasonable assurance on financial reporting, operations, safeguarding of assets and compliance.
- Understanding it helps identify potential misstatements and risk factors, and design further audit procedures.
- Five components, A to E: control environment; risk assessment process; information system and communication; control activities; monitoring of controls.
- Control environment: culture of honesty and a foundation for the rest; seven elements; not an absolute deterrent to fraud.
- Six limitations: reasonable assurance only, human judgment, not understanding the purpose, collusion and override, management's judgments, small entities.
- Relevance of a control is the auditor's judgment; design is evaluated before implementation; inquiry alone is not enough.
- Evaluation is an indispensable part of the audit programme: narrative record, check list, questionnaire (most widely used), flow chart (most concise).
- Management maintains internal control; the auditor evaluates it. (Raju may keep the café, but not the cash, the book and the bank slip all at once.)

## Lesson 3.5: Testing internal control: walkthroughs, tests of controls and reporting

*How the auditor checks that controls actually worked through the year: walkthroughs, the four kinds of test, what deviations mean, and how control deficiencies are assessed and reported.*

### Key idea

Tests of controls are performed to obtain audit evidence about the effectiveness of the design of the internal control system and of its operation throughout the period. Inquiry alone is never sufficient. Deviations can change the assessment of control risk, and with it the nature, timing and extent of substantive procedures.

### Exam angle

**[exam] What ICAI asked, May 2024 to September 2026**
In the 9 ICAI documents read for this lesson, testing internal control or reporting findings was examined 12 times.

- **The four tests and walkthroughs, 4 or 5 marks.** Re-performance of a bank reconciliation, plus the other procedures in tests of control: September 2024 Q2(a), 4 marks. The four audit tests in an automated environment and the role of judgment: January 2025 Q6(c) (an OR question), 4 marks. Ways of testing in an automated environment and the most effective combination: May 2026 Q4(a), 5 marks. Testing methods and what to do when general IT controls are ineffective: MTP May 2026 Series II Q6(c), 4 marks. Inspection as a test of controls (evidence of authorisation): MTP January 2026 Series II Q3(a).
- **Reporting findings, 4 marks.** Points to consider in assessing and reporting IT findings: September 2025 Q6(c). Assess and report findings and deficiencies in an automated environment: MTP September 2026 Series I Q6(c).
- **Tests of controls over sales for receivables, 4 or 5 marks**: MTP May 2024 Series II Q4(a), May 2025 Q4(c), MTP September 2026 Series I Q5(b), each answered with the Study Material's nine points.
- **Communicating deficiencies.** A one-line description of significant deficiencies is not proper: MTP May 2024 Series II Q6(b). Significant deficiencies must go in writing to those charged with governance, not an oral briefing: MTP January 2026 Series II MCQ 15.

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
"What kind of test?" questions are answered by naming the test and quoting its definition with the Study Material's example (bank reconciliation for re-performance; authorisation for inspection). The automated-environment questions are answered with the four tests, the inquiry/reperformance trade-off, "inquiry combined with inspection gives the most effective and efficient audit evidence", the judgment factors, documentation, and the three common methods, walkthrough first.

**[trick] Ask, watch, look, redo**
Inquiry, observation, inspection, reperformance. ICAI ranks only the two ends: **ask** is the cheapest and weakest (never alone), **redo** the strongest and dearest. **Watch** proves only the moment you watched; **ask + look** is the usual best buy. Which one to use in a given case is still the auditor's judgment.

### Answer frame

### "What test of control is the auditor performing?"

1. **Purpose**: tests of controls give evidence about the effectiveness of the design and of the operation of internal control throughout the period.
2. **Name the test** and give its definition with the Study Material's example.
3. **Apply** it to the facts.
4. **Add the other procedures** (inspection; inquiry and observation; re-performance; tests of computerised controls) if asked.

### "How are findings assessed and reported?"

| Step | What to write |
|---|---|
| Consider | Weaknesses in IT controls? Impact on the overall audit? |
| Assess | Each finding: impact on the audit; is it a deficiency (definition)? |
| Judge | Quantitative and qualitative; individually and in the aggregate |
| Report | Management: internal controls memo or management letter |
| Escalate | Significant deficiencies: in writing to those charged with governance |

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the manual that tested itself** **Fix:** understanding design and implementation is not a test of operating effectiveness; the auditor needs evidence that the control operated throughout the period (SM 3.41, 3.52).
- **"The staff said they always do it"** **Fix:** inquiry is the most efficient test but gives the least evidence; inquiry alone is not sufficient, so combine it with observation, inspection or reperformance (SM 3.59).
- **"No misstatements found, so the controls work"** **Fix:** the absence of misstatements detected by substantive procedures does not provide evidence that controls are effective (SM 3.69).
- **The one-line management letter** **Fix:** a written communication of significant deficiencies describes them and explains their potential effects, with enough context to understand it (SM 7.49).

### Recap

- Tests of controls give evidence about the effectiveness of the design and the operation of internal control throughout the period; planned selectively, important areas covered over, say, three years.
- Understanding design and implementation is not a test of operating effectiveness, except that an implemented automated control may serve as one if program change controls are effective.
- Procedures: inspection (authorisation), inquiry and observation (controls with no audit trail), re-performance (bank reconciliation), tests of computerised controls (access, program change).
- Inquiry: most efficient, least evidence, never alone. Reperformance: best evidence, least efficient. Inquiry plus inspection: generally the most effective and efficient.
- Walkthrough: one end-to-end transaction traced with inquiry, observation and inspection.
- Deviations (staff changes, seasonal volume, human error): specific inquiries, cover the period, revise control risk if needed and modify substantive procedures.
- Report deficiencies to management in an internal controls memo or management letter; significant deficiencies in writing to those charged with governance.
- (Bunty now redoes one reconciliation before he ticks twelve.)

## Lesson 3.6: Internal control in an IT environment

*What changes when the books live in an ERP: the IT risks, the three types of control (general IT, application and IT-dependent), when manual controls still suit better, and the audit approach.*

### Key idea

Automated application controls and IT-dependent controls are effective and reliable only if general IT controls are effective. When IT risks are not mitigated, the auditor cannot rely on system data or automated controls, and must test more, not less.

### Exam angle

**[exam] What ICAI asked, May 2024 to September 2026**
In the 9 new-scheme ICAI documents read for this lesson, the topic was examined 9 times.

- **Types of control, 3 or 4 marks.** Mention the types of control and classify access security, sequence number checks, data centre and network operations and mandatory data fields: May 2025 Q1(b), 4 marks. Classify reasonableness checks, data centre and network operations, application system acquisition, development and maintenance, and program change controls: MTP May 2024 Series I Q6(d), 3 marks.
- **General IT controls in depth.** Objectives and activities of access security and program change: RTP May 2025 Q11. A control "to ensure that production systems are processed to meet financial reporting objectives" is a data centre and network operations control, not an application control: January 2025 MCQ 5.
- **The automated environment.** How to understand and evaluate it after a shift to a fully automated ERP: January 2026 Q3(c), 4 marks (the answer lists the nine points of SM 3.55 and the "understand and evaluate" column of SM 3.61). Testing methods, and the course of action where general IT controls are ineffective: MTP May 2026 Series II Q6(c), 4 marks.
- **Manual elements.** When are manual elements more suitable? May 2024 Q3(c), 3 marks.
- **Impact of IT risks.** In what ways IT-related risks can affect the audit: RTP May 2024 Q6 (the answer gives the three impacts, on substantive checking, controls and reporting).
- **MCQ.** Unauthorised access to an inventory system by production supervisors indicates control risk: MTP January 2026 Series II MCQ 11.

In the old scheme, the impact of unmitigated IT risks on the audit was asked in November 2020 Q3(d), 3 marks. The Study Material's own MCQs test the same points: automation makes the business environment more complex; "processing of large volumes of data" is not an IT risk; validation of input data is not a general IT control (SM 3.79–3.81).

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
The Suggested Answers to classification questions are a two-column table with one type against each control: name the type precisely ("General IT controls", "Application controls") and, where asked, the area. Descriptive answers reproduce the Study Material's lists in its order: the nine points for understanding the automated environment, the four general IT control areas with their objective ("to ensure that ... to meet financial reporting objectives"), and the manual-element circumstances, opening with "where judgment and discretion are required".

**[trick] Spot the general IT control**
If a control protects **many applications at once** (backups, data centre, who may change programs, who may log in, how systems are built), it is a **general IT control**. If it sits **inside one application's transaction screen** (a field, a sequence, a limit, a reasonableness range), it is an **application control**. If a **person** acts on a **system report**, it is **IT-dependent**. Condition: application controls can also be manual at the business-process level (SM 3.58), so read what the control does, not just who does it.

### Answer frame

### "Discuss the relationship between general IT controls and application controls"

1. **Define general IT controls**: policies and procedures relating to many applications that support the effective functioning of application controls; pervasive or indirect; four areas.
2. **Define application controls**: automated or manual at the business process level; embedded in applications; completeness, accuracy, integrity; examples.
3. **The relationship**: interrelated; general IT controls are needed to support application controls; both needed for complete and accurate processing.
4. **Consequence**: if general IT controls fail, application controls and IT-dependent controls cannot be relied on.

### "How would you understand and evaluate the automated environment?"

| Part | What to write |
|---|---|
| Requirement | Understand the entity and its business, including IT: IT organisation, activities, dependencies, risks, controls |
| Points | The nine points: systems used, purpose, location, architecture, version, interfaces, in-house or packaged, outsourcing, key persons |
| Evaluate | Flowcharts or narratives, RCM, walkthroughs, entity level controls and segregation of duties, general IT and application controls |
| Close | Document the understanding |

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the computer that cannot err** **Fix:** the more automated the environment, the more complex it is likely to be (SM 3.54, 3.79), and if IT risks are not mitigated the auditor does more substantive checking, not less (SM 3.56).
- **Calling an input validation a general IT control** **Fix:** edit checks and validation of input data are application controls; general IT controls are the four pervasive areas (SM 3.57–3.58). ICAI's own MCQ uses exactly this trap (SM 3.80).
- **Forgetting that IT-dependent controls depend on IT** **Fix:** its effectiveness depends on the reliability of the source data and so on effective general IT controls (SM 3.58).

### Recap

- Automated environment: business run on IS/IT systems; faster, accurate, high volume, integrated; more automation means more complexity (ERP more complex than off-the-shelf software).
- Understand IT: nine points (systems, purpose, location, architecture, version, interfaces, in-house or packaged, outsourcing, key persons), and document it.
- Eight IT risks: inaccurate processing (or inaccurate data), unauthorised access to data, direct (backend) data changes, excessive or privileged access (super users), lack of segregation of duties, unauthorised program changes, failure to make necessary program changes, loss of data.
- Unmitigated IT risks: more substantive checking, no reliance on automated controls, possibly a modified report (internal financial controls).
- Three types: general IT controls (pervasive or indirect: data centre and network operations, program change, access security, system acquisition, development and maintenance), application controls, IT-dependent controls.
- General IT controls support application controls; both are needed for complete and accurate processing; ineffective general IT controls mean alternative procedures.
- Manual elements suit judgment: large, unusual or non-recurring items, unpredictable errors, changing circumstances, monitoring automated controls.
- (The computer repeats its mistakes perfectly; the auditor's job is to find out whether anyone can change the program.)

## Lesson 3.7: Digital audit and data analytics

*How auditors use data analytics and CAATs (spreadsheets, IDEA, ACL) on a client's electronic records, the seven uses ICAI lists, and what "digital audit" means from planning to opinion.*

### Key idea

Data analytics lets the auditor work through large volumes of electronic records, but its results are only as good as the completeness of the data it is run on, and every exception still needs the auditor's judgment and follow-up.

### Exam angle

**[exam] What ICAI asked**
In the 3 new-scheme ICAI documents read for this lesson, this topic was examined 3 times; in the 5 old-scheme documents read, 6 times.

- **New scheme.** Tools and techniques used in applying data analytics, and the areas where auditors can use it: RTP September 2024 Q11. Analyse the functions an auditor can perform using data analytics tools and techniques: MTP May 2025 Series II Q6(b), 5 marks. Explain the use of digital technology in the conduct of an audit, for a company digitising its operations: September 2024 Q5(c), 3 marks.
- **Old scheme** (Paper 6, similar wording): functions using data analytics, November 2020 Q3(b), 4 marks; how data analytics tests electronic records with spreadsheets and audit tools, May 2023 Q2(d), 3 marks; explain CAATs and data analytics (with other terms such as database), MTP November 2022 Series II Q2(d), 3 marks, and RTP May 2023 Q13; the full form of CAAT, RTP May 2023 MCQ 4; "evaluating impact of control deficiencies" as a use of data analytics, RTP May 2022 MCQ 1.5.
- The Study Material's own theoretical question asks in what areas data analytics can be useful in an automated environment (SM 3.81).

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
The Suggested Answers read for this lesson follow the Study Material's order: the meaning of data analytics (MTP May 2025 Series II; RTP September 2024 starts directly with CAATs), CAATs as the auditor's tools, "spreadsheets and specialised audit tools viz., IDEA and ACL", then the uses as a list. RTP September 2024 prints journal entries and fraud investigation as one bullet ("Analysis of journal entries of fraud investigation"); the Study Material (SM 3.62) and MTP May 2025 Series II list them separately, so write all seven. For digital audit, the answer is the Study Material's paragraph: digitisation, technology used from planning to the final opinion, AI and data analytics to understand business processes, more attention to areas requiring greater focus, better identification of risks.

**[trick] Seven uses: "Complete Samples Rebuild, Redo, Journal, Fraud, Fix"**
**C**ompleteness of population; **S**ample selection; **R**e-computation of balances; **R**eperformance of calculations; **J**ournal entry analysis; **F**raud investigation; impact of control de**f**iciencies ("Fix"). ICAI's order, all seven.

### Answer frame

### "Analyse the functions an auditor can perform using data analytics"

1. **Meaning** of data analytics, in the Study Material's words, with its opening point: companies benefit (increased profitability, better customer service, competitive advantage, more efficient operations), and auditors can use similar tools and techniques in the audit process and obtain good results.
2. **Tools**: CAATs; testing of electronic records and data residing in IT systems using spreadsheets and specialised audit tools viz. IDEA and ACL.
3. **The seven uses**, in order, each with its example (random or systematic sampling; trial balance reconstruction; depreciation and bank interest).

### "Explain the use of digital technology in the conduct of an audit"

The points, from SM 3.62:

| Point | What to write |
|---|---|
| Context | Entities digitise; business models restructured around technology; automation is key |
| When | Right from planning to the expression of the final opinion |
| What | Artificial intelligence, data analytics and other latest technologies |
| Why | Understand business processes better; conduct the audit better |
| Result | More attention to areas requiring greater focus; risks identified better |

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the incomplete file, perfectly tested** **Fix:** the first use in ICAI's list is checking completeness of data and population used in tests; until the population agrees, every later result is unreliable (SM 3.62, 3.61).
- **"The software found nothing, so there is nothing"** **Fix:** analytics locates exceptions; the auditor still investigates them and obtains audit evidence. CAATs are tools for applying data analytics in the audit process, not a substitute for it (SM 3.62).
- **Writing only "IDEA and ACL"** **Fix:** name CAATs, and both kinds of tool: spreadsheets and specialised audit tools such as IDEA and ACL (SM 3.62).

### Recap

- Data analytics: the combination of processes, tools and techniques used to tap vast amounts of electronic data to obtain meaningful information.
- CAATs: the tools and techniques auditors use to apply data analytics; spreadsheets and specialised audit tools viz. IDEA and ACL.
- Seven uses: completeness of data and population; sample selection (random, systematic); re-computation of balances (trial balance from transactions); reperformance of calculations (depreciation, interest); journal entry analysis; fraud investigation; impact of control deficiencies.
- Completeness of the population comes before any test that relies on it.
- Digital audit: AI, data analytics and latest technology from planning to final opinion; better understanding of processes, more focus on key areas, better risk identification.
- Analytics finds exceptions; the auditor still investigates them and forms the opinion. (The thali shows every grain; only you can tell the stone from the burnt rice.)

## Lesson 3.8: SA 330: the auditor's responses to assessed risks

*How the auditor answers the risks found under SA 315: overall responses, further audit procedures, when tests of controls are compulsory, and why substantive procedures are never skipped.*

### Key idea

SA 330: respond to financial statement level risks with overall responses, and to assertion level risks with further audit procedures whose nature, timing and extent are based on the assessed risk. Test controls when relying on them or when substantive procedures alone cannot give sufficient appropriate evidence; perform substantive procedures for every material class of transactions, account balance and disclosure regardless; and obtain more persuasive evidence the higher the risk.

### Exam angle

**[exam] Where SA 330 appeared, May 2024 to September 2026**
The syllabus asks only for an "Overview and Introduction" of SA 330. In the 32 new-scheme Paper 5 documents searched, a question on it appeared in 10.

- **Descriptive, 4 or 5 marks.** When tests of controls must be performed, the effect of greater reliance, and when higher assurance is sought: MTP January 2025 Series I Q4(b) (5 marks), MTP January 2026 Series I Q1(a) (5 marks, a bank's core banking system). Matters in determining the extent of tests of controls: RTP September 2025 Q11. Reperformance and other tests of controls: September 2024 exam Q2(a) (4 marks).
- **Multiple choice, 2 marks.** A whole case on responses (high risk, contradictory inquiries, tests of controls failing, then substantive procedures): RTP May 2026 MCQs 1–5. Choosing tests of details, substantive analytical procedures or both by judgment: MTP May 2026 Series I MCQ 13, MTP September 2026 Series I MCQ 13. What further audit procedures comprise: MTP September 2026 Series II MCQ 12. The Standard that deals with designing such tests: MTP May 2024 Series I MCQ 3.2. 100% examination as a test of details but not as a test of controls: MTP January 2025 Series I MCQ 1, September 2025 MCQ 4.

**[exam] What earns the marks**
The two "when" conditions for tests of controls, word for word, joined by "or"; "more persuasive audit evidence the greater the reliance"; "higher level of assurance ... where it is not possible or practicable to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence only from substantive procedures"; and "irrespective of the assessed risks ... substantive procedures for each material class of transactions, account balance, and disclosure".

**[trick] Rely or can't-do-without**
Tests of controls are compulsory in exactly two cases: you **rely** on the control, or you **can't do without** it (substantive procedures alone cannot give sufficient appropriate evidence). Substantive procedures are compulsory in one case: **always**, for every material class, balance and disclosure. Condition: "compulsory" here means required by SA 330; the auditor may still choose to test other controls.

### Answer frame

### "When shall the auditor perform tests of controls?" in a case

1. **Provision**: the two circumstances (reliance intended; substantive procedures alone insufficient), and "more persuasive evidence the greater the reliance".
2. **Facts**: why the case falls in one of them (high volume, automation, no documents; or planned reliance).
3. **Analysis**: what the tests must cover (how, how consistently, by whom or by what means; throughout the period of reliance; inquiry not alone), and the higher assurance where the approach is mainly tests of controls.
4. **Conclusion**: tests of controls required, and substantive procedures still required for each material item.

### "Distinguish tests of controls from substantive procedures"

| Point | Tests of controls | Substantive procedures |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Evaluate the operating effectiveness of controls in preventing, or detecting and correcting, material misstatements | Detect material misstatements at the assertion level |
| When required | When relying on controls, or when substantive procedures alone cannot give sufficient appropriate evidence | Always, for each material class of transactions, account balance and disclosure |
| Forms | Inquiry combined with inspection, observation or reperformance | Tests of details (vouching, verification) and substantive analytical procedures |
| Effect of results | Deviations may change reliance and the substantive plan | Misstatements found may indicate that controls are not effective |

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: "the controls are strong, so no substantive procedures"** **Fix:** substantive procedures are required for each material class of transactions, account balance and disclosure irrespective of the assessed risk. Strong controls may reduce their extent, never to nothing.
- **No misstatements found, so the controls work** **Fix:** the absence of misstatements detected by substantive procedures is not evidence that controls are effective (SM 3.69). Test the controls.
- **Testing a control by asking about it** **Fix:** inquiry alone is not sufficient to test operating effectiveness; combine it with inspection or reperformance, which may give more assurance than inquiry and observation, since an observation is pertinent only at the point in time at which it is made (SM 3.67).
- **Writing "tests of controls are always compulsory"** **Fix:** they are required in two circumstances only. Outside them the auditor may choose not to test controls, but then the risk assessment cannot be reduced for the effective operation of controls (SM 4.58).

### Recap

- Objective: sufficient appropriate audit evidence about the assessed risks, through appropriate responses.
- Financial statement level risks: overall responses. Assertion level risks: further audit procedures whose nature, timing and extent respond to the risk.
- Design: consider why the risk was assessed (inherent risk; control risk), and get more persuasive evidence the higher the risk.
- Tests of controls required when relying on controls, or when substantive procedures alone cannot give sufficient appropriate evidence; more persuasive evidence the greater the reliance; inquiry alone never enough. (Check that the generator starts before you rely on it.)
- Extent of tests of controls: frequency, period of reliance, expected deviation, relevance and reliability, evidence from other controls.
- Deviations: specific inquiries, then decide reliance, more tests of controls, or substantive procedures. Clean substantive results do not prove controls work.
- Substantive procedures for every material class, balance and disclosure, always: tests of details (vouching, verification) and substantive analytical procedures, alone or combined.
