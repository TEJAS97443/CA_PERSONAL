## Lesson 6.1: SA 230: audit documentation

*What SA 230 requires the auditor to record, the "experienced auditor" test, what to leave out, assembling the final audit file within about 60 days, retention for seven years, significant matters, the completion memorandum and who owns the file.*

### Key idea

Audit documentation is the record of audit procedures performed, relevant audit evidence obtained and conclusions reached. It must let an experienced auditor with no previous connection with the audit understand the work, the evidence and the significant judgments; it is prepared on a timely basis, assembled into the final file ordinarily within 60 days of the report, kept for at least seven years, and is the property of the auditor.

### Formats and formulas

### What every working paper must let a reader see

*Build each working paper so that these items are visible on its face (SM 6.6).*

| Item on the working paper | SA 230 requirement it meets |
|---|---|
| Items tested, by identifying characteristic | Identifying characteristics of the items or matters tested |
| Prepared by, and date completed | Who performed the work and the date it was completed |
| Reviewed by, date, extent of review | Who reviewed it, and the date and extent of review |
| Results, evidence, conclusion | Results of procedures, evidence obtained, conclusions |
| Discussions: what, when, with whom | Discussions of significant matters |

### The two numbers

**[formula] The assembly deadline and the retention period**
Assembly of final file complete: ordinarily not more than **60 days** after the date of the auditor's report.

Retention: ordinarily no shorter than **7 years** from the date of the auditor's report or, if later, the group auditor's report.

Worked dates for a report dated 22 May 2026: assembly ordinarily by **21 July 2026** (60 days: 9 days of May, 30 of June, 21 of July); retention at least until **22 May 2033**.

**[exam] How the marks are earned**
The suggested answers quote SA 230 or SQC 1 in its words ("administrative process", "not more than 60 days", "no shorter than seven years", "experienced auditor, having no previous connection with the audit", "property of the auditor"), apply each limb to the facts with the dates, and conclude in one line ("Thus, the approach of team member Y is not correct"). In "list" questions, the Study Material's lists (six purposes, seven factors, seven examples, four exclusions, four administrative changes, four significant-matter examples) earn a mark per point.

**[trick] 60 and 7, counted from the same day**
Both limits run from the **date of the auditor's report**: assembly within 60 days of it, retention for at least 7 years from it (or from the group auditor's report, if later). Neither runs from the year-end or from the completion of fieldwork. Both are "ordinarily", as the firm's SQC 1 policies set them.

### Answer frame

### A case question on the audit file

1. **Provision**: SA 230 (and SQC 1) in its words: timely preparation; assembly as an administrative process ordinarily within 60 days of the report; administrative changes only; no deletion after assembly; retention at least seven years; property of the auditor.
2. **Facts with dates**: count the days from the date of the auditor's report.
3. **Analysis**: limb by limb, allowed or not and why.
4. **Conclusion**: "Thus, the action of CA … is not valid / is in order."

### "State the matters the documentation must enable an experienced auditor to understand"

| Must understand (three) | Must record for nature, timing and extent (three) |
|---|---|
| Nature, timing and extent of procedures performed | Identifying characteristics of items tested |
| Results of procedures and evidence obtained | Who performed the work, and the date completed |
| Significant matters, conclusions and significant judgments | Who reviewed it, and the date and extent of review |

Add: discussions of significant matters (what, when, with whom) and how any inconsistency with the final conclusion was addressed.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the memo dated today, about last May** **Fix:** documentation is prepared on a timely basis; assembly after the report date is administrative only and draws no new conclusions. Back-filling papers puts a question mark on the quality of the whole audit.
- **"The 60 days are for preparing the documentation"** **Fix:** documentation is prepared during the audit, on a timely basis. The 60 days are only for the administrative assembly of the final file after the report date (RTP May 2024 Q14 turns on this).
- **Keeping every draft "to be safe"** **Fix:** superseded drafts of working papers and financial statements, notes of preliminary thinking, copies corrected for typing errors and duplicates need not be included; the final versions and the significant matters must be.
- **"The client paid for it, so the file is the client's"** **Fix:** unless law or regulation says otherwise, audit documentation is the property of the auditor. Extracts may be given at the auditor's discretion, if that does not undermine the validity of the work or the auditor's independence.

### Recap

- Audit documentation: the record of audit procedures performed, relevant audit evidence obtained, and conclusions reached ("working papers").
- Objective: a sufficient and appropriate record of the basis for the report, and evidence that the audit was planned and performed in accordance with SAs and law.
- Six purposes: plan and perform; direct, supervise and review; accountability; matters of continuing significance; SQC 1 reviews; external inspections.
- The experienced auditor test: nature, timing and extent; results and evidence; significant matters, conclusions and judgments. Record items tested, who did it and when, who reviewed it, when and how far; discussions of significant matters; how inconsistencies were addressed.
- Paper or electronic; not a substitute for the entity's records; drafts, preliminary notes, corrected copies and duplicates need not be kept.
- Prepare on a timely basis; assemble the final file ordinarily within 60 days of the report (administrative changes only); no deletion afterwards; retain at least 7 years from the report date (or the group auditor's report, if later).
- Completion memorandum: significant matters and how they were addressed. The file is the auditor's property; extracts at discretion. (The case sheet stays with the hospital.)
