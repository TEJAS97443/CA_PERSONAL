## Lesson 2.1: Liquidity and capital structure ratios

*Can the business pay what falls due this year, who funded it, and how many times do its earnings cover interest and instalments: the liquidity, capital structure and coverage ratios.*

### Key idea

A single figure says little; a figure compared with another says a lot. Liquidity ratios test whether short-term liabilities can be paid from assets that turn into cash within the year; capital structure ratios show how much of the business owners fund and how much lenders; coverage ratios show how many times earnings cover interest, instalments and preference dividend.

### Formats and formulas

*Reproduce the two lists and every formula from memory; ICAI's lists are what its answers count as current.*

| Current assets (SM 3.5) | Current liabilities (SM 3.6) |
|---|---|
| Inventories | Creditors for goods and services |
| Sundry debtors | Short-term loans |
| Cash and bank balances | Bank overdraft |
| Receivables / accruals | Cash credit |
| Loans and advances | Outstanding expenses |
| Disposable investments | Provision for taxation |
| Any other current assets | Proposed dividend |
| | Unclaimed dividend |
| | Any other current liabilities |

**[definition] Ratio (SM 3.3)**
"The indicated quotient of two mathematical expressions and as the relationship between two or more things." A financial or accounting ratio is a mathematical expression of the relationship between two accounting figures.

**[formula] Current ratio and quick (acid-test) ratio**
$$\text{Current ratio} = \frac{\text{Current assets}}{\text{Current liabilities}} \qquad \text{Quick ratio} = \frac{\text{Quick assets}}{\text{Current liabilities}}$$
Quick assets = current assets − inventories − prepaid expenses. ICAI's norms: 2:1 and 1:1 (SM 3.38).

**[formula] Cash ratio (absolute liquidity ratio)**
$$\text{Cash ratio} \qquad = \frac{\text{Cash} + \text{Marketable securities}}{\text{Current liabilities}}$$
Cash = cash and bank balances; current investments may replace marketable securities (SM 3.7).

**[formula] Basic defense interval (interval measure)**
$$\text{BDI} = \frac{C + R + M}{\text{Daily operating expenses}} \qquad = \frac{\text{CA} - \text{Prepaid} - \text{Inventories}}{\text{Daily operating expenses}}$$
C = cash and bank balances, R = net receivables, M = marketable securities; CA = current assets; prepaid = prepaid expenses (SM 3.7).
$$\text{Daily operating expenses} \qquad = \frac{\text{COGS} + S - N}{\text{No. of days in a year}}$$
S = selling, administration and other general expenses; N = depreciation and other non-cash expenditure. The answer is in days; take 360 days unless the question says otherwise.

**[formula] Net working capital**
$$\text{NWC} = \text{CA} - \text{CL}$$
NWC = net working capital; CA = current assets; CL = current liabilities.
The Study Material's definition adds "(excluding short-term bank borrowing)" to current liabilities (SM 3.8); see Under the hood.

**[formula] Equity ratio and debt ratio**
$$\text{Equity ratio} = \frac{\text{Shareholders’ equity}}{\text{Net assets}} \qquad \text{Debt ratio} = \frac{\text{Total debt}}{\text{Net assets}}$$
Shareholders' equity = equity share capital + reserves and surplus (excluding fictitious assets). Net assets (capital employed) = net fixed assets + net current assets. Total debt = short and long-term borrowings from financial institutions, debentures and bonds, deferred payment arrangements for capital equipment, bank borrowings, public deposits and any other interest-bearing loan (SM 3.9–3.10).

**[formula] Debt to equity ratio**
$$\text{D/E} = \frac{\text{Total outside liabilities}}{\text{Shareholders’ equity}} \qquad \text{or} \qquad \frac{\text{Long-term debt}}{\text{Shareholders’ equity}}$$
The first counts both current and non-current liabilities; sometimes only interest-bearing long-term debt is used (SM 3.10).

**[formula] Debt to total assets, capital gearing and proprietary ratio**
$$\text{Debt to total assets} \qquad = \frac{\text{Total outside liabilities}}{\text{Total assets}}$$
$$\text{Capital gearing} = \frac{P + D + B}{E + R - L}$$
$$\text{Proprietary ratio} = \frac{\text{Proprietary fund}}{\text{Total assets}}$$
Debt to total assets may use total debt in the numerator. For gearing, P = preference share capital, D = debentures, B = other borrowed funds, E = equity share capital, R = reserves and surplus, L = losses. Proprietary fund = equity share capital + preference share capital + reserves and surplus; total assets exclude fictitious assets and losses (SM 3.10–3.11).

**[formula] Coverage ratios**
$$\text{DSCR} = \frac{\text{Earnings for debt service}}{\text{Interest} + \text{Instalments}} \qquad \text{Interest coverage} = \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Interest}}$$
Earnings (available) for debt service = EAT + depreciation and other non-cash operating expenses + interest + other adjustments such as loss on sale of fixed assets (SM 3.11–3.12).
$$\text{Preference dividend coverage} \qquad = \frac{\text{EAT}}{\text{Preference dividend}}$$
$$\text{Equity dividend coverage} \qquad = \frac{\text{EAT} - \text{Preference dividend}}{\text{Equity dividend}}$$
$$\text{Fixed charges coverage} \qquad = \frac{\text{EBIT} + \text{Depreciation}}{\text{Interest} + \text{Repayment of loan}}$$
ICAI's norms (SM 3.39): DSCR 2; interest, preference dividend and fixed charges coverage above 1.

**[note] ICAI's notes for calculating ratios (SM 3.13)**
1. EBIT = PBIT (profit before interest and taxes); EAT = PAT (profit after taxes); EBT = PBT (profit before taxes).
2. Ratios shall be calculated based on requirement and availability of information and may deviate from the original formulae. If required, assumptions should be given.
3. The numerator should be taken in correspondence with the denominator, and vice versa.

**[exam] The traps ICAI set**
- Two equations, two unknowns: a current ratio and a quick ratio together give current liabilities only after inventory is known (January 2026 Q1(a)); a current ratio with working capital gives CL = WC ÷ (CR − 1).
- The basic defense interval uses **cash** operating expenses: depreciation and other non-cash items are deducted (January 2026 Q1(a)).
- "Future instalments" are the instalments in the DSCR denominator, not the whole loan (RTP January 2025 Q4).
- The paper's own instruction: "Working notes should form part of the answer."

**[trick] Current liabilities in one line**
From working capital: CL = working capital ÷ (current ratio − 1), and CA = CL × current ratio; then inventory = CA − (quick ratio × CL) − prepaid expenses. From inventory: CL = (inventory + prepaid expenses) ÷ (current ratio − quick ratio). Conditions: the working capital is CA − all CL, and quick assets exclude exactly inventory and prepaid expenses.

### Answer frame

### "Discuss liquidity ratios" and "Discuss gearing ratio"

The chapter's own theory questions include "Discuss Stock Turnover ratio and Gearing ratio" and "Discuss the financial ratios for evaluating company performance on ... liquidity position" (SM 3.65). For each ratio write, in this order:

1. What it measures, in ICAI's words (liquidity: ability to pay short-term liabilities; gearing: proportion of fixed interest or dividend bearing capital to equity shareholders' funds).
2. The formula, with the components defined (what is a quick asset; what is in the denominator of gearing).
3. The interpretation and the norm where ICAI gives one (2:1, 1:1; higher gearing may indicate more risk and signifies the advantage of financial leverage to equity shareholders).
4. Who uses it (short-term creditors and banks for liquidity; long-term lenders for leverage).
5. A one-line caution: a ratio needs comparison (past years, competitors, industry), and liquidity ratios should be read with the turnover ratios.

### Three answers to "calculate the DSCR"

**[cols] **Loses marks****

"DSCR = 3.08."

A bare figure: no formula, no basis, nothing to give method marks for.
---
**Passes**

"DSCR = 37 ÷ 12 = 3.08 times."

Right, but the examiner cannot see that 37 is profit after tax + depreciation + interest.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the prepaid insurance that paid a supplier** **Fix:** deduct inventories **and** prepaid expenses (SM 3.6).
- **Interest coverage on profit after tax** **Fix:** the numerator is EBIT, because interest is paid before tax and is tax-deductible.
- **The DSCR that forgot the principal** **Fix:** the denominator is interest + instalments due; "debt service" means both.
- **The preference shares that became equity** **Fix:** preference capital carries a fixed dividend, so it goes in the **numerator** with the debt; the denominator is equity share capital + reserves − losses.
- **"Higher current ratio, better company"** **Fix:** say that both too little and too much liquidity are bad (SM 3.5), and check the turnover ratios before praising it.

### Recap

- Current ratio = CA ÷ CL (norm 2:1); quick ratio = (CA − inventories − prepaid expenses) ÷ CL (norm 1:1); cash ratio = (cash and bank + marketable securities) ÷ CL.
- Basic defense interval = quick assets ÷ daily cash operating expenses (COGS + selling, admin and general − depreciation, ÷ days): days of survival without revenue.
- Net working capital = CA − CL. Too little and too much liquidity are both bad.
- Debt-equity = total outside liabilities (or long-term debt) ÷ shareholders' equity: the indicator of financial leverage. Say which debt you use.
- Capital gearing = (preference + debentures + other borrowings) ÷ (equity capital + reserves − losses); proprietary ratio = (equity + preference + reserves) ÷ total assets.
- Interest coverage = EBIT ÷ interest; DSCR = (EAT + non-cash expenses + interest) ÷ (interest + instalments), norm 2; fixed charges coverage = (EBIT + depreciation) ÷ (interest + repayment).
- With a current ratio above 1, equal additions to CA and CL lower it; equal payments raise it.
- CL = working capital ÷ (current ratio − 1). (The bank discounts "sab adjust ho jayega" to zero.)

## Lesson 2.2: Activity (turnover) ratios

*How hard the assets work: sales or cost of goods sold divided by total, fixed, net or current assets and working capital, and the inventory, debtors and creditors turnovers and their days.*

### Key idea

An activity ratio divides a year's sales (or cost of goods sold) by an asset. The higher it is, the more efficiently the asset is used; and 360 divided by a turnover gives the number of days the asset holds each rupee: days in stock, days to collect from debtors, days taken to pay creditors.

### Formats and formulas

*Reproduce these from memory, with the numerator that belongs to each denominator.*

| Ratio | Numerator | Denominator |
|---|---|---|
| Total assets turnover | Sales or COGS | Total assets |
| Fixed assets turnover | Sales or COGS | Fixed assets |
| Capital (net assets) turnover | Sales or COGS | Net assets |
| Current assets turnover | Sales or COGS | Current assets |
| Working capital turnover | Sales or COGS | Working capital |
| Inventory turnover | COGS or sales | Average inventory |
| Raw material inventory turnover | Raw material consumed | Average raw material stock |
| Receivables turnover | Credit sales | Average receivables |
| Payables turnover | Net credit purchases | Average payables |

**[formula] Asset-based turnovers**
$$\text{Turnover} = \frac{\text{Sales or COGS}}{\text{Asset base}}$$
Asset base = total assets, fixed assets, net assets (capital employed), current assets or working capital; their averages may be used (SM 3.14–3.16).

**[formula] Inventory turnover**
$$\text{Inventory turnover} \qquad = \frac{\text{COGS}}{\text{Average inventory}}$$
$$\text{Average inventory} \qquad = \frac{\text{Opening stock} + \text{Closing stock}}{2}$$
$$\text{Raw material inventory turnover} \qquad = \frac{\text{Raw material consumed}}{\text{Average raw material stock}}$$
ICAI prints the numerator as "Cost of Goods Sold / Sales" (SM 3.16): use COGS unless the question says "based on sales".

**[formula] Receivables turnover and average collection period**
$$\text{Receivables turnover} \qquad = \frac{\text{Credit sales}}{\text{Average receivables}}$$
$$\text{Average collection period} \qquad = \frac{\text{Average receivables}}{\text{Average daily credit sales}} \qquad = \frac{\text{360 days}}{\text{Receivables turnover}}$$
Average daily credit sales = credit sales ÷ number of days in the year (say 360); with months or weeks, use 12 or 52 in place of 360 (SM 3.17).

**[formula] Payables turnover and average payment period**
$$\text{Payables turnover} \qquad = \frac{\text{Net credit purchases}}{\text{Average payables}}$$
$$\text{Average payment period} \qquad = \frac{\text{Average payables}}{\text{Average daily credit purchases}} \qquad = \frac{\text{360 days}}{\text{Payables turnover}}$$
Net credit purchases are the year's (annual) credit purchases; 12 months or 52 weeks may replace 360 days (SM 3.17).

**[formula] Purchases from the trading account**
$$\text{Purchases} \qquad = \text{COGS} + \text{Closing} - \text{Opening stock}$$
Needed when a question gives sales, gross profit and stocks but not purchases.

**[note] ICAI's notes for turnover ratios (SM 3.18)**
1. Only selling and distribution expenses differentiate cost of goods sold (COGS) and cost of sales (COS); in their absence, COGS = cost of sales.
2. Cost of goods sold or cost of sales can be used to calculate turnover ratios, eliminating the profit part.
3. Averages of total, fixed, current or net assets or working capital can also be taken in the denominator; when average figures are available they may be preferred.
4. Ratios shall be calculated based on requirement and availability of information and may deviate from the original formulae; if required, assumptions should be given.

**[exam] The traps ICAI set**
- Purchases are not given: purchases = COGS + closing stock − opening stock (RTP September 2024 MCQ 2, RTP September 2026 MCQ 3).
- "Velocity" or "collection period" in months or days must be turned into a turnover (12 ÷ months, 360 ÷ days) before it is used, or used directly as a fraction of the year.
- The year length: 360 in most questions, 365 in September 2026 Q1(b), MTP Series II for May 2026 Q1(c) and the Study Material's Illustrations 1 and 5. Use what is given.
- "Based on sales" or "based on cost of goods sold" decides the numerator; read the brackets.

**[trick] Balance from days in one step**
Balance = annual flow × days ÷ 360 (or × months ÷ 12). Average debtors = credit sales × collection period ÷ 360; average stock = COGS × days in stock ÷ 360; average creditors = credit purchases × payment period ÷ 360. Conditions: the period is based on the same flow and on average balances, and the year length matches the question's.

### Answer frame

### "Discuss stock turnover ratio" and "ratios for operating efficiency"

The chapter's theory questions include "Discuss Stock Turnover ratio and Gearing ratio" and "Discuss the financial ratios for evaluating company performance on operating efficiency and liquidity position aspects" (SM 3.65). For a turnover ratio, write:

1. What it measures: the efficiency with which the firm manages and utilises the asset (activity ratios are asset management ratios).
2. The formula, with the numerator that matches the denominator (COGS for stock, credit sales for debtors, credit purchases for creditors) and the average balance.
3. The conversion to days or months.
4. The interpretation in ICAI's words (a high stock turnover is good for liquidity; a low one means stock stays on the shelf; a low debtors' turnover reflects liberal credit terms).
5. The comparison that gives it meaning: previous years, competitors, the industry.

For **operating efficiency** (SM 3.34): ratio analysis throws light on the degree of efficiency in the management and utilisation of assets; the activity ratios measure this operational efficiency; and the solvency of a firm is, in the ultimate analysis, dependent upon the sales revenues generated by the use of its assets, total as well as its components.

### Three answers to "calculate the average collection period"

**[cols] **Loses marks****

"ACP = 250 ÷ 1,380 × 360 = 65 days."

Closing debtors used although the opening balance is given; no average, no working.
---
**Passes**

"ACP = 195 ÷ 1,380 × 360 = 50.9 days."

Right, but 195 appears from nowhere.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: stock at cost, sales at selling price** **Fix:** use cost of goods sold for inventory unless the question says "based on sales".
- **Total sales in the debtors turnover** **Fix:** credit sales ÷ average receivables; take total sales only when credit sales are not given, and say so.
- **Cost of goods sold in the creditors turnover** **Fix:** derive purchases (COGS + closing stock − opening stock) and use the credit part of them.
- **The closing balance that pretended to be an average** **Fix:** average whenever you can; use the closing balance only when nothing else is given, and state it.

### Recap

- Activity ratios (efficiency, performance, turnover; "asset management ratios") = sales or COGS ÷ an asset, in "times".
- Total assets, fixed assets, capital (net assets), current assets and working capital turnover: sales or COGS ÷ that base; averages may be used.
- A very high working capital turnover means more working capital will be needed.
- Inventory turnover = COGS ÷ average inventory; raw material turnover = materials consumed ÷ average raw material stock.
- Debtors turnover = credit sales ÷ average receivables; collection period = 360 ÷ turnover (or average receivables ÷ daily credit sales).
- Creditors turnover = credit purchases ÷ average payables; payment period = 360 ÷ turnover. Purchases = COGS + closing − opening stock.
- Balance = annual flow × days ÷ 360. (A full godown is only impressive if it keeps emptying.)

## Lesson 2.3: Profitability and market ratios, and the DuPont analysis

*Profit as a share of sales, as a return on assets, capital employed and equity, per share and against the share price; and the DuPont split of ROE into margin, turnover and leverage.*

### Key idea

Profitability ratios relate profit to sales (margins), to the money invested (ROA, ROCE, ROE), to each share (EPS, DPS) and to the share's price (P/E, yields, market-to-book). Return on investment = margin × turnover, and return on equity = net profit margin × asset turnover × equity multiplier: two companies with the same ROE can be very different businesses.

### Formats and formulas

*Reproduce these from memory. Every "× 100" ratio is a percentage; the others are in times or rupees.*

**[formula] Margins on sales**
$$\text{Gross profit ratio} \qquad = \frac{\text{Gross profit}}{\text{Sales}} \times 100 \qquad \text{Net profit ratio} = \frac{\text{EAT}}{\text{Sales}} \times 100$$
$$\text{Pre-tax profit ratio} = \frac{\text{EBT}}{\text{Sales}} \times 100 \qquad \text{Operating profit ratio} = \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Sales}} \times 100$$
Operating profit = sales − COGS − operating expenses (SM 3.20–3.21).

**[formula] Expenses ratios**
$$\text{COGS ratio} = \frac{\text{COGS}}{\text{Sales}} \times 100 \qquad \text{Operating expenses ratio} \qquad = \frac{A + S}{\text{Sales}} \times 100$$
$$\text{Operating ratio} \qquad = \frac{\text{COGS} + \text{Operating expenses}}{\text{Sales}} \times 100$$
$$\text{Financial expenses ratio} \qquad = \frac{\text{Financial expenses}}{\text{Sales}} \times 100$$
A = administrative expenses; S = selling and distribution overheads. Financial expenses exclude taxes, loss due to theft, goods destroyed by fire etc. (SM 3.21).

**[formula] Return on investment**
$$\text{ROI} = \frac{\text{Return}}{\text{Investment}} \times 100 \qquad = \frac{\text{Return}}{\text{Sales}} \times \frac{\text{Sales}}{\text{Investment}}$$
(SM 3.22)

**[formula] Return on assets**
$$\text{ROA} = \frac{\text{PAT}}{\text{Average total assets}} \qquad \text{or} \qquad \frac{\text{PAT} + \text{Interest}}{\text{Average total assets}}$$
$$\text{ROTA} = \frac{\text{EBIT}(1 - t)}{\text{Average total assets}} \qquad \text{RONA} = \frac{\text{EBIT}(1 - t)}{\text{Average net assets}}$$
PAT = net profit after taxes; t = tax rate. Average tangible assets or average fixed assets may replace total assets in the first two; total assets are sometimes used instead of average assets (SM 3.23).

**[formula] Return on capital employed**
$$\text{ROCE (pre-tax)} \qquad = \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Capital employed}} \times 100$$
$$\text{ROCE (post-tax)} \qquad = \frac{\text{EBIT}(1 - t)}{\text{Capital employed}} \times 100$$
Sometimes (PAT + interest) ÷ capital employed. Capital employed = total assets − current liabilities = fixed assets + working capital = equity + long-term debt; include intangibles (goodwill, patents, trade marks), exclude fictitious assets (deferred expenses); use average capital employed if available (SM 3.24, 3.30).

**[formula] Return on equity and the DuPont model**
$$\text{ROE} \qquad = \frac{\text{PAT} - \text{Preference dividend}}{\text{Equity shareholders funds}} \times 100$$
$$\text{ROE} = \text{Net profit margin} \qquad \times \text{Asset turnover} \qquad \times \text{Equity multiplier}$$
Net profit margin = net income ÷ sales; asset (investment, capital) turnover = sales ÷ investment, assets or capital; equity multiplier = investment, assets or capital ÷ shareholders' equity (SM 3.25–3.26).

**[formula] Owner's ratios**
$$\text{EPS} = \frac{\text{Equity earnings}}{\text{No. of equity shares}} \qquad \text{DPS} = \frac{\text{Equity dividend}}{\text{No. of equity shares}}$$
$$\text{Dividend pay-out ratio} = \frac{\text{DPS}}{\text{EPS}}$$
Equity earnings = net profit available to equity shareholders; equity dividend = total dividend paid to equity shareholders; both are divided by the number of equity shares outstanding. In the absence of preference dividend, PAT is the earnings available to equity shareholders (SM 3.27–3.28, 3.30).

**[formula] Market ratios**
$$\text{P/E ratio} = \frac{\text{MPS}}{\text{EPS}} \qquad \text{Earnings yield} = \frac{\text{EPS}}{\text{MPS}} \times 100$$
$$\text{Dividend yield} = \frac{\text{DPS}}{\text{MPS}} \times 100 \qquad \text{or} \qquad \frac{\text{Dividend} \pm \text{Price change}}{\text{Initial share price}} \times 100$$
$$\text{MV/BV} = \frac{\text{Share price}}{\text{Book value per share}} \qquad \text{Q ratio} \qquad = \frac{\text{Market value of a company}}{\text{Replacement cost of assets}}$$
Share price = the average or the closing share price; book value per share = net worth ÷ number of equity shares; price change = the change in share price over the period. The Q ratio is also written as market value of equity and liabilities ÷ estimated replacement cost of assets (SM 3.28–3.29).

**[exam] The traps ICAI set**
- EPS and ROE use profit **after** preference dividend; P/E then follows from EPS.
- Operating ratio and operating profit ratio add up to 100% (May 2024 Q1(a)).
- "Return on assets (after tax)" in September 2025 Q1(b) meant EBIT(1 − t) ÷ assets; name your basis.
- In DuPont MCQs a distractor may invert the multiplier (equity ÷ assets): 12.5% × 1.5 × 0.625 = 11.72% is one of the wrong options in September 2024 MCQ 8, whose answer is 12.5% × 1.5 × 1.6 = 30%.

**[trick] Three shortcuts from ICAI's identities**
ROE = ROA (on PAT) × equity multiplier; EPS ÷ book value per share = ROE; earnings yield = 1 ÷ P/E. Conditions: no preference capital or dividend (otherwise use profit after preference dividend and equity shareholders' funds throughout), and the same profit figure and the same balance sheet date on both sides.

### Answer frame

### "Discuss the DuPont model" (SM 3.65, theory questions 4 and 6)

1. Origin: created in 1919 by a finance executive at E.I. Du Pont de Nemours and Co.; used worldwide as the basis of the components of ROE.
2. The model: ROE = net profit margin × asset (investment) turnover × equity multiplier.
3. Each component, its formula and meaning: net profit margin (profit for each rupee of revenue; a safety cushion); asset turnover (how effectively assets are converted into sales; inversely related to margin); equity multiplier (financial leverage; shows what portion of ROE results from debt).
4. Use: by examining each input, the sources of a company's ROE can be discovered and compared with competitors; a high ROE from leverage is less attractive than one from margins and turnover.
5. A two-line numerical illustration.

### "Discuss any three ratios computed for investment analysis" (SM 3.65, theory question 1)

The Study Material's answer key points to paragraph 3.4.2, the return on investment ratios. For each of ROA, ROCE and ROE: definition, formula with the components named, interpretation (ROCE above the borrowing rate; ROE as an indicator of profitability and potential growth), and who uses it.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the preference dividend that went to the equity shareholders** **Fix:** deduct the preference dividend first; only what is left is available to equity shareholders.
- **The return that did not match its investment** **Fix:** returns before interest (EBIT, EBIT(1 − t), PAT + interest) go over assets or capital employed; returns after interest and preference dividend go over equity shareholders' funds.
- **DuPont with two engines** **Fix:** that product is a return on assets; ROE needs the equity multiplier too.
- **Fictitious assets in capital employed** **Fix:** include intangibles such as goodwill, patents and trade marks; exclude fictitious assets (SM 3.24).

### Recap

- Gross profit, operating profit (EBIT) and net profit ratios = that profit ÷ sales × 100; operating ratio = (COGS + operating expenses) ÷ sales, and operating ratio + operating profit ratio = 100%.
- ROI = margin × investment turnover; ROA = PAT (or PAT + interest, or EBIT(1 − t)) ÷ average assets.
- ROCE = EBIT (pre-tax) or EBIT(1 − t) (post-tax) ÷ capital employed; it should exceed the borrowing rate; intangibles in, fictitious assets out.
- ROE = (PAT − preference dividend) ÷ equity shareholders' funds.
- DuPont: ROE = net profit margin × asset turnover × equity multiplier (assets ÷ equity); the multiplier shows the part of ROE that comes from debt.
- EPS = earnings for equity ÷ equity shares; DPS; pay-out = DPS ÷ EPS.
- P/E = MPS ÷ EPS; dividend yield = DPS ÷ MPS (or dividend ± price change ÷ initial price); earnings yield = EPS ÷ MPS; MV/BV; Q ratio = market value ÷ replacement cost (1 = equilibrium).
- Same ROE, different businesses: ask which engine produced it. (Borrowed money sends a bill every month.)

## Lesson 2.4: Building financial statements from ratios; limitations of ratios

*Rebuilding the trading and profit and loss account and balance sheet from a set of ratios and one rupee figure; who uses ratios and for what; and ICAI's eight limitations of financial ratios.*

### Key idea

Rebuilding statements from ratios is solving equations: start from a rupee figure, use each ratio to find one new figure, solve two ratios together when they share two unknowns, call sales "x" when no chain starts from the figure you have, and let total assets = total liabilities fix the balancing figure. Then remember that a ratio, however exact, is a clue for an expert, not a conclusion.

**[formula] The identities behind every rebuild**
$$\text{Total assets} = \text{Total liabilities} \qquad \text{GP} = \text{Sales} - \text{COGS}$$
$$\text{COGS} = \text{Opening stock} + \text{Purchases} \qquad - \text{Closing stock}$$
$$\text{Net worth} = \text{Share capital} + \text{Reserves} \qquad \text{Average} = \frac{\text{Opening} + \text{Closing}}{2}$$
Net worth here means equity shareholders' funds; total liabilities include capital unless the question says otherwise.

**[formula] Balances from ratios**
$$\text{Debtors} = \frac{\text{Credit sales} \times \text{Days}}{360} \qquad \text{Stock} = \frac{\text{COGS}}{\text{Stock turnover}}$$
$$\text{CL} = \frac{\text{Working capital}}{\text{Current ratio} - 1} \qquad \text{Fixed assets} = \frac{\text{Sales}}{\text{Fixed assets turnover}}$$
Days = the collection period in days. Use the basis the question gives (sales or COGS; closing or average; 360 or 365 days).

**[exam] The traps ICAI set**
- A ratio on cost of goods sold used on sales (or the reverse); gross profit given on cost.
- An opening stock that forces the average-stock route (Kshipra).
- An absolute figure that only enters through the totals, forcing "let sales be x" (the Study Material's Practical Problem 11).
- Items that must be classified before the balance sheet balances: outstanding interest as a current liability, preference capital outside debt-equity, bills receivable with debtors.

**[trick] DISWANDI: the eight limitations in ICAI's order**
**D**iversified product lines, **I**nflation, **S**easonal factors, **W**indow dressing, **A**ccounting policies and period, **N**o standard set of ratios, **D**ifficulty to generalise, **I**nter-related ratios. The magic wand that does not work: ratios give clues, not conclusions.

### Answer frame

### "Explain briefly the limitations of financial ratios"

1. One line for each of the eight limitations, in ICAI's order, with its explanation (aggregate data of diverse divisions; historical cost under inflation; seasonal stock and year-end figures; year-end window dressing of popular ratios; non-comparable policies and periods; no standard set of ratios; a high or low ratio not good or bad in itself; inter-related ratios and multivariate analysis).
2. The closing line: financial ratios provide clues but not conclusions; they are tools only in the hands of experts, as there is no standard ready-made interpretation.
3. For a case ("Comment", "Advise"), apply each relevant limitation to the facts, then conclude what the analyst should do.

### Common mistakes (title and fix)

- **Hall of Shame: the balance sheet that balanced by accident** **Fix:** find every figure a ratio gives first; only the one figure no ratio gives may balance the sheet, and recompute two ratios at the end.
- **Gross profit on cost used as gross profit on sales** **Fix:** convert first: 25 ÷ (100 + 25) = 20% of sales, so COGS = 80% of sales.
- **Closing stock where the ratio uses the average** **Fix:** when an opening stock is given, work with the average, (opening + closing) ÷ 2; when the average is what you find, closing = 2 × average − opening.
- **The limitations answer that listed but did not apply** **Fix:** name the limitation, quote the fact from the case that shows it, and say what the analyst should do.

### Recap

- Method: sort anchors from ratios; one ratio, one new figure; two ratios for two unknowns; "let sales be x" when the anchor enters only through the totals; balancing figure last; verify two ratios.
- CL = working capital ÷ (current ratio − 1); with inventory I, CL = I ÷ (current ratio − quick ratio).
- Closing stock = 2 × average stock − opening stock; GP g% on cost = g ÷ (100 + g) of sales.
- Present the rebuilt statements in the asked format with every balancing figure labelled.
- ICAI's six uses of ratio analysis: liquidity, long-term solvency, operating efficiency, overall profitability, inter-firm comparison, budgeting.
- Horizontal analysis compares years; vertical analysis expresses one year's items as % of gross sales or total assets.
- Eight limitations (DISWANDI): diversified lines, inflation, seasonal factors, window dressing, accounting policies and period, no standard set, difficulty to generalise, inter-related ratios.
- Ratios provide clues, not conclusions. (Even after the masala chai.)
