# PassBook, Out Loud — CA Inter Group II chapter-wise revision (binding spec)

A 40-episode Hinglish audio revision of CA Intermediate **Group II** (new scheme, January 2027 exams), one episode per syllabus
chapter, narrated by **Arjun** (Higgs Audio 3, Hindi voice `dm_hi`). About **12 minutes** per episode, about 8 hours in all.
Listened to by ear alone (on a bus, on a walk), in the last weeks before the exams: P4 Costing on 8 January, P5 Audit on
10 January, P6 (6A FM + 6B SM) on 12 January 2027.

| Episodes | Paper | Chapters |
|---|---|---|
| P4-01 … P4-15 | Paper 4 Cost and Management Accounting | 15 |
| P5-01 … P5-11 | Paper 5 Auditing and Ethics | 11 |
| P6A-01 … P6A-09 | Paper 6A Financial Management | 9 |
| P6B-01 … P6B-05 | Paper 6B Strategic Management | 5 |

## 1. Sources — non-negotiable

- **Facts come only from the episode's brief** `~/CA_PERSONAL/ca_revision/briefs/<EP>.md` and, when a point needs more detail, from the
  PassBook lesson files the brief names (`~/CA_PERSONAL/ca_passbook/content/subjects/<paper>/<id>.md`). Those lessons were written from and
  reviewed against the ICAI Study Material (July 2024 edition, Reprint August 2025, applicable for January 2027).
- **Never add a fact from memory** — no section number, SA paragraph, threshold, rate, limit, date or formula that is not in the
  brief or the lesson. If a point is not there, leave it out. A wrong number in an exam-revision audio costs marks.
- No web research, and never any Amazon-internal source or tool (builder-mcp, internal search, wikis, Slack). This is public
  ICAI material only.
- ICAI's definitions and standard wording (SA titles, definitions, the "true and fair" opinion wording, format headings) are
  spoken **in English, as ICAI words them** in the brief, inside a Hindi sentence that explains them. That is what the student
  must write in the exam.
- A long ICAI definition that does not fit one 32-word line: use the shortened ICAI wording the brief gives, or carry the rest
  into the next line as its own complete sentence ("ICAI आगे जोड़ता है कि …"). Never paraphrase a defined term into a different
  term.
- The briefs' common-mistake titles sometimes say "Hall of Shame": never use that phrase in a script.
- Where a lesson marks a fact as version-dependent (amended thresholds, CARO clauses, Companies Act limits), say it plainly and
  add once per episode that the student should check it against the Study Material and the Statutory Update for January 2027.

## 2. The listener

A CA Intermediate student who has studied Group II once and now revises. They know the terms but forget the exact formats,
formulas, the order of points and the traps. They want, for each chapter: what the chapter is really about, the definitions and
formats ICAI expects, every formula said clearly in words, how ICAI asks it (MCQ, numerical, distinguish, case), the common
mistakes, and a recap they can replay the night before the paper.

## 3. Style — the user's rules (same as AWS, Out Loud v2, which the user approved)

1. **Write prose, not fragments.** Every line is one or two complete, grammatical sentences of about 12–32 words. Never a
   phrase, a list of nouns, or a sentence that only makes sense with the line before it. No "चलो देखते हैं", no "बस इतना ही".
2. **Paragraphs carry the thinking.** Lines group into paragraphs of 3–6 lines, separated by a blank line. Each paragraph makes
   one point: the idea, how it works, and what it means for the exam answer. Paragraphs follow each other logically, like a
   well-written revision note read aloud.
3. **One continuous storyline.** The whole series follows **Tapri Tea Co. Ltd** and its people (§5) through one financial
   year: Ritu costs its products (P4), Iyer & Associates audit it (P5), Dev Malhotra finances it (P6A), Kesar and Ananya plan
   its strategy (P6B). Each episode opens by connecting to where the previous one ended. **Never a time-of-day opener** (no
   सुबह, रात, शाम, "आठ बजे"), never a dramatic cold open. The story is the frame for examples; revision is the substance, and
   a story beat never displaces a definition, a formula or a trap.
4. **Stay on the thread.** No tangents. If a detail does not help the student answer an ICAI question on this chapter, leave
   it out. Cover the most-asked sub-topics (the brief's frequency table) first and most fully.
5. **Numbers only when they matter.** Thresholds, rates, days and limits that ICAI tests are said with their meaning in the same
   sentence ("नब्बे दिन से ज़्यादा overdue हो, तो loan account NPA बन जाता है"). No arithmetic of more than three short steps;
   no worked examples read out line by line. Give a formula, then what each term means, then one sentence on how to use it.
6. **Humour sometimes.** Two or three light, natural moments per episode at most, inside complete sentences, never inside a
   definition or formula. No sarcasm, no running gags.
7. **Calm, documentary narration.** Emotion tags on about a quarter to a third of lines (contemplation, determination,
   enthusiasm, contentment, mostly); surprise, laughter and sigh at most once each per episode. No pause-and-think breaks.
8. **Natural spoken Hinglish**, never textbook Hindi ("मतलब" not "तात्पर्य", "इस्तेमाल" not "उपयोग"). Address the listener as
   **आप** throughout (never तुम).

A model paragraph (match this register):

> Tapri की Indore unit हर साल छत्तीस हज़ार kilo Assam चाय खरीदती है, और Ritu के सामने सवाल ये है कि एक बार में कितना order किया जाए।
> बहुत बड़ा order देने से godown में stock पड़ा रहता है और carrying cost बढ़ती है, जबकि बहुत छोटे orders देने से हर साल ordering cost बढ़ती जाती है।
> Economic Order Quantity वो order size है जिस पर ये दोनों costs बराबर हो जाती हैं, और इसी point पर total cost सबसे कम होती है।
> इसका formula है square root of two A O by C, जहाँ A साल की demand है, O एक order की cost है, और C एक unit को साल भर रखने की carrying cost है।
> Exam में सबसे आम गलती ये होती है कि carrying cost percentage में दी हो और students उसे unit price से multiply करना भूल जाते हैं।

## 4. Episode structure

- `## Introduction: <Hindi>` — one paragraph of 3–5 lines (the series opener P4-01 and the paper openers P5-01, P6A-01 and
  P6B-01 may use up to 8 lines in one or two paragraphs): the welcome ("नमस्ते, मैं हूँ Arjun, और ये है PassBook, Out Loud,
  CA Inter Group Two की chapter-wise revision।"), the paper and chapter ("आज Paper four का chapter three, Material Cost"),
  a link to where the previous episode ended (P4-01: introduce Tapri, Ritu and the series instead; P5-01, P6A-01 and P6B-01:
  open the new paper and link from the last chapter of the previous paper), and what the listener will be able to answer
  by the end. Mention once how much of this chapter ICAI asks (from the brief's frequency numbers, in words).
- **2–4 topic chapters** `## <English topic>: <Hindi>` covering the chapter's sub-topics, most-asked first in weight (keep
  the syllabus order unless the logic needs otherwise). For each sub-topic: the core idea, ICAI's definition or format in
  English, every formula in words, the decision rule, and where it connects to the next idea. Open each chapter with a
  complete sentence that links it to the previous one (the title itself is not spoken).
- `## Exam angle: <Hindi>` — 2 paragraphs: how ICAI asks this chapter (which sub-topics come most, MCQs vs numericals vs
  distinguish vs case questions, typical marks), the two or three questions ICAI has repeated (described, not read verbatim),
  the traps and common mistakes from the brief with their fixes, and how to frame a full-marks answer (the answer frame).
- `## Summary: <Hindi>` — one recap paragraph (3–6 lines) in prose as a sixty-second revision, then a closing paragraph of
  exactly two lines: one that leads into the next episode's chapter (by its name in `briefs/INDEX.json`), and the sign-off that
  names "PassBook, Out Loud" and "Arjun". The very last episode (P6B-05)
  closes the series and wishes the student well for 12 January.

## 6. Length (the 8-hour total depends on it)

Arjun reads this prose at about **139 spoken words per minute**. Target **T = 1,700 spoken words** per episode (≈ 12 min).
Linter hard window **1,450–1,750**; aim **1,550–1,700**. **Long chapters** (seven or more sub-topics: P4-01, P4-03, P4-14,
P5-03, P5-04, P5-05, P5-08, P6A-09) may go to **1,850** (aim 1,700–1,850).
Every sub-topic gets at least its core idea in one or two lines. A sub-topic ICAI asked in three documents or fewer may get only
that; the most-asked sub-topics (the brief's frequency table) get the depth. Say in `notes/<EP>.md` what you left out.

## 7. Format rules (the TTS engine reads exactly what you write)

- First line: `# <EP> · <slug> · <Hindi title>` (e.g. `# P4-03 · material-cost · Material Cost: खरीद से issue तक`).
- One line = one voiced chunk: one or two complete sentences, **12–32 words** (linter error below 8 or above 34). Blank line
  between paragraphs. `## ` starts a chapter (title not spoken). `#` lines are comments.
- **Spell abbreviations out** as they should be heard: "Tapri Tea Company Limited", "Tapri Cold Chain Private Limited",
  "Iyer and Associates" (never "Co.", "Ltd", "Pvt", "&"). Initialisms ICAI students say as letters stay letters (EOQ, NPV,
  WACC, ABC, ROI, CARO, SA, NPA, GST, ERP).
- A run of English words inside a Hindi line (a list of ICAI's points) should stay under about sixteen words; split a long
  list over two lines, each a complete sentence ("पहले तीन factors हैं …", "बाकी factors हैं …"). An ICAI definition quoted
  in English is the exception: keep it whole (the linter only warns).
- Hindi in **Devanagari**; accounting, audit and finance terms in **English Latin letters** (cost sheet, prime cost, variance,
  EOQ, materiality, audit evidence, NPV, WACC, working capital, BCG matrix …). Names: "Tapri", "TapriNow", "PassBook", "Arjun",
  "Iyer & Associates" in Latin (write the ampersand as "and": "Iyer and Associates"); people in Devanagari as in §5.
- **No digits and no symbols** in spoken lines (`% / = < > * & # @ _ + | \ { } [ ] ~ ^ $ ₹`). Amounts and Hindi numbers in Hindi
  words ("डेढ़ करोड़ रुपये", "पाँच प्रतिशत", "नब्बे दिन"). Inside an English name, numbers are English words:
  - Standards on Auditing: "SA two hundred", "SA three hundred", "SA seven hundred" for round hundreds; otherwise in pairs:
    "SA three fifteen", "SA three twenty", "SA five thirty", "SA seven oh one", "SA seven oh five", "SA seven oh six",
    "SA two forty", "SA five sixty", "SA two sixty-five". "SQC one". "CARO twenty twenty".
  - Sections: "section one forty-three", "section one forty-one, sub-section three". Papers: "Paper four", "Paper six A".
  - Formulas in words: "square root of two A O by C", "contribution divided by sales", "P by V ratio", "EBIT minus interest",
    "one plus r to the power n". Fractions as "by", products as "into", powers as "to the power".
- Never read a table, a format or an account literally; describe it in words ("cost sheet में ऊपर से नीचे पहले prime cost,
  फिर works cost …").
- Higgs tags at line start only: `<|emotion:X|>` with X one of affection, amusement, awe, contemplation, contentment,
  determination, enthusiasm, fear, helplessness, pride, relief, sadness, surprise, confusion; about 25–35 % of lines; never more
  than three lines in a row with the same emotion. `<|prosody:speed_slow|>` at most once per chapter, for a formula or a
  definition the student must memorise.
