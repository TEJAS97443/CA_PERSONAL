## Facts I am less than certain about (with the page checked)
1. **Consumer behaviour: three domains or four (SM 2.40, checked on the rendered page).** The SM says "the following three conceptual domains" and then gives four headings: external influences, internal influences, decision making and post-decision processes. Its figure (SM 2.41) shows three boxes, and it calls post-decision "the final phase in the decision-making process". MTP January 2026 Series II Q1(a) says "four major conceptual domains". The lesson notes this neutrally and advises writing all four headings.
2. **The rivalry "industry leader" condition (SM 2.31–2.32).** The SM lists the industry leader first among the cut-throat conditions, but explains that a *strong* leader *discourages* price wars. I wrote the condition as "no strong leader to discipline price-cutters, or discipline weakening as rivals multiply". That is my reading. It matches the wording of ICAI's suggested answer (January 2025 Q8(a); RTP January 2026 Q10).
3. **The SM's closing definition of value creation (SM 2.38)** ("the value consumer wants to pay, over and above the price … is called value creation") describes the same gap that the figure labels "customer's surplus". I quoted it as written, without comment. The "profitable pricing band" running from cost up to value was read from the rendered figure (SM 2.36).
4. **Two MTP keys go beyond the SM text.** Highest marketing spend per unit sold is introduction (MTP January 2025 Series II, B(iii)). R&D that rejuvenates a saturated product is maturity (MTP May 2025 Series I, A(ii)). The lesson cites both as ICAI MTP keys, not as SM statements.
5. **One-force-per-fact classifications are ICAI's choices** and are cited as such. RTP September 2026 Q9 puts in-house delivery by e-commerce firms under the threat of new entrants and a fuel price rise under supplier power. MTP September 2024 Series I puts "Vocal for Local" under the threat of substitutes.
6. **MTP January 2026 Series I MCQ 1(A)(ii)** ("numerous players offering functionally similar, low-priced products") is keyed "(b) Bargaining Power of Suppliers" in the text file. Rivalry, option (a), looks right. Every 1(A) answer in that file reads (b), so this may be an extraction error. Not cited; worth checking on the PDF.
7. **Brand identity in the BrewBucks premix example.** I say it is a weaker barrier for a frequently bought, low-cost product. That is my application of SM 2.30, not an SM statement.
8. **The experience curve numbers are invented example data**: 10% lower unit cost per doubling of cumulative output, and 20% in the quiz. The generic curve's caption says the SM gives no rate.
9. **The counts in the Exam angle sections** (17, 23 and 16 of the 32 new-scheme documents) come from grep plus reading. PYQ question papers are OCR scans, so an item could have been missed.

## What got in the way
- **The phone probe does not name what scrolls.** It reports `scrolls-inside=N` but not which element. I had to find the cause by trial: the five-forces `graph` at `w: 420` scrolled inside the 390 px column, and `w: 360` fixed it. The guide's "w of about 450 or less" is too loose for graphs. Suggestion: have the probe print the deepest scrolling element, as it already does for page and clipped overflow.
- **`preview_lesson.py` writes a `-full.png` that is 30,000 px tall and untrimmed.** Only the slices are trimmed. To stay inside the image budget I located diagrams with a low-resolution contact sheet of the full PNG and then viewed crops.
- **Exemplar previews.** To save images I read the two exemplar sources and probed `aud.report.sa705` at phone width, but did not view their rendered previews. I used about 30 images in total.
