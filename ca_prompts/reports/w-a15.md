- **Consistency with the npa lab:** the due date counts as day 1, so a term loan is an NPA on day 91. D1, D2 and D3 start 12, 24 and 48 months after the NPA date. Erosion below 50% sends a younger NPA to D1. Borrower-wise classification takes the worse category.
- **Less certain:**

  2. SM 10.27's first out-of-order test ("remains continuously in excess") gives no number of days. Under the hood says so and points to the lab's 90-day convention. No worked conclusion depends on it.
  3. The SM gives SMA-0 only as "accounts showing stress signals". I gave no day range for SMA-0. (ICAI's May 2026 MCQ 12 treats 20 and 30 days overdue as SMA-0.)
  4. The interest-free festival advance as an "employer" advance is my illustration. The SM only distinguishes advances given "as an employer and not as a banker" (SM 10.33).
  5. ICAI's RTP Jan 2025 Q19 treats a provision above the required rate as "incorrect". My example likewise shows the unneeded provision on the FD-backed overdraft as an excess.

## Originality
None of the worked examples or quiz questions reuse an ICAI scenario. Where a concept matches an ICAI question, the facts, numbers and twist differ:
- **Provisioning table:** worked from NPA dates, with the branch's errors to find.
- **Drawing power:** includes LC acceptances and a stock statement more than three months old.
- **Theory cases:** staff housing loan, employer advance, long-duration crop.
- **Revenue:** seven-item income case, including a recovery paid from a fresh facility and commission on bills for collection; a weighted-average deposit-rate test; a "which Form B head" case.
- **Auditor:** a draft-report case (CARO, section 143, unaudited branches) and a confidentiality-versus-RBI-referral case.

Two income items share concepts with PYQ May 2026 MCQ 10 (interest on FD-backed advances; discount on a bill spanning the year-end), but with different facts and numbers inside a wider case.
