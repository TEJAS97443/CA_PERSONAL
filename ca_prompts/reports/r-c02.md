## cma.intro.classify (9 fixes)
1. **Direct expenses list was incomplete.** The lesson listed only part of the SM 6.4 list. The full list also has **cost of utilities such as power and fuel**, hire charges of specific equipment, patents, and design or drawing. I added the SM 6.4 test ("directly traced … in an economically feasible manner") and the complete list.
2. **The Formats table taught the wrong order.** It told students to "reproduce from memory" the SM 1.27 chart order, which puts administration overheads before cost of goods sold. The specimen cost sheet (SM 6.7–6.8) puts **general administration after cost of goods sold**, and only production-related administration before it. I replaced the table with the SM 6.8 order (no stocks) and added one line explaining the SM 1.27 chart.
3. **Example 1's working note had the wrong cost of goods sold.** It said "cost of goods sold = works cost + administration = ₹32,85,000". Under SM 6.7–6.8, with no stocks, cost of goods sold = cost of production = works cost = **₹31,05,000**, and the company secretary's salary is added after it. I fixed the note and relabelled the statement rows to match.
4. **Example 1 item (vi) clashed with SM 6.4.** "Factory electricity" was classed as an overhead, but SM 6.4 and Illustration 6.17 treat power and fuel used in manufacture as a direct expense. I reworded it as electricity for factory lighting, fans and the canteen, which is clearly a production overhead (SM 1.26), and added a note explaining both treatments.
5. **Example 2 called shut-down costs relevant.** Part (iii) listed the ₹1,90,000 (rent, depreciation, insurance and salary during the shutdown) as a relevant cost of replacing the fryer. These costs are incurred whether or not the fryer is replaced, so they are not differential, and depreciation is not even a cash outflow. They now appear among the figures to ignore, with the right reasoning: the real cost of the closure is the contribution lost, which the case does not give.
6. **Wrong citation in an exam trap.** "Overtime caused by a customer's urgency" was cited as an SM 6.4 direct expense. The correct rule is SM 3.18: overtime premium worked at the customer's request is charged to the job directly. Fixed.
7. **Unsupported claim in an Answer frame table.** The row "Reported separately, not charged to the manager's performance" had no source. I reworded it to SM 1.22: a cost centre is held accountable for the costs under its control.
8. The Recap bullet and the flow caption now match SM 6.8.
9. Three display formulas overflowed at phone width. I added `\qquad` wrap points.

Checked and correct: the six bases and their ICAI order; the element and overhead definitions; all 18 decision-making costs (SM 1.34–1.36); controllability and normality wording; the SM 4.3–4.5 overhead classification; the exam-angle claims. The Jan 2026 answer accepting "Semi-variable/Variable" for delivery vans is confirmed, and so is the Jul 2021 examiners' comment.

## cma.intro.behaviour (8 fixes)
1. **High-low method misattributed.** The lesson said only "ICAI's illustration" uses sales value. In fact SM 1.30 *defines* the method with sales value ("the rate of variable cost in relation to sales value"). I reworded the step, the formula note, the trap and the flashcard, and noted that the same arithmetic works with units or hours.
2. **A pro rata cost was called a step cost.** The Exam angle filed MTP May 2026 S2 Q5(a) under "Steps". Its wording is "₹60,000 for every 20% increase in capacity **or proportionately**", which is pro rata: ICAI's answer charges ₹90,000 for going from 60% to 90% capacity, i.e. ₹1,00,000 + ₹90,000 = ₹1,90,000 for eight months. I fixed the bullet. I also added a short teaching paragraph and a trap, and fixed the mistake wording: "or part thereof" (SM 8.18) means whole steps, while "or proportionately" means pro rata.
3. "ICAI's favourite, the bus problem" was an unsupported frequency claim. Softened.
4. **Quiz Q2 copied an SM illustration's twist.** It repeated SM Illustration 2 (analytical method with a rise in activity). I changed it to a 25% fall; the new answer is ₹4,32,000.
5. **Quiz Q5 gave its answer away.** It reused the worked example's power figures, so the answer ₹2,85,000 was visible in the table above it. I gave it new numbers: a water bill, answer ₹1,29,000, with distractors computed from the common errors.
6. **The title showed literal quotes in the app.** The frontmatter `title: "…"` was rendered with the quote marks. I removed them.
7. Five display formulas overflowed at 390 px. I added wrap points and shorter wording.
8. Varied the position of the correct option in an MCQ.
