## In plain words

### Same biscuits, different audit

It is October, and Iyer & Associates hold their first planning meeting for the 2025-26 audit of Tapri Tea Co. Ltd. Kesar has dropped in, uninvited, with a box of biscuits.

> **Kesar:** Lakshmi-ji, last year you finished us in six weeks. Same team, same weeks, same biscuits. Why a planning meeting?
> **Lakshmi:** Because this year you opened twelve cafés in Pune, moved all billing to the new delivery app and changed your inventory software in August.
> **Bunty:** So we just add twelve cafés to last year's file, ma'am?
> **Lakshmi:** No, Bunty. We decide again what matters, who looks at it, and when. And we keep deciding until the day we sign.

That is **planning an audit**. Before it plans anything, the firm asks three questions: should we continue with this client, are we still independent of it, and has the client agreed the terms? These are the **preliminary engagement activities**.

Then come two layers of planning. The **overall audit strategy** is the big picture: which areas need the most attention (the delivery-app revenue, the new inventory system), who audits them (an experienced senior, an IT expert), and when (an interim visit in January, cut-off checks on 31 March). The **audit plan** is the detail beneath it: which procedures, when, and on how many items.

Neither is framed and hung on the wall. When something unexpected turns up, both change, and the auditor records what changed and why.

::: analogy The captain's game plan
Before a Test series the captain picks the squad, decides who opens, and when the spinners will play: that is the overall audit strategy. Before each session he sets the field for each batter and decides the bowling changes: that is the audit plan. When the pitch starts turning on day two, both change, and the coach's notebook says why.
:::

::: key
The overall audit strategy sets the scope, timing and direction of the audit and guides the audit plan; the audit plan sets the nature, timing and extent of the audit procedures. Planning is a continual and iterative process that ends only with the audit, and both the strategy and the plan remain the auditor's responsibility.
:::

## How it works

### 1. The objective, and why planning pays

SA 300, "Planning an Audit of Financial Statements": the objective of the auditor is ==to plan the audit so that it will be performed in an effective manner== (SM 2.4). Adequate planning benefits the audit of financial statements in several ways, including (SM 2.4):

1. Helping the auditor to devote appropriate attention to important areas of the audit.
2. Helping the auditor identify and resolve potential problems on a timely basis.
3. Helping the auditor properly organize and manage the audit engagement so that it is performed in an effective and efficient manner.
4. Assisting in the selection of engagement team members with appropriate levels of capabilities and competence to respond to anticipated risks, and the proper assignment of work to them.
5. Facilitating the direction and supervision of engagement team members and the review of their work.
6. Assisting, where applicable, in coordination of work done by others such as experts.

Therefore planning ensures that audit risk is reduced to an acceptably low level: proper planning reduces the risk of an inappropriate opinion (SM 2.4).

### 2. Planning never stops

Planning is **not a discrete phase** of an audit, but rather a continual and iterative process that often begins shortly after (or in connection with) the completion of the previous audit and continues until the completion of the current audit engagement (SM 2.5).

Planning does consider the timing of activities that must be completed before further audit procedures. Before the risks of material misstatement are identified and assessed, it considers such matters as (SM 2.5):

1. The analytical procedures to be applied as risk assessment procedures.
2. Obtaining a general understanding of the legal and regulatory framework applicable to the entity and how the entity is complying with that framework.
3. The determination of materiality.
4. The involvement of experts.
5. The performance of other risk assessment procedures.

::: definition Risk assessment procedures
Audit procedures performed to obtain an understanding of the entity and its environment, including the entity's internal control, to identify and assess the risks of material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, at the financial statement and assertion levels (SM 2.6). They are taught in [[aud.risk.sa315|SA 315]]; materiality in [[aud.risk.sa320|SA 320]].
:::

**Who plans.** The engagement partner and other key members of the engagement team shall be involved in planning the audit, including planning and participating in the discussion among engagement team members. Their experience and insight enhance the effectiveness and efficiency of the planning process (SM 2.6).

**Talking to management.** The auditor may decide to discuss elements of planning with the entity's management to facilitate the conduct and management of the audit engagement. When discussing matters included in the overall audit strategy or audit plan, care is required in order not to compromise the effectiveness of the audit (SM 2.6). The overall audit strategy and the audit plan, however, **remain the auditor's responsibility**: it is the auditor who establishes the strategy and develops the plan (SM 2.15–2.16).

### 3. The elements of planning

The elements of planning are of two kinds: (I) preliminary engagement activities and (II) planning activities (SM 2.7).

#### (I) Preliminary engagement activities

Should the relationship continue, and are ethical requirements, including independence, still complied with? They include (SM 2.7–2.9):

**(A) Performing procedures regarding the continuance of the client relationship and the audit engagement.** Appropriate acceptance and continuance procedures should have been followed and their conclusions should be appropriate. The firm obtains the information it needs before accepting a new client, continuing an existing engagement, or accepting a new engagement with an existing client: for example, the integrity of principal owners and key management, the competence of the engagement team, and the implications of matters arising in the current and previous engagements. In initial engagements, the predecessor auditor should be communicated with where there has been a change of auditors (SM 2.8; the opening balances of such an engagement are [[aud.evid.sa510|SA 510]]).

**(B) Evaluating compliance with ethical requirements, including independence.** The auditor shall continuously evaluate compliance with ethical requirements, including independence ("independence" means that the judgement of a person is not subordinate to the wishes or direction of another person who might have engaged him). The engagement partner remains alert for non-compliance by team members and, in consultation with others in the firm, determines the appropriate action (SM 2.8).

The engagement partner shall form a conclusion on compliance with the independence requirements that apply to the engagement. In doing so, the engagement partner shall (SM 2.8):

- (i) obtain relevant information from the firm to identify and evaluate circumstances and relationships that create threats to independence;
- (ii) evaluate information on identified breaches, if any, of the firm's independence policies and procedures, to determine whether they create a threat to independence for the audit engagement; and
- (iii) take appropriate action to eliminate such threats or reduce them to an acceptable level by applying safeguards or, if considered appropriate, withdraw from the audit engagement, where withdrawal is permitted by law or regulation. The engagement partner shall promptly report to the firm any inability to resolve the matter, for appropriate action.

(Threats and safeguards: [[aud.ethics.independence|independence, threats and safeguards]].)

**(C) Establishing an understanding of the terms of engagement.** It is in the interests of both the entity and the auditor that the auditor sends an audit engagement letter before the commencement of the audit, to help avoid misunderstandings with respect to the audit ([[aud.ethics.sa210|SA 210]]; SM 2.9).

Performing preliminary engagement activities ==assists the auditor in identifying and evaluating events or circumstances that may affect the auditor's ability to plan and perform the audit engagement== (SM 2.9).

#### (II-A) Establishing the overall audit strategy

The auditor shall establish an overall audit strategy that sets the **scope, timing and direction** of the audit, and that guides the development of the audit plan (SM 2.9). The process of establishing it assists the auditor to determine, subject to the completion of the risk assessment procedures, such matters as (SM 2.10):

- (i) **The resources to deploy** for specific audit areas, such as the use of appropriately experienced team members for high-risk areas or the involvement of experts on complex matters.
- (ii) **The amount of resources** to allocate to specific audit areas, such as the number of team members assigned to observe the inventory count at material locations, the extent of review of other auditors' work in group audits, or the audit budget in hours to allocate to high-risk areas.
- (iii) **When** these resources are to be deployed, such as at an interim audit stage or at key cut-off dates.
- (iv) **How** such resources are managed, directed and supervised, such as when team briefing and debriefing meetings are expected to be held, how engagement partner and manager reviews are expected to take place (on-site or off-site), and whether to complete engagement quality control reviews.

In establishing the overall audit strategy, the auditor shall take five factors into consideration (SM 2.10–2.13):

| Factor (in ICAI's order) | ICAI's examples |
|---|---|
| (a) Identify the characteristics of the engagement that define its scope | Financial reporting framework; business segments, and specialised knowledge needed; industry regulators' reporting requirements; expected use of evidence from previous audits |
| (b) Ascertain the reporting objectives, to plan the timing of the audit and the nature of communications required | Entity's reporting timetable; meetings with management on the audit work; expected type and timing of reports; communications on the status of work; team meetings and timing of reviews |
| (c) Consider the factors that, in the auditor's professional judgement, are significant in directing the engagement team's efforts | Volume of transactions (is relying on internal control more efficient?); changes in industry regulations and reporting requirements; changes in accounting standards; changes in the legal environment |
| (d) Consider the results of preliminary engagement activities and, where applicable, knowledge from the partner's other engagements for the entity | Previous results of testing internal control, deficiencies and action taken; how the team is reminded to keep a questioning mind and professional skepticism |
| (e) Ascertain the nature, timing and extent of resources necessary to perform the engagement | Selecting the team and assigning work; experienced members for higher-risk areas; budgeting more time for them |

Preliminary identification of material classes of transactions, account balances and disclosures helps here (SM 2.11), and overall materiality is determined when establishing the strategy (SM 3.20).

#### (II-B) Developing the audit plan

Once the strategy is established, an audit plan is developed to address the matters identified in it, through the efficient use of the auditor's resources. Understanding the client's business is one of the important principles in developing it: without adequate knowledge of the business, a proper audit is not possible (SM 2.13).

The auditor shall develop an audit plan that shall include a description of (SM 2.13–2.14):

- (i) the nature, timing and extent of planned **risk assessment procedures**;
- (ii) the nature, timing and extent of planned **further audit procedures at the assertion level**; and
- (iii) **other planned audit procedures** that are required to be carried out so that the engagement complies with SAs.

The audit plan is **more detailed** than the overall audit strategy: it sets the nature, timing and extent of procedures to be performed by team members, including matters such as sample size. It develops over the course of the audit: risk assessment procedures are planned early, further audit procedures depend on their outcome, and some further procedures may begin before all the others are planned (SM 2.14; responses are [[aud.risk.sa330|SA 330]]).

### 4. Strategy and plan: one conversation, not two documents

The strategy sets the broad overall approach; the plan describes how it is going to be implemented (SM 2.15). The establishment of the overall audit strategy and the detailed audit plan are ==not necessarily discrete or sequential processes, but are closely inter-related== since changes in one may result in consequential changes to the other (SM 2.15).

### 5. Changes during the audit

The auditor shall update and change the overall audit strategy and the audit plan as necessary during the course of the audit. Unexpected events, changes in conditions, or the audit evidence obtained may require modifying them, and thereby the planned nature, timing and extent of further audit procedures, based on the revised consideration of assessed risks: for example, when audit evidence from detailed checking contradicts the evidence from testing internal controls (SM 2.16).

### 6. Planning direction, supervision and review

The auditor shall plan the nature, timing and extent of direction and supervision of engagement team members and the review of their work. These vary depending on many factors, including (SM 2.16–2.17):

1. The size and complexity of the entity.
2. The area of the audit.
3. The assessed risks of material misstatement.
4. The capabilities and competence of the individual team members performing the audit work.

### 7. Documentation

The auditor shall document (SM 2.17):

- (a) **the overall audit strategy**: a record of the key decisions considered necessary to properly plan the audit and to communicate significant matters to the engagement team;
- (b) **the audit plan**: a record of the planned nature, timing and extent of risk assessment procedures and further audit procedures at the assertion level, which can be reviewed and approved before the procedures are performed; standard audit programs or completion checklists may be used, tailored to the engagement; and
- (c) **any significant changes made during the audit engagement to the overall audit strategy or the audit plan, and the reasons for such changes**: this explains why they were made and the strategy and plan finally adopted, and reflects the appropriate response to them.

Documentation in general is [[aud.doc.sa230|SA 230]].

## Worked examples

:::: example Lakshmi's planning notebook
Lakshmi's notebook for the 2025-26 audit of Tapri Tea Co. Ltd has seven entries:

1. A confirmation from the firm that no partner or team member holds Tapri shares, and a review of a breach of the firm's independence policy reported by one article assistant.
2. Of the 1,400-hour audit budget, 490 hours go to the delivery-app revenue and the new inventory software, with an IT expert for the software.
3. An interim visit in the third week of January, and cut-off work on 31 March at the Indore and Pune warehouses.
4. Analytical procedures comparing each café's monthly sales and wastage with last year's, to identify unusual movements that may indicate risks of material misstatement, before those risks are assessed.
5. Cut-off testing of 40 dispatches either side of 31 March, and confirmation requests to the 25 largest distributors.
6. Tapri's quarterly results timetable under its listing obligations, and a meeting with Dev to agree the expected type and timing of the reports to be issued.
7. The engagement letter for 2025-26, sent before the audit commences.

Classify each entry as a preliminary engagement activity, part of the overall audit strategy or part of the audit plan under SA 300, giving reasons.

::: answer Model answer
**Relevant provision.** As per SA 300, preliminary engagement activities cover client continuance, ethical requirements including independence, and the terms of engagement. The overall audit strategy sets the scope, timing and direction of the audit: which resources, how much, when and how managed, considering among other factors the reporting objectives. The audit plan describes the nature, timing and extent of planned risk assessment procedures, further audit procedures at the assertion level, and other procedures required by SAs.

**Facts and analysis.**

| Entry | Element | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Shareholdings, independence breach | Preliminary engagement activity | Evaluating compliance with ethical requirements, including independence |
| 2. 490 hours, IT expert | Overall audit strategy | Amount of resources and resources to deploy for high-risk areas |
| 3. January visit, 31 March cut-off | Overall audit strategy | When resources are deployed |
| 4. Café-wise analytical review | Audit plan | Planned risk assessment procedures (analytical procedures applied as risk assessment procedures) |
| 5. Cut-off and confirmations | Audit plan | Planned further audit procedures at the assertion level |
| 6. Results timetable, reports | Overall audit strategy | Reporting objectives of the engagement |
| 7. Engagement letter | Preliminary engagement activity | Understanding of the terms of engagement |

**Conclusion.** Entries 1 and 7 are preliminary engagement activities; entries 2, 3 and 6 belong to the overall audit strategy; entries 4 and 5 belong to the audit plan. The strategy and plan entries are documented under SA 300, with any significant change and its reasons.
:::

:::: example The fire at the Dewas samosa factory
Iyer & Associates' audit plan for Tapri, approved in November 2025, relies on Tapri's controls over café cash sales and provides for one inventory count at the Dewas samosa factory on 31 March 2026. On 18 February 2026, a fire destroys part of the factory's frozen stock and one cold store, and an insurance claim is lodged. In the same week, tests of details on cash sales at six cafés reveal shortages that the earlier tests of controls had not indicated.

> **Bunty:** Ma'am, the plan was approved in November. If we change it now, the file will show we planned badly.
> **Lakshmi:** Is that SA 300 talking, Bunty, or your fear of my review notes?

Lakshmi also proposes to send Bunty, a first-year article assistant, alone to Dewas to handle the post-fire inventory work.

Required:

(i) Is Bunty's view correct?

(ii) What does SA 300 require to be documented?

(iii) Comment on sending Bunty alone to Dewas.

::: answer Model answer
**(i) Relevant provision.** As per SA 300, planning is a continual and iterative process, and the auditor shall update and change the overall audit strategy and the audit plan as necessary during the course of the audit. As a result of unexpected events, changes in conditions, or the audit evidence obtained, the auditor may need to modify them and the planned nature, timing and extent of further audit procedures, based on the revised consideration of assessed risks; for example, when evidence from detailed checking contradicts evidence from testing internal controls.

**Facts and analysis.** The fire is an unexpected event affecting the existence and valuation of inventory, the cold store and the insurance claim receivable. The cash shortages are audit evidence from detailed checking that contradicts the evidence from tests of controls. Both are information that differs significantly from the information available in November.

**Conclusion.** Bunty's view is **not correct**. The strategy and the plan must be revised: for example, more procedures on the remaining Dewas inventory and the insurance claim, and less reliance on café cash controls with more substantive procedures on cash sales.

**(ii)** The auditor shall document the overall audit strategy, the audit plan, and **any significant changes made during the audit engagement to the overall audit strategy or the audit plan, and the reasons for such changes**. That record explains why the changes were made and the strategy and plan finally adopted, and reflects the appropriate response to the significant changes. A file showing the changes and their reasons is evidence of good planning, not bad.

**(iii)** As per SA 300, the auditor shall plan the nature, timing and extent of direction and supervision of engagement team members and the review of their work, which vary with the size and complexity of the entity, the area of the audit, the assessed risks of material misstatement, and the capabilities and competence of the individual team members. The post-fire inventory is now a higher-risk area, and Bunty is a first-year assistant. Sending him alone is not appropriate: an experienced team member should lead the work, with closer direction, supervision and review of Bunty's part of it.
:::
::::

:::: example Dev's two money-saving ideas
Dev Malhotra, Tapri's CFO, wants this year's audit fee cut. He sends Iyer & Associates two suggestions:

(a) "Partner hours are your most expensive hours. Let Bunty and the two seniors who did last year's fieldwork plan this year's audit among themselves. Lakshmi can skip the planning discussion and look at the file in May, before she signs."

(b) "Tell us the dates of the surprise cash counts at the kulhad cafés, so that the café managers keep the cash ready and your team does not have to wait."

Comment on each suggestion with reference to SA 300.

::: answer Model answer
**(a) Relevant provision.** As per SA 300, the engagement partner and other key members of the engagement team shall be involved in planning the audit, including planning and participating in the discussion among engagement team members. Their involvement draws on their experience and insight, thereby enhancing the effectiveness and efficiency of the planning process. The auditor shall also plan the nature, timing and extent of direction and supervision of engagement team members and the review of their work, which vary with, among other factors, the assessed risks of material misstatement and the capabilities and competence of the individual team members.

**Facts and analysis.** Dev proposes that planning be left to a first-year article assistant and two seniors, and that the engagement partner only look at the file at the end. This year's audit has new risks (billing moved to the delivery app, new inventory software). Leaving the engagement partner out of planning loses exactly the experience SA 300 relies on, and a single look at the file before signing is not planned direction, supervision and review.

**Conclusion.** The suggestion is not acceptable. Lakshmi, as engagement partner, and the other key members must be involved in planning and in the team discussion; a fee saving cannot override SA 300.

**(b) Relevant provision.** As per SA 300, the auditor may discuss elements of planning with the entity's management to facilitate the conduct and management of the audit engagement. When discussing matters included in the overall audit strategy or audit plan, care is required in order not to compromise the effectiveness of the audit.

**Facts and analysis.** Telling management in advance of a visit to test controls facilitates the audit and does not compromise its effectiveness (SM 2.32). A surprise cash count is different: unless cash balances are checked by surprise, there can be no certainty that the cash produced for inspection was in fact held by the custodian, so the check is made without notice of the visit to the entity or its staff (SM 5.46). Once the dates are known, the count shows only what the managers chose to have ready.

**Conclusion.** Lakshmi may discuss other elements of planning with Dev, such as the timing of the interim visit and the reports expected, but should not disclose the dates of the surprise cash counts.
:::

## Under the hood

### Where the strategy meets other Standards

- **Materiality.** When establishing the overall audit strategy, the auditor shall determine materiality for the financial statements as a whole and, where needed, materiality levels for particular classes of transactions, balances or disclosures (SM 3.20; [[aud.risk.sa320|SA 320]]).
- **Risk assessment.** The strategy's resource decisions are made "subject to the completion of the auditor's risk assessment procedures" (SM 2.9), so they firm up, and change, as [[aud.risk.sa315|SA 315]] work comes in.
- **Quality control.** SA 220 also gives the engagement partner responsibilities on relevant ethical requirements: identifying threats to independence that safeguards may not reduce, and reporting them within the firm for action, which may include withdrawal (SM 11.32; [[aud.ethics.qc|SQC 1 and SA 220]]).
- **Initial engagements.** SA 300's preliminary activities include communicating with the predecessor auditor where auditors have changed (SM 2.8); the audit of opening balances in an initial audit engagement is [[aud.evid.sa510|SA 510]].

### What the Study Material's own exercises say

Its "Test Your Understanding" answers (SM 2.32–2.33): considering whether experts are needed is planning; telling management in advance of a visit to test controls does not compromise effectiveness, so hiding it for "surprise" is not proper; an inquiry of in-house legal counsel to identify risks is a planned risk assessment procedure (audit plan); a change in laws affecting the company is a strategy factor.

## Answer frame

### A case question: provision, facts, analysis, conclusion

1. **Relevant provision**: "As per SA 300, Planning an Audit of Financial Statements, …" and only the requirement the case needs (strategy factors, plan contents, changes, supervision, documentation, responsibility).
2. **Facts of the case**: "In the given case, …" the facts that trigger the requirement.
3. **Analysis**: which element of planning it is, and why (scope, timing and direction, or nature, timing and extent).
4. **Conclusion**: "Thus, … is (not) in line with SA 300", plus what the auditor should do.

### "Explain the relationship between the overall audit strategy and the audit plan"

The five points ICAI's answers give (SM 2.15), each a mark:

1. Strategy sets the broad overall approach; the plan addresses the matters identified in the strategy.
2. Strategy determines scope, timing and direction; the plan describes how the strategy is implemented.
3. The plan is more detailed: the nature, timing and extent of procedures by team members; it develops over the course of the audit.
4. Once the strategy is established, the plan is developed, using the auditor's resources efficiently.
5. They are not necessarily discrete or sequential processes but closely inter-related: a change in one may change the other.

### Three answers to "the client's finance head will write the strategy"

The question type of SM Illustration 2 and MTP May 2025 Series II Q2(c).

**Loses marks**

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: the plan laminated in November
Bunty treats the November plan as final after the fire. **Fix:** planning is continual and iterative; update the strategy and plan as necessary and document each significant change with its reasons.
:::

::: mistake Putting resources in the audit plan
Writing "the audit plan contains the team, budgeted hours and an IT expert". **Fix:** resources (which, how much, when, how managed) belong to the **overall audit strategy**; the plan describes the nature, timing and extent of procedures. September 2024 MCQ 14 turns on this.
:::

::: mistake "Management may prepare the strategy if the auditor reviews it"
**Fix:** the overall audit strategy and the audit plan remain the auditor's responsibility. The auditor may only *discuss elements* of planning with management, with care not to compromise the effectiveness of the audit.
:::

::: mistake Documenting two things out of three
Listing "strategy and plan" as SA 300's documentation. **Fix:** three items: the overall audit strategy, the audit plan, and any significant changes made during the engagement to either, with the reasons for them.
:::

## Practice

::: quiz Check yourself
Q: Case scenario. CA Farah Siddiqui, auditing Narmada Agro Ltd, decides to assign two seniors experienced in derivatives to the commodity-hedging area, to involve a valuer for biological assets, and to hold partner reviews on-site in the last week of April. These decisions are part of:
- [ ] Preliminary engagement activities
- [x] The overall audit strategy
- [ ] The audit plan
- [ ] Documentation required only by SA 230
> Which resources to deploy for high-risk and complex areas, and how reviews take place, are strategy matters (SM 2.10). The audit plan describes procedures, not people.

Q: Which of these entries belongs in Tapri's overall audit strategy rather than in its audit plan?
- [ ] Testing 40 dispatches either side of 31 March for cut-off
- [ ] An inquiry of in-house legal counsel to identify risks of material misstatement
- [ ] Sending confirmation requests to the 25 largest distributors
- [x] Whether an engagement quality control review will be completed, and when partner reviews will take place
> How resources are managed, directed and supervised, including reviews, is a strategy matter. The other three are planned procedures: the audit plan.

Q: According to SA 300, when does planning take place?
- [ ] Only after the engagement letter is signed and before fieldwork starts
- [ ] Only after the risks of material misstatement have been assessed
- [x] Often from shortly after the completion of the previous audit until the completion of the current audit
- [ ] Only in an initial audit engagement
> Planning is not a discrete phase but a continual and iterative process (SM 2.5).

Q: Which of the following does SA 300 require the auditor to document? Select all that apply.
- [x] The overall audit strategy
- [x] The audit plan
- [x] Significant changes made during the audit to the strategy or plan, and the reasons for them
- [ ] Management's written approval of the audit plan
> The three items are in SM 2.17. SA 300 requires no approval by management: the strategy and the plan remain the auditor's responsibility (the documented plan is a record that can be reviewed and approved before the procedures are performed).

Q: Tapri's management asks to discuss the timing of the interim visit and the reports Iyer & Associates expect to issue. Lakshmi should:
- [ ] Refuse, because planning matters are confidential to the auditor
- [ ] Give management the complete audit plan, including the procedures and sample sizes
- [x] Discuss these elements of planning to facilitate the audit, taking care not to compromise its effectiveness
- [ ] Let management approve the strategy, since it knows the business best
> SA 300 permits discussing elements of planning, with care (SM 2.6). Refusing everything is the commonest wrong answer; handing over the whole plan, or the approval, goes too far.
:::

## Recap

- Objective of SA 300: plan the audit so that it will be performed in an effective manner; planning reduces audit risk to an acceptably low level.
- Six benefits: important areas, problems solved on time, organised audit, right team, direction-supervision-review, coordination with experts.
- Planning is continual and iterative, from shortly after the previous audit to the completion of the current one; before risks are assessed it considers analytical procedures, legal framework, materiality, experts and other risk assessment procedures.
- Preliminary engagement activities: client continuance, ethical requirements including independence, understanding of the terms of engagement.
- Overall audit strategy: scope, timing and direction; decides which resources, how much, when, and how managed; five factors (scope, reporting objectives, significant factors, preliminary results, resources).
- Audit plan: nature, timing and extent of planned risk assessment procedures, further audit procedures at the assertion level, and other procedures required by SAs; more detailed than the strategy.
- Both remain the auditor's responsibility; update them as necessary; plan direction, supervision and review by entity size and complexity, audit area, assessed risks and team competence.
- Document the strategy, the plan and significant changes with reasons. (A captain who never changes the field on a turning pitch loses the Test.)

## Flashcards

Q:: SA 300: what is the objective of the auditor?
A:: To plan the audit so that it will be performed in an effective manner.

Q:: SA 300: list the benefits of adequate planning.
A:: Appropriate attention to important areas; identifying and resolving potential problems on time; organising and managing the engagement effectively and efficiently; selecting team members with appropriate capabilities and assigning work; direction, supervision and review; coordinating work done by others such as experts.

Q:: SA 300: which matters does planning consider before the risks of material misstatement are identified and assessed?
A:: Analytical procedures as risk assessment procedures; a general understanding of the legal and regulatory framework and compliance with it; determination of materiality; involvement of experts; other risk assessment procedures.

Q:: SA 300: what are the preliminary engagement activities?
A:: Performing procedures regarding the continuance of the client relationship; evaluating compliance with ethical requirements, including independence; establishing an understanding of the terms of engagement.

Q:: SA 300: what does the overall audit strategy set?
A:: The scope, timing and direction of the audit; it guides the development of the audit plan.

Q:: SA 300: which four resource matters does establishing the overall audit strategy help determine?
A:: The resources to deploy for specific areas; the amount of resources to allocate; when they are deployed; how they are managed, directed and supervised.

Q:: SA 300: which five factors are considered in establishing the overall audit strategy?
A:: Characteristics defining the scope; reporting objectives; factors significant in directing the team's efforts; results of preliminary engagement activities and knowledge from other engagements; nature, timing and extent of resources needed.

Q:: SA 300: what shall the audit plan describe?
A:: The nature, timing and extent of planned risk assessment procedures; of planned further audit procedures at the assertion level; and other planned audit procedures required so that the engagement complies with SAs.

Q:: SA 300: on what does the direction, supervision and review of the team depend?
A:: The size and complexity of the entity; the area of the audit; the assessed risks of material misstatement; the capabilities and competence of the individual team members.

Q:: SA 300: what must the auditor document?
A:: The overall audit strategy; the audit plan; any significant changes made during the engagement to either, and the reasons for them.

Q:: SA 300: in forming a conclusion on independence, what shall the engagement partner do?
A:: Obtain information from the firm to identify threats; evaluate identified breaches of the firm's independence policies; eliminate threats or reduce them by safeguards, or withdraw where law permits, reporting any unresolved matter to the firm promptly.

Q:: SA 300: may the auditor discuss the audit plan with management?
A:: The auditor may discuss elements of planning to facilitate the audit, with care not to compromise its effectiveness; the strategy and plan remain the auditor's responsibility.
