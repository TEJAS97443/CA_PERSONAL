## In plain words

### Four envelopes on Dev's desk

Dev Malhotra, Tapri's CFO, walks into Iyer & Associates with four requests and one purchase order.

> **Dev:** Lakshmi, the annual audit as usual. A quick look at the June quarter before we publish it. And a stamp on our three-year café forecast for an investor. I have already discounted every rupee in it.
> **Lakshmi:** *(sighing)* The present value of that stamp is lower than you think, Dev. And these are three different engagements, not three audits.
> **Bunty:** Ma'am, a CA signs at the bottom of all three. What is the difference?
> **Lakshmi:** How much confidence the signature carries, Bunty, and how much work stands behind it.

Each request asks a CA to look at information someone else prepared and tell other people how far they can trust it. That is an **assurance engagement**. Three parties are always involved: the CA (the **practitioner**), the people who prepared the information (the **responsible party**, here Tapri's management) and the people who will rely on the report (the **intended users**: shareholders, the investor, a bank).

The difference is one of degree. The **audit** of the annual financial statements performs extensive procedures and gives **reasonable assurance**, a high level. A **review** of the quarter performs fewer procedures and gives **limited assurance**, lower than an audit. The forecast is about the future, which no one can verify yet: the CA can only check whether its assumptions are not unreasonable and whether it is properly prepared and presented, and say that nothing suggests otherwise, a moderate level of assurance.

ICAI has a family of Standards for each kind of work: **Standards on Auditing** for audits, **Standards on Review Engagements** for reviews, **Standards on Assurance Engagements** for assurance on anything other than past financial information, and **Standards on Related Services** for jobs that give no assurance, such as compiling accounts for a client. Over all of them sits **SQC 1**, which makes the firm itself keep a system of quality control.

::: analogy Buying a flat
A structural engineer's full inspection before you buy is the audit: thorough, and you can rely on it. A friend who is an engineer walking through for an hour is the review: useful, but less comfort. The builder's brochure promising possession in 2028 is prospective information: an expert can tell you whether its assumptions look reasonable, never that the keys will arrive on time. And the clerk who fills in your loan form is a related service: helpful, but nobody is vouching for anything.
:::

::: key
An assurance engagement is a practitioner's conclusion that raises the confidence of intended users, other than the responsible party, in a subject matter measured against criteria. Audit gives reasonable (high) assurance, review limited (lower) assurance, prospective information only moderate assurance; related services give none. SAs, SREs, SAEs and SRSs are the Engagement Standards; SQC 1 applies to all of them.
:::

## How it works

### 1. What an assurance engagement is

::: definition Assurance engagement
"Assurance engagement" means an engagement in which a practitioner expresses a conclusion designed to enhance the degree of confidence of the intended users other than the responsible party about the outcome of the evaluation or measurement of a subject matter against criteria (SM 1.22).
:::

It means that the practitioner gives an opinion about specific information due to which users of information are able to make confident decisions, knowing well that the chance of the information being incorrect is diminished (SM 1.22). An audit is one type of assurance engagement, in which the auditor gives an opinion on whether the financial statements give a true and fair view; but assurance engagements are not restricted to audit alone (SM 1.22).

### 2. The five elements

The following elements comprise an assurance engagement, in this order (SM 1.23–1.24):

1. **A three party relationship involving a practitioner, a responsible party and intended users.** A **practitioner** is the person who provides the assurance. The term practitioner is broader than auditor: audit is related to historical information, whereas a practitioner may provide assurance not necessarily related to historical financial information. A **responsible party** is the party responsible for preparation of the subject matter. **Intended users** are the persons for whom the assurance report is prepared; they may use it in making decisions.
2. **An appropriate subject matter**: the information to be examined by the practitioner, for example the financial information contained in financial statements in an audit of financial statements.
3. **Suitable criteria**: the benchmarks used to evaluate the subject matter, like standards, guidance, laws, rules and regulations.
4. **Sufficient appropriate evidence.** The practitioner performs an assurance engagement to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence; it is on the basis of evidence that conclusions are arrived at and an opinion is formed. "Sufficient" relates to the **quantity** of evidence, "appropriate" to its **quality**: evidence that gives the auditor more comfort is qualitative and so appropriate. Evidence should be both.
5. **A written assurance report in appropriate form**, containing a conclusion that conveys the assurance about the subject matter. The written assurance report is the outcome of an assurance engagement.

### 3. Audit and review: the same information, different degrees

Audit is a **reasonable assurance engagement**: it provides reasonable assurance. **Review** is a **limited assurance engagement**: it provides a lower level of assurance than audit. Review involves fewer procedures and gathers sufficient appropriate evidence on the basis of which limited conclusions can be drawn. Both "audit" and "review" are related to financial statements prepared on the basis of historical financial information (SM 1.24).

Assurance engagements provide assurance to users; the difference is of degree. Reasonable assurance, as in an audit, is a high level of assurance; limited assurance, as in a review, is lower than audit, only a moderate level (SM 1.24):

::: compare Reasonable assurance engagement and limited assurance engagement (SM 1.25)
| Reasonable assurance engagement | Limited assurance engagement |
|---|---|
| Provides a high level of assurance | Provides a lower level of assurance than a reasonable assurance engagement |
| Performs elaborate and extensive procedures to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence | Performs fewer procedures as compared to a reasonable assurance engagement |
| Draws reasonable conclusions on the basis of sufficient appropriate evidence | Involves obtaining sufficient appropriate evidence to draw limited conclusions |
| Example: an audit engagement | Example: a review engagement |
:::

### 4. Assurance on matters other than historical financial information

Besides reasonable and limited assurance engagements, there is another kind of assurance, related to matters other than historical financial information: prospective financial information, or assurance on internal controls in an entity (SM 1.25).

::: definition Prospective financial information
"Prospective financial information" means financial information based on assumptions about events that may occur in the future and possible actions by an entity. It can be in the form of a forecast or projection or combination of both (SM 1.25).
:::

::: compare Historical and prospective financial information (SM 1.11, 1.25)
| Point | Historical financial information | Prospective financial information |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Expressed in financial terms, about economic events, conditions or circumstances of past periods | Based on assumptions about the occurrence of future events and possible actions by an entity |
| Rooted in | Past events that have already occurred | Future events |
| Example | Purchases and sales in the financial statements | A forecast or projection |
| Engagements | Audit (SAs) and review (SREs) | Examination under the SAEs |
:::

**What the practitioner does with a forecast** (SM 1.26). In assurance reports involving prospective financial information, the practitioner obtains sufficient appropriate evidence that:

- management's assumptions on which the prospective financial information is based are **not unreasonable**;
- the prospective financial information is **properly prepared** on the basis of the assumptions;
- it is **properly presented**; and
- all material assumptions are **adequately disclosed**.

Evidence may be available to support the assumptions, but such evidence is itself generally future-oriented. The auditor is therefore **not in a position to express an opinion as to whether the results shown will be achieved**. So the practitioner provides a report assuring that **nothing has come to the practitioner's attention to suggest that these assumptions do not provide a reasonable basis for the projection**. Hence such an engagement provides only a **"moderate" level of assurance** (SM 1.26).

### 6. The engagement and quality control standards

The following Standards issued under the authority of the ICAI Council are collectively known as **Engagement Standards** (SM 1.28):

1. **Standards on Auditing (SAs)**, which apply in audit of historical financial information.
2. **Standards on Review Engagements (SREs)**, which apply in review of historical financial information.
3. **Standards on Assurance Engagements (SAEs)**, which apply in assurance engagements other than audits and review of historical financial information.
4. **Standards on Related Services (SRSs)**, which apply in agreed upon procedures to information, compilation engagements and other related service engagements.

The purpose of issue of these standards is ==to establish high quality standards and guidance in the areas of financial statement audits and in other types of assurance services== (SM 1.28). Engagement Standards deal with the responsibilities of the auditor or practitioner (SM 1.31).

**Standards on Auditing** apply in the context of an audit of financial statements by an independent auditor, in audit of historical information. They establish high quality benchmarks and cover all significant aspects of an audit, from the overall objectives of the independent auditor, audit documentation, planning, identifying and assessing risks of material misstatement, audit sampling and audit evidence to forming an opinion and reporting. Examples: SA 200 (Overall Objectives of the Independent Auditor and the Conduct of an Audit in accordance with Standards on Auditing), SA 230 (Audit Documentation), SA 315 (Identifying and Assessing the Risks of Material Misstatement through Understanding the Entity and its Environment), SA 500 (Audit Evidence), Revised SA 700 (Forming an Opinion and Reporting on Financial Statements) (SM 1.29).

**Standards on Review Engagements** apply in the context of review of financial statements. Review provides lower assurance than audit because it involves fewer procedures; since it also provides assurance, it too involves obtaining sufficient appropriate evidence, for example when an auditor performs a review of interim financial information of an entity. Examples: SRE 2400 (Revised) Engagements to Review Historical Financial Statements; SRE 2410 Review of Interim Financial Information Performed by the Independent Auditor of the Entity. Both SAs and SREs apply to engagements involving historical financial information (SM 1.29–1.30).

**Standards on Assurance Engagements** apply in assurance engagements dealing with subject matters other than historical financial information: they do not include audit or review of historical financial information. Example: an examination of prospective financial information; or the engagement may relate to non-financial matters like the design and operation of internal control in an entity. Examples: SAE 3400 The Examination of Prospective Financial Information; SAE 3420 Assurance Engagements to Report on the Compilation of Pro Forma Financial Information Included in a Prospectus (SM 1.30).

**Standards on Related Services** apply in (SM 1.30–1.31):

- **Agreed-upon procedures** regarding financial information. Such an engagement may require the auditor to perform certain procedures on individual items of financial data (accounts payable, accounts receivable, purchases from related parties, sales and profits of a segment of an entity), on a financial statement (say, a balance sheet) or even on a complete set of financial statements.
- **Compilation engagements**, in which the practitioner is called upon to assist management with the preparation and presentation of historical financial information **without obtaining assurance** on that information. The practitioner issues a report clearly stating that it is not an assurance engagement and no opinion is being expressed.

Examples: SRS 4400 Engagements to perform agreed-upon procedures regarding financial information; SRS 4410 (Revised) Compilation engagements.

**Standards on Quality Control (SQCs)** have been issued to establish standards and provide guidance regarding a firm's responsibilities for its system of quality control for the conduct of audit and review of historical financial information and for other assurance and related service engagements. **SQC 1** requires auditors and practitioners to establish a system of quality control so that the firm and its personnel comply with professional standards and regulatory and legal requirements, and that reports issued are appropriate. ==SQCs are to be applied for all services covered by Engagement Standards== (SM 1.31). Its full title says as much: SQC 1 "Quality Control for Firms that Perform Audits and Reviews of Historical Financial Information, and Other Assurance and Related Services Engagements" (SM 11.25; [[aud.ethics.qc|SQC 1 and SA 220]]).

### 8. Why Standards are needed

The Study Material gives five reasons (SM 1.31–1.32):

1. Standards ensure carrying out of audit against established benchmarks at par with global practices.
2. Standards improve quality of financial reporting, thereby helping users to make diligent decisions.
3. Standards promote uniformity, as audit of financial statements is carried out following these Standards.
4. Standards equip professional accountants with professional knowledge and skill.
5. Standards ensure audit quality.

### 9. Following the Standards, and departing from a procedure

It is the duty of professional accountants to see that Standards are followed in engagements undertaken by them. Ordinarily they are to be followed; but a situation may arise in which a specific procedure required by a Standard would be ineffective in a particular engagement. Then (SM 1.32):

::: steps
1. **Document** how the alternative procedures performed achieve the purpose of the required procedure.
2. **Document the reason for the departure**, unless it is clear.
3. **The report should draw attention** to such departures.
4. Remember that **a mere disclosure in the report does not absolve** a professional accountant from complying with applicable Standards.
:::

## Worked examples

:::: example Four assignments, four families
Iyer & Associates receive the following requests in September 2026. For each, identify the nature of the engagement, the Engagement Standards issued by ICAI that apply (with the Standard named in the Study Material, where there is one), and the level of assurance, if any. Also state whether SQC 1 applies.

(a) Shubh Labh Foods LLP, a Tapri franchisee, wants its annual financial statements reviewed for a landlord who is also an investor. The firm is not its auditor and will perform fewer procedures than an audit.

(b) Tapri Cold Chain Pvt Ltd's accountant has resigned. The company asks the firm to prepare its financial statements for the year from the trial balance and records, without giving any assurance on them.

(c) The franchisees' association asks the firm to recompute, for twelve months, the royalty Tapri charged each franchisee at 4% of reported sales and to report the differences found.

(d) Tapri Express Pvt Ltd, planning a public issue, includes in its prospectus pro forma financial information showing the effect of acquiring a rival delivery app; the merchant banker wants a CA's report on its compilation.

::: answer Model answer
**Relevant provision.** Engagement Standards issued under the authority of the ICAI Council comprise Standards on Auditing (audit of historical financial information), Standards on Review Engagements (review of historical financial information), Standards on Assurance Engagements (assurance engagements other than audit and review of historical financial information) and Standards on Related Services (agreed-upon procedures, compilation and other related services). Standards on Quality Control are to be applied for all services covered by Engagement Standards.

| Request | Nature | Standards | Assurance |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Review of the LLP's annual financial statements | Review of historical financial information | SREs: SRE 2400 (Revised) | Limited |
| (b) Preparing the company's financial statements | Compilation engagement | SRSs: SRS 4410 (Revised) | None: a related service |
| (c) Recomputing royalty | Agreed-upon procedures regarding financial information | SRSs: SRS 4400 | None: a related service |
| (d) Pro forma information in a prospectus | Assurance engagement other than audit or review of historical financial information | SAEs: SAE 3420 | Assurance on the compilation |

**Analysis.**

(a) A review performs fewer procedures than an audit but still obtains sufficient appropriate evidence, on which limited conclusions are drawn: a limited assurance engagement, lower than the reasonable assurance of an audit.

(b) Assisting management with the preparation and presentation of historical financial information without obtaining assurance is a compilation engagement. The firm's report must state clearly that it is not an assurance engagement and that no opinion is expressed.

(c) Performing specified procedures on an individual item of financial data (the royalty) is an engagement to perform agreed-upon procedures regarding financial information: a related service, not an assurance engagement.

(d) The Study Material names SAE 3420, Assurance Engagements to Report on the Compilation of Pro Forma Financial Information Included in a Prospectus, as a Standard on Assurance Engagements.

**Conclusion.** SQC 1 applies to all four: it is applied for all services covered by Engagement Standards, including compilations and agreed-upon procedures, and requires the firm to have a system of quality control so that the firm and its personnel comply with professional standards and legal requirements and its reports are appropriate.
:::

::::: example The forecast that grows like a hockey stick
Kesar wants equity from Narmada Growth Partners for a café expansion. Tapri prepares a three-year forecast and engages CA Farah Sheikh to report on it. The historical figures and the forecast are given below:

| Year | Cafés | Revenue per café (₹ lakh) | Revenue (₹ crore) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2023-24 (actual) | 120 | 27.0 | 32.40 |
| 2024-25 (actual) | 140 | 28.5 | 39.90 |
| 2025-26 (actual) | 160 | 30.0 | 48.00 |
| 2026-27 (forecast) | 240 | 40.0 | 96.00 |
| 2027-28 (forecast) | 320 | 55.0 | 176.00 |
| 2028-29 (forecast) | 400 | 75.0 | 300.00 |

Kesar asks CA Farah to "certify that Tapri's café revenue will reach ₹300 crore in 2028-29".

Required:

(i) Which Engagement Standards apply, and what level of assurance can the report give?

(ii) Can CA Farah give the certificate Kesar wants? What must she obtain evidence about, and what will her report say?

(iii) Comment, with workings, on the forecast's assumption about revenue per café.

:::: answer Model answer
**(i) Standards and assurance.** A forecast is prospective financial information: financial information based on assumptions about events that may occur in the future and possible actions by an entity. Its examination is an assurance engagement on a matter other than historical financial information, governed by the Standards on Assurance Engagements (SAE 3400, The Examination of Prospective Financial Information), with SQC 1 applying to the firm. Such an engagement provides only a moderate level of assurance.

**(ii) The certificate.** Prospective financial information relates to future events. Evidence may be available to support the assumptions, but such evidence is itself generally future-oriented, so the practitioner is not in a position to express an opinion as to whether the results shown will be achieved. CA Farah cannot certify that revenue will reach ₹300 crore.

She will obtain sufficient appropriate evidence that management's assumptions are not unreasonable, that the forecast is properly prepared on the basis of the assumptions, that it is properly presented, and that all material assumptions are adequately disclosed, including their consistency with the actual results. Her report will state that nothing has come to her attention to suggest that the assumptions do not provide a reasonable basis for the forecast.

**(iii) Revenue per café.** Working note:

| Year | Revenue per café (₹ lakh) | Growth on previous year |
|---|---|---|
| 2024-25 (actual) | 28.5 | 5.6% |
| 2025-26 (actual) | 30.0 | 5.3% |
| 2026-27 (forecast) | 40.0 | 33.3% |
| 2027-28 (forecast) | 55.0 | 37.5% |
| 2028-29 (forecast) | 75.0 | 36.4% |

- Revenue per café grew about 5.4% a year over the two actual years; the forecast assumes about 35.7% a year, 2.5 times in three years.
- Total revenue rises 6.25 times, from ₹48 crore to ₹300 crore (about 84.2% a year), against about 20–23% a year in the actual years.
- At the historical growth of about 5.4% a year, 400 cafés would earn about ₹35.14 lakh each in 2028-29, a revenue of about ₹140.5 crore. About ₹159.5 crore of the ₹300 crore therefore rests on the revenue-per-café assumption alone.

This assumption is inconsistent with the actual results. CA Farah must obtain sufficient appropriate evidence supporting it (for example, the basis for the jump in sales per café) and see that it is adequately disclosed. Unless it is supported, she could not report that nothing has come to her attention to suggest that the assumptions do not provide a reasonable basis for the forecast.

:::: example The refund-controls report
Tapri Express Pvt Ltd runs Tapri's delivery app. Its payment partner, PayKaro Ltd, asks for an independent report on whether Tapri Express's controls over customer refunds were suitably designed and operated during 2025-26, measured against the control requirements in the partner agreement. Mehta & Rao, Chartered Accountants, are engaged. Tapri Express's internal audit team, which has already tested the controls, offers its working papers.

Bunty, on loan to Mehta & Rao, drafts the planning note: "Three parties: Mehta & Rao, Tapri Express's internal audit team and PayKaro. Subject matter: Tapri Express's financial statements. Evidence: we can rely on the internal audit team's conclusion that the controls work."

Identify the elements of this assurance engagement, correcting Bunty's note where needed, and state which Engagement Standards apply.

::: answer Model answer
**Relevant provision.** An assurance engagement is one in which a practitioner expresses a conclusion designed to enhance the degree of confidence of the intended users other than the responsible party about the outcome of the evaluation or measurement of a subject matter against criteria. Its elements are a three party relationship, an appropriate subject matter, suitable criteria, sufficient appropriate evidence and a written assurance report in appropriate form.

**Elements in the case.**

1. **Three party relationship.** Practitioner: Mehta & Rao, who provide the assurance. Responsible party: the management of Tapri Express, responsible for the subject matter, the refund controls it designed and operates. Intended users: PayKaro Ltd, for whom the report is prepared. The internal audit team is part of Tapri Express, the responsible party's own organisation, not a separate party: Bunty's list is incorrect.
2. **Subject matter.** The design and operation of the refund controls during 2025-26, not the financial statements: Bunty's note is incorrect.
3. **Suitable criteria.** The control requirements set out in the partner agreement, the benchmark against which the controls are evaluated.
4. **Sufficient appropriate evidence.** Mehta & Rao must obtain sufficient (enough in quantity) and appropriate (of good quality) evidence on which to base their conclusion. The internal audit team's conclusion comes from within the responsible party; it does not replace the evidence the practitioner must obtain, so Bunty's last line is also incorrect.
5. **Written assurance report.** A written report in appropriate form, containing a conclusion that conveys the assurance about the design and operation of the controls.

**Standards.** Assurance regarding the design and operation of controls is an assurance engagement on a matter other than historical financial information: the Standards on Assurance Engagements apply, together with SQC 1 for the firm.

## Under the hood

### Evidence is not optional in a review or a forecast

"Sufficient appropriate evidence" is an element of every assurance engagement, not just audits. ICAI's RTP for January 2026 (MCQ 2) marks as correct that it is to be obtained in audit engagements, review engagements and engagements involving examination of prospective financial information. The Study Material's answer to a client who wants a quarterly assurance report "by skipping required detailed procedures": the report would be in the nature of a review, and the auditors would still have to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence (SM 1.27, 1.41).

### Assurance engagement risk

RTP May 2024 (Q2) adds a sentence the chapter itself does not: audit, a reasonable assurance engagement, aims at reducing assurance engagement risk to an acceptably low level in the circumstances; a review, a limited assurance engagement, at reducing it to a level that is acceptable in the circumstances. Use it to enrich an "SA versus SRE" answer; the four-row table is still the core.

### When the client wants to switch

A client who asks, mid-audit, to turn the audit into a review (an engagement conveying a lower level of assurance) is dealt with by SA 210: the auditor needs reasonable justification before agreeing, and a request to avoid a qualified opinion or disclaimer is not one (SM 11.22–11.23; [[aud.ethics.sa210|SA 210]]).

### SQC 1 and SA 220

Both deal with quality control. SQC 1 deals with all engagements, including audits, reviews and other assurance and related service engagements, and applies to the entire firm; SA 220 applies to audit engagements only, to a particular audit engagement (SM 11.24; [[aud.ethics.qc|SQC 1 and SA 220]]).

::: exam The traps
Three wrong options ICAI keeps offering: a limited assurance engagement obtains "only limited evidence" (it obtains sufficient appropriate evidence and draws limited conclusions); the third party is the bank, the tax department or the internal auditors (it is the intended users); SQC 1 does not apply because no audit report is issued (it applies to every service covered by Engagement Standards).
:::

## Answer frame

### "Which Standards apply, and what assurance?" (a case)

1. **Provision**: the four families of Engagement Standards, with where each applies, and "SQCs are to be applied for all services covered by Engagement Standards".
2. **Facts**: what is examined (historical or prospective; financial or controls) and whether a conclusion giving assurance is expressed.
3. **Analysis**: the family; the Standard the Study Material names, if any (SRE 2400 (Revised), SRE 2410, SAE 3400, SAE 3420, SRS 4400, SRS 4410 (Revised)); the level of assurance.
4. **Conclusion**: "Hence, the Standards on … are relevant; SQC 1 applies".

### "Distinguish reasonable and limited assurance engagements"

The four rows of ICAI's table: level of assurance; procedures (elaborate and extensive, or fewer); conclusions (reasonable, or limited, both on sufficient appropriate evidence); example (audit, or review).

### "Distinguish historical and prospective financial information" and its effect on assurance

Definitions (past events that have occurred; assumptions about future events and possible actions), then the four things the practitioner obtains evidence on, why no opinion on achievement is possible (future-oriented evidence), the "nothing has come to the practitioner's attention" report, and "moderate".

### "Discuss the elements of an assurance engagement"

The five in ICAI's order, one line each, with the three parties defined.

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: "limited assurance, so limited evidence"
Bunty writes that a review needs less than sufficient appropriate evidence. **Fix:** a limited assurance engagement involves obtaining **sufficient appropriate evidence** to draw **limited conclusions**; it performs fewer procedures (SM 1.25). This was the wrong statement in MTP September 2026 Series II MCQ 15.
:::

::: mistake The bank as the third party
Naming the practitioner, management and "the bank" or "the income tax authorities" as the three parties. **Fix:** the third party is the **intended users**, the persons for whom the report is prepared, other than the responsible party.
:::

::: mistake SQC 1 only for audits
Saying SQC 1 does not apply to a compilation because no opinion is issued. **Fix:** SQCs are to be applied for all services covered by Engagement Standards, related services included (SM 1.31; Case Study 2 answer (c), SM 1.36).
:::

::: mistake Certifying the future
Writing that the practitioner confirms a forecast "will be achieved" or checks only its arithmetic. **Fix:** he obtains evidence that the assumptions are not unreasonable, the forecast is properly prepared and presented and material assumptions are disclosed; he cannot give an opinion on achievement.
:::

::: mistake "Assurance means historical financial information"
**Fix:** assurance engagements are not restricted to audit, nor to historical financial information: prospective information and controls are subject matters of assurance engagements too (SM 1.25, 1.38–1.40).
:::

## Practice

::: quiz Check yourself
Q: Case scenario. Kesar's cousin Pinky runs Mehra Masala Traders. Her bank asks for the firm's half-yearly financial statements with "a CA's report". Pinky gives CA Joseph only a trial balance and draft accounts and asks him to put the statements together and sign them; he will not verify anything. Which statement is most appropriate?
- [ ] CA Joseph should issue a review report under the Standards on Review Engagements
- [ ] CA Joseph cannot assist in preparing the statements, because preparation is management's responsibility
- [x] CA Joseph can assist in preparing them and issue a compilation report under the Standards on Related Services, stating that it is not an assurance engagement and no opinion is expressed
- [ ] No ICAI Standard deals with such work, so SQC 1 does not apply
> Assisting management with preparation and presentation of historical financial information without obtaining assurance is a compilation engagement (SRS 4410 (Revised)). It is a related service, and SQC 1 applies to every service covered by Engagement Standards.

Q: Which of the following is NOT true about a limited assurance engagement?
- [ ] It provides a lower level of assurance than a reasonable assurance engagement
- [x] It needs only limited evidence, not sufficient appropriate evidence
- [ ] It performs fewer procedures than a reasonable assurance engagement
- [ ] A review of interim financial information is an example
> It obtains sufficient appropriate evidence to draw limited conclusions. The other three are ICAI's own statements.

Q: In the statutory audit of Tapri Tea Co. Ltd, which combination is the three party relationship?
- [ ] Iyer & Associates, Bunty and Tapri's management
- [ ] Iyer & Associates, Tapri's management and the income tax department
- [ ] Tapri's internal auditors, Tapri's management and the shareholders
- [x] Iyer & Associates, Tapri's management and the intended users of the financial statements
> Practitioner, responsible party (management prepares the financial statements) and intended users. A team member is part of the practitioner; the tax department is one possible user, not the defined third party.

Q: A practitioner examining a five-year projection obtains evidence on several matters. Which of these is NOT one of them?
- [ ] Management's assumptions are not unreasonable
- [x] The projected results will be achieved
- [ ] The projection is properly prepared on the basis of the assumptions
- [ ] All material assumptions are adequately disclosed
> Evidence about the future is itself future-oriented, so no opinion on achievement is possible; the report says nothing has come to the practitioner's attention to suggest the assumptions do not provide a reasonable basis.

Q: Which are collectively known as the Engagement Standards? Select all that apply.
- [x] Standards on Auditing
- [x] Standards on Review Engagements
- [x] Standards on Related Services
- [ ] Standards on Quality Control
> The four Engagement Standards are SAs, SREs, SAEs and SRSs. SQCs are separate: they deal with the firm's system of quality control and apply to all services the Engagement Standards cover.

Q: A Standard requires a procedure that would be ineffective in a particular engagement, so the auditor performs alternative procedures. Which is correct?
- [ ] Disclosing the departure in the report is enough to absolve the auditor from complying with the Standard
- [ ] The auditor simply omits the procedure; nothing needs to be documented
- [x] He documents how the alternative procedures achieve the purpose of the required procedure, and the reason for departure unless it is clear; the report draws attention to the departure
- [ ] The auditor must withdraw from the engagement
> SM 1.32. A mere disclosure in the report does not absolve him from complying with the applicable Standards.

## Recap

- Assurance engagement: a practitioner expresses a conclusion designed to enhance the degree of confidence of intended users other than the responsible party about the evaluation or measurement of a subject matter against criteria.
- Five elements: three party relationship (practitioner, responsible party, intended users); appropriate subject matter; suitable criteria; sufficient appropriate evidence; written assurance report in appropriate form.
- Audit: reasonable assurance (high), elaborate and extensive procedures. Review: limited assurance (lower), fewer procedures, still sufficient appropriate evidence, limited conclusions.
- Prospective financial information: based on assumptions about future events and possible actions; evidence on assumptions not unreasonable, proper preparation, presentation and disclosure; no opinion on achievement; "nothing has come to our attention"; moderate assurance.
- Engagement Standards: SAs (audit of HFI), SREs (review of HFI), SAEs (other assurance), SRSs (agreed-upon procedures, compilation). Purpose: high quality standards and guidance.
- SQC 1: firm's system of quality control; applies to all services covered by Engagement Standards.
- Standards needed for benchmarks at par with global practice, better reporting, uniformity, knowledge and skill, audit quality.
- Departure: document how alternatives achieve the purpose, and the reason unless clear; report draws attention; disclosure does not absolve. (Dev's stamp stays undiscounted.)

## Flashcards

Q:: Define "assurance engagement".
A:: An engagement in which a practitioner expresses a conclusion designed to enhance the degree of confidence of the intended users other than the responsible party about the outcome of the evaluation or measurement of a subject matter against criteria.

Q:: List the five elements of an assurance engagement in ICAI's order.
A:: A three party relationship; an appropriate subject matter; suitable criteria; sufficient appropriate evidence; a written assurance report in appropriate form.

Q:: Assurance engagement: who are the three parties?
A:: The practitioner (provides the assurance), the responsible party (responsible for preparing the subject matter) and the intended users (for whom the report is prepared).

Q:: Reasonable versus limited assurance engagement: four points?
A:: High versus lower level of assurance; elaborate and extensive versus fewer procedures; reasonable versus limited conclusions, both on sufficient appropriate evidence; audit versus review.

Q:: Define prospective financial information.
A:: Financial information based on assumptions about events that may occur in the future and possible actions by an entity: a forecast, a projection or both.

Q:: Prospective financial information: on what does the practitioner obtain evidence?
A:: That management's assumptions are not unreasonable, the information is properly prepared on their basis, it is properly presented, and all material assumptions are adequately disclosed.

Q:: Prospective financial information: what does the report say, and what level of assurance?
A:: That nothing has come to the practitioner's attention to suggest the assumptions do not provide a reasonable basis for the projection; moderate assurance.

Q:: Name the four Engagement Standards and where each applies.
A:: SAs: audit of historical financial information. SREs: review of historical financial information. SAEs: assurance engagements other than audit and review of HFI. SRSs: agreed-upon procedures, compilation and other related services.

Q:: Purpose of the Engagement Standards?
A:: To establish high quality standards and guidance in the areas of financial statement audits and in other types of assurance services.

Q:: To which services does SQC 1 apply?
A:: To all services covered by Engagement Standards: audits and reviews of historical financial information, and other assurance and related services engagements.

Q:: Give the Study Material's example Standards for SREs, SAEs and SRSs.
A:: SRE 2400 (Revised), SRE 2410; SAE 3400, SAE 3420; SRS 4400, SRS 4410 (Revised).

Q:: A required procedure would be ineffective: what must the auditor do?
A:: Document how the alternative procedures achieve its purpose and the reason for the departure unless clear; the report draws attention to the departure; a mere disclosure does not absolve him from complying.
