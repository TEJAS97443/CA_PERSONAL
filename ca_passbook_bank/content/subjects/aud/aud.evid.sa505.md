## In plain words

### The confirmations that came home

Bunty has a brilliant idea for Tapri Tea Co. Ltd's receivables. He gives Tapri's accounts team a list of thirty distributors and asks them to "get balance confirmations and forward them". Two days later, twenty-eight neat replies arrive, all agreeing to the rupee, every one forwarded from sales@tapritea.in.

> **Bunty:** Ma'am, 28 out of 30 agree! The receivables are confirmed.
> **Lakshmi:** Who chose the distributors, Bunty? Who wrote the letters? Who sent them, and where did the replies go?
> **Bunty:** Tapri's team, Ma'am. They know the customers better.
> **Lakshmi:** And they also know what answer we want.

An **external confirmation** is evidence the auditor gets as a **direct written reply from a third party**, such as a customer, a bank or a supplier. It is strong evidence because it comes from outside the company and straight to the auditor. Both halves matter: once the request or the reply passes through the company's hands, it is no longer evidence from an independent source. So SA 505 makes the auditor **keep control** of the whole process: what to confirm, whom to ask, how the request is worded and addressed, and sending it and any follow-ups.

There are two kinds of request. A **positive** request asks the third party to reply either way ("yes, we owe ₹1.10 crore" or "no, we owe ₹62 lakh"), or to fill in the figure. A **negative** request asks for a reply **only if** the third party disagrees. Silence is cheap: it may mean "agreed", or it may mean the letter is lying unopened in a drawer. So negative requests give much weaker evidence, and SA 505 allows them as the only substantive procedure only in four narrow conditions. Replies then fall into four groups: agreement, an unreliable reply, no reply, or a reply showing a difference (an **exception**).

::: analogy The wedding RSVP
Card A says "Please reply by 10 May whether you will attend." Card B says "Reply only if you cannot come." With card A, every reply tells you something, and a missing reply tells you to phone. With card B, silence from a hundred guests might mean a hundred guests, or a hundred cards lost in the post. And either way, you post the cards yourself and have the replies sent to your own address, not to the cousin who keeps saying everyone is coming.
:::

::: key
SA 505's core rule: ==the auditor shall maintain control over external confirmation requests==, from choosing the information and the confirming party to designing, addressing, sending and following up. A reply that passes through the entity is not a direct response to the auditor. Negative confirmations give significantly less persuasive evidence than positive ones.
:::

## How it works

SA 505, "External Confirmations", deals with the auditor's use of external confirmation procedures to obtain audit evidence in accordance with the requirements of SA 500 (SM 4.77).

### 1. Why confirmations are strong evidence

SA 500 says the reliability of audit evidence is influenced by its source and nature, and depends on the circumstances in which it is obtained. Its generalisations include (SM 4.77):

- audit evidence is more reliable when it is obtained from independent sources outside the entity;
- audit evidence obtained directly by the auditor is more reliable than audit evidence obtained indirectly or by inference;
- audit evidence is more reliable when it exists in documentary form, whether paper, electronic or other medium.

So, depending on the circumstances, external confirmations received directly by the auditor from confirming parties may be more reliable than evidence generated internally by the entity (SM 4.77). The **objective** of the auditor, when using external confirmation procedures, is to design and perform such procedures to obtain relevant and reliable audit evidence (SM 4.78). Which assertions a confirmation proves (existence of a receivable, yes; its valuation, no) is covered in [[aud.evid.assertions|assertions and audit procedures]].

### 2. The vocabulary

::: definition External confirmation
Audit evidence obtained as a direct written response to the auditor from a third party (the confirming party), in paper form, or by electronic or other medium (SM 4.78).
:::

| Term | Meaning (SM 4.78) |
|---|---|
| **Positive confirmation request** | A request that the confirming party respond directly to the auditor indicating whether the confirming party agrees or disagrees with the information in the request, or providing the requested information |
| **Negative confirmation request** | A request that the confirming party respond directly to the auditor only if the confirming party disagrees with the information provided in the request |
| **Non-response** | A failure of the confirming party to respond, or fully respond, to a positive confirmation request, or a confirmation request returned undelivered |
| **Exception** | A response that indicates a difference between information requested to be confirmed, or contained in the entity's records, and information provided by the confirming party |

The exception needs to be assessed against the entire population after analysing the reason for the difference (SM 4.78).

### 3. Keeping control of the requests

When using external confirmation procedures, the auditor shall maintain control over external confirmation requests, including (SM 4.79–4.80):

- (a) **determining the information** to be confirmed or requested;
- (b) **selecting the appropriate confirming party**;
- (c) **designing the confirmation requests**, including determining that requests are properly addressed and contain return information for responses to be sent directly to the auditor; and
- (d) **sending the requests**, including follow-up requests when applicable, to the confirming party.

**(a) The information.** Confirmations are frequently used for account balances and their elements. They may also confirm terms of agreements, contracts or transactions between the entity and other parties, or the absence of certain conditions, such as a "side agreement" (SM 4.80).

**(b) The confirming party.** Responses give more relevant and reliable evidence when sent to a confirming party the auditor believes is knowledgeable about the information; for example, a financial institution official who knows the transactions or arrangements concerned (SM 4.80).

**(c) Designing the request.** The design may directly affect the response rate and the reliability and nature of the evidence. Factors to consider include (SM 4.80–4.81):

1. the assertions being addressed;
2. specific identified risks of material misstatement, including fraud risks;
3. the layout and presentation of the confirmation request;
4. prior experience on the audit or similar engagements;
5. the method of communication (for example, in paper form, or by electronic or other medium);
6. management's authorisation or encouragement to the confirming parties to respond to the auditor (confirming parties may only be willing to respond to a request containing management's authorisation);
7. the ability of the intended confirming party to confirm or provide the requested information (for example, an individual invoice amount versus the total balance).

A positive request asks the confirming party to reply in all cases, and a response is ordinarily expected to give reliable evidence. But the party may reply without verifying that the information is correct. The auditor can reduce this risk by using positive requests that **do not state the amount** and ask the party to fill it in; such "blank" requests may, however, get **lower response rates** because they need more effort (SM 4.81). Determining that requests are **properly addressed** includes testing the validity of some or all of the addresses before the requests are sent (SM 4.81).

**(d) Follow-up.** When a reply has not been received within a reasonable time, the auditor may send an additional request; for example, after re-verifying the accuracy of the original address (SM 4.82).

### 4. When management refuses

If management refuses to allow the auditor to send a confirmation request, the auditor shall (SM 4.82):

- (a) **inquire** as to management's reasons for the refusal, and seek audit evidence as to their validity and reasonableness;
- (b) **evaluate the implications** of the refusal on the auditor's assessment of the relevant risks of material misstatement, including the risk of fraud, and on the nature, timing and extent of other audit procedures; and
- (c) **perform alternative audit procedures** designed to obtain relevant and reliable audit evidence.

If the auditor concludes that the refusal is **unreasonable**, or the auditor is **unable to obtain relevant and reliable audit evidence from alternative procedures**, the auditor shall communicate with those charged with governance in accordance with SA 260. The auditor shall also determine the implications for the audit and the auditor's opinion in accordance with SA 705 (SM 4.82–4.83).

Why the reasons matter (SM 4.83–4.84):

- A refusal is a limitation on the evidence the auditor may wish to obtain. A **common reason** advanced is the existence of a legal dispute or ongoing negotiation with the intended confirming party, the resolution of which may be affected by an untimely confirmation request.
- The auditor seeks evidence of the validity and reasonableness of the reasons because of the risk that management may be attempting to **deny access to evidence that may reveal fraud or error**.
- The auditor may revise the assessment of the risks of material misstatement at the assertion level and modify planned procedures; for example, if management's request not to confirm is unreasonable, this may indicate a **fraud risk factor** to be evaluated under SA 240.
- **Alternative audit procedures**, for example: for accounts receivable, examining specific subsequent cash receipts, shipping documentation and sales near the period end; for accounts payable, examining subsequent cash disbursements or correspondence from third parties, and other records such as goods received notes.

### 5. Negative confirmations

Negative confirmations provide **less persuasive** audit evidence than positive confirmations. So the auditor shall not use negative confirmation requests as the **sole substantive audit procedure** to address an assessed risk of material misstatement at the assertion level **unless all of the following are present** (SM 4.85):

- (a) the auditor has assessed the risk of material misstatement as **low** and has obtained sufficient appropriate audit evidence regarding the **operating effectiveness of controls** relevant to the assertion;
- (b) the population comprises a **large number of small, homogeneous** account balances, transactions or conditions;
- (c) a **very low exception rate** is expected; and
- (d) the auditor is **not aware of circumstances** or conditions that would cause recipients to disregard such requests.

Why silence is weak (SM 4.85): the failure to receive a response to a negative request does not explicitly indicate that the intended confirming party received the request or verified the information in it. So a failure to respond to a negative request gives significantly less persuasive evidence than a response to a positive request. Confirming parties are also more likely to respond indicating disagreement when the information is **not in their favour**, and less likely to respond otherwise. Holders of bank deposit accounts, for example, are more likely to respond if they believe their balance is understated in the request; negative requests to them may help in considering whether balances are **understated**, but are unlikely to be effective for **overstatement**.

### 6. Evaluating the evidence

The auditor shall evaluate whether the results of the external confirmation procedures provide relevant and reliable audit evidence, or whether performing further audit procedures is necessary (SM 4.86). The results of individual requests may be categorised as:

- (a) a response by the appropriate confirming party indicating agreement with the information provided in the request, or providing requested information without exception;
- (b) a response deemed unreliable;
- (c) a non-response; or
- (d) a response indicating an exception.

This evaluation, taken with the other procedures performed, helps the auditor conclude whether sufficient appropriate audit evidence has been obtained or whether further audit procedures are necessary, as SA 330 requires (SM 4.86; [[aud.risk.sa330|SA 330]]).

## Worked examples

:::: example The confirmations that came home
Continue Bunty's story above. For Tapri's 2025-26 audit, Bunty asked Tapri's accounts team to choose thirty distributors, draft the confirmation requests, email them from Tapri's server and forward the replies, which arrived via sales@tapritea.in. He wants to file the 28 agreeing replies as evidence of the receivables.

Comment on Bunty's approach with reference to SA 505, and state how it should be done.

::: answer Model answer
**Relevant provision.** As per SA 505, "External Confirmations", when using external confirmation procedures, the auditor shall maintain control over external confirmation requests, including (a) determining the information to be confirmed or requested; (b) selecting the appropriate confirming party; (c) designing the confirmation requests, including determining that requests are properly addressed and contain return information for responses to be sent directly to the auditor; and (d) sending the requests, including follow-up requests when applicable, to the confirming party. An external confirmation is audit evidence obtained as a direct written response to the auditor from a third party.

**Facts of the case.** Tapri's own team chose the distributors, designed and sent the requests and received the replies, which reached the auditor only through Tapri's mailbox.

**Analysis.** None of the four elements of control was with the auditor. Replies routed through the entity are not direct written responses to the auditor, and the entity could have chosen, altered or filtered them. They are not reliable external confirmations.

**Conclusion.** Bunty's approach is not in line with SA 505, and the 28 replies cannot be relied on as external confirmations. Iyer & Associates should select the distributors, decide the information to be confirmed, design the requests (with management's authorisation to respond, if needed), verify the addresses, send the requests themselves with their own return address, follow up non-responses and receive all replies directly.
:::
::::

:::: example Kesar blocks one confirmation
Lakshmi selects Annapurna Retail Pvt Ltd, which owes Tapri ₹3.20 crore, for a positive confirmation. Kesar refuses: Annapurna has filed a suit in the commercial court over a damaged consignment, settlement talks are at a delicate stage, and "a letter from the auditors now will spoil everything". Lakshmi sees the court filing and the settlement correspondence, which show the dispute is genuine and near settlement; she finds no fraud risk factors.

How should Lakshmi proceed? Would your answer change if the refusal were unreasonable?

::: answer Model answer
**Relevant provision.** As per SA 505, if management refuses to allow the auditor to send a confirmation request, the auditor shall (a) inquire as to management's reasons and seek audit evidence as to their validity and reasonableness; (b) evaluate the implications of the refusal on the assessment of the relevant risks of material misstatement, including the risk of fraud, and on the nature, timing and extent of other procedures; and (c) perform alternative audit procedures designed to obtain relevant and reliable audit evidence. A common reason advanced is a legal dispute or ongoing negotiation with the confirming party whose resolution may be affected by an untimely request. If the refusal is unreasonable, or alternative procedures do not provide relevant and reliable evidence, the auditor shall communicate with those charged with governance in accordance with SA 260 and determine the implications for the audit and the opinion in accordance with SA 705.

**Facts of the case.** The reason given is a pending suit and settlement talks; the court filing and correspondence support it; no fraud risk factors exist.

**Analysis.** The reason is one SA 505 recognises, and it is supported by evidence, so the refusal is reasonable. Lakshmi should still re-assess the risk for this balance and perform alternative procedures, such as examining specific subsequent cash receipts from Annapurna, shipping documentation for the invoices and sales near the period end. The dispute may also bear on the valuation of the receivable.

**Conclusion.** The refusal is justified; Lakshmi should rely on alternative procedures for this balance. If the refusal were unreasonable, it might indicate a fraud risk factor to be evaluated under SA 240; and if it were unreasonable or the alternative procedures failed, she would communicate with those charged with governance (for Tapri, its board and audit committee) under SA 260 and determine the effect on her opinion under SA 705.
:::

:::: example Thirty replies, four kinds
Iyer & Associates sent positive confirmation requests, with the amounts stated, to 30 of Tapri's distributors, with balances totalling ₹24.60 crore. Results after one follow-up:

| Result | Requests | Balance per Tapri (₹ crore) |
|---|---|---|
| Agreed, directly to the auditor | 21 | 15.82 |
| Replied with a lower balance | 3 | 2.43 |
| No reply even after follow-up | 4 | 5.20 |
| Replies from Gmail addresses not matching the distributors' records | 2 | 1.15 |
| **Total** | **30** | **24.60** |

The three lower replies: Kaveri Distributors (₹1.10 crore per Tapri) shows ₹48 lakh less, a payment Tapri received on 2 April; Malabar Chai Agencies (₹78 lakh) shows ₹22 lakh less, for goods dispatched on 30 March and received on 3 April; Shree Ganesh Traders (₹55 lakh) shows ₹35 lakh less, rejecting goods as sub-standard.

Classify the results as SA 505 does and state the further action for each.

::: answer Model answer
**Relevant provision.** As per SA 505, the auditor shall evaluate whether the results of the external confirmation procedures provide relevant and reliable audit evidence, or whether further audit procedures are necessary. Results may be categorised as (a) a response indicating agreement, or providing requested information without exception; (b) a response deemed unreliable; (c) a non-response; (d) a response indicating an exception. A non-response includes a failure to respond to a positive request; an exception is a response indicating a difference between the information requested and that provided by the confirming party, and is to be assessed against the entire population after analysing the reason for the difference.

| Category | Requests | ₹ crore | Further action |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Agreement | 21 | 15.82 | Relevant and reliable evidence of existence |
| (d) Exception | 3 | 2.43 | Analyse each difference |
| (c) Non-response | 4 | 5.20 | Alternative procedures |
| (b) Unreliable | 2 | 1.15 | Verify the source; further procedures |

**Analysis.**

1. **Exceptions (difference ₹1.05 crore).** Kaveri (₹48 lakh) and Malabar (₹22 lakh) are timing differences of ₹70 lakh: verify the 2 April receipt in the bank statement and the transporter's records for the 30 March dispatch. Shree Ganesh's ₹35 lakh is a dispute: it may be a misstatement (sales returns or a provision), so its cause must be investigated and its effect assessed against the whole receivables population; where the 30 were an audit sample, SA 530 requires the misstatement to be projected to the population ([[aud.evid.sa530|SA 530]]).
2. **Non-responses (₹5.20 crore, 21.1% of the value tested).** No evidence yet: perform alternative procedures such as examining subsequent cash receipts, shipping documentation and sales near the period end.
3. **Unreliable responses (₹1.15 crore).** Replies from addresses that do not match the distributors' records cannot be relied on: re-verify the addresses, send fresh requests to the verified addresses, and consider whether this indicates a fraud risk.

**Conclusion.** Only the 21 agreements (₹15.82 crore, 64.3% of the value tested) are relevant and reliable evidence as they stand; the remaining ₹8.78 crore needs the procedures above before Iyer & Associates can conclude, under SA 330, that sufficient appropriate audit evidence has been obtained.
:::

:::: example Can negative confirmations do the job?
State with reasons whether negative confirmation requests may be the sole substantive procedure in each case.

(a) Tapri holds security deposits from 1,850 café franchisees, averaging ₹40,000 each (₹7.40 crore in all). Iyer & Associates have assessed the risk of material misstatement as low, tested the controls over deposits and found them operating effectively, expect very few differences, and know of nothing that would make franchisees ignore a request.

(b) Six large distributors owe Tapri between ₹1.20 crore and ₹4 crore each; the auditor has assessed the risk of material misstatement for these receivables as high.

::: answer Model answer
**Relevant provision.** As per SA 505, the auditor shall not use negative confirmation requests as the sole substantive audit procedure to address an assessed risk of material misstatement at the assertion level unless all of the following are present: (a) the risk of material misstatement is assessed as low and sufficient appropriate evidence has been obtained regarding the operating effectiveness of controls relevant to the assertion; (b) the population comprises a large number of small, homogeneous account balances, transactions or conditions; (c) a very low exception rate is expected; and (d) the auditor is not aware of circumstances that would cause recipients to disregard such requests.

**(a) Permitted.** All four conditions are present: low risk with controls tested, 1,850 small and similar balances, a very low exception rate expected and no reason for franchisees to disregard requests. Since deposit holders are more likely to reply when a balance understates what Tapri owes them, the requests suit testing for understatement of this liability.

**(b) Not permitted.** The risk is high, and the population is a few large balances rather than many small ones, so conditions (a) and (b) fail. Positive requests (possibly without stating the amounts) and other substantive procedures are needed.

## Under the hood

### Small print that decides MCQs

- **Blank requests.** A positive request without the amount reduces the risk that the party agrees without checking, at the cost of lower response rates (SM 4.81).
- **Management's authorisation** in the request is a design factor, not a loss of control: some parties will reply only if management authorises it (SM 4.81).
- **Beyond balances.** Confirmations can cover terms of agreements, contracts and transactions, and the absence of conditions such as a "side agreement" (SM 4.80); a third party's confirmation of inventory it holds is SA 501's procedure ([[aud.evid.sa501|SA 501]]).
- **Internal audit.** Delegating selection, design and receipt of confirmations to the internal audit department, with replies to its mailbox, is not in line with SA 505 (SM 4.86, 4.140, Test Your Understanding 8); see [[aud.evid.sa610|SA 610]].
- **A justified refusal.** A CFO's refusal for a customer in a sub judice quality dispute, near resolution with no fraud risk factors, is justifiable (SM 4.86–4.87, 4.140–4.141, Test Your Understanding 9).
- **The date and the form (audit of receivables, SM 5.39–5.40).** Trade receivables may be asked to confirm balances as at the balance sheet date or as at another selected date reasonably close to it, decided by the auditor in consultation with the company; so ICAI's September 2025 MCQ 8 marked "should necessarily be as on the date of balance sheet" as not correct. The same pages say that all significant balances should necessarily be confirmed, call the form without the balance "preferable" (SA 505 adds that such blank requests may get lower response rates, SM 4.81), and let the entity prepare the request letters from the auditor's list while the auditor keeps strict control of their correctness and despatch, with replies and undelivered letters returned to the auditor, not the client. How receivables are audited is in [[aud.items.current|audit of current assets]].

## Answer frame

### A case question on SA 505

1. **Relevant provision**: "As per SA 505, External Confirmations, …", the list the facts call for.
2. **Facts of the case**: who controlled the requests; the type of request; the population and risk; the refusal and its reason; the replies.
3. **Analysis**: element by element, condition by condition, reply by reply.
4. **Conclusion**: proper or not; which request type; justified refusal or TCWG and SA 705; what further procedures.

### "Is the CFO's refusal justified?"

::: cols
**Loses marks**

"No. The auditor must always confirm."

Ignores SA 505's steps and the legitimate reasons.
---
**Passes**

"The auditor inquires into the reasons and performs alternative procedures."

Two of the three steps; no test of reasonableness.
---
**Full marks**

All three steps; the common reason (legal dispute or negotiation) and the fraud risk; a conclusion on the facts; and what follows if the refusal is unreasonable or alternatives fail (TCWG under SA 260, SA 705).

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: replies forwarded by the client
Accepting confirmations that came back through the entity's mailbox. **Fix:** the response must be a direct written response to the auditor; the auditor sends the requests and receives the replies.
:::

::: mistake Silence counted as agreement
Treating unanswered negative requests as "confirmed". **Fix:** a failure to respond to a negative request does not show the request was received or the information verified; it is significantly less persuasive evidence.
:::

::: mistake Negative requests for the big balances
**Fix:** negative requests can be the sole substantive procedure only with low risk and tested controls, many small homogeneous items, a very low expected exception rate, and no reason for recipients to ignore them.
:::

::: mistake Every exception is a misstatement
**Fix:** analyse the reason first: payments and goods in transit are timing differences; disputes may be misstatements, to be assessed against the whole population.
:::

## Recap

- External confirmation: audit evidence obtained as a direct written response to the auditor from a third party, on paper or by electronic or other medium.
- Positive: reply in all cases (agree, disagree or fill in); negative: reply only if disagreeing; non-response: no or incomplete reply to a positive request, or returned undelivered; exception: a difference.
- Control: determine the information, select the confirming party, design (properly addressed, replies to the auditor), send and follow up.
- Blank positive requests reduce agree-without-checking, but lower response rates.
- Refusal: inquire and test the reasons, re-assess risks (including fraud), perform alternatives; if unreasonable or alternatives fail, TCWG under SA 260 and SA 705.
- Negative requests as the sole substantive procedure only if: low risk with controls tested, many small homogeneous items, very low expected exceptions, no reason to disregard.
- Results: agreement, unreliable, non-response, exception; evaluate, then decide on further procedures (SA 330).
- If the reply went to sales@client, it confirmed nothing.

## Flashcards

Q:: SA 505: define external confirmation.
A:: Audit evidence obtained as a direct written response to the auditor from a third party (the confirming party), in paper form, or by electronic or other medium.

Q:: SA 505: positive versus negative confirmation request?
A:: Positive: the confirming party responds directly to the auditor indicating agreement or disagreement, or providing the information. Negative: it responds directly to the auditor only if it disagrees with the information in the request.

Q:: SA 505: define non-response.
A:: A failure of the confirming party to respond, or fully respond, to a positive confirmation request, or a confirmation request returned undelivered.

Q:: SA 505: define exception.
A:: A response that indicates a difference between information requested to be confirmed, or contained in the entity's records, and information provided by the confirming party.

Q:: SA 505: over what must the auditor maintain control?
A:: External confirmation requests, including determining the information to be confirmed, selecting the appropriate confirming party, designing the requests (properly addressed, replies directly to the auditor) and sending them, including follow-ups.

Q:: SA 505: list the factors in designing confirmation requests.
A:: Assertions addressed; identified risks including fraud; layout and presentation; prior experience; method of communication; management's authorisation or encouragement to respond; the confirming party's ability to confirm the information.

Q:: SA 505: what three steps when management refuses a confirmation request?
A:: Inquire into the reasons and seek evidence of their validity and reasonableness; evaluate the implications for risks (including fraud) and other procedures; perform alternative audit procedures.

Q:: SA 505: when does a refusal go to those charged with governance?
A:: When the auditor concludes the refusal is unreasonable, or cannot obtain relevant and reliable evidence from alternative procedures; the auditor communicates under SA 260 and determines the implications under SA 705.

Q:: SA 505: when may negative requests be the sole substantive procedure?
A:: Only if all are present: RoMM assessed as low with controls tested as operating effectively; a large number of small, homogeneous items; a very low expected exception rate; no known reason for recipients to disregard the requests.

Q:: SA 505: how may the results of individual requests be categorised?
A:: Agreement (or information provided without exception); a response deemed unreliable; a non-response; a response indicating an exception.

Q:: SA 505: alternative procedures for receivables and payables?
A:: Receivables: subsequent cash receipts, shipping documentation, sales near the period end. Payables: subsequent cash disbursements, correspondence from third parties, other records such as goods received notes.
