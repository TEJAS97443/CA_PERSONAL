## In plain words

### The kiln that came with a playback singer

Tapri Tea Co. Ltd has bought the biggest machine in its history: an automated kiln at the new Dewas plant that fires 60,000 clay kulhads a day. Kesar celebrated with a ribbon-cutting, a playback singer and a newspaper campaign. Then he sent every bill to accounts with one instruction.

> **Kesar:** It is all for the kiln, no? Put everything in the kiln. Sab adjust ho jayega.
> **Bunty:** *(ticking the singer's invoice)* Ma'am, it says "kiln inauguration". So it is kiln cost.
> **Lakshmi:** Bunty, can the singer fire a kulhad?
> **Bunty:** No, ma'am.
> **Lakshmi:** Then she is not part of the kiln.

That exchange is most of the audit of **property, plant and equipment (PPE)**: the land, buildings, plant, furniture, vehicles, office equipment and computers a business holds to use, not to sell.

**Intangible assets** (software, brands, designs, licences, goodwill) face the same questions with one twist: you cannot touch an app. So the auditor proves that an intangible exists by showing that it is **in use**.

::: analogy Buying a flat in Indore
Before you call a flat yours, you visit it (it exists), read the sale deed in your name (you own it; if your home-loan bank keeps the original deed, the bank confirms it holds it), count stamp duty in the price but not the housewarming party (the right cost), and check the society's register (disclosure). An auditor does the same for every building, kiln and app, and also asks how much value has worn away (depreciation).
:::

::: key
For PPE and intangible assets the auditor proves five things: the asset **exists** (physically verified, or for an intangible, in active use); every addition and deletion is **completely** recorded; the entity has **rights** to it (invoices and title deeds in its name, charges known); it is **valued** at cost as AS 10 or AS 26 allows, less depreciation or amortisation and impairment; and it is **disclosed** as Schedule III requires.
:::

### 1. What may be capitalised: AS 10

First the auditor analyses whether expenditure on PPE is capital or revenue in nature (SM 5.56). The test is AS 10 (Revised).

::: definition Recognition criteria for PPE
The cost of an item of property, plant and equipment should be recognised as an asset if, and only if: (a) it is probable that future economic benefits associated with the item will flow to the enterprise, and (b) the cost of the item can be measured reliably. An enterprise evaluates under this recognition principle all its costs on PPE at the time they are incurred. These include costs incurred (a) initially to acquire or construct an item; and (b) subsequently to add to, replace part of, or service it (SM 5.56–5.57).
:::

An item that qualifies is **measured at its cost**. The cost comprises (SM 5.57):

1. its **purchase price**, including import duties and non-refundable purchase taxes, after deducting trade discounts and rebates;
2. any **costs directly attributable** to bringing the asset to the location and condition necessary for it to be capable of operating in the manner intended by management;
3. the **initial estimate of the costs of dismantling, removing the item and restoring the site** on which it is located, referred to as decommissioning, restoration and similar liabilities, the obligation for which an enterprise incurs either when the item is acquired or as a consequence of having used the item during a particular period for purposes other than to produce inventories during that period.

**Examples of directly attributable costs** (SM 5.57–5.58):

- (a) costs of employee benefits (as defined in AS 15, Employee Benefits) arising directly from the construction or acquisition of the item of property, plant and equipment;
- (b) costs of site preparation;
- (c) initial delivery and handling costs;
- (d) installation and assembly costs;
- (e) costs of testing whether the asset is functioning properly, after deducting the net proceeds from selling any items produced while bringing the asset to that location and condition (such as samples produced when testing equipment); and
- (f) professional fees.

**Examples of costs that are not costs of an item of PPE** (SM 5.58):

- (a) costs of opening a new facility or business, such as inauguration costs;
- (b) costs of introducing a new product or service (including costs of advertising and promotional activities);
- (c) costs of conducting business in a new location or with a new class of customer (including costs of staff training); and
- (d) administration and other general overhead costs.

The expenses have to be analysed and properly classified: revenue expenses, like regular repairs on assets, are charged off to the Statement of Profit and Loss (SM 5.58).

### 2. Existence: physical verification

To establish the existence of PPE as at the period-end, the auditor (SM 5.58–5.59):

::: steps
1. **Reviews the entity's plan** for performing physical verification of PPE: whether it is performed by its own staff or by a third party, and the policy on periodicity (annually, or once in two or three years).
2. **Examines evidence of appropriate supervision** of those performing the physical verification.
3. **Obtains the physical verification report**, backed by the working sheets, and assesses whether all items of PPE are properly **tagged** and carry identification marks or numbers, and whether the working papers capture the identification numbers of the assets verified.
4. **Reconciles** the items physically verified with the **fixed asset register** as at the date or period of verification, specifically verifying that additions up to the date of verification have been updated in the register.
5. **Verifies the discrepancies** noted and how they have been dealt with in the books and financial statements: for example, identified shortages and assets not in working condition or not in active use should be accounted for as **deletions** in the books after approval by management, and depreciation should have ceased to be charged after the date of deletion.
:::

### 3. Completeness: the movement schedule, additions and deletions

The completeness assertion here is that additions to PPE during the period have been recorded, and that PPE belonging to third parties is not included but PPE owned and controlled by the entity and lying with a third party is (SM 5.59). The auditor verifies the PPE movement schedule that management compiles for each class of asset (building, plant and machinery and so on), tallies the closing balances to the books, checks its arithmetical accuracy and tallies the opening balances to the previous year's audited financial statements (SM 5.59):

::: formula The PPE movement schedule
$$\text{Opening balance} + \text{Additions} - \text{Deletions} = \text{Closing balance}$$
Class by class; the same identity applies to intangibles (SM 5.67).
:::

For **additions**, the auditor obtains management's listing of all additions and (SM 5.60–5.61):

- for all material additions, verifies whether the expenditure meets the criteria of PPE as per AS 10 (Revised), covering costs incurred initially to acquire or construct an item and costs incurred subsequently to add to, replace part of, or service it, and that the cost of the item is as per AS 10 (Revised);
- checks that spare parts, stand-by equipment and servicing equipment are recognised as PPE only when they meet its definition (otherwise they are classified as inventory), and that the entity is not recognising the costs of **day-to-day servicing** in the carrying amount of an item;
- tests the purchase invoice, installation certificate or report or other similar documentation to verify the **date of addition**, for the samples of additions selected;
- verifies whether the additions have been **approved by authorised personnel**;
- verifies whether proper internal processes and procedures, like inviting **competitive quotations or floating tenders**, were followed before finalising the vendor for procuring PPE or awarding a work contract for capital projects, by checking the supporting documents of the samples selected.

For **deletions** the auditor (SM 5.61) understands from management the reason and rationale for the deletion (for example, a new purchase of a similar asset once the old asset was no longer fit to be used in the production process) and the manner of disposal; obtains management's approval and the **discard note** authorising disposal of the asset from its active use; verifies the process followed for the sale of discarded PPE (for example, inviting competitive quotes, tenders, and the basis of calculating the sale proceeds); and verifies that management has accurately recorded the deletion (original cost and accumulated depreciation up to the date of disposal) and the resulting gain or loss on disposal.

### 4. Valuation: depreciation and impairment

PPE loses value through efflux of time, use and obsolescence. That loss is a cost of earning the period's revenue; unless it is charged as depreciation, profit is wrong and PPE is shown at higher amounts (SM 5.61). The auditor should (SM 5.61–5.62):

- verify that depreciation is charged on **all** items of PPE, unless an item is non-depreciable, like freehold land;
- assess that the **depreciation method** reflects the pattern in which the asset's future economic benefits are expected to be consumed: straight line, diminishing value or units of production, as applicable;
- verify that management has made an **impairment assessment** as per AS 28, Impairment of Assets.

How the charge itself is recomputed (rates, residual value, components, the date the asset is ready for use) is in [[aud.items.expenses|the audit of depreciation and amortisation]].

### 5. Rights and obligations: whose kiln is it?

The assertion: the entity has valid legal ownership rights over the PPE claimed to be held by it and recorded in the financial statements. In addition to the completeness procedures on additions, the auditor (SM 5.62):

1. verifies that all PPE purchase invoices are **in the name of the entity**, which entitles it to legal title;
2. for all additions to land and building, checks the **conveyance deed or sale deed** to see whether the entity is the legal and valid owner;
3. insists on and verifies the **original title deeds** of all immovable properties held at the balance sheet date;
4. where a property is given as security for borrowings and the original deeds are not with the entity, asks management to obtain a **confirmation from the lenders** that they hold the original title deeds as security;
5. verifies the **register of charges** to see whether any charge has been created against the PPE.

### 6. Intangible assets: the asset you cannot tap

::: definition Intangible asset
An identifiable non-monetary asset, without physical substance, held for use in the production or supply of goods or services, for rental to others, or for administrative purposes (SM 5.65).
:::

Common examples are computer software, patents, copyrights, motion picture films, customer lists, mortgage servicing rights, fishing licences, import quotas, franchises, customer or supplier relationships, customer loyalty, market share and marketing rights; goodwill is another example, which either arises on acquisition or may be internally generated (SM 5.66). Not all of them meet the definition of an intangible asset, that is, **identifiability, control over a resource and expectation of future economic benefits** flowing to the enterprise; expenditure on an item that does not is recognised as an expense when incurred. Four rules settle most exam cases (SM 5.66, 5.68):

- **Internally generated goodwill** should not be recognised as an asset (AS 26).
- The **physical substance** containing an intangible (a compact disk for software, legal documentation for a licence or patent, film for a motion picture) usually costs little, so though tangible it is commonly treated as part of the intangible asset.
- Where an asset has inseparable intangible and tangible elements, judgement decides which is predominant. Software that is an **integral part of the related hardware** (for a computer-controlled machine tool that cannot operate without that specific software, or a computer's operating system) is treated as a **fixed asset**; software that is not an integral part of the related hardware is an **intangible asset**.
- No intangible asset arises from **research** (or the research phase of an internal project): research expenditure is an expense when incurred.

The procedures follow the same assertions as PPE (SM 5.66–5.69):

- **Existence.** Since an intangible has no physical substance, verify that it is **in active use** in the production or supply of goods or services, for rental to others or for administration. For software, verify the sale of the related goods or services during the period in which it was used; for designs or drawings, verify production data showing that the products are being produced and sold. An intangible **not in active use** should have been deleted in the books after management's approval, and amortisation should have ceased beyond the date of deletion.
- **Completeness** (all additions recorded appropriately). Verify the movement in the intangible assets schedule compiled by management, class-wise (software, designs and drawings, goodwill and so on): opening balances + additions − deletions = closing balances; tally the closing balances to the books and check its arithmetical accuracy. Obtain a listing of all additions and:
  - for all material additions, verify whether the expenditure meets the criterion for recognition of an intangible asset as per AS 26;
  - ensure that no intangible asset arising from **research** (or from the research phase of an internal project) is recognised: that expenditure is an expense when incurred;
  - check the certificate or report or other similar documentation to verify the **date of use** of the intangible, which could be linked to the date of commencement of commercial production or economic use, for all additions;
  - verify whether the additions (acquisitions) have been approved by appropriate personnel;
  - verify whether proper internal processes, like inviting competitive quotations or proper tenders, were followed before finalising the vendor, by testing those documents on a sample basis;
  - for **deletions**, as for PPE: understand the reason, rationale and manner of disposal; obtain management's approval and the **disposal note** authorising disposal of the asset from its active use; verify the process followed for the sale of the discarded asset; and verify the accurate recording of the original cost, accumulated amortisation up to the date of disposal and the resulting gain or loss.
- **Valuation.** The value of intangible assets may diminish due to efflux of time, use or obsolescence; unless amortisation is charged, profit is wrong and the assets are shown at higher amounts. Verify that amortisation is charged on all intangible assets; that the amortisation method reflects the pattern in which the asset's future economic benefits are expected to be consumed; and whether management has done an impairment assessment, applying AS 28 to review the carrying amount and determine the recoverable amount and any impairment loss.
- **Rights and obligations.** In addition to the completeness procedures on additions, verify that all expense invoices and purchase contracts are in the name of the entity, which entitles it to legal title.

### Schedule III: the PPE note (SM 5.63)

*Learn the classification in this order; "state the disclosures" questions mark each head.*

1. **Classification**: (a) Land; (b) Buildings; (c) Plant and Equipment; (d) Furniture and Fixtures; (e) Vehicles; (f) Office equipment; (g) Others (specify nature).
2. **Assets under lease** separately specified under each class of asset.
3. A **reconciliation of the gross and net carrying amounts** of each class at the beginning and end of the reporting period, showing additions, disposals, acquisitions through business combinations, the amount of change due to revaluation (if the change is 10% or more in the aggregate of the net carrying value of each class) and other adjustments, and the related depreciation and impairment losses or reversals, disclosed separately.
4. Where sums have been **written off on a reduction of capital or revaluation**, or **added on revaluation**, every subsequent balance sheet shows the reduced or increased figures and, by a note, the amount of the reduction or increase with its date, for the first **five years** after it.

### Schedule III: the intangible assets note (SM 5.70)

1. **Classification**: (a) Goodwill; (b) Brands/trademarks; (c) Computer software; (d) Mastheads and publishing titles; (e) Mining rights; (f) Copyrights, and patents and other intellectual property rights, services and operating rights; (g) Recipes, formulae, models, designs and prototypes; (h) Licences and franchise; (i) Others (specify nature).
2. The **reconciliation** of gross and net carrying amounts, as for PPE (10% revaluation test for each class of intangible assets).
3. The **five-year note** on write-offs and revaluation, as for PPE.

### Additional Regulatory Information (SM 5.64–5.65, 5.71)

**(i) Title deeds of immovable property not held in the name of the company.** Details of all immovable property (other than properties where the company is the lessee and the lease agreements are duly executed in favour of the lessee) whose title deeds are not held in the name of the company; where the property is jointly held with others, details are given to the extent of the company's share. Columns, in order: relevant line item in the Balance Sheet (PPE, investment property, PPE retired from active use and held for disposal: land and building each; others); description of item of property; gross carrying value; title deeds held in the name of; whether the holder is a promoter, director, relative of a promoter or director, or employee of a promoter or director (as defined in the Companies Act, 2013); property held since which date; reason for not being held in the name of the company (also indicate if in dispute).

**(ii) Revaluation.** Whether the revaluation of PPE is based on the valuation by a **registered valuer** as defined under rule 2 of the Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017.

**(iii) Capital work-in-progress (CWIP).** (a) The ageing schedule below, whose total shall tally with CWIP in the balance sheet; (b) for CWIP whose completion is overdue or has exceeded its cost compared to its original plan, a completion schedule (Project 1, Project 2 ... against "to be completed in" less than 1 year, 1–2 years, 2–3 years, more than 3 years), with details of projects where activity has been suspended given separately.

## Worked examples

:::: example The kiln's real cost
Tapri Tea Co. Ltd installed a kulhad kiln at its Dewas plant in 2025-26. Management capitalised ₹4,88,40,000, made up of:

| Item | Amount (₹) |
|---|---:|
| Kiln, list price ₹3,60,00,000 less 5% trade discount | 3,42,00,000 |
| Basic customs duty | 34,20,000 |
| IGST on import (input tax credit taken) | 68,40,000 |
| Freight and transit insurance to Dewas | 6,50,000 |
| Site levelling and foundation | 8,20,000 |
| Installation and assembly by the supplier | 5,80,000 |
| Salaries of Tapri engineers, for the installation only | 2,40,000 |
| Clay and fuel used in trial firing | 3,10,000 |
| Consulting engineer's fees | 1,50,000 |
| Inauguration ceremony | 4,50,000 |
| Launch advertising | 7,00,000 |
| Training of plant staff | 1,80,000 |
| Head office administration overheads allocated | 3,00,000 |
| **Total** | **4,88,40,000** |

The 40,000 kulhads fired in trials were sold for ₹60,000, credited to other income. The kiln stands on leased land, which the lease requires Tapri to restore; management's initial estimate of dismantling and restoration is ₹9,00,000, not recorded anywhere.

Required:

(i) Compute the cost at which the kiln should be recognised.

(ii) State the amount by which PPE is misstated, and reconcile it.

::: answer Show the working
As per AS 10 (Revised), cost comprises the purchase price (with import duties and non-refundable purchase taxes, less trade discounts and rebates), the directly attributable costs and the initial estimate of dismantling and restoration costs.

**(i) Cost of the kiln as per AS 10 (Revised)**

| Element | Amount (₹) |
|---|---:|
| Purchase price after trade discount | 3,42,00,000 |
| Import duty (non-refundable) | 34,20,000 |
| Initial delivery and handling | 6,50,000 |
| Site preparation | 8,20,000 |
| Installation and assembly | 5,80,000 |
| Employee benefits arising directly from the acquisition | 2,40,000 |
| Testing, ₹3,10,000 less proceeds of trial output ₹60,000 | 2,50,000 |
| Professional fees | 1,50,000 |
| Initial estimate of dismantling and site restoration | 9,00,000 |
| **Cost to be recognised** | **4,12,10,000** |

**Working notes**

1. IGST of ₹68,40,000 is recoverable as input tax credit; only non-refundable taxes form part of cost. It is a balance with government authorities.
2. Inauguration ₹4,50,000, launch advertising ₹7,00,000, staff training ₹1,80,000 and allocated overheads ₹3,00,000 (together ₹16,30,000) are not costs of PPE; they are charged to the Statement of Profit and Loss.
3. The ₹60,000 from trial output reduces the cost of testing; it is not other income.
4. The dismantling estimate is part of cost; its other side is a provision (see [[aud.items.liabilities|provisions]]).

**(ii) Misstatement**

| Reconciliation | Amount (₹) |
|---|---:|
| Capitalised by management | 4,88,40,000 |
| Less: IGST, input tax credit | (68,40,000) |
| Less: trial output proceeds | (60,000) |
| Less: costs that are not PPE | (16,30,000) |
| Add: dismantling and restoration estimate | 9,00,000 |
| **Cost as per AS 10 (Revised)** | **4,12,10,000** |

PPE is overstated by ₹76,30,000 (₹4,88,40,000 − ₹4,12,10,000). Profit is overstated by ₹16,30,000 + ₹60,000 = ₹16,90,000 before the depreciation effect; other current assets are understated by ₹68,40,000 and provisions by ₹9,00,000. The auditor should ask management to correct the misstatement and, if it is not corrected, evaluate it under [[aud.complete.sa450|SA 450]].
:::

::::: example The deed in the bank's locker
In 2025-26 Tapri bought an Indore warehouse for ₹12.60 crore and mortgaged it to State Bank of India, which holds the original sale deed; the accounts team shows Bunty a photocopy. The fixed asset register also holds café land at Bhopal (gross carrying value ₹2.80 crore), registered since 2019 in the name of Kesar Mehra, promoter and managing director, "because the seller wanted to deal with him". Physical verification could not find 14 deep fryers at the Jaipur café (cost ₹9.80 lakh). The café manager says they were scrapped on 30 September 2025, with no approval or discard note; they are still in the register, with accumulated depreciation of ₹6.60 lakh at 31 March 2026, including ₹0.70 lakh charged for the six months after scrapping.

Advise Iyer & Associates on the procedures to establish Tapri's rights over its PPE, and on how the Bhopal land and the missing fryers should be dealt with.

:::: answer Model answer

**Relevant provision.** To establish rights and obligations over PPE, the auditor should verify that purchase invoices are in the entity's name; check the conveyance or sale deed for additions to land and building; insist on and verify the original title deeds of all immovable properties; where a property is given as security and the original deeds are not with the entity, request management to obtain the lenders' confirmation that they hold them; and verify the register of charges. Schedule III requires, as Additional Regulatory Information, details of immovable property whose title deeds are not held in the company's name. Shortages found on physical verification are accounted for as deletions after management's approval, and depreciation ceases after the date of deletion.

**Facts of the case.** The Indore deed is with SBI; the Bhopal land stands in the promoter-director's name; 14 fryers, scrapped on 30 September 2025, remain in the books at a net carrying amount of ₹3.20 lakh (₹9.80 lakh − ₹6.60 lakh), still depreciated.

**Analysis.**

1. **Indore warehouse.** A photocopy is not sufficient: the auditor should check the sale deed for Tapri's name, ask management to obtain SBI's confirmation that it holds the original deed as security, and verify the charge in the register of charges.
2. **Bhopal land.** The title deed is not in Tapri's name, so the land must be disclosed as Additional Regulatory Information: line item PPE (land); description; gross carrying value ₹2.80 crore; held in the name of Kesar Mehra, a promoter and director; held since 2019; the reason, and whether it is in dispute.
3. **Missing fryers.** Shortages are accounted for as deletions after management's approval: the auditor asks for that approval and the discard note, and verifies that the deletion is recorded as at 30 September 2025: cost ₹9.80 lakh and accumulated depreciation up to that date, ₹5.90 lakh (₹6.60 lakh − ₹0.70 lakh), removed, giving a loss on disposal of ₹3.90 lakh; the ₹0.70 lakh charged after the date of deletion is reversed. PPE and profit are overstated by ₹3.20 lakh (₹3.90 lakh − ₹0.70 lakh).

**Conclusion.** Iyer & Associates should obtain the lender's confirmation and verify the register of charges, require the Bhopal disclosure and the deletion of the fryers; uncorrected, these are misstatements to be evaluated for their effect on the opinion. As Tapri is listed, the same evidence supports reporting on title deeds and physical verification under [[aud.report.caro|CARO 2020]].
::::

:::: example Five intangibles, one of them honest
Tapri's intangible assets note for 2025-26 shows these additions and balances:

(i) ₹1.60 crore paid to an outside developer for the "Tapri Go" delivery app, launched on 1 August 2025.

(ii) ₹45 lakh of laboratory trials to find out whether stevia can replace sugar in the chai premix (the project has not gone beyond finding out).

(iii) ₹28 lakh for the software that runs Tapri's kulhad vending machines, which cannot operate without it.

(iv) ₹50 crore of "Tapri brand goodwill", recorded on management's own valuation with a credit to capital reserve.

(v) The "ChaiPoints" loyalty app (cost ₹36 lakh, bought 1 October 2023, amortised at ₹12 lakh a year), switched off on 30 September 2025 but amortised for the full year.

Comment on each item, and state the procedures Iyer & Associates should perform to verify the existence and the valuation of Tapri's intangible assets.

::: answer Model answer
**Relevant provision.** As per AS 26, an intangible asset is an identifiable non-monetary asset without physical substance held for use in the production or supply of goods or services, for rental to others or for administrative purposes. Research expenditure is an expense when incurred; internally generated goodwill should not be recognised; software that is an integral part of the related hardware is a fixed asset, other software an intangible asset. An intangible not in active use should be deleted after management's approval, with amortisation ceasing beyond the date of deletion.

**Facts and analysis.**

1. **Delivery app, ₹1.60 crore.** Acquired software, not integral to any hardware, identifiable, controlled and bringing future benefits: correctly an intangible asset (computer software), amortised from 1 August 2025.
2. **Stevia trials, ₹45 lakh.** Research phase of an internal project: an expense. Profit and intangible assets are overstated by ₹45 lakh.
3. **Vending machine software, ₹28 lakh.** The machines cannot operate without it: it belongs in PPE with the machines.
4. **Brand goodwill, ₹50 crore.** Internally generated goodwill should not be recognised; the entry is reversed from intangible assets and capital reserve.
5. **ChaiPoints, switched off.** Not in active use since 30 September 2025: a deletion should be recorded after management's approval. At that date accumulated amortisation was ₹24 lakh (24 months at ₹1 lakh), so the carrying amount of ₹12 lakh is written off as a loss on deletion. The correct charge for the year is ₹6 lakh amortisation plus ₹12 lakh loss = ₹18 lakh, against ₹12 lakh charged: profit and intangible assets are overstated by ₹6 lakh.

**Procedures.**

- **Existence.** An intangible asset has no physical substance, so verify whether each one is in active use in the production or supply of goods or services, for rental to others or for administrative purposes. For the delivery app, verify the sale of the related goods during the period through the orders placed on it. For ChaiPoints, which is not in active use, verify that the deletion has been recorded after management's approval and that amortisation has ceased beyond the date of deletion.
- **Valuation.** Verify that amortisation is charged on all intangible assets (the delivery app from 1 August 2025); that the amortisation method reflects the pattern in which each asset's future economic benefits are expected to be consumed; and whether management has done an impairment assessment, applying AS 28 to review the carrying amount and determine the recoverable amount and any impairment loss.

**Conclusion.** Only item (i) is correct; items (ii) to (v) should be corrected as above.
:::

## Under the hood

### Small print from Test Your Knowledge

- A building at ₹50 lakh asserts existence, rights and obligations, valuation and completeness, not existence alone (SM 5.5, 5.123–5.124; see [[aud.evid.assertions|assertions]]).
- Materials and wages used to construct a building are **capital expenditure** under AS 10, added to the cost of the building (SM 5.124).
- Depreciation begins when the asset is **available for use**, not when actual use begins: a machine ready in April but used from October is depreciated from April (SM 5.115, 5.137).

### Where CARO 2020 uses the same evidence

Under clause (i) of paragraph 3 of CARO 2020 the auditor reports on proper records of PPE and intangible assets, physical verification at reasonable intervals and material discrepancies, title deeds not held in the company's name, revaluation by a registered valuer, and benami property proceedings (SM 8.75–8.76). The procedures in this lesson produce that evidence; the reporting is in [[aud.report.caro|CARO 2020]].

::: trick "Price, Path, Pull-down": what goes into PPE
**Price**: purchase price with import duty and non-refundable taxes, less trade discounts. **Path**: every cost directly attributable to getting the asset to its location and working condition (site, delivery, installation, testing net of sale proceeds, professional fees, the employees who built it). **Pull-down**: the initial estimate of dismantling and restoring the site. Anything that is not one of the three (inauguration, advertising, training, general overheads, regular repairs) is an expense. Condition: it applies to the cost at recognition under AS 10; later costs are tested against the same recognition criteria.
:::

## Answer frame

### "How should the auditor verify [assertion] for PPE?"

1. **Name the assertion** and what it means here ("the entity has valid legal ownership rights over the PPE recorded").
2. **List the procedures** of SM 5.58–5.63 for it, in the Study Material's order and words.
3. **Apply them**: the mortgaged property gets the lender's confirmation and the register of charges; the new plant the AS 10 cost test.
4. **Conclude**: what the auditor obtains, and what follows if it cannot.

### PPE against intangibles, assertion by assertion

| Assertion | PPE | Intangible assets |
|---|---|---|
| Existence | Physical verification, tagging, reconciliation with the register | Active use: related sales or production data |
| Completeness | Movement schedule; AS 10 test of additions | Movement schedule; AS 26 test; no research |
| Valuation | Depreciation on all but freehold land; AS 28 | Amortisation on all; AS 28 |
| Rights | Invoices, title deeds, lender confirmation, charges | Invoices and contracts in the entity's name |

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: the singer capitalised with the kiln
Bunty reasons that a cost "for the kiln" is a cost of the kiln. **Fix:** only the purchase price, costs directly attributable to bringing the asset to its location and working condition, and the dismantling estimate join PPE. Inauguration, advertising, training and overheads are expenses however close to the asset they sit.
:::

::: mistake Proving an app exists by looking at the server
Inspecting a compact disk or a login screen does not prove that an intangible asset exists. **Fix:** existence of an intangible is shown by active use: the sales of the goods or services in which the software was used, or production data for a design.
:::

::: mistake Accepting the photocopy of a mortgaged deed
**Fix:** insist on the original title deeds; where the property is given as security and the bank holds them, ask management to obtain the lender's confirmation, and check the register of charges.
:::

::: mistake Treating all software as intangible
**Fix:** software that is an integral part of its hardware (the machine cannot operate without it, or an operating system) goes with the hardware as a fixed asset; only software that is not integral is an intangible asset.
:::

## Recap

- PPE cost = purchase price (import duties and non-refundable taxes in, trade discounts out) + directly attributable costs + initial dismantling and restoration estimate.
- Not PPE: inauguration, launch advertising, staff training for a new location, general overheads, regular repairs.
- Existence: verification plan, supervision, tagging, reconciliation with the fixed asset register; shortages deleted after approval, depreciation stops.
- Completeness: opening + additions − deletions = closing, tallied both ways; additions tested for AS 10, date, approval, quotations; deletions for approval, discard note, sale process, gain or loss.
- Rights: invoices and deeds in the entity's name, original title deeds, lender's confirmation for pledged deeds, register of charges.
- Valuation: depreciation on all PPE except freehold land, method matching the pattern of benefits, AS 28 impairment.
- Intangibles: existence by active use; nothing from research; no internally generated goodwill; integral software is a fixed asset.
- Schedule III adds title deeds not in the company's name, registered-valuer revaluation and CWIP ageing. (The singer still cannot fire a kulhad.)

## Flashcards

Q:: AS 10: when should the cost of an item of PPE be recognised as an asset?
A:: If, and only if, it is probable that future economic benefits associated with the item will flow to the enterprise, and the cost of the item can be measured reliably.

Q:: What three elements make up the cost of an item of PPE?
A:: Purchase price (including import duties and non-refundable purchase taxes, after trade discounts and rebates); costs directly attributable to bringing it to the location and condition for its intended operation; the initial estimate of dismantling, removal and site restoration costs.

Q:: Give the four examples of costs that are not costs of PPE.
A:: Opening a new facility or business (inauguration); introducing a new product or service (advertising and promotion); conducting business in a new location or with a new class of customer (staff training); administration and other general overheads.

Q:: How does the auditor establish the existence of PPE?
A:: Review the physical verification plan and its periodicity, examine evidence of supervision, obtain the verification report with working sheets, check tagging, reconcile with the fixed asset register, and verify how discrepancies were dealt with.

Q:: How does the auditor establish rights and obligations over PPE?
A:: Purchase invoices in the entity's name; conveyance or sale deeds for additions to land and building; original title deeds of all immovable properties; lenders' confirmation where deeds are held as security; the register of charges.

Q:: How is the existence of an intangible asset verified?
A:: By verifying that it is in active use: for software, the sale of related goods or services in which it was used; for designs or drawings, production data showing the products are made and sold.

Q:: What should the auditor check for an intangible asset not in active use?
A:: That the deletion was recorded in the books after management's approval, and that amortisation ceased beyond the date of deletion.

Q:: When is computer software a fixed asset and when an intangible asset?
A:: When it is an integral part of the related hardware (the machine cannot operate without it, or an operating system) it is a fixed asset; otherwise it is an intangible asset.

Q:: Schedule III: the classification of PPE?
A:: Land; Buildings; Plant and Equipment; Furniture and Fixtures; Vehicles; Office equipment; Others (specify nature).

Q:: Schedule III: what does the CWIP ageing schedule show?
A:: Amounts in CWIP for less than 1 year, 1–2 years, 2–3 years and more than 3 years, for projects in progress and projects temporarily suspended; the total tallies with CWIP in the balance sheet.

Q:: Schedule III: which immovable property must be reported under "title deeds not held in the name of the company"?
A:: All immovable property whose title deeds are not in the company's name, other than property where the company is the lessee under a duly executed lease; jointly held property to the extent of the company's share.

Q:: When does depreciation of a new machine begin?
A:: When it is available for use, that is, in the location and condition necessary to operate as management intends, not from the date of actual use.
