## In plain words

### Bunty's galti register

It is May 2025 and Iyer & Associates are in the last weeks of the 2024-25 audit of Tapri Tea Co. Ltd. Bunty has been keeping a spreadsheet of every mistake the team has found. He calls it the galti register.

> **Bunty:** Ma'am, the register has 41 rows! Even a ₹3,200 taxi bill booked as staff welfare.
> **Lakshmi:** The taxi bill comes off the list, Bunty. Anything under ₹5 lakh is clearly trivial on this audit.
> **Kesar:** And the rest are also small, na? Sab adjust ho jayega next year.
> **Lakshmi:** Small ones add up, Kesar-ji. And next year's accounts will inherit whatever you leave in this year's.

A **misstatement** is a difference between what the accounts show and what the financial reporting framework requires: a wrong amount, a wrong head, a missing disclosure. Every audit finds some. What matters at the end is what happens to them.

So the auditor keeps one running list of every misstatement found, leaving out only those so tiny they are **clearly trivial**. The list is shown to management early, with a request to correct **all** of it. Management usually corrects most. Whatever it refuses to correct is an **uncorrected misstatement**, and the auditor must then decide whether those leftovers matter: one by one and added together, by their size and by their nature, against a materiality figure checked afresh against the actual results. The board's watchdogs (**those charged with governance**) are told, management signs a statement that it believes the leftovers do not matter, and the whole story goes into the audit file.

::: analogy The wedding card proofreader
Before a wedding card goes to print, the proofreader marks every typo except a stray double space. He hands the list to the family and asks them to fix all of it. If Mausi insists her name stays "Suneeta", he still judges whether the leftover typos, alone or together, spoil the card, and a wrong wedding date spoils it however small the typo. Then the family signs "we accept the remaining typos" with the list stapled to it. The signature does not make a wrong date right.
:::

::: key
Accumulate every misstatement above the clearly trivial, ask management to correct all of them, and judge whatever stays uncorrected, individually and in aggregate, by size and nature, against materiality reassessed on the actual results. Then tell those charged with governance, obtain a written representation with the list attached, and document.
:::

## How it works

### Where SA 450 sits

::: definition Misstatement
A difference between the amount, classification, presentation, or disclosure of a reported financial statement item and the amount, classification, presentation, or disclosure that is required for the item to be in accordance with the applicable financial reporting framework. Misstatements can arise from error or fraud (SM 3.4).
:::

::: definition Uncorrected misstatements
Those misstatements that the auditor has accumulated during the audit and that have not been corrected (SM 7.24).
:::

Before forming an opinion, the auditor evaluates the effects of identified misstatements on the audit, and of uncorrected misstatements on the financial statements, after consideration of materiality (SM 7.23). SA 450, "Evaluation of Misstatements Identified during the Audit", deals with that responsibility (SM 7.24). Its numbers come from [[aud.risk.sa320|SA 320 materiality]]; its conclusion feeds [[aud.report.sa700|SA 700]], whose opinion must take into account whether uncorrected misstatements are material, individually or in aggregate (SM 8.5).

**Objective.** The objective of the auditor is to evaluate (SM 7.24):

- (a) the effect of identified misstatements on the audit; and
- (b) the effect of uncorrected misstatements, if any, on the financial statements.

### The requirements, in the Study Material's order

::: steps
1. **Accumulate.** The auditor shall accumulate misstatements identified during the audit, ==other than those that are clearly trivial==. A misstatement may arise from an inaccuracy in gathering or processing data, or from the omission of an amount or a disclosure. ICAI's example: machinery repair expenses of ₹5 lakh wrongly capitalised, overstating profit (SM 7.24).
2. **Consider them as the audit progresses.** The auditor shall determine whether the **overall audit strategy and audit plan need to be revised** if (a) the nature of identified misstatements and the circumstances of their occurrence indicate that other misstatements may exist that, aggregated with those accumulated, could be material; or (b) the aggregate of misstatements accumulated approaches materiality determined under SA 320 (SM 7.24–7.25).
3. **Management may be asked to examine.** The auditor may request management to examine a class of transactions, account balance or disclosure, to understand the cause of a misstatement, determine the actual misstatement and make adjustments; for example, based on the auditor's projection of misstatements. If management has done so and corrected the misstatements detected, the auditor shall perform **additional audit procedures** to determine whether misstatements remain (SM 7.25).
4. **Communicate and request correction.** The auditor shall communicate on a timely basis **all** misstatements accumulated with the appropriate level of management, unless prohibited by law or regulation, and shall request management to correct those misstatements (SM 7.25).
5. **If management refuses.** The auditor shall obtain an understanding of management's reasons for not making the corrections and take that understanding into account when evaluating whether the financial statements as a whole are free from material misstatement (SM 7.25).
6. **Reassess materiality first.** Prior to evaluating the effect of uncorrected misstatements, the auditor shall reassess materiality determined under SA 320 to confirm whether it remains appropriate in the context of the entity's **actual financial results** (SM 7.25–7.26).
7. **Evaluate.** The auditor shall determine whether uncorrected misstatements are material, individually or in aggregate, considering (a) their size and nature, both in relation to particular classes of transactions, account balances or disclosures and the financial statements as a whole, and the particular circumstances of their occurrence; and (b) the effect of uncorrected misstatements related to **prior periods** on the relevant classes, balances or disclosures and the financial statements as a whole (SM 7.26).
8. **Communicate with those charged with governance.** Unless prohibited by law or regulation, the auditor shall communicate uncorrected misstatements and the effect they, individually or in aggregate, may have on the opinion; the communication shall identify **material uncorrected misstatements individually**; the auditor shall request that they be corrected; and the auditor shall also communicate the effect of uncorrected misstatements related to prior periods (SM 7.26).
9. **Written representation.** The auditor shall request a written representation from management and, where appropriate, those charged with governance whether they believe the effects of uncorrected misstatements are immaterial, individually and in aggregate, to the financial statements as a whole. A **summary of such items** shall be included in or attached to the written representation (SM 7.26).
10. **Document.** The audit documentation shall include the three items listed under Formats below (SM 7.27).
:::

### Why tell management early, and why correct even the small ones

**Timely communication** to the appropriate level of management enables management to evaluate whether the items are misstatements, inform the auditor if it disagrees, and take action as necessary. **Correction of all misstatements**, including those communicated by the auditor, enables management to maintain accurate books and records and reduces the risks of material misstatement of future financial statements because of the ==cumulative effect of immaterial uncorrected misstatements related to prior periods== (SM 7.25). That is Lakshmi's "next year's accounts will inherit" line.

### Size is not the only test

Materiality fixed at planning is not an amount below which uncorrected misstatements will always be immaterial: the circumstances of some misstatements may make them material even below it, so the auditor considers their size, their **nature** and the particular circumstances of their occurrence (SM 3.18). A required statutory disclosure is material whatever its amount (SM 3.18), and a small amount lost through fraud can point to a serious flaw in internal control (SM 3.17).

## Formats and formulas

*Reproduce both from memory: each is a frequent short question.*

### The audit documentation under SA 450 (SM 7.27)

1. The amount below which misstatements would be regarded as **clearly trivial**;
2. **All misstatements accumulated** during the audit and whether they have been corrected; and
3. The auditor's **conclusion** as to whether uncorrected misstatements are material, individually or in aggregate, and the **basis** for that conclusion.

### The SA 450 line in ICAI's illustrative representation letter (SM 7.36)

"The effects of uncorrected misstatements are immaterial, both individually and in the aggregate, to the financial statements as a whole. A list of the uncorrected misstatements is attached to the representation letter. (SA 450)"

The rest of that letter is in [[aud.complete.sa580|SA 580: written representations]].

::: note
The Study Material gives no percentage for "clearly trivial". The amount is the auditor's judgement, and it must be documented. It is not materiality: misstatements above it are accumulated even when each is far below materiality, because they may add up.
:::

## Worked examples

:::: example The register at mid-audit
Iyer & Associates set materiality for Tapri's 2024-25 financial statements as a whole at ₹1.60 crore (5% of the profit before tax expected at planning, ₹32.00 crore) and treat misstatements below ₹5 lakh as clearly trivial. In the first fortnight of the final audit, Bunty's register shows:

| Item found | Effect | ₹ crore |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Taxi bill of ₹3,200 booked as staff welfare instead of conveyance | Classification | 0.0003 |
| (b) March 2025 electricity bills of the 9 Pune cafés not provided for | Expenses and liabilities understated | 0.30 |
| (c) Repairs to the samosa factory's fryers capitalised as plant | Profit and plant overstated | 0.45 |
| (d) Depreciation on delivery scooters charged at the wrong rate | Depreciation understated | 0.12 |
| (e) March 2025 credit note to a franchisee not recorded | Revenue and receivables overstated | 0.35 |

The year-end accrual of café expenses is done city by city by the same accounts clerk. Tapri has cafés in 40 cities.

Required: Advise Lakshmi under SA 450 on

(i) which items go on the list of accumulated misstatements;

(ii) whether the overall audit strategy and audit plan need revision;

(iii) what she may ask management to do, and what she must do afterwards; and

(iv) what she must communicate now, and to whom.

::: answer Model answer
**Relevant provision.** As per SA 450, the auditor shall accumulate misstatements identified during the audit, other than those that are clearly trivial. The auditor shall determine whether the overall audit strategy and audit plan need to be revised if the nature of identified misstatements and the circumstances of their occurrence indicate that other misstatements may exist that, when aggregated with misstatements accumulated, could be material, or if the aggregate of accumulated misstatements approaches materiality. The auditor may request management to examine a class of transactions, account balance or disclosure; if management corrects the misstatements detected, the auditor shall perform additional audit procedures to determine whether misstatements remain. The auditor shall communicate on a timely basis all accumulated misstatements with the appropriate level of management, unless prohibited by law or regulation, and request their correction.

**(i) The list.** Item (a), ₹3,200, is below the ₹5 lakh clearly trivial amount and is not accumulated. Items (b) to (e) are accumulated:

| Accumulated misstatement | ₹ crore |
|---|---|
| (b) Pune electricity not provided | 0.30 |
| (c) Fryer repairs capitalised | 0.45 |
| (d) Scooter depreciation understated | 0.12 |
| (e) Franchisee credit note not recorded | 0.35 |
| **Total accumulated** | **1.22** |

All four overstate profit, so they add up: ₹1.22 crore is 76% of materiality of ₹1.60 crore.

**(ii) Revision.** Both conditions are present. The aggregate, ₹1.22 crore, approaches materiality. And the nature and circumstances of item (b), a failure of a city-wise accrual process run by one clerk, indicate that the other 39 cities may have the same misstatement, which together with the ₹1.22 crore could be material. Lakshmi should revise the audit plan, for example by extending the search for unrecorded liabilities to all cities.

**(iii) Management's examination.** Lakshmi may request management to examine the March 2025 bills of all cafés, to understand the cause, determine the actual amount of the misstatement and make appropriate adjustments. If management does so and corrects what it finds, she shall perform additional audit procedures to determine whether misstatements remain.

**(iv) Communication.** She shall communicate items (b) to (e), on a timely basis, to the appropriate level of management (here Dev Malhotra, the CFO), and request management to correct all of them. Timely communication lets management evaluate whether the items are misstatements, say so if it disagrees, and act; correcting all of them keeps the books accurate and reduces the risk that immaterial uncorrected misstatements accumulate into future periods.
:::

::::: example Kesar refuses three corrections
Two weeks later, management has examined all 40 cities and corrected unprovided electricity of ₹0.62 crore (Pune's ₹0.30 crore included) and the scooter depreciation. Lakshmi's further procedures found no other unprovided bills. The final accounts show profit before tax of ₹28.00 crore, against the ₹32.00 crore expected at planning. Kesar refuses the following:

| Uncorrected item | Kesar's reason | ₹ crore |
|---|---|---|
| (c) Fryer repairs capitalised | "The fryers will last longer now" | 0.45 |
| (e) Franchisee credit note | "He will buy more next year" | 0.35 |
| (f) No provision against Chaiwala Junction, a franchisee declared insolvent in April 2025, owing Tapri | "Insolvent after 31 March, so next year's problem" | 0.70 |
| (g) An advance to a supplier who closed down in 2023-24, left uncorrected last year, still in other current assets | "Auditors passed it last year" | 0.55 |
| (h) Kesar's own performance bonus booked as miscellaneous expenses and left out of the managerial remuneration note | "Miscellaneous is a perfectly good head" | 0.06 |

Kesar offers to sign a representation that all of this is immaterial: "Each item is below your ₹1.60 crore. Sign a clean report."

Required: Advise Lakshmi on the materiality of the uncorrected misstatements, and on what she must do before signing the report.

**Relevant provision.** As per SA 450, if management refuses to correct misstatements, the auditor shall obtain an understanding of management's reasons and take it into account. Prior to evaluating uncorrected misstatements, the auditor shall reassess materiality to confirm whether it remains appropriate in the context of the entity's actual financial results. The auditor shall determine whether uncorrected misstatements are material, individually or in aggregate, considering their size and nature and the particular circumstances of their occurrence, and the effect of uncorrected misstatements related to prior periods. The auditor shall communicate uncorrected misstatements and their possible effect on the opinion to those charged with governance, identifying material ones individually and requesting their correction, request a written representation (with a summary of the items attached), and document the matter.

**Facts of the case.** Five items remain uncorrected; Kesar's reasons are on record; actual profit before tax is ₹28.00 crore against ₹32.00 crore planned.

**Analysis.**

*Materiality reassessed.* Actual profit before tax is substantially below the planning figure. At the same 5%, materiality on the actual results is 5% × ₹28.00 crore = **₹1.40 crore**, not ₹1.60 crore. (Lakshmi uses the reported profit; on the profit after the uncorrected items, ₹26.50 crore, it would be ₹1.325 crore, and the conclusion would not change.) She should also consider whether performance materiality and the further audit procedures remain appropriate (SM 3.22).

*Size, in aggregate.* Items (c), (e) and (f) each overstate profit before tax:

| Effect on profit before tax | ₹ crore |
|---|---|
| (c) Fryer repairs capitalised | 0.45 |
| (e) Franchisee credit note | 0.35 |
| (f) Provision against the insolvent franchisee | 0.70 |
| **Total overstatement** | **1.50** |

No item is material on its own, but together they exceed reassessed materiality of ₹1.40 crore: they are **material in aggregate**. Kesar's reason for (f) does not hold: an insolvency declared after the year end but before the report gives evidence on the recoverability of the amount due at 31 March (SM 7.4; [[aud.complete.sa560|SA 560]]).

*Prior periods.* Item (g) does not touch this year's profit, but it overstates other current assets and equity by ₹0.55 crore, adding to the ₹1.50 crore by which items (c), (e) and (f) overstate assets and equity: ₹2.05 crore in all. "Passed last year" is the cumulative effect SA 450 warns about.

*Nature.* Item (h), ₹6 lakh, is tiny, but the remuneration of management is an item where law, regulation or the framework shapes users' expectations, so that misstatements of lesser amounts than overall materiality could influence them (SM 3.22); and a disclosure the law requires is material irrespective of its amount (SM 3.18).

**Conclusion.**

1. The uncorrected misstatements are **material**: (c), (e) and (f) in aggregate against reassessed materiality, (h) by nature, and (g) adds to the overstatement of the balance sheet.
2. Lakshmi should communicate them to those charged with governance (Tapri's audit committee), identifying the material ones individually, explaining the effect they may have on the opinion, including the effect of the prior-period item (g), and requesting their correction.
3. She should request the written representation, with a summary of the uncorrected items attached. Kesar's signature records management's belief; it does not make the items immaterial, because written representations do not on their own provide sufficient appropriate audit evidence.
4. She should document the clearly trivial amount, every misstatement accumulated and whether it was corrected, and her conclusion with its basis.
5. If the items stay uncorrected, the financial statements are materially misstated. The effects are confined to particular items (plant, receivables, other current assets, profit and one note) and are not a substantial proportion of the financial statements, so they are material but not pervasive: a **qualified opinion** under [[aud.report.sa705|SA 705]]. A clean report is not possible.

## Under the hood

### Where the register meets other Standards

| Link | What it adds |
|---|---|
| SA 320 | Materiality may need revision during the audit; if the auditor lowers it, performance materiality and the further procedures are reconsidered, and every revision is documented (SM 3.22–3.23) |
| SA 530 | A projection gives a broad view of the scale of misstatement but may not determine the amount to record (SM 4.66): hence management's examination of the class at the auditor's request |
| SA 700 | The opinion takes into account whether uncorrected misstatements are material, individually or in aggregate (SM 8.5) |
| SA 700 | **Selective correction** of the misstatements brought to management's attention (correcting those that increase earnings, not those that decrease them) indicates a possible lack of neutrality; with uncorrected misstatements, it can make the financial statements as a whole materially misstated (SM 8.5–8.6) |
| SA 705 | If the auditor withdraws over a management-imposed limitation, the auditor first tells those charged with governance of any misstatements found that would have given rise to a modification (SM 8.41) |
| SA 580 | The written representation on uncorrected misstatements sits in the representation letter with SA 580's own representations (SM 7.36) |

### Corrected misstatements still go on the list

The documentation covers **all** misstatements accumulated and whether they were corrected, not only the leftovers. A misstatement management corrected is still evidence: of how well its controls worked, and of where else to look.

::: trick ACCORD: the SA 450 sequence
**A**ccumulate (above clearly trivial), **C**onsider revising the strategy and plan, **C**ommunicate to management and ask for correction, **O**btain the reasons for any refusal, then reassess materiality and evaluate the uncorrected items (individually and in aggregate, size and nature, prior periods), **R**eport to those charged with governance and request the representation, **D**ocument. Condition: the order is the Study Material's; the reassessment of materiality always comes before the evaluation of uncorrected misstatements, and the evaluation hides inside the "O": do not drop it.

## Answer frame

### A case question on misstatements

1. **Relevant provision**: "As per SA 450, …", only the requirements the facts need (accumulation, revision of the plan, communication, refusal, reassessment, evaluation, those charged with governance, representation, documentation).
2. **Facts of the case**: list the misstatements with amounts and say which are corrected.
3. **Analysis**: clearly trivial? Reassessed materiality? Individually, in aggregate (show the total), by nature, and the prior-period effect.
4. **Conclusion**: material or not, then what the auditor must do next, and the effect on the opinion if they stay uncorrected (SA 705).

### "State the documentation required under SA 450"

Three points, in order: the clearly trivial amount; all misstatements accumulated and whether corrected; the conclusion on whether uncorrected misstatements are material, individually or in aggregate, with its basis. Three marks are often exactly these three lines.

### "Discuss the auditor's responsibility to communicate with management"

| Point | What earns the mark |
|---|---|
| What and when | All accumulated misstatements, on a timely basis, to the appropriate level of management, unless prohibited by law or regulation |
| Request | Request management to correct those misstatements |
| Why timely | Lets management evaluate whether the items are misstatements, inform the auditor if it disagrees, and act |
| Why correct all | Accurate books and records; lower risk of material misstatement in future periods from the cumulative effect of immaterial uncorrected prior-period misstatements |
| If refused | Understand management's reasons and take them into account in evaluating the financial statements as a whole |

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: "each one is below materiality, so none matters"
Bunty clears three uncorrected items one at a time. **Fix:** SA 450 asks whether they are material "individually or in aggregate": add them up, and weigh their nature too.
:::

::: mistake The yardstick nobody re-read
Judging leftovers against the materiality set at planning when actual profit came in far lower. **Fix:** reassess materiality against the actual financial results *before* evaluating uncorrected misstatements.
:::

::: mistake "We passed it last year"
Ignoring an uncorrected misstatement from the prior period because it was immaterial then. **Fix:** consider the effect of prior-period uncorrected misstatements on this year's balances and the financial statements as a whole, and communicate it to those charged with governance.
:::

::: mistake Treating the representation as the evidence
"Management has confirmed the uncorrected misstatements are immaterial, so they are." **Fix:** the representation records management's belief with the list attached; the auditor still reaches and documents a conclusion, and a written representation does not on its own provide sufficient appropriate audit evidence.
:::

## Practice

::: quiz Check yourself
Q: Case scenario. CA Rekha set materiality for Narmada Agro Ltd at ₹45 lakh: 5% of the profit before tax of ₹9 crore expected at planning. The final accounts show a profit before tax of ₹7 crore, and she keeps the 5%. Two misstatements remain uncorrected, both overstating profit: a March cold-storage rent bill of ₹21 lakh not provided for, and fertiliser stock of ₹16 lakh counted twice. Nothing else is outstanding. Which of the following is correct?
- [ ] The misstatements are immaterial, as their total of ₹37 lakh is below materiality of ₹45 lakh
- [x] The misstatements are material in aggregate, as materiality reassessed on the actual results is ₹35 lakh, and their effect on the opinion should be considered
- [ ] The misstatements are immaterial, as SA 450 judges each misstatement on its own and each is below materiality
- [ ] The misstatements are material, so CA Rekha should pass the correcting entries herself
> Before evaluating uncorrected misstatements, materiality is reassessed on the actual results: 5% × ₹7 crore = ₹35 lakh. Together the items overstate profit by ₹21 lakh + ₹16 lakh = ₹37 lakh: below the ₹45 lakh set at planning, above the reassessed ₹35 lakh. SA 450 asks "individually or in aggregate". Correcting the accounts is management's job, not the auditor's.

Q: Materiality for Sabarmati Snacks Ltd is ₹1.50 crore, and misstatements below ₹3 lakh are clearly trivial. The misstatements found so far, all overstating profit, are ₹2.5 lakh, ₹40 lakh, ₹1.8 lakh, ₹55 lakh and ₹35 lakh. What is the total of the accumulated misstatements, in ₹ lakh?
= 130
> The ₹2.5 lakh and ₹1.8 lakh items are clearly trivial and are not accumulated: ₹40 + ₹55 + ₹35 = ₹130 lakh. That is about 87% of materiality of ₹150 lakh: the aggregate approaches materiality, so the auditor determines whether the overall audit strategy and audit plan need to be revised.

Q: Which of the following must the audit documentation under SA 450 include? Select all that apply.
- [x] The amount below which misstatements would be regarded as clearly trivial
- [x] All misstatements accumulated during the audit and whether they have been corrected
- [x] The conclusion on whether uncorrected misstatements are material, individually or in aggregate, and its basis
- [ ] Management's reasons for every misstatement it corrected
> These are the Study Material's three items. Management's reasons are obtained for misstatements it refuses to correct, not for those it corrects.

Q: Management of Ganga Foods Ltd refuses to correct two misstatements the auditor has communicated. What does SA 450 require the auditor to do first?
- [ ] Express a qualified opinion
- [ ] Withdraw from the engagement
- [x] Obtain an understanding of management's reasons and take it into account in evaluating whether the financial statements as a whole are free from material misstatement

## Recap

- Misstatement: a difference in amount, classification, presentation or disclosure from what the framework requires; from error or fraud.
- Objective: evaluate the effect of identified misstatements on the audit, and of uncorrected misstatements on the financial statements.
- Accumulate all misstatements except the clearly trivial; revise the strategy and plan if more may exist or the total approaches materiality.
- Tell management on a timely basis, request correction of all; if refused, understand the reasons.
- Reassess materiality on the actual results, then judge uncorrected items individually and in aggregate, by size and nature, with the prior-period effect.
- Tell those charged with governance (material items individually), request correction; obtain a written representation with the list attached.
- Document: the clearly trivial amount, every misstatement and whether corrected, the conclusion and its basis.
- Small ones add up, and old ones carry forward. (Mausi's name can stay; the wedding date cannot.)

## Flashcards

Q:: SA 450: what is the objective of the auditor?
A:: To evaluate (a) the effect of identified misstatements on the audit; and (b) the effect of uncorrected misstatements, if any, on the financial statements.

Q:: SA 450: what are "uncorrected misstatements"?
A:: Misstatements that the auditor has accumulated during the audit and that have not been corrected.

Q:: SA 450: which misstatements does the auditor accumulate?
A:: All misstatements identified during the audit, other than those that are clearly trivial.

Q:: SA 450: when must the auditor consider revising the overall audit strategy and audit plan?
A:: When the nature and circumstances of identified misstatements indicate other misstatements may exist that, aggregated, could be material; or when the aggregate of accumulated misstatements approaches materiality.

Q:: SA 450: management examined a class of transactions at the auditor's request and corrected what it found. What must the auditor do?
A:: Perform additional audit procedures to determine whether misstatements remain.

Q:: SA 450: what must the auditor do if management refuses to correct misstatements?
A:: Obtain an understanding of management's reasons and take it into account when evaluating whether the financial statements as a whole are free from material misstatement.

Q:: SA 450: what must the auditor do before evaluating the effect of uncorrected misstatements?
A:: Reassess materiality determined under SA 320 to confirm whether it remains appropriate in the context of the entity's actual financial results.

Q:: SA 450: what two things does the auditor consider in deciding whether uncorrected misstatements are material?
A:: (a) Their size and nature, in relation to particular classes, balances or disclosures and the financial statements as a whole, and the circumstances of their occurrence; (b) the effect of uncorrected misstatements related to prior periods.

Q:: SA 450: what is communicated to those charged with governance?
A:: Uncorrected misstatements and the effect they may have on the opinion, with material ones identified individually; a request for correction; and the effect of prior-period uncorrected misstatements. Unless prohibited by law or regulation.

Q:: SA 450: what written representation is requested?
A:: Whether management (and, where appropriate, those charged with governance) believe the effects of uncorrected misstatements are immaterial, individually and in aggregate, with a summary of the items included in or attached to it.

Q:: SA 450: what must the audit documentation include?
A:: The amount below which misstatements are clearly trivial; all misstatements accumulated and whether corrected; the conclusion on whether uncorrected misstatements are material, individually or in aggregate, and its basis.
