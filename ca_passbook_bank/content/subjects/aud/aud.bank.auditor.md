## In plain words

### Three banks, three sets of rules

Kesar has opened accounts with three banks in one month: Tapri's main bank, **Malwa Commercial Bank Ltd**, a private-sector banking company; **Narmada Bank of India**, a nationalised bank, for the new Indore cold store; and **Malwa Gramin Bank**, a Regional Rural Bank, because the kulhad potters in Tapri's villages bank there. Now he wants to know why his auditor's article assistant looks worried.

> **Bunty:** Ma'am, an auditor is an auditor. Shareholders appoint, shareholders pay. Same for every bank?
> **Lakshmi:** Not even close, Bunty. A nationalised bank has no shareholders' meeting choosing its auditor, and none of these three can appoint anyone until the RBI or the Central Government says yes.
> **Kesar:** Sab adjust ho jayega. Let the bank's chairman sign the appointment.
> **Lakshmi:** The law chose who signs, Kesar-ji. Not the chairman, and not you.

The rules for a bank's auditor answer four questions. **Who can be the auditor?** Anyone qualified to audit a company. **Who appoints and who fixes the fee?** That depends on the kind of bank, and the RBI or the Central Government must approve. **What powers?** The same access to books and vouchers as a company's auditor. **Who receives the report?** For a nationalised bank, the Central Government; and every bank's auditor also produces extra reports, the biggest being the **Long Form Audit Report** (LFAR), and must tell the RBI about anything that smells of fraud.

::: analogy The board-exam invigilator
A school appoints the invigilator for the board exam, but the board must approve the name first. The invigilator can walk into any classroom and open any answer sheet. And if a copying racket turns up, the invigilator tells the board as well as the principal: the principal's comfort is not the point. A bank's auditor sits in exactly that chair.
:::

::: key
A bank's auditor must be qualified to audit companies and has a company auditor's powers of access, but the appointing body, the approving authority and the fee-fixing authority differ for a banking company, a nationalised bank and a Regional Rural Bank; a nationalised bank's auditor reports to the Central Government; and suspected frauds are reported to the RBI as well as to the bank's chief executive.
:::

## How it works

### 1. Who may audit a bank

**Section 30(1) of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949** requires that the balance sheet and profit and loss account of a banking company be audited by ==a person duly qualified under any law for the time being in force to be an auditor of companies== (SM 10.12). The **eligibility, qualifications and disqualifications** are those applicable to a company auditor (SM 10.12).

Two initial considerations come from the RBI (SM 10.15):

- **Declaration of indebtedness**: before appointing their statutory central or branch auditors, banks should obtain a declaration of indebtedness. Indebtedness means owing money to the bank in any case whatsoever.
- **Internal assignments**: audit firms should not undertake a statutory audit assignment while they are associated with internal assignments in the bank during the same year, like concurrent audits (the internal audit of banks conducted monthly during the year).

### 2. Who appoints, who approves, who fixes the fee

As per the provisions of the relevant enactments (SM 10.12–10.13):

| Bank | Appointed by | Prior approval | Fee fixed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Banking company | Shareholders at the AGM | RBI | General meeting (section 142, Companies Act) |
| Nationalised bank | The bank, through its Board | RBI | RBI, with the Central Government |
| Regional Rural Bank | The bank concerned | Central Government | Not given in the Study Material |

The State Bank of India's auditors' remuneration is also fixed by the RBI in consultation with the Central Government (SM 10.13).

### 3. Powers

The auditor of a banking company, a nationalised bank, the State Bank of India or a Regional Rural Bank has **the same powers as a company's auditor** in the matter of access to the books, accounts, documents and vouchers (SM 10.13).

### 4. The auditor's report

**Nationalised bank.** The auditor reports to the **Central Government**, stating (SM 10.13):

1. whether, in his opinion, the financial statements present a **true and fair view** of the affairs of the bank, and, where he called for any explanation or information, whether it has been given and whether it is satisfactory;
2. whether or not the transactions of the bank which have come to his notice have been **within the powers of that bank**;
3. whether or not the **returns received from the offices and branches** of the bank have been found adequate for the purpose of his audit; and
4. **any other matter** which he considers should be brought to the notice of the Central Government.

The report of the auditors of the **State Bank of India** is also made to the Central Government and is almost identical (SM 10.13).

**Format.** The auditors, central as well as branch, ensure that the report complies with the Standards on Auditing on audit reports ([[aud.report.sa700|SA 700]], [[aud.report.sa705|SA 705]] and the rest). The report gives not only the **number of unaudited branches** but also the **quantification of the advances, deposits, interest income and interest expense** of those branches, which is in line with the best international trends and useful to users (SM 10.13–10.14).

**Banking company.** Its auditor also states the matters covered by **section 143 of the Companies Act, 2013** ([[aud.report.companies|company auditor's reporting]]). But the **Companies (Auditor's Report) Order, 2020 does not apply to a banking company** as defined in clause (c) of section 5 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949 (SM 10.14; [[aud.report.caro|CARO 2020]]).

### 5. The other reports of the statutory central auditors

Besides the main audit report, the **Statutory Central Auditors (SCAs)** presently furnish (SM 10.7–10.8):

| | Report |
|---|---|
| (a) | On the adequacy and operating effectiveness of **internal controls over financial reporting**, for banks registered as companies, under section 143(3)(i) of the Companies Act, 2013, normally as an annexure to the main report, as per ICAI's Guidance Note |
| (b) | The **Long Form Audit Report** (LFAR) |
| (c) | On compliance with **SLR** requirements |
| (d) | Whether **treasury operations** were conducted in accordance with the RBI's instructions |
| (e) | Whether **income recognition, asset classification and provisioning** were made as per the RBI's guidelines |
| (f) | Whether any **serious irregularity** was noticed in the working of the bank which requires immediate attention |
| (g) | Status of compliance with the recommendations of the **Ghosh Committee** (frauds and malpractices) and the **Jilani Committee** (internal control and inspection/credit system) |
| (h) | Instances of **adverse credit-deposit ratio** in the rural areas |

### 6. The Long Form Audit Report

The terms of appointment of the auditors of **public sector banks, private sector banks and foreign banks** (and of their branches) require them to furnish an LFAR besides the statutory report. The matters the LFAR must deal with are **specified by the Reserve Bank of India**. The SCAs submit the LFAR to the banks ==latest by 30th June every year==, so it must be planned for. The format **does not require an executive summary**, but members may consider giving one to bring out the key observations (SM 10.14).

### 7. Reporting to the RBI

The RBI's circular implementing the recommendations of the **Committee on Legal Aspects of Bank Frauds**, applicable to all scheduled commercial banks (excluding Regional Rural Banks), says (SM 10.14–10.15):

> "If an accounting professional, whether in the course of internal or external audit or in the process of institutional audit finds anything susceptible to be fraud or fraudulent activity or act of excess power or smell any foul play in any transaction, he should refer the matter to the regulator. Any deliberate failure on the part of the auditor should render himself liable for action".

So the member must report such matters to the RBI. Two Standards back this up:

- **SA 250**, "Consideration of Laws and Regulations in an Audit of Financial Statements": the duty of confidentiality is **over-ridden by statute, law or courts**.
- **SA 240**, "The Auditor's Responsibilities Relating to Fraud in an Audit of Financial Statements": the auditor obtains reasonable assurance that the financial statements as a whole are free from material misstatement, whether caused by fraud or error.

The auditor is not expected to look into each and every transaction but to evaluate the system as a whole. If, while performing his normal duties, he comes across any such instance, he reports it **to the RBI in addition to the Chairman/Managing Director/Chief Executive** of the bank (SM 10.15).

## Worked examples

:::: example Two drafts on Lakshmi's desk
Iyer & Associates are among the statutory central auditors of Narmada Bank of India, a nationalised bank, and the auditors of Malwa Commercial Bank Ltd, a banking company, for 2025-26. Bunty has drafted both reports.

Narmada Bank's draft is addressed to "the Members of Narmada Bank of India", gives the opinion on the true and fair view, and says nothing else of substance.

Malwa Commercial Bank's draft has three features: it states that "38 branches have not been audited" without any figures; it carries an annexure "Report under the Companies (Auditor's Report) Order, 2020"; and it has no paragraph on the matters in section 143 of the Companies Act, 2013, "because the Banking Regulation Act governs banks".

Required:

(i) To whom should the report on Narmada Bank be made, and what must it state?

(ii) Comment on each feature of Malwa Commercial Bank's draft.

::: answer Model answer
**(i) Narmada Bank of India.** In the case of a nationalised bank, the auditor is required to make a report to the **Central Government**, not to members, stating: (a) whether, in his opinion, the financial statements present a true and fair view of the affairs of the bank and, where any explanation or information was called for, whether it has been given and whether it is satisfactory; (b) whether or not the transactions of the bank which have come to his notice have been within the powers of the bank; (c) whether or not the returns received from the offices and branches of the bank have been found adequate for the purpose of the audit; and (d) any other matter which he considers should be brought to the notice of the Central Government (SM 10.13). Bunty's draft has the wrong addressee and covers only part of (a): it must be re-addressed and completed.

**(ii) Malwa Commercial Bank Ltd.**

1. **Unaudited branches.** The auditor should ensure that not only the number of unaudited branches is given but also the quantification of advances, deposits, interest income and interest expense of those branches is disclosed in the audit report (SM 10.13–10.14). The draft should quantify these four figures for the 38 branches.
2. **CARO annexure.** The reporting requirements of the Companies (Auditor's Report) Order, 2020 are not applicable to a banking company as defined in clause (c) of section 5 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949 (SM 10.14). The annexure should be removed.
3. **Section 143 matters.** The auditor of a banking company is also required to state in his report the matters covered by section 143 of the Companies Act, 2013 (SM 10.14). The Banking Regulation Act does not displace this, so the paragraph must be added.

The report must also comply with the requirements of the Standards on Auditing on the auditor's report (SM 10.13).
:::
::::

:::: example The deposits that closed themselves
During the 2025-26 branch audit of Narmada Bank of India's Indore Main Branch, Bunty notices that eleven fixed deposits of senior citizens, ₹1.84 crore in all, were closed before maturity on withdrawal forms whose signatures do not match the specimen signatures, and the proceeds were credited to a savings account opened two months earlier. The Zonal Manager tells Lakshmi that the bank's vigilance department "will handle it internally", reminds her of the declaration in the appointment letter that the auditors will not communicate the bank's affairs to anyone not legally entitled to them, and asks that nothing be sent outside the bank.

Advise Iyer & Associates.

::: answer Model answer
**Relevant provisions.** The RBI's circular implementing the recommendations of the Committee on Legal Aspects of Bank Frauds provides that if an accounting professional, in the course of internal or external audit, finds anything susceptible to be fraud or fraudulent activity or act of excess power, or smells any foul play in any transaction, he should refer the matter to the regulator, and any deliberate failure on the part of the auditor renders him liable for action. SA 250 explains that the duty of confidentiality is over-ridden by statute, law or courts. Under SA 240 the auditor is responsible for obtaining reasonable assurance that the financial statements as a whole are free from material misstatement, whether caused by fraud or error. The auditor is not expected to look into each transaction but, if he comes across any such instance while performing his normal duties, he should report the matter to the RBI in addition to the Chairman/Managing Director/Chief Executive of the bank (SM 10.14–10.15).

**Facts of the case.** Premature closure of eleven deposits on mismatched signatures, with proceeds of ₹1.84 crore credited to a newly opened account, came to notice during normal audit work; the Zonal Manager relies on the confidentiality declaration and on an internal vigilance review.

**Analysis.** The transactions are susceptible to be fraud. The confidentiality declaration binds the auditors against disclosure to persons not legally entitled; the RBI, to whom the auditor is required to refer such matters, is entitled, and the duty of confidentiality is over-ridden where law requires disclosure. An internal vigilance review does not discharge the auditor's own reporting duty; a deliberate failure to report would make the auditor liable for action.

**Conclusion.** Iyer & Associates should refer the matter to the **RBI** and report it to the **Chairman/Managing Director/Chief Executive** of Narmada Bank, document the facts and the communication, and consider the effect of the matter on their audit procedures and report. The Zonal Manager's request should be declined.
:::

## Under the hood

### Gaps the Study Material leaves

The Study Material gives the appointing authority for a banking company, a nationalised bank and a Regional Rural Bank, but not for the State Bank of India; and it gives the remuneration rule for banking companies, nationalised banks and the State Bank of India, but not for Regional Rural Banks. Do not fill these gaps from memory in an exam answer.

### Fraud reporting under the Companies Act, as the chapter's own test puts it

The chapter's Test Your Knowledge (correct or incorrect statement 10) applies the Companies Act fraud-reporting route to a banking company: for a fraud of ₹150 lakh, the auditor reports to the Board or the Audit Committee immediately but not later than 2 days of his knowledge of the fraud, seeking their reply or observations within 45 days, and the banking company discloses the remedial action taken in the Board's report (SM 10.57–10.58). The section 143(12) thresholds and destinations are in [[aud.report.companies|company auditor's reporting]].

## Exam angle

::: exam What ICAI asked, May 2024 to September 2026
In the ICAI documents read for this lesson, the bank auditor's appointment and reports were asked mostly as 2-mark MCQs:

- **LFAR**: January 2026 MCQ 13 (four statements: who must furnish it, who specifies its matters, the submission date, whether an executive summary is mandatory); the old-scheme MTP May 2023 Series II asked the submission date directly (30th June).
- **SCAs' other reports**: MTP September 2024 Series I MCQ 5. The trap: the option list said "adverse credit-**lending** ratio"; the Study Material's report is on adverse **credit-deposit** ratio, so ICAI's answer left that item out.
- **CARO and banks**: MTP September 2024 Series I Q3(a) and MTP January 2025 Series II Q2(b) asked which companies CARO 2020 exempts (5 marks); a banking company is the first on the list.
- **Accepting a branch audit**: MTP September 2024 Series II MCQs 9 and 10 (an undertaking that the firm is not disqualified under section 141(3)(d)(ii) for indebtedness: in ICAI's answer the provision addresses self-interest threats and the firm can give the undertaking after verifying that it complies; and a non-disclosure declaration, which is about confidentiality). The old-scheme MTP November 2023 Series II Q6(a) (3 marks) asked whether a bank's concurrent auditor could take its statutory branch audit in the same year.
- **Pressure from the branch**: MTP September 2026 Series I MCQ 12: a branch manager suggesting that irregularities not be reported calls on integrity and objectivity.

The chapter's Test Your Knowledge adds an MCQ on who appoints a nationalised bank's branch auditor, correct/incorrect statements on the AGM appointment and on fraud reporting, and "Is the statutory auditor of a bank required to report under CARO 2020?".
:::

::: exam How the marks are earned
For appointment questions, the marks are in three words per bank: who appoints, whose approval, who fixes the fee. For the report of a nationalised bank's auditor, ICAI's points are the four matters (a) to (d) in order, addressed to the Central Government.

Banking company and nationalised bank: **RBI** approval before appointment. Regional Rural Bank: **Central Government** approval. Condition: this is the approval for appointment only; the remuneration rules are separate (general meeting under section 142 for a banking company; RBI with the Central Government for nationalised banks and the State Bank of India).

### A reporting case

1. **Provision**: whom the report goes to and what it must state, in the Study Material's order; or the RBI circular, SA 250 and SA 240 for a fraud case.
2. **Facts**: the kind of bank, the gap in the draft or the suspicious transaction.
3. **Analysis**: why the provision applies (confidentiality is over-ridden; CARO excluded for a banking company; unaudited branches need figures).
4. **Conclusion**: the corrected report or the report to the RBI and the Chairman/MD/CEO.

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: the nationalised bank's AGM
Writing that a nationalised bank's auditor "is appointed at the annual general meeting". **Fix:** it is appointed by the bank acting through its Board of Directors, with the RBI's prior approval; the AGM appointment is for a banking company.
:::

::: mistake The CARO annexure that follows every report
Attaching CARO 2020 to a bank's report out of habit. **Fix:** CARO 2020 does not apply to a banking company as defined in section 5(c) of the Banking Regulation Act; the section 143 matters are still stated.
:::

::: mistake "The vigilance department will handle it"
Treating an internal investigation or a confidentiality clause as a reason not to report. **Fix:** anything susceptible to be fraud goes to the RBI, in addition to the Chairman/MD/CEO; confidentiality is over-ridden by statute, law or courts (SA 250).
:::

## Recap

- A bank is audited by a person duly qualified to be an auditor of companies (section 30(1), Banking Regulation Act); eligibility and disqualifications are as for a company auditor.
- Appointment: banking company at the AGM, nationalised bank by its Board, both with RBI approval; Regional Rural Bank by the bank with Central Government approval.
- Remuneration: banking company under section 142 (general meeting); nationalised banks and SBI by the RBI in consultation with the Central Government.
- Powers: the same access to books, accounts, documents and vouchers as a company's auditor.
- A nationalised bank's auditor reports to the Central Government: true and fair view and explanations; transactions within powers; adequacy of branch returns; any other matter.
- Report the number of unaudited branches and their advances, deposits, interest income and interest expense; banking company: section 143 matters yes, CARO 2020 no.
- LFAR: matters specified by the RBI; SCAs submit it latest by 30 June; executive summary optional.
- Anything susceptible to be fraud: report to the RBI in addition to the Chairman/MD/CEO; confidentiality is over-ridden by law (SA 250). (The invigilator tells the board, not just the principal.)

## Flashcards

Q:: Banking Regulation Act, section 30(1): who must audit a banking company's accounts?
A:: A person duly qualified under any law for the time being in force to be an auditor of companies.

Q:: Bank audit: who appoints the auditor of a banking company, and with whose approval?
A:: The shareholders at the annual general meeting, with the prior approval of the Reserve Bank of India.

Q:: Bank audit: who appoints the auditor of a nationalised bank?
A:: The bank, acting through its Board of Directors, with the prior approval of the Reserve Bank of India.

Q:: Bank audit: who appoints the auditors of a Regional Rural Bank?
A:: The bank concerned, with the approval of the Central Government.

Q:: Bank audit: who fixes the remuneration of the auditors of nationalised banks and SBI?
A:: The Reserve Bank of India, in consultation with the Central Government. (Banking company: under section 142, by the general meeting.)

Q:: Bank audit: what four matters does a nationalised bank's auditor state in his report to the Central Government?
A:: True and fair view, and whether explanations called for were given and satisfactory; whether transactions noticed were within the bank's powers; whether returns from offices and branches were adequate; any other matter for the Central Government's notice.

Q:: Bank audit: what must the report disclose about unaudited branches?
A:: Their number, and the quantification of their advances, deposits, interest income and interest expense.

Q:: CARO 2020: does it apply to a banking company?
A:: No, not to a banking company as defined in section 5(c) of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949; its auditor still states the matters covered by section 143 of the Companies Act, 2013.

Q:: LFAR: who specifies its matters, and by when do the SCAs submit it?
A:: The Reserve Bank of India specifies the matters; the Statutory Central Auditors submit it to the bank latest by 30th June every year.

Q:: Bank audit: to whom does the auditor report a suspected fraud found in normal audit work?
A:: To the RBI, in addition to the Chairman/Managing Director/Chief Executive of the bank (RBI circular on the Committee on Legal Aspects of Bank Frauds).

Q:: Bank audit: what is the "declaration of indebtedness"?
A:: A declaration banks obtain before appointing statutory central or branch auditors; indebtedness means owing money to the bank in any case whatsoever.
