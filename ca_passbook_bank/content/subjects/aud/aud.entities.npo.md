## In plain words

### Kesar starts a foundation

Kesar has decided that Tapri should give back. He sets up **Tapri Foundation**, a public charitable trust in Indore, to train women who run roadside tea stalls and to feed people after disasters. Every kulhad café gets a donation box.

> **Kesar:** It is charity, Lakshmi-ji. No profit, no shareholders, no audit drama. Sab adjust ho jayega.
> **Lakshmi:** No profit is exactly why it needs an audit, Kesar-ji. Donors give money for a purpose. Somebody must check it reached that purpose.
> **Bunty:** Ma'am, but what do we even tick? There are no sales!
> **Lakshmi:** The trust deed, the donor's letter, the receipt book and the donation box, Bunty.

A **non-governmental organisation (NGO)** raises money from members, donors and contributors (and time, energy and skills from volunteers) to achieve social objectives such as education, medical help or disaster relief. In India it usually takes one of three legal forms: a **trust**, a **society**, or a **section 8 company**. Each form has its own law, and on top of that the Income Tax Act decides its tax exemption and the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act governs any money from abroad.

With no profit to measure, the audit asks other questions. Is the organisation run as its constitution (the trust deed, or the society's rules) says? Was every rupee received recorded, from cheques to coins in a donation box? Was money given for a purpose (a building, a revolving loan fund, flood relief) used for it? Did any trustee or committee member benefit? Were the special laws, especially for foreign money, complied with?

::: analogy The wedding gift envelope
At an Indian wedding, envelopes are counted by a trusted uncle with a register: who gave, how much, against which receipt. Some envelopes come with instructions ("this is for the couple's house, not the party"). A careful family keeps that money aside. An NGO audit is checking the uncle's register: every envelope recorded, every instruction followed, and nobody's cousin quietly "borrowing" from the pile.
:::

::: key
In an NGO, trust or society, the auditor's anchor is the constitution and the donor's terms: verify that all receipts (including box collections and foreign contributions) are recorded and banked, that restricted money (corpus, revolving and earmarked funds, specific grants) is used only as directed, that no trustee or committee member benefits, and that the governing laws are complied with.
:::

### 2. What an NGO is and how it is formed

::: definition NGO
NGOs can be defined as non-profit making organisations which raise funds from members, donors or contributors apart from receiving donation of time, energy and skills for achieving their social objectives like imparting education, providing medical facilities, economic assistance to poor, managing disasters and emergent situations (SM 9.27).
:::

The definition includes religious organisations, voluntary health and welfare agencies, charitable organisations, hospitals, old age homes, research foundations and so on (SM 9.27). There are three basic legal forms of charitable entities under Indian law (SM 9.28, 9.75):

::: diagram Three legal forms, and the law that governs each
{"type": "layers", "layers": [{"label": "Trust", "note": "State Public Trust Act; if none, Indian Trusts Act, 1882"}, {"label": "Society", "note": "Societies Registration Act, 1860"}, {"label": "Section 8 company", "note": "Companies Act, 2013"}, {"label": "Every form", "note": "Income Tax Act, 1961; FCRA, 2010 for foreign contributions"}], "caption": "The legal form decides the governing law; the Income Tax Act applies to all, and the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act when foreign contributions are received (SM 9.75)."}
:::

- **Registration.** None of these Acts makes registration mandatory for an NGO. But a trust relating to immovable property worth more than one hundred rupees must comply with section 17(1) of the Registration Act, 1908 read with section 123 of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882, so its registration becomes mandatory. In some States, such as Maharashtra and Gujarat, all charitable trusts must register under their Public Trusts Acts (for example the Bombay Public Trusts Act, 1950). Registration under the Income Tax Act, 1961 and the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act, 2010 is also invoked in many cases (SM 9.28).
- **Basis of accounting.** NGOs registered under the Companies Act, 2013 must keep their books on the **accrual basis** (section 128); otherwise it is non-compliance with the Act. ==NGOs not registered under the Companies Act may keep accounts on either the accrual or the cash basis== (SM 9.28).

### 3. Where the money comes from, and where it goes

The main **sources of funds** are grants and donations, fund raising programmes, advertisements, fees from members, technical assistance fees or fees for services, subscriptions, gifts, and sale of produce or publications. The Study Material then describes five kinds (SM 9.28–9.29):

- (a) **Donations and grants in the nature of promoter's contribution** are capital receipts, shown as **liabilities** in the NGO's balance sheet, either as a corpus contribution or as a contribution to a revolving fund.
- (b) Under **section 11(1)(d) of the Income Tax Act, 1961**, voluntary contributions made with a specific direction that they shall form part of the corpus of the trust or institution are not included in total income.
- (c) **Revolving fund** contributions (defined below).
- (d) **Donations and grants for specific fixed assets**: their primary condition is that the NGO purchases, constructs or otherwise acquires the assets for which they are given.
- (e) **Contributions in kind**: assets such as land, buildings, vehicles and office equipment, and articles for programmes such as food, books, building materials, clothes, beds, and raw material for training (wool, reeds, cloth).

::: definition Corpus contribution
A contribution made towards the capital or the corpus of an NGO is known as corpus contribution. The donors are generally required to specify whether the donation or grant given by them shall form part of the corpus of the NGO. Such contributions are generally given with reference to the total funds required by an NGO (SM 9.28).
:::

::: definition Revolving fund
The objective of a contribution or grant towards a revolving fund is to rotate the amount by giving temporary loans from the fund to other NGOs or beneficiaries for their projects, and then recover the loan so as to give temporary loans again, and so on. Any interest earned from the beneficiary on such loans could be either added back to the fund or credited to the Income and Expenditure Account, depending on the restrictions laid down by the authority providing the contribution or by the NGO's own rules (SM 9.29).
:::

The **application of funds** covers establishment costs, office and administrative expenses, maintenance expenses, programme or project expenses, and charity, donations and contributions given (SM 9.29).

### 4. Appointment, statutes and planning

The auditors of an NGO registered as a society under the Societies Registration Act, 1860 (or a corresponding State law) or as a trust under the Indian Trusts Act, 1882 are **normally appointed by the management** of the society or trust; those of a section 8 company **by its members**. Statutes such as the Companies Act, 2013, the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act, 2010 and the Income Tax Act, 1961 require the accounts to be audited and submitted to the prescribed authorities, and failure can mean **forfeiture of exemptions and benefits**. The FCRA prescribes the format of the audit report, to be furnished to the Ministry of Home Affairs within 60 days from the close of the financial year, i.e. by 30 May each year (SM 9.29–9.30).

::: warning
Statutory formats and due dates under the FCRA and the Income Tax Act are version-sensitive. Verify against the Study Material and Statutory Update for your attempt; this lesson states them as the Study Material does.
:::

**While planning the audit, the auditor may concentrate on** (SM 9.30):

- (i) knowledge of the NGO's work, its mission and vision, areas of operations and the environment in which it operates;
- (ii) updating knowledge of relevant statutes, especially recent amendments, circulars and judicial decisions (the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act, 2010, the Societies Registration Act, 1860, the Income Tax Act, 1961 and so on) and the rules under them;
- (iii) reviewing the legal form of the organisation and its Memorandum of Association, Articles of Association, Rules and Regulations;
- (iv) reviewing the NGO's organisation chart, its Financial and Administrative Manuals, Project and Programme Guidelines, funding agencies' requirements and formats, and budgetary policies if any;
- (v) examining the minutes of the Board, Managing Committee, Governing Body or Management, and their committees, to ascertain the impact of any decisions on the financial records;
- (vi) studying the accounting system, procedures, internal controls and internal checks, and verifying their applicability;
- (vii) setting materiality levels for audit purposes;
- (viii) the nature and timing of reports or other communications;
- (ix) the involvement of experts and their reports; and
- (x) reviewing the previous year's audit report.

### 5. The audit programme: funds, assets, liabilities and expenses

The audit programme should include, ==in a sequential order, all assets, liabilities, income and expenditure, ensuring that no material item is omitted== (SM 9.30–9.32):

| Item | What the auditor does |
|---|---|
| (i) Corpus fund | Vouch contributions towards corpus with special reference to the donors' letters; check interest income with the Investment Register and the investments physically in hand |
| (ii) Reserves | Vouch transfers from projects and programmes with donors' letters and board resolutions; check transfer of the gross value of assets sold from capital reserve to general reserve, and adjustments during the year |
| (iii) Earmarked funds | Check the donor institutions' requirements, the board resolution and the rules of the schemes of the earmarked funds (earmarking: setting aside a determined amount for a specific goal) |
| (iv) Project or agency balances | Vouch disbursements and expenditure as per the agreements with donors, for each balance |
| (v) Loans | Vouch with the loan agreements and the counterfoils of receipts issued |
| (vi) Fixed assets | Vouch all acquisitions, sales or disposals, depreciation and their authorisations; check donors' letters or agreements for grants; for immovable property, check title |
| (vii) Investments | Check the Investment Register and the investments physically, ensuring they are in the NGO's name; verify investments and disinvestments for approval by the appropriate authority, and trace principal and interest in the bank accounts |
| (viii) Cash in hand | Physically verify cash and imprest balances at the close of the year and agree them with the books |
| (ix) Bank balances | Check the bank reconciliation statements; ascertain details of old outstanding and unadjusted amounts |
| (x) Inventory | Verify inventory in hand; obtain a management certificate for quantities and valuation |
| (xi) Programme and project expenses | Verify the donor agreement for the conditions of the programme; for contracts, ensure income tax is deducted, deposited and returns filed, and verify the contract terms |
| (xii) Establishment expenses | Verify that provident fund, life insurance premium, employees' state insurance and their administrative charges are deducted, contributed and deposited in time; check postage, stationery, travelling and other office expenses |

### 6. Receipts of income of an NGO

The receipt of income may be checked on these lines (SM 9.32):

- (i) **Contributions and grants for projects and programmes**: check agreements with donors and grant letters to ensure that funds received have been accounted for. ==Check that all foreign contribution receipts are deposited in the foreign contribution bank account as notified under the FCRA, 2010==.
- (ii) **Receipts from fund raising programmes**: verify in detail the internal control system; ascertain who is responsible for collecting funds and the mode of receipt; ensure collections are counted and deposited in the bank daily.
- (iii) **Membership fees**: check with the Membership Register; ensure proper classification between entrance, annual and life membership fees; reconcile fees received with fees receivable for the year.
- (iv) **Subscriptions**: check with the subscription register and receipts issued; reconcile with the printing and dispatch of the corresponding magazines, circulars or periodicals; check against the subscription rate schedule.
- (v) **Interest and dividends**: check those received and receivable with the investments held during the year.

### 7. Audit of a charitable institution

Attention should be paid to the following matters (SM 9.40–9.42):

1. **General**: (i) study the constitution under which the institution has been set up; (ii) verify whether it is managed in the manner contemplated by the law under which it has been set up; (iii) examine the system of internal check, especially over amounts collected; (iv) verify the income in detail and confirm that amounts received have been deposited in the bank regularly and promptly; (v) examine the Trust Deed or the Regulations.
2. **Subscriptions and donations**: (i) ascertain any changes in the amount of annual or life membership subscription during the year; (ii) whether official receipts are issued:
   - (a) confirm adequate control over unused receipt books;
   - (b) obtain all receipt books covering the period under review;
   - (c) test check the counterfoils with the cash book, looking specially into any cancelled receipts;
   - (d) obtain the printed list of subscriptions and donations and agree it with the total collections in the accounts;
   - (e) examine the internal check over money from box collections, flag days and so on, check amounts received from representatives with the correspondence and official receipts, and pay special attention to the controls ensuring all collections are accounted for;
   - (f) verify total subscriptions and donations with any figures published in the charity's reports.
3. **Legacies**: verify the amounts received with the correspondence and other available information.
4. **Grants**: vouch with correspondence, receipts and minute books; obtain a certificate from a responsible official of the grants received.
5. **Investment income**: (i) vouch with dividend and interest counterfoils; (ii) check calculations of interest on fixed-interest securities; (iii) check that the appropriate dividend is received where an investment is sold ex-dividend or bought cum-dividend; (iv) compare dividends received with the schedule of investments, enquiring into any investment on which no dividend was received.
6. **Rent**: examine the rent roll and tenancy agreements, noting the amounts and due dates of rent; vouch the rent onto the rent roll from the receipt counterfoils and check the cash book totals.
7. **Special functions**: vouch gross receipts and outgoings of concerts, dramatic performances and the like held in aid of the charity; verify that the proceeds of all tickets issued are accounted for, after allowing for returns.
8. **Income-tax refunds**: where tax has been deducted at source from investment income, see that a refund has been obtained, since charitable institutions are exempt from income tax: vouch the refund with the correspondence with the Income-tax Department and check the calculation of the claims.
9. **Expenditure**: (i) vouch payments of grants and verify that they were paid only for charitable purposes within the objects, and that no trustee, director or member of the Managing Committee has benefited directly or indirectly; (ii) verify the schedules of securities and the inventories of movable and immovable properties, by inspecting securities and title deeds and physically verifying movable properties on a test basis; (iii) verify cash and bank payments; (iv) ascertain that funds contributed for a special purpose have been used for that purpose.

### 8. Audit of trusts

Charitable and religious trusts should maintain regular books of account; this lets the management demonstrate due discharge of its responsibilities. **The auditor is required to report whether the trust has maintained proper books of account**, including (SM 9.75–9.76): (i) cash book; (ii) ledger; (iii) journal; (iv) copies of bills (machine-numbered or otherwise serially numbered) wherever the trust issues bills, and copies or counterfoils of such serially numbered receipts issued by the trust; (v) original bills wherever issued to the person, and receipts for payments made by the person; (vi) any other book needed to give a true and fair view of the state of affairs and explain the transactions. Every year the trust prepares a balance sheet and income and expenditure statement from its books, in the format prescribed under the respective State law.

**Auditor's responsibility** (SM 9.76–9.77). Obtain the list of books and records maintained and match it with the mandatory requirements, then verify the records. Comply with the Accounting Standards and Standards on Auditing prescribed and made mandatory by ICAI; use professional skill and apply such audit tests as the circumstances require; apply generally accepted auditing procedures, with **test checks depending on the evaluation of internal control**; keep **materiality** in mind; and keep detailed notes of the evidence relied on, and all working papers.

**The auditor of a trust has to ascertain** (SM 9.77–9.79):

- (a) whether accounts are maintained regularly and in accordance with the provisions of the applicable Act and the rules;
- (b) whether receipts and disbursements are properly and correctly shown, and money received as donations is applied as per the objects of the trust and as per the donor's specific direction, if any;
- (c) whether the cash balance and vouchers in the custody of the manager or trustee on the date of audit agreed with the accounts;
- (d) whether all books, deeds, accounts, vouchers or other documents or records required by the auditor were produced;
- (e) whether a register of movable and immovable properties is maintained, its changes communicated from time to time to the regional office, and the defects mentioned in the previous audit report complied with and rectified;
- (f) whether the manager, trustee or any other person required to appear before the auditor did so and furnished the necessary information;
- (g) whether any property or funds of the trust were applied for any object or purpose other than the trust's;
- (h) the amounts outstanding for more than one year and the amounts written off, if any;
- (i) whether any money of the public trust has been invested contrary to the provisions of the applicable Act, as came to the auditor's notice;
- (j) all cases of irregular, illegal or improper expenditure, failure or omission to recover money or property of the trust, or loss or waste of its money or property, and whether caused by breach of trust, misapplication or other misconduct of the trustees or any other person managing the trust;
- (k) whether the maximum and minimum number of trustees is maintained;
- (l) whether the meetings are held regularly as provided in the instrument;
- (m) whether the minute books of the proceedings of meetings are maintained;
- (n) whether any of the trustees has any interest in the investments of the trust;
- (o) whether any of the trustees is a debtor or creditor of the trust;
- (p) whether anonymous donations received are properly accounted for, and cash donations are not received above the prescribed limit for accepting cash donations;
- (q) whether the irregularities pointed out by the auditors in the previous year's accounts have been duly complied with by the trustees; and
- (r) any special matter the auditor thinks fit or necessary to bring to the notice of the Deputy or Assistant Charity Commissioner.

### 9. Audit of societies

**The auditor's considerations** (SM 9.79–9.80):

- (a) Ascertain the governing legislation of the society, i.e. the Societies Registration Act, 1860 or any applicable State law under which it has been registered.
- (b) Ascertain the object of the society from its memorandum of association or bye-laws; its activities may include charitable, social, cultural or educational activities.
- (c) Ascertain whether it has obtained registration under the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act, 2010, in case foreign contributions are received.
- (d) Ascertain whether it is also registered under the relevant provisions of the Income Tax Act, which may make it eligible for tax exemption.
- (e) Obtain an understanding of internal control to design audit procedures, with special reference to donations and to expenditures incurred in relation to achieving the society's objects.
- (f) Evaluate the appropriateness of accounting policies, with special reference to donations and grants, and to specific grants.
- (g) Where expenses incurred by the society are reimbursed by donors, ascertain how these are recognised in the financial statements.
- (h) Ascertain whether any inquiry has been held by the Registrar under the applicable law into the working or financial condition of the society, and its implications for the auditor's opinion.
- (i) Ascertain all cases of irregular, illegal or improper expenditure, failure or omission to recover money or property of the society, or loss or waste of its money or property.
- (j) Ascertain whether such expenditure or waste was caused by breach of trust, misapplication or any other misconduct on the part of the governing body.

## Worked examples

:::: example Four letters, four treatments
In 2025-26 Tapri Foundation received the following. The accountant's treatment is shown against each:

| Receipt | Donor's letter | Accountant's treatment |
|---|---|---|
| ₹25 lakh from Kesar, a promoter | "shall form part of the corpus of the Foundation" | Income and Expenditure A/c |
| ₹20 lakh from Kamla Mehra, a promoter | "for a revolving fund for loans to women-run tea stalls; interest on the loans to pay trainees' stipends" | Revolving Fund (liability); interest to Income and Expenditure A/c |
| ₹40 lakh from Dev Malhotra | "for the Foundation's good work" | Corpus Fund |
| ₹12 lakh from Malwa Gramin Bank | "for buying a mobile kitchen van" | Income and Expenditure A/c |

From the revolving fund the Foundation lent ₹50,000 each to 40 stalls and received interest of ₹84,000. On 31 March 2026 the van had not been bought; the ₹12 lakh was in a fixed deposit.

Required:

(i) Comment on the accountant's treatment of each receipt.

(ii) State how the auditor should verify the corpus fund, the revolving fund and the van grant.

::: answer Model answer
**(i) Treatment** (SM 9.28–9.29).

- **Kesar's ₹25 lakh.** Donations in the nature of promoter's contribution are capital receipts shown as liabilities in the balance sheet; a contribution towards the corpus, which the donor has specified shall form part of the corpus, is a corpus contribution. Crediting it to income is wrong: it belongs to the Corpus Fund.
- **Kamla Mehra's ₹20 lakh.** A promoter's contribution towards a revolving fund is a capital receipt shown as a liability: correct. Interest on its loans is added back to the fund or credited to the Income and Expenditure Account depending on the restrictions laid down by the contributor. She directs it to stipends, so crediting the ₹84,000 to income is in order.
- **Dev's ₹40 lakh.** Donors are generally required to specify whether a donation shall form part of the corpus, and section 11(1)(d) of the Income Tax Act also turns on such a specific direction. His letter gives none, so the amount cannot be shown as corpus contribution; it is a general donation of the year.
- **The bank's ₹12 lakh.** A grant for a specific fixed asset, whose primary condition is that the Foundation acquires the van. The condition is unfulfilled at the year-end, so the auditor evaluates whether crediting it to income is an appropriate accounting policy for a specific grant.

**(ii) Audit procedures** (SM 9.30–9.31).

- **Corpus fund**: vouch the ₹25 lakh with special reference to Kesar's letter; check interest on corpus investments with the Investment Register and the investments physically in hand.
- **Revolving fund**: check the contributor's requirements and the board resolution for the fund; vouch the 40 loans of ₹50,000 (₹20 lakh) with the loan agreements and receipt counterfoils; see that the ₹84,000 is applied to stipends as directed.
- **Van grant**: check the bank's letter; verify the fixed deposit with the Investment Register and the bank, in the Foundation's name, so that the money has not gone to any other purpose; when the van is bought, vouch its acquisition, depreciation and authorisation.
:::

::::: example The scholarship drive
In 2025-26 Tapri Foundation ran a "Kulhad Scholarship" drive for children of tea-stall workers. It raised ₹1.40 crore (₹38.40 lakh in donation boxes at 40 cafés, ₹79.60 lakh online and ₹22 lakh from a UK-based diaspora trust) and passed all of it to four partner organisations (₹45, 35, 30 and 30 lakh), which select the students. Bunty's working papers note:

(i) Each café's box is opened and counted by the café manager alone, and the cash is banked weekly. Of 12 receipt books issued to volunteers for a walkathon, 9 have come back.

(ii) The ₹22 lakh from the UK trust was credited to the Foundation's ordinary savings account.

(iii) One ₹30 lakh remittance went by demand draft to Gyan Jyoti Society; the address on the draft differs from its letterhead, and nobody has seen its 80G registration number.

(iv) No partner has acknowledged its remittance; one partner's annual report shows an office "bought from scholarship receipts".

(v) The four partners were chosen on one trustee's recommendation, with no record of how.

> **Bunty:** Ma'am, ₹1.40 crore in, ₹1.40 crore out. It tallies, I can tick it!
> **Lakshmi:** A tally is where we start, Bunty, not where we stop.

Comment on each observation, stating the audit procedures for the receipt of donations and their remittance to the partners.

:::: example Annapurna Trust's awkward audit
Shri Annapurna Annakshetra Trust is a public charitable trust in Pune running free kitchens, registered under the Bombay Public Trusts Act, 1950. The auditor notes:

(i) The trust deed requires at least five trustees; two have died, and only three remain.

(ii) A trustee's catering firm has owed the trust ₹4.20 lakh of hall rent for 18 months.

(iii) ₹25 lakh of trust money was placed in a private finance company in which another trustee is a director; the Act under which the trust works does not permit such an investment.

(iv) Minutes were not written for two of the year's four trustee meetings.

(v) Last year's audit report pointed out that the register of immovable properties was incomplete; it still is.

What matters should the auditor ascertain and report?

::: answer Model answer
**Relevant provision.** The auditor of a public trust has to ascertain the matters listed in the Study Material, (a) to (r), including the number of trustees, trustees' interests and debts, amounts outstanding over one year, investments contrary to the applicable Act, minute books, the register of properties, last year's irregularities, and special matters for the Deputy or Assistant Charity Commissioner (SM 9.77–9.79).

**Analysis.**

- (i) The minimum number of trustees is not maintained: report under (k).
- (ii) The trustee's firm is a **debtor** of the trust (o), and the ₹4.20 lakh has been outstanding for more than one year (h): report both, and enquire into recovery (j).
- (iii) Trust money invested contrary to the applicable Act (i), in which a trustee has an interest (n): report; consider whether it was caused by breach of trust or misconduct of the trustees (j).
- (iv) Minute books not maintained for two meetings (m); whether meetings are held regularly as per the instrument is also checked (l).
- (v) The register of properties is incomplete and the previous audit's defect is not rectified (e), (q).

**Conclusion.** The auditor should report each matter, and bring the investment in the trustee-linked company and the trustee debtor to the notice of the Deputy or Assistant Charity Commissioner as special matters (r).

## Under the hood

### What the trust auditor's working papers must show

The working papers should include notes on (a) the work done and by whom; (b) explanations and information given, and by whom; (c) decisions on the various points; (d) judicial pronouncements relied upon in drafting the report; (e) client certificates and management letters. They must show that (i) the opinion rests on the auditor's own examination; (ii) the information and explanations were given due cognizance, so it is not arbitrary; (iii) all necessary information was called for; (iv) explanations were not merely relied on but subjected to reasonable tests (SM 9.76–9.77). It is [[aud.doc.sa230|SA 230]] applied to a trust.

## Answer frame

### A list question ("points while planning", "receipts of income", "audit programme")

1. One opening line from the Study Material ("While planning the audit, the auditor may concentrate on the following").
2. ICAI's points, in ICAI's order, each with its heading in bold.
3. In a case: a closing line applying the points to the facts (the weekly banking, the demand draft).

### A trust or society case

**Relevant provision** (the matters the auditor ascertains) → **facts** → **analysis** (each fact to its point, by letter) → **conclusion** (report, and for a public trust any special matter to the Deputy or Assistant Charity Commissioner).

### "Distinguish corpus contribution and revolving fund"

| Point | Corpus contribution | Revolving fund |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Capital or corpus of the NGO | Temporary loans to other NGOs or beneficiaries, recovered and lent again |
| Donor's role | Specifies that it forms part of the corpus | Lays down restrictions, including on interest |
| Presentation | Capital receipt, liability in the balance sheet | Capital receipt, liability in the balance sheet |

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: the corpus that became income
Bunty sees a corpus donation credited to the Income and Expenditure Account and ticks it, because "donations are income". **Fix:** promoter's contributions towards corpus or a revolving fund are **capital receipts, shown as liabilities**; vouch them with the donors' letters, which carry the corpus direction.
:::

::: mistake Ticking the total and forgetting the trustees
A trust audit that agrees cash and bank and stops. **Fix:** the list of matters includes the people: the minimum and maximum number of trustees, a trustee's interest in investments, a trustee as debtor or creditor, and any benefit to a trustee or committee member from grants paid.
:::

::: mistake "Every NGO must use the accrual basis"
**Fix:** only NGOs registered under the Companies Act, 2013 must (section 128); others may use the accrual or the cash basis.
:::

## Practice

::: quiz Check yourself
Q: Case scenario. CA Neha Bansal audits three NGOs, each keeping its books on the cash basis: Asha Trust (a public trust), Sahyog Samiti (a society registered under the Societies Registration Act, 1860) and Prerna Foundation (a section 8 company). Which of the following is correct?
- [ ] All three are non-compliant, since every NGO must keep accrual-basis accounts
- [ ] Only Asha Trust is non-compliant, since public trusts must keep accrual-basis accounts
- [ ] None is non-compliant, since the basis of accounting is a matter for the governing body
- [x] Only Prerna Foundation is non-compliant, since an NGO registered under the Companies Act, 2013 must keep its books on the accrual basis under section 128
> NGOs not registered under the Companies Act may keep accounts on the accrual or the cash basis; a section 8 company must use the accrual basis (SM 9.28).

Q: A donor gives ₹10 lakh to a charitable trust. What decides whether it is a corpus contribution?
- [ ] Whether the amount exceeds ₹5 lakh
- [x] Whether the donor specifies that it shall form part of the corpus of the trust
- [ ] Whether the trustees decide to invest it in fixed deposits
- [ ] Whether it is received by cheque
> The donor specifies it; section 11(1)(d) of the Income Tax Act also turns on that specific direction (SM 9.28–9.29).

Q: Saathi Society's revolving fund earned interest of ₹60,000 on loans to self-help groups. Where should it go?
- [ ] Always to the Income and Expenditure Account, since interest is revenue
- [ ] Always back to the revolving fund, since the fund must keep growing
- [x] Back to the fund or to the Income and Expenditure Account, depending on the restrictions of the contributing authority or the society's own rules
- [ ] To the corpus fund
> It depends on the contributor's restrictions or the NGO's rules (SM 9.29); the auditor checks those documents.

Q: Select all the matters that the auditor of a public trust has to ascertain.
- [x] Whether any of the trustees is a debtor or creditor of the trust
- [x] Whether the maximum and minimum number of trustees is maintained
- [ ] Whether the trust has distributed its surplus among the trustees in proportion to their contributions
- [x] Whether anonymous donations are properly accounted for and cash donations are within the prescribed limit
> Not on the list: distributing surplus to trustees would be property applied for a purpose other than the trust's, reported under (g) (SM 9.77–9.79).

Q: Navjeevan Welfare Society lets its hall to a firm owned by the president's brother. Rent of ₹3.60 lakh has not been collected for two years, and the governing body has resolved to "drop the matter". What should the auditor do?
- [ ] Accept the resolution, since the governing body may waive any amount due to the society
- [x] Ascertain this failure to recover money belonging to the society, and whether it was caused by misconduct on the part of the governing body
- [ ] Report it to the Registrar of Societies and withdraw from the audit
- [ ] Ignore it, since uncollected rent does not affect a society's Income and Expenditure Account
> Considerations (i) and (j): ascertain all cases of failure or omission to recover monies belonging to the society, and whether caused by breach of trust, misapplication or misconduct on the part of the governing body (SM 9.80). The resolution does not end the auditor's duty.

## Recap

- NGO: a non-profit organisation raising funds from members, donors or contributors, plus donated time and skills, for social objectives. Forms: trust (State Public Trust Act, else Indian Trusts Act, 1882), society (Societies Registration Act, 1860), section 8 company; plus the Income Tax Act and, for foreign money, the FCRA, 2010.
- Section 8 companies must use the accrual basis; other NGOs may use accrual or cash.
- Promoter's contributions (corpus, revolving fund) are capital receipts shown as liabilities; corpus needs the donor's direction; revolving-fund interest follows the contributor's restrictions or the NGO's rules.
- Auditors: appointed by management (trust, society) or members (section 8 company); FCRA report to the Ministry of Home Affairs within 60 days of year-end.
- Planning: ten points (knowledge, statutes, legal form, organisation and manuals, minutes, systems, materiality, reports, experts, previous report). Programme: every asset, liability, income and expenditure in sequence.
- Receipts: grant agreements; foreign contributions in the FCRA account; fund-raising collections counted and banked daily; fees and subscriptions to registers.
- Charitable institutions: constitution, receipt books, box collections, legacies, grants, investment income, rent, special functions, tax refunds, and grants paid only for the objects, with no benefit to trustees.
- Trusts: proper books; matters (a) to (r), including trustees' numbers, interests and debts, anonymous and cash donations, and special matters for the Charity Commissioner. Societies: governing law, objects, FCRA and tax registration, Registrar's inquiry, misconduct of the governing body. (Every envelope in the uncle's register.)

## Flashcards

Q:: Define an NGO (ICAI).
A:: A non-profit making organisation which raises funds from members, donors or contributors, apart from receiving donation of time, energy and skills, for achieving social objectives like education, medical facilities, economic assistance to the poor, and managing disasters.

Q:: What are the three basic legal forms of charitable entities in India, and the law for each?
A:: Trust (the State's Public Trust Act, or the Indian Trusts Act, 1882 where none exists); society (Societies Registration Act, 1860); section 8 company (Companies Act, 2013). The Income Tax Act applies to all, and the FCRA, 2010 to foreign contributions.

Q:: When does registration of an NGO trust become mandatory?
A:: When the trust relates to immovable property worth more than one hundred rupees (section 17(1) of the Registration Act, 1908 read with section 123 of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882); in Maharashtra and Gujarat all charitable trusts register under the Public Trusts Acts.

Q:: Which NGOs must keep accounts on the accrual basis?
A:: Those registered under the Companies Act, 2013 (section 128). Others may use the accrual or the cash basis.

Q:: What is a corpus contribution?
A:: A contribution towards the capital or corpus of an NGO; donors generally specify that it shall form part of the corpus. It is a capital receipt shown as a liability.

Q:: What is a revolving fund, and where does its interest go?
A:: A fund rotated by giving temporary loans to other NGOs or beneficiaries and recovering them to lend again. Interest is added back to the fund or credited to the Income and Expenditure Account, depending on the contributor's restrictions or the NGO's rules.

Q:: How does the auditor verify the corpus fund of an NGO?
A:: Vouch contributions with special reference to the donors' letters; check the interest income with the Investment Register and the investments physically in hand.

Q:: How should contributions and grants for NGO projects be checked?
A:: Check agreements with donors and grant letters to ensure funds received are accounted for, and that all foreign contribution receipts are deposited in the foreign contribution bank account notified under the FCRA, 2010.

Q:: Charitable institution: name four checks over receipt books for subscriptions and donations.
A:: Control over unused receipt books; obtain all receipt books for the period; test check counterfoils with the cash book, especially cancelled receipts; agree the printed list of subscriptions and donations with the collections in the accounts.

Q:: Charitable institution: what must be verified when vouching grants paid?
A:: That they were paid only for charitable purposes within the institution's objects, and that no trustee, director or member of the Managing Committee benefited directly or indirectly.

Q:: Name six matters the auditor of a public trust has to ascertain about trustees and donations.
A:: Minimum and maximum number of trustees maintained; trustee's interest in the trust's investments; trustee as debtor or creditor; donations applied per objects and donor's direction; anonymous donations accounted for; cash donations within the prescribed limit.

Q:: Society audit: which inquiry must the auditor ascertain, and why?
A:: Any inquiry by the Registrar under the applicable law into the society's working or financial condition, because of its implications for the auditor's opinion.
