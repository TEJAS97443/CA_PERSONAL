## In plain words

### "Every report says true and fair. What was hard?"

Tapri Tea Co. Ltd has been listed on NSE and BSE since 2024, and this year's AGM has a sharp shareholder in the second row.

> **Shareholder:** Ms Iyer, your report says "true and fair". Every report says that. What was difficult in this audit?
> **Lakshmi:** Turn to the section headed "Key Audit Matters". That is the answer.
> **Bunty:** *(whispering)* Ma'am, so those are the problems we qualified?
> **Lakshmi:** No, Bunty. The opinion is on the accounts as a whole. These are the places where the audit had to work hardest.

For a listed company the auditor's report has a section of **key audit matters** (KAMs). They are picked in two cuts. Start with everything the auditor discussed with **those charged with governance** (for Tapri, the board and its audit committee). First cut: keep the matters that needed **significant auditor attention**, such as high-risk areas, big judgements and estimates, and the year's big events. Second cut: keep the few that were **of most significance** in this year's audit. Each one gets its own subheading in the report.

A KAM is extra information about the audit. It is not a separate opinion on that item, and it never replaces the company's own disclosures, a modified opinion or going concern reporting.

::: analogy The pilot's announcement
"We have landed safely" is the opinion. "The parts of the flight that took most of the crew's attention were the crosswind at take-off and the storm over Nagpur" are the key audit matters. The landing is no less safe for the mention, nobody gets a separate certificate for the crosswind, and the announcement does not replace the safety card in your seat pocket. Had the plane landed in the wrong city, mentioning the storm would not do: that needs a very different announcement.
:::

::: key
Key audit matters are chosen from the matters communicated with those charged with governance: first those that required significant auditor attention, then those of most significance in the current period's audit. They add transparency; they are never a substitute for disclosures, a modified opinion or going concern reporting, and never a separate opinion.
:::

## How it works

SA 701, "Communicating Key Audit Matters in the Independent Auditor's Report", deals with the auditor's responsibility to communicate key audit matters in the auditor's report. It addresses both the auditor's judgement as to **what** to communicate and the **form and content** of that communication (SM 8.51).

::: definition Key audit matters
Those matters that, in the auditor's professional judgement, were ==of most significance in the audit of the financial statements of the current period==. Key audit matters are selected from matters communicated with those charged with governance (SM 8.52).
:::

### 1. Purpose

The purpose of communicating key audit matters is to **enhance the communicative value** of the auditor's report by providing **greater transparency about the audit that was performed**. It provides additional information to intended users to help them understand the matters that, in the auditor's professional judgement, were of most significance in the audit of the current period's financial statements. It may also help users understand the entity and the areas of significant management judgement in the audited financial statements (SM 8.52).

### 2. Objectives

The objectives of the auditor are to **determine** key audit matters and, **having formed an opinion** on the financial statements, **communicate** those matters by describing them in the auditor's report (SM 8.52).

### 3. Who must, and who may

SA 701 applies to audits of complete sets of general purpose financial statements (SM 8.14, 8.55):

- of **listed entities**: the auditor shall communicate key audit matters;
- where the auditor is **required by law or regulation** to communicate them, for example for entities that law characterises as public interest entities;
- where the auditor **decides** to communicate them, for example for entities of significant public interest because they have a large number and wide range of stakeholders, considering the nature and size of the business: financial institutions (banks, insurance companies, pension funds) and others such as charitable institutions.

The exception: when the auditor **disclaims an opinion**, the report shall not include a Key Audit Matters section, unless law or regulation requires it (SM 8.47, 8.55; [[aud.report.sa705|SA 705]]).

### 4. Determining key audit matters: two cuts

**Cut 1.** The auditor shall determine, from the matters communicated with those charged with governance, those matters that **required significant auditor attention** in performing the audit, taking into account (SM 8.52–8.53):

- (a) areas of **higher assessed risk of material misstatement**, or **significant risks** identified in accordance with [[aud.risk.sa315|SA 315]];
- (b) **significant auditor judgements** relating to areas in the financial statements that involved **significant management judgement**, including accounting estimates identified as having **high estimation uncertainty**; and
- (c) the effect on the audit of **significant events or transactions** that occurred during the period.

**Cut 2.** The auditor shall determine which of those matters were **of most significance** in the audit of the financial statements of the current period: these are the key audit matters (SM 8.53).

### 5. Communicating key audit matters

The auditor shall describe **each** key audit matter, using an **appropriate subheading**, in a separate section of the report under the heading "**Key Audit Matters**". Its introductory language shall state that (SM 8.53):

- (a) key audit matters are those matters that, in the auditor's professional judgement, were of most significance in the audit of the financial statements [of the current period]; and
- (b) these matters were addressed in the context of the audit of the financial statements as a whole, and in forming the auditor's opinion thereon, and the auditor **does not provide a separate opinion** on these matters.

In ICAI's illustrative report the section comes directly after the Basis for Opinion (SM 8.27). For listed entities and others reporting KAMs, [[aud.report.sa700|SA 700]] also requires the auditor's responsibilities section to say that the auditor determines key audit matters from the matters communicated with those charged with governance and describes them unless law or regulation precludes public disclosure (SM 8.20).

### 6. What communicating a key audit matter is not

Communicating key audit matters is in the context of the auditor having formed an opinion on the financial statements as a whole. It is **not** (SM 8.54):

- (a) a substitute for **disclosures** in the financial statements that the applicable financial reporting framework requires management to make, or that are otherwise necessary to achieve fair presentation;
- (b) a substitute for the auditor expressing a **modified opinion** when required by the circumstances of a specific audit engagement in accordance with SA 705 (Revised);
- (c) a substitute for reporting in accordance with **[[aud.complete.sa570|SA 570]]** when a material uncertainty exists relating to events or conditions that may cast significant doubt on an entity's ability to continue as a going concern; or
- (d) a **separate opinion** on individual matters.

### 7. Communication with those charged with governance

The auditor shall communicate with those charged with governance (SM 8.54):

- (a) those matters the auditor has determined to be the key audit matters; or
- (b) if applicable, the auditor's determination that there are **no key audit matters** to communicate in the report.

## Formats and formulas

### The Key Audit Matters section

*Reproduce from memory (SM 8.27, 8.53–8.54).*

**Key Audit Matters**

Key audit matters are those matters that, in our professional judgement, were of most significance in our audit of the financial statements of the current period. These matters were addressed in the context of our audit of the financial statements as a whole, and in forming our opinion thereon, and we do not provide a separate opinion on these matters.

[Description of each key audit matter in accordance with SA 701.]

Each key audit matter goes under an appropriate subheading of its own (SM 8.53).

**When there are none** (SM 8.54):

**Key Audit Matters**

[Except for the matter described in the Basis for Qualified (Adverse) Opinion section or Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern section,] We have determined that there are no [other] key audit matters to communicate in our report.

## Worked examples

:::: example The audit committee's list
Iyer & Associates audit Tapri Tea Co. Ltd, a listed company, for 2025-26. During the audit they communicated these matters to its audit committee:

1. Revenue from the Tapri app, about 3.2 crore orders a year processed by an automated system with discounts and cashbacks, assessed as a significant risk of material misstatement.
2. Impairment of café assets in six loss-making cities, carrying amount ₹86 crore, where the recoverable amount rests on management's footfall forecasts: an accounting estimate with high estimation uncertainty.
3. The acquisition during the year of Nagpur Samosa Works Pvt Ltd for ₹240 crore.
4. Petty cash at two cafés not counted weekly, with shortages of ₹18,000, reported as a deficiency in internal control.
5. The planned scope and timing of the audit.

Required:

(i) How should the auditor determine which of these are key audit matters, and which are likely to be?

(ii) How are they presented in the report?

(iii) Kesar asks that the impairment be "described as a key audit matter, so that the assumptions need not be disclosed in the notes". Advise.

::: answer Model answer
**(i) Relevant provision.** As per SA 701, the auditor determines, from the matters communicated with those charged with governance, those that required significant auditor attention, taking into account areas of higher assessed risk of material misstatement or significant risks identified under SA 315; significant auditor judgements relating to areas involving significant management judgement, including accounting estimates with high estimation uncertainty; and the effect on the audit of significant events or transactions of the period. Of these, the matters of most significance in the audit of the current period are the key audit matters.

**Analysis.** Matter 1 is a significant risk; matter 2 an estimate with high estimation uncertainty; matter 3 a significant transaction of the year. All three required significant auditor attention. Matter 4 (a petty cash deficiency of ₹18,000) and matter 5 (the routine scope and timing communication) were communicated but did not. Of matters 1 to 3, those of most significance in the current audit, in the auditor's professional judgement, are the key audit matters; all three are likely candidates.

**(ii) Presentation.** A separate section headed "Key Audit Matters", after the Basis for Opinion, opens with the two introductory statements (most significance in the current period's audit; addressed in the context of the audit as a whole, with no separate opinion). Each matter has its own subheading. The key audit matters determined are first communicated to those charged with governance.

**(iii) Advice.** Communicating key audit matters is not a substitute for disclosures that the applicable financial reporting framework requires management to make, or that are otherwise necessary to achieve fair presentation. The impairment assumptions remain management's disclosure. If a required disclosure is omitted, the auditor considers its effect on the opinion under SA 705. Kesar's request cannot be accepted.
:::
::::

:::: example Two shortcuts the CFO wants
CA Farhan Qureshi audits Malwa Masala Mills Ltd, a listed company, for 2025-26 (overall materiality ₹3 crore; profit before tax ₹62 crore; total assets ₹410 crore).

(a) The company has not provided for a customs demand of ₹14 crore that, on the evidence obtained, is a present liability. Nothing else is affected. The CFO proposes: "Describe it in the Key Audit Matters section and keep the opinion clean."

(b) A loan covenant breach creates a material uncertainty that may cast significant doubt on the company's ability to continue as a going concern. The going concern basis is still appropriate and Note 41 discloses the uncertainty adequately. The board wants it mentioned only as a key audit matter.

Advise CA Farhan on each proposal, taking each on its own.

::: answer Model answer
**Relevant provision.** As per SA 701, communicating key audit matters is in the context of the auditor having formed an opinion on the financial statements as a whole. It is not a substitute for expressing a modified opinion when required in accordance with SA 705, nor for reporting in accordance with SA 570 when a material uncertainty exists relating to going concern.

**(a) Facts and analysis.** The unprovided ₹14 crore is a misstatement supported by evidence. It is 4.67 times overall materiality and 22.6% of profit before tax, so it is material; it is confined to provisions and profit, 3.4% of total assets, so it is not pervasive. SA 705 therefore requires a qualified opinion; a KAM cannot replace it.

**Conclusion (a).** Express a **qualified opinion**, describing and quantifying the matter in the Basis for Qualified Opinion section. If there are no other key audit matters, the KAM section states: "Except for the matter described in the Basis for Qualified Opinion section, we have determined that there are no other key audit matters to communicate in our report."

**(b) Facts and analysis.** The going concern basis is appropriate and the material uncertainty is adequately disclosed. Under SA 570 the auditor then expresses an unmodified opinion and includes a separate section headed "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern", drawing attention to Note 41 and stating that the opinion is not modified in respect of the matter (SM 7.22).

**Conclusion (b).** The board's proposal cannot be accepted: the separate going concern section is required, and a key audit matter cannot substitute for it. If there are no other key audit matters, the KAM section begins "Except for the matter described in the Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern section".
:::

## Under the hood

### When a key audit matter is not described

ICAI's illustrative report says the auditor describes key audit matters "unless law or regulation precludes public disclosure about the matter or when, in extremely rare circumstances, we determine that a matter should not be communicated in our report because the adverse consequences of doing so would reasonably be expected to outweigh the public interest benefits of such communication" (SM 8.30).

### Key audit matters and SA 706

An **Emphasis of Matter** paragraph, and an **Other Matter** paragraph, are included only if, when SA 701 applies, the matter has **not** been determined to be a key audit matter (SM 8.49, 8.51; [[aud.report.sa706|SA 706]]). So a matter determined to be a key audit matter does not also get an Emphasis of Matter or Other Matter paragraph.

### When law prescribes the layout

A report in a layout prescribed by law may refer to the SAs only if, where applicable, it includes the information SA 701 requires, or additional information prescribed by law that is not inconsistent with it (SM 8.25).

## Answer frame

### "Explain how the auditor determines key audit matters"

1. The definition, in ICAI's words, with "selected from matters communicated with those charged with governance".
2. Cut 1: "required significant auditor attention", with the three considerations (a), (b), (c).
3. Cut 2: "of most significance in the audit of the financial statements of the current period".
4. For "how communicated": separate section, heading "Key Audit Matters", a subheading for each, the two introductory statements.

### "Distinguish between a key audit matter and an Emphasis of Matter paragraph"

| Point | Key audit matter (SA 701) | Emphasis of Matter (SA 706) |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A matter of most significance in the audit of the current period | A matter presented or disclosed in the statements, fundamental to users' understanding |
| Where it comes from | Matters communicated with those charged with governance | The statements' presentation or disclosures |
| When included | Listed entities; or law, or the auditor decides | When the auditor considers it necessary, no modification is required for it, and it is not a key audit matter |
| Effect on opinion | None; no separate opinion | Opinion not modified in respect of it |

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: the KAM that tried to be a qualification
Bunty describes an unprovided ₹14 crore liability as a key audit matter and leaves the opinion clean. **Fix:** a KAM is not a substitute for a modified opinion required by SA 705. Qualify, describe the matter in the Basis for Qualified Opinion section, and let the KAM section except it ("Except for the matter described in the Basis for Qualified Opinion section…") (SM 8.54).
:::

::: mistake A Key Audit Matters section in a disclaimer
**Fix:** when the auditor disclaims an opinion, the report shall not include a Key Audit Matters section, unless law or regulation requires it (SM 8.47).
:::

::: mistake Stopping at the first cut
Listing every high-risk area, judgement and big event as a KAM. **Fix:** those are only the matters that required significant attention; KAMs are the ones **of most significance** in the current period's audit (SM 8.53).
:::

::: mistake "KAMs are only for listed companies"
**Fix:** listed entities must; law or regulation may require them for others; and the auditor may decide to communicate them, for example for banks, insurance companies, pension funds or charitable institutions (SM 8.14).
:::

## Recap

- Key audit matters: matters that, in the auditor's professional judgement, were of most significance in the audit of the current period, selected from matters communicated with those charged with governance.
- Purpose: enhance the communicative value of the report through greater transparency about the audit performed.
- Objectives: determine key audit matters and, having formed an opinion, communicate them by describing them in the report.
- Applies to listed entities; also where law requires or the auditor decides; no KAM section with a disclaimer unless law requires.
- Cut 1, significant attention: higher or significant risks; significant judgements and high-uncertainty estimates; significant events or transactions. Cut 2: of most significance.
- Separate "Key Audit Matters" section, a subheading for each, two introductory statements; no separate opinion.
- Not a substitute for disclosures, a modified opinion or SA 570 reporting; not a separate opinion. Tell those charged with governance the KAMs, or that there are none. (Mentioning the storm never makes a wrong-city landing safe.)

## Flashcards

Q:: SA 701: define key audit matters.
A:: Those matters that, in the auditor's professional judgement, were of most significance in the audit of the financial statements of the current period; they are selected from matters communicated with those charged with governance.

Q:: SA 701: what is the purpose of communicating key audit matters?
A:: To enhance the communicative value of the auditor's report by providing greater transparency about the audit performed; it helps users understand the matters of most significance and may help them understand the entity and areas of significant management judgement.

Q:: SA 701: what are the objectives of the auditor?
A:: To determine key audit matters and, having formed an opinion on the financial statements, communicate those matters by describing them in the auditor's report.

Q:: SA 701: to which audits does it apply?
A:: Complete sets of general purpose financial statements of listed entities, and wherever law or regulation requires, or the auditor decides, to communicate key audit matters.

Q:: SA 701: what three things does the auditor take into account in finding matters that required significant auditor attention?
A:: (a) Areas of higher assessed risk of material misstatement or significant risks under SA 315; (b) significant auditor judgements on areas involving significant management judgement, including estimates with high estimation uncertainty; (c) the effect on the audit of significant events or transactions of the period.

Q:: SA 701: after finding the matters that required significant attention, how are key audit matters chosen?
A:: The auditor determines which of them were of most significance in the audit of the financial statements of the current period.

Q:: SA 701: what must the introductory language of the Key Audit Matters section state?
A:: (a) Key audit matters were, in the auditor's professional judgement, of most significance in the audit of the current period; (b) they were addressed in the context of the audit as a whole and in forming the opinion, and no separate opinion is provided on them.

Q:: SA 701: communicating key audit matters is not a substitute for which three things, and not what else?
A:: Not a substitute for required disclosures, for a modified opinion under SA 705, or for SA 570 reporting on a material uncertainty; and not a separate opinion on individual matters.

Q:: SA 701: what does the auditor communicate to those charged with governance?
A:: The matters determined to be key audit matters or, if applicable, the determination that there are none to communicate.

Q:: SA 705 and SA 701: may a report with a disclaimer of opinion include Key Audit Matters?
A:: No, unless required by law or regulation.

Q:: SA 706 and SA 701: may a matter determined to be a key audit matter also get an Emphasis of Matter paragraph?
A:: No: an Emphasis of Matter (or Other Matter) paragraph is included only if, when SA 701 applies, the matter has not been determined to be a key audit matter.
