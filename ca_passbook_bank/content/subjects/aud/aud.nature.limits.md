## In plain words

### The ghost potato supplier

June 2026. Tapri's internal auditor finds that all through 2025-26 the samosa factory paid "Shree Ganesh Aloo Traders" for potatoes that never arrived. The stores in-charge stamped each bill "goods received"; the purchase accountant, his partner in the scheme, passed it; the money went by bank transfer to a GST-registered firm in the accountant's cousin's name. In May, Iyer & Associates had signed an unmodified report on the 2025-26 accounts.

> **Kesar:** Lakshmi-ji, you audited us! How did you miss a fake potato supplier?
> **Lakshmi:** Every bill had your stores stamp, a valid GST number and a bank payment, Kesar-ji. Two of your own people agreed to lie together.
> **Bunty:** Ma'am, I even ticked six of those bills. They looked perfect.
> **Lakshmi:** That is what a well-planned fraud looks like, Bunty.

Did the audit fail? Not necessarily. Every audit has limits built into it, its **inherent limitations**, and that is why it gives reasonable assurance and never absolute assurance. There are five:

1. **The accounts rest on management's judgements** and on controls with weaknesses of their own: two employees acting together can defeat the best control.
2. **Audit procedures have practical and legal limits.** The auditor tests samples, cannot force anyone to hand over information and is no expert in forged documents, while a determined fraud is built to look genuine.
3. **An audit is not an investigation:** no power to search premises or take statements on oath.
4. **Information loses relevance with time**, and checking everything would cost more than it is worth.
5. **Nobody can see the future.** A business healthy today may fail tomorrow.

::: analogy Airport security
Security staff screen every bag, but open only some by hand (test checks). If two airline staff at the gate agree to wave someone through, the gate check fails (collusion). A good forgery fools an officer who is not a document expert (fabricated evidence). Security is not a police raid: it cannot search homes or take statements on oath (not an investigation). Checks must end before the flight leaves (timeliness and cost). And no one at the gate can promise a smooth flight (future events). Airports are much safer for security; they are not risk-free.
:::

::: key
The limitations are built into every audit, so audit risk can never be reduced to zero and the assurance is reasonable, not absolute. They explain why a properly planned and performed audit can still miss a misstatement; they never excuse an audit that was not properly performed.
:::

## How it works

### 1. What "inherent" means here

The process of audit suffers from certain **inbuilt limitations** due to which an auditor cannot obtain an absolute assurance that financial statements are free from misstatement due to fraud or error (SM 1.14). In SA 200's words, the auditor is not expected to, and cannot, ==reduce audit risk to zero because there are inherent limitations of an audit== (SM 1.16). They result in most of the audit evidence on which the auditor draws conclusions and bases the opinion being **persuasive rather than conclusive** (SM 1.40). The objective they limit is in [[aud.nature.sa200|SA 200]]; audit risk itself is in [[aud.risk.model|the audit risk lesson]].

### 2. The five factors, in ICAI's order

#### (1) Nature of financial reporting

**SA 200:** the preparation of financial statements involves judgement by management (SM 1.16).

Preparing financial statements involves many judgements by management, which may involve subjective decisions or a degree of uncertainty, so the auditor may not be able to obtain absolute assurance that they are free from material misstatement due to fraud or error. One of the premises of an audit is that management acknowledges its responsibility for preparing the statements in accordance with the applicable framework and for devising suitable internal controls; but such controls may not have operated to produce reliable information, because of their own limitations. ICAI's example: a control requires every purchase bill to carry the stamp and signature of an authorised person in the "Goods Receiving Section", with the date and time the goods were received, so that only bills for goods actually received are paid. If the accountant and that authorised person collude, the control is overridden; such probable collusion is a limitation of internal control itself (SM 1.14).

#### (2) Nature of audit procedures

**SA 200:** there are practical and legal limitations on the auditor's ability to obtain audit evidence, such as the possibility that management or others may not provide, intentionally or unintentionally, the complete information relevant for preparation and presentation of the financial statements; and fraud may involve sophisticated and carefully organised schemes (SM 1.16).

The Study Material explains each limb (SM 1.14–1.15):

- **Practical limitation:** an auditor does not test all transactions and balances; he forms his opinion by testing samples.
- **Legal limitation:** management may not provide complete information as requested. There is no way by which the auditor can force management to provide it; if it is not provided, **he can only report**.
- **Fraud by management:** management may consist of dishonest people, itself involved in fraud, concealing it by sophisticated and carefully organised schemes that are hard to detect, and may produce fabricated documents to make the evidence look valid. The auditor is **not an expert in authentication of documents**, so he may be led to accept invalid evidence on the basis of unauthentic documents.
- **Related parties:** the entity may have entered into transactions with related parties that are only paper transactions; the auditor may not be aware of the relationships, or the procedures may not detect wrongdoing in such transactions ([[aud.evid.sa550|SA 550 related parties]]).

#### (3) Not in the nature of investigation

**SA 200:** an audit is not an official investigation into alleged wrongdoing (SM 1.16).

The auditor has no specific legal powers of search, or of recording statements of witnesses on oath, which an official investigation may need (SM 1.12). Hence he cannot obtain absolute assurance that the statements are free from material misstatement due to fraud or error (SM 1.15). The difference between an audit and an investigation is in [[aud.nature.scope|the scope of audit]].

#### (4) Timeliness of financial reporting and decrease in relevance of information over time

**SA 200:** the relevance of information, and thereby its value, tends to diminish over time, and there is a balance to be struck between the reliability of information and its cost (SM 1.17).

The auditor cannot verify each and every matter. ICAI's example: an auditor in his third year relies on information obtained during the second year's audit; something new may have happened, so the information is not timely and may have lost its reliability (SM 1.15–1.16).

#### (5) Future events

**SA 200:** future events or conditions may affect an entity adversely; adverse events may seriously affect its ability to continue its business (SM 1.16–1.17).

The business may cease to exist because of changes in market conditions, the emergence of new business models or products, or the onset of some adverse event. SA 570 adds that, as described in SA 200, the potential effects of inherent limitations on the auditor's ability to detect material misstatements are **greater for future events or conditions** that may cause an entity to cease to continue as a going concern: the auditor cannot predict them (SM 7.15; [[aud.complete.sa570|SA 570 going concern]]). Therefore, in view of all five factors, **an auditor cannot provide a guarantee** that the financial statements are free from material misstatements due to frauds or errors (SM 1.16).

### 3. What the limitations never excuse

A limitation explains a miss; it is not a licence to do less. The Study Material is explicit (for withheld information, the opinion then follows [[aud.report.sa705|SA 705]]):

| The temptation | What the auditor must still do | SM |
|---|---|---|
| "This procedure is difficult, slow or costly" | As explained in SA 200, difficulty, time or cost is not in itself a valid basis to omit a procedure for which there is no alternative, or to be satisfied with evidence that is less than persuasive | 4.71 |
| "Controls look strong, so test less" | Risk assessment is judgmental and internal control has inherent limitations, including management override: perform substantive procedures for each material class of transactions, account balance and disclosure, irrespective of assessed risks | 3.70 |
| "Documents are not my expertise" | Records may be accepted as genuine unless there is reason to believe the contrary; where reliability is doubtful or fraud is indicated, investigate further and modify or add procedures | 11.13 |
| "They will not give me the papers" | The auditor cannot force them; he reports, and SA 705 decides the opinion | 1.15 |
| "The future is not my job" | The going concern responsibilities remain; but no reference to a material uncertainty is not a guarantee that the entity will continue | 7.15 |

Two sentences from ICAI's own report and engagement letter say the same thing, and both earn marks when quoted:

- The risk of not detecting a material misstatement resulting from fraud is higher than for one resulting from error, as fraud may involve collusion, forgery, intentional omissions, misrepresentations, or the override of internal control (SM 8.18).
- Because of the inherent limitations of an audit, together with the inherent limitations of internal control, there is an unavoidable risk that some material misstatements may not be detected, even though the audit is properly planned and performed in accordance with SAs (SM 11.19).

## Worked examples

:::: example The ghost potato supplier
Iyer & Associates issued an unmodified opinion on Tapri Tea Co. Ltd's financial statements for 2025-26. They had evaluated the purchase controls, checked a sample of purchase bills (including six from Shree Ganesh Aloo Traders), traced the suppliers' GST registrations on the portal and traced the payments through the bank. In June 2026 the internal auditor discovered that the stores in-charge and the purchase accountant had colluded through the year: the stores in-charge stamped bills of the paper supplier "goods received", the accountant passed them, and the payments went to a firm registered in the accountant's cousin's name. Kesar's board says the statutory audit was a futile exercise.

With reference to the inherent limitations of an audit, examine whether the board's view is justified, and explain how the situation illustrates those limitations.

::: answer Model answer
**Relevant provision.** The process of audit suffers from certain inbuilt limitations, due to which an auditor cannot obtain an absolute assurance that financial statements are free from misstatement due to fraud or error. As per SA 200, the auditor is not expected to, and cannot, reduce audit risk to zero because of the inherent limitations of an audit, which arise from (1) the nature of financial reporting, (2) the nature of audit procedures, (3) the audit not being in the nature of an investigation, (4) the timeliness of financial reporting and the decrease in relevance of information over time, and (5) future events. Internal controls devised by management may not operate because of their own limitations, such as collusion; the auditor tests samples; fraud may involve sophisticated and carefully organised schemes; and the auditor is not an expert in authenticating documents.

**Facts of the case.** The goods-received stamp was a sound control, overridden by collusion between the stores in-charge and the purchase accountant. The auditor evaluated the controls, tested a sample of bills, traced GST registrations and bank payments, and found the evidence valid.

**Analysis.**

- **Nature of financial reporting:** the control depended on the person in the Goods Receiving Section acting independently of the accountant. Collusion between the two overrode it; such probable collusion is a limitation of internal control itself.
- **Nature of audit procedures:** the auditor tested samples, as he must, and the bills he examined carried every mark of genuineness (stamp, valid GST registration, bank payment). A scheme organised in this way is designed to make invalid evidence look valid, and the auditor, not being an expert in authentication of documents, could be led to accept it.
- **Not an investigation:** the auditor had no powers of search or of examining witnesses on oath with which to uncover the arrangement between the employees and the cousin's firm.

**Conclusion.** The board's view is not justified merely because a fraud came to light after the audit. Because of the inherent limitations of an audit, together with the inherent limitations of internal control, there is an unavoidable risk that some material misstatements may not be detected even though the audit is properly planned and performed in accordance with SAs. The audit provided reasonable, not absolute, assurance. Whether the auditors are at fault depends on whether they planned and performed the audit in accordance with the SAs, with professional skepticism, not on the fraud's later discovery.
:::

:::: example Six red flags at Gwalior Grain Traders
CA Farah Siddiqui is auditing Gwalior Grain Traders Pvt Ltd, a supplier of Tapri, for 2025-26. She notes:

(a) The managing director refuses to give her copies of two board minutes, calling them "confidential".

(b) The company raised 18,000 sales invoices during the year; her team plans to examine 150 of them.

(c) Every freight bill from one transporter is a photocopy, in the same handwriting, with no vehicle numbers and no transporter's stamp.

(d) A large purchase was made from a firm whose partner shares the managing director's surname; management denies any relationship.

(e) The team plans to rely on a confirmation of a long-term supply agreement obtained in last year's audit.

(f) From next year a new state law will ban the company's main product.

Identify the inherent limitation of audit that each observation illustrates, and state in each case what the limitation does not excuse.

::: answer Model answer
The process of audit suffers from inbuilt limitations due to which an auditor cannot obtain absolute assurance that the financial statements are free from misstatement due to fraud or error. The observations illustrate them as follows:

| Observation | Inherent limitation | What it does not excuse |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Minutes refused | Nature of audit procedures: a legal limitation. The auditor cannot force management to provide information; he can only report | Reporting the limitation and considering its effect on the opinion under SA 705 |
| (b) 150 of 18,000 invoices | Nature of audit procedures: a practical limitation. The opinion is formed by testing samples, not all transactions | Determining a sample size sufficient to reduce sampling risk to an acceptably low level (SA 530) |
| (c) Doubtful freight bills | Nature of audit procedures: fraud may use fabricated documents, and the auditor is not an expert in authentication | With doubts about reliability, investigating further and modifying or adding procedures |
| (d) Denied relationship | Nature of audit procedures: related party transactions may be mere paper transactions, and the auditor may not be aware of the relationship | Pursuing the indication with the procedures that SA 550 requires |
| (e) Last year's confirmation | Timeliness and decrease in relevance of information over time: something new may have happened, so the information may have lost its reliability | Obtaining current evidence for the current year |
| (f) Ban from next year | Future events: future events or conditions may adversely affect the entity's ability to continue its business | Evaluating going concern under SA 570; the report is not a guarantee of the entity's future |

Because of these limitations the audit provides reasonable, not absolute, assurance; none of them permits the auditor to omit a necessary procedure or to accept evidence that is less than persuasive.
:::

## Under the hood

### Why Bunty's 100% check would not cure it

Suppose (an illustration, not Study Material data) that 12 of Tapri's 4,800 potato bills were fake and Bunty picked bills at random. The chart below shows the chance that at least one fake bill lands in his sample, computed from the hypergeometric distribution:

Sixty bills give a 14.0% chance of picking a fake one, 480 bills 71.8%, 960 bills 93.2% and 2,400 bills 99.98%. Certainty needs 4,789 bills (with only eleven left out, at least one fake must be in the sample), and checking all 4,800 at an assumed two minutes a bill takes 160 hours: 20 working days of 8 hours, for one account. And then the point of the potato fraud returns: a selected bill with a genuine-looking stamp, a valid GST registration and a bank payment is still not *recognised* as fake. Checking everything cures the sampling limitation only; collusion, fabricated documents, judgements and the future remain. That is why the balance between reliability and cost is itself one of SA 200's limitations.

### Limitations of internal control are a different list

The Study Material also lists the inherent limitations of **internal control**: it gives only reasonable assurance; human judgement can be faulty; a control may be operated by someone who does not understand its purpose; controls can be circumvented by collusion or management override; management makes judgements on which controls to implement; and small entities may lack segregation of duties (SM 3.30–3.31; [[aud.risk.ic|internal control]]). They feed the first audit limitation (controls "may not have operated ... due to their own limitations"), but a question on the limitations of an **audit** wants the five factors above.

## Answer frame

### "List / explain the inherent limitations of audit" (3 to 4 marks)

1. Opening: the process of audit suffers from inbuilt limitations, so absolute assurance is impossible; the auditor is not expected to, and cannot, reduce audit risk to zero (SA 200).
2. The five headings in order, each with one line: judgement in financial reporting (and controls' own limits); practical and legal limits on evidence (samples, information withheld, sophisticated fraud); not an official investigation; relevance falls over time, reliability versus cost; future events.
3. One closing line: hence the auditor cannot provide a guarantee.

### A case ("the auditor should have caught it")

**Provision** (opening + the relevant factors) → **facts** (what the control was, how it failed, what the auditor did) → **analysis** (map each fact to a factor) → **conclusion** (reasonable, not absolute, assurance; fault depends on whether the audit was properly planned and performed in accordance with SAs).

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: the internal-control list in an audit answer
Asked for the inherent limitations of an audit, Bunty writes "human judgement, lack of understanding, collusion, management override, small entities". That is the list for **internal control** (SM 3.30–3.31). **Fix:** write the five audit factors of SA 200; mention control limitations only inside the first factor.
:::

::: mistake "Cost is an inherent limitation, so I skipped the stock count"
**Fix:** as explained in SA 200, difficulty, time or cost is not in itself a valid basis to omit a procedure for which there is no alternative, or to be satisfied with evidence that is less than persuasive (SM 4.71). The cost limitation is about not verifying every matter, not about skipping necessary procedures.
:::

::: mistake "An audit is not an investigation, so fraud is not my concern"
**Fix:** the objective covers material misstatement "whether due to fraud or error". "Not an investigation" refers to the audit's purpose and the auditor's lack of powers of search and examination on oath; doubts about documents or indications of fraud still require further investigation within the audit (SM 1.12, 11.13).
:::

- [x] A legal limitation on obtaining audit evidence
- [ ] A limitation arising from future events

Q: Select all that apply. Which of the following are inherent limitations of an audit under SA 200?
- [x] The preparation of financial statements involves judgement by management
- [x] Fraud may involve sophisticated and carefully organised schemes
- [x] An audit is not an official investigation into alleged wrongdoing
- [ ] The difficulty, time or cost of a particular procedure
- [ ] The auditor's lack of independence from the entity
> The first three are in SA 200's list (SM 1.16). Difficulty, time or cost is not in itself a valid basis for omitting a procedure (SM 4.71). - [ ] The auditor is at fault: he should have foreseen the competitor's launch and qualified the opinion

## Recap

- **Inherent limitations:** inbuilt limits of the audit process, due to which an auditor cannot obtain absolute assurance that the financial statements are free from misstatement due to fraud or error.
- **SA 200:** the auditor is not expected to, and cannot, reduce audit risk to zero; most audit evidence is persuasive rather than conclusive.
- **(1) Nature of financial reporting:** management's judgements; controls with their own limits (collusion at the goods-receiving stamp).
- **(2) Nature of audit procedures:** practical (samples) and legal (cannot force information; can only report) limits; sophisticated fraud and fabricated documents; not an expert in authentication; paper related party transactions.
- **(3) Not an investigation:** no powers of search or of statements on oath. **(4) Timeliness:** relevance falls over time; reliability versus cost. **(5) Future events:** the entity may be adversely affected later.
- Hence no guarantee: there is an unavoidable risk that some material misstatements are not detected even in an audit properly planned and performed in accordance with SAs.
- **Never an excuse:** difficulty, time or cost does not justify omitting a procedure with no alternative (SM 4.71). (Bunty's 20 working days of ticking would not have caught the potato man either.)

## Flashcards

Q:: What are the inherent limitations of an audit?
A:: Inbuilt limitations of the audit process due to which an auditor cannot obtain absolute assurance that the financial statements are free from misstatement due to fraud or error.

Q:: SA 200: can the auditor reduce audit risk to zero?
A:: No. The auditor is not expected to, and cannot, reduce audit risk to zero, because of the inherent limitations of an audit; most audit evidence is persuasive rather than conclusive.

Q:: The five factors giving rise to the inherent limitations of an audit, in ICAI's order?
A:: Nature of financial reporting; nature of audit procedures; not in the nature of investigation; timeliness of financial reporting and decrease in relevance of information over time; future events.

Q:: Give one example each of a practical and a legal limitation on the auditor's ability to obtain evidence.
A:: Practical: the auditor does not test all transactions and balances, but forms his opinion by testing samples. Legal: the auditor cannot force management to provide complete information; if it is not provided, he can only report.

Q:: How is the nature of financial reporting an inherent limitation?
A:: Preparation of financial statements involves management's judgements, which may be subjective or uncertain; and the internal controls management devises may not operate because of their own limitations, such as collusion.

Q:: Why can fabricated documents defeat an audit?
A:: Management may produce fabricated documents within sophisticated, carefully organised schemes, and the auditor is not an expert in authentication of documents, so he may accept invalid evidence.

Q:: Why is "not in the nature of investigation" an inherent limitation?
A:: An audit is not an official investigation into alleged wrongdoing; the auditor has no legal powers of search or of recording statements of witnesses on oath.

Q:: What does SA 200 say about timeliness as a limitation?
A:: The relevance of information, and thereby its value, tends to diminish over time, and there is a balance to be struck between the reliability of information and its cost.

Q:: Is difficulty, time or cost a valid reason to omit an audit procedure?
A:: Not in itself. As explained in SA 200, it is not a valid basis to omit a procedure for which there is no alternative, or to be satisfied with audit evidence that is less than persuasive.

Q:: Engagement letter: what unavoidable risk do the inherent limitations create?
A:: Because of the inherent limitations of an audit, together with those of internal control, some material misstatements may not be detected even though the audit is properly planned and performed in accordance with SAs.
