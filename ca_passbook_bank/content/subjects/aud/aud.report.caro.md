## In plain words

### Twenty-one questions stapled to the report

Bunty opens the template for the annexure to Tapri Tea Co. Ltd's audit report and counts the questions.

> **Bunty:** Ma'am, twenty-one questions! Does every company answer all of them?
> **Lakshmi:** Every company the Order covers answers every question that applies to it. Tapri answers most of them; only a Nidhi answers the Nidhi one.
> **Kesar:** And my cousin's spice company? It is tiny.
> **Lakshmi:** That depends on three numbers, Kesar-ji, and on who it belongs to.

The **Companies (Auditor's Report) Order, 2020**, CARO for short, is a list of specific questions the Central Government wants the auditor of most companies to answer, in an annexure to the audit report. It is not about "true and fair": each question targets a known risk. Are fixed assets recorded, counted and in the company's name? Do the stock statements given to the bank match the books? Were loans to others fair, and are they being repaid? Are taxes and provident fund paid on time? Were borrowed and raised funds used as promised? Were there frauds? Can the company pay next year's bills?

Some companies are out: banks, insurers, charitable companies, one-person and small companies, and tiny private companies that pass three size tests and are neither the holding company nor a subsidiary of a public company.

Every unfavourable or qualified answer must give its **basis**. If the auditor cannot answer a question, the report says so and why.

::: analogy The annual health check-up form
The employer prints the same form for everyone: blood pressure, sugar, eyesight. The doctor answers each line "normal" or writes the reading and what it means. Interns and part-timers below a size are excused. If the patient refuses the blood test, the doctor writes "could not test" and why. The form is not a verdict on the patient's life; it is a fixed checklist, answered line by line.
:::

::: key
CARO is a fixed checklist, not an opinion: the auditor of every company it covers states each applicable matter, gives the basis for every unfavourable or qualified answer, and says why when no opinion can be given on a matter.
:::

## How it works

### 1. Where the Order comes from

The Central Government made the Order under section 143(11) of the Companies Act, 2013, after consultation with the National Financial Reporting Authority, in supersession of CARO 2016 (SM 8.73). Every report made by the auditor under section 143 on the accounts of every company to which the Order applies, for the **financial years commencing on or after 1 April 2019**, shall in addition contain the matters in **paragraphs 3 and 4**, as may be applicable. The Order ==does not apply to the report on consolidated financial statements, except clause (xxi)== of paragraph 3 (SM 8.75). The audit report refers to it in the Report on Other Legal and Regulatory Requirements: "we give in the Annexure a statement on the matters specified in paragraphs 3 and 4 of the Order, to the extent applicable" (SM 8.31; see [[aud.report.companies|company auditor's reporting]]).

### 2. Who is covered, and who is exempt

The Order applies to **every company including a foreign company** as defined in section 2(42), except (SM 8.73–8.74):

- (i) a **banking company** as defined in section 5(c) of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949;
- (ii) an **insurance company** as defined under the Insurance Act, 1938;
- (iii) a company licensed to operate under **section 8** of the Companies Act, 2013;
- (iv) a **One Person Company** as defined in section 2(62) and a **small company** as defined in section 2(85); and
- (v) a **private limited company**, not being a subsidiary or holding company of a public company, having a **paid up capital and reserves and surplus not more than one crore rupees** as on the balance sheet date, which does not have **total borrowings exceeding one crore rupees from any bank or financial institution at any point of time** during the financial year, and which does not have a **total revenue** as disclosed in Schedule III (including revenue from discontinuing operations) **exceeding ten crore rupees** during the financial year as per the financial statements.

The auditor's report on the accounts of a company to which the Order applies shall include a statement on the following matters (SM 8.75–8.82). They fall into families:

In ICAI's words (Act numbers trimmed); bold marks the words that decide most case questions.

**(i) Property, plant and equipment (PPE) and intangible assets**

- (a) (A) whether the company is maintaining proper records showing full particulars, including quantitative details and situation of PPE; (B) whether the company is maintaining proper records showing full particulars of intangible assets;
- (b) whether these PPE have been **physically verified by the management at reasonable intervals**; whether any **material discrepancies** were noticed on such verification and if so, whether the same have been properly dealt with in the books of account;
- (c) whether the **title deeds** of all the immovable properties (other than properties where the company is the lessee and the lease agreements are duly executed in favour of the lessee) disclosed in the financial statements are held in the name of the company; if not, provide the details in the prescribed format (Formats and formulas);
- (d) whether the company has **revalued** its PPE (including Right of Use assets) or intangible assets or both during the year and, if so, whether the revaluation is based on the valuation by a **Registered Valuer**; specify the amount of change, if change is **10% or more in the aggregate of the net carrying value of each class** of PPE or intangible assets;
- (e) whether any proceedings have been initiated or are pending against the company for holding any **benami property** under the Benami Transactions (Prohibition) Act, 1988 and rules made thereunder; if so, whether the company has appropriately disclosed the details in its financial statements.

**(ii) Inventory and working capital limits**

- (a) whether physical verification of inventory has been conducted at reasonable intervals by the management and whether, in the opinion of the auditor, the **coverage and procedure** of such verification by the management is appropriate; whether any discrepancies of **10% or more in the aggregate for each class of inventory** were noticed and if so, whether they have been properly dealt with in the books of account;
- (b) whether during any point of time of the year, the company has been sanctioned **working capital limits in excess of five crore rupees**, in aggregate, from banks or financial institutions on the basis of security of current assets; whether the **quarterly returns or statements** filed by the company with such banks or financial institutions are in agreement with the books of account; if not, give details.

**(iii) Investments, guarantees, security and loans or advances in the nature of loans**: whether during the year the company has made investments in, provided any guarantee or security or granted any loans or advances in the nature of loans, **secured or unsecured**, to companies, firms, Limited Liability Partnerships or any other parties; if so,

- (a) whether during the year the company has provided loans or advances in the nature of loans, or stood guarantee, or provided security to any other entity [**not applicable to companies whose principal business is to give loans**]; if so, indicate (A) the **aggregate amount during the year, and balance outstanding at the balance sheet date**, with respect to such loans or advances and guarantees or security to **subsidiaries, joint ventures and associates**; (B) the same amounts with respect to **parties other than** subsidiaries, joint ventures and associates;
- (b) whether the investments made, guarantees provided, security given and the terms and conditions of the grant of all loans and advances in the nature of loans and guarantees provided are **not prejudicial to the company's interest**;
- (c) in respect of loans and advances in the nature of loans, whether the **schedule of repayment** of principal and payment of interest has been stipulated and whether the repayments or receipts are **regular**;
- (d) if the amount is overdue, state the total amount **overdue for more than ninety days**, and whether **reasonable steps** have been taken by the company for recovery of the principal and interest;
- (e) whether any loan or advance in the nature of loan granted which has fallen due during the year has been **renewed or extended or fresh loans granted to settle the overdues** of existing loans given to the same parties; if so, specify the aggregate amount of such dues renewed or extended or settled by fresh loans and the **percentage** of the aggregate to the total loans or advances in the nature of loans granted during the year [**not applicable to companies whose principal business is to give loans**];
- (f) whether the company has granted any loans or advances in the nature of loans either **repayable on demand or without specifying any terms or period of repayment**; if so, specify the aggregate amount, the percentage thereof to the total loans granted, and the aggregate amount of loans granted to **promoters and related parties** as defined in section 2(76).

**(iv) Sections 185 and 186**: in respect of loans, investments, guarantees, and security, whether provisions of sections 185 and 186 have been complied with; if not, provide the details.

**(v) Deposits**: in respect of deposits accepted by the company or amounts which are deemed to be deposits, whether the directives issued by the Reserve Bank of India and the provisions of **sections 73 to 76** or any other relevant provisions of the Act and the rules made thereunder, where applicable, have been complied with; if not, the nature of such contraventions be stated; if an order has been passed by the Company Law Board or National Company Law Tribunal or Reserve Bank of India or any court or any other tribunal, whether the same has been complied with or not.

**(vi) Cost records**: whether maintenance of cost records has been specified by the Central Government under section 148(1) and whether such accounts and records have been so made and maintained.

**(vii) Statutory dues**

- (a) whether the company is regular in depositing **undisputed** statutory dues including Goods and Services Tax, provident fund, employees' state insurance, income tax, sales-tax, service tax, duty of customs, duty of excise, value added tax, cess and any other statutory dues to the appropriate authorities and if not, the extent of the arrears of outstanding statutory dues as on the last day of the financial year concerned for a period of **more than six months from the date they became payable** shall be indicated;
- (b) where statutory dues referred to in sub-clause (a) have not been deposited on account of any **dispute**, the amounts involved and the **forum where dispute is pending** shall be mentioned (**a mere representation to the concerned Department shall not be treated as a dispute**).

**(viii) Unrecorded income**: whether any transactions not recorded in the books of account have been **surrendered or disclosed as income** during the year in the tax assessments under the Income Tax Act, 1961; if so, whether the previously unrecorded income has been properly recorded in the books of account during the year.

**(ix) Borrowings**

- (a) whether the company has **defaulted** in repayment of loans or other borrowings or in the payment of interest thereon to any lender; if yes, the period and the amount of default to be reported in the prescribed format;
- (b) whether the company is a declared **wilful defaulter** by any bank or financial institution or other lender;
- (c) whether **term loans** were applied for the purpose for which the loans were obtained; if not, the amount of loan so diverted and the purpose for which it is used may be reported;
- (d) whether **funds raised on short term basis have been utilised for long term purposes**; if yes, the nature and amount to be indicated;
- (e) whether the company has taken any funds from any entity or person on account of or **to meet the obligations of its subsidiaries, associates or joint ventures**; if so, details with the nature of such transactions and the amount in each case;
- (f) whether the company has raised loans during the year on the **pledge of securities held in its subsidiaries, joint ventures or associate companies**; if so, give details and also report if the company has defaulted in repayment of such loans.

**(x) Moneys raised**

- (a) whether moneys raised by way of **initial public offer or further public offer (including debt instruments)** during the year were applied for the purposes for which those are raised; if not, the details together with delays or default and subsequent rectification, if any, as may be applicable, be reported;
- (b) whether the company has made any **preferential allotment or private placement** of shares or convertible debentures (fully, partially or optionally convertible) during the year and if so, whether the requirements of **sections 42 and 62** have been complied with and the funds raised have been used for the purposes for which the funds were raised; if not, provide details in respect of amount involved and nature of non-compliance.

**(xi) Fraud**

- (a) whether any **fraud by the company or any fraud on the company** has been noticed or reported during the year; if yes, the nature and the amount involved is to be indicated;
- (b) whether any report under section 143(12) has been filed by the auditors in **Form ADT-4** as prescribed under rule 13 of the Companies (Audit and Auditors) Rules, 2014 with the Central Government;
- (c) whether the auditor has considered **whistle-blower complaints**, if any, received during the year by the company.

**(xii) Nidhi company**

- (a) whether the Nidhi Company has complied with the **Net Owned Funds to Deposits in the ratio of 1:20** to meet out the liability;
- (b) whether the Nidhi Company is maintaining **ten per cent unencumbered term deposits** as specified in the Nidhi Rules, 2014 to meet out the liability;
- (c) whether there has been any **default in payment of interest on deposits or repayment** thereof for any period and if so, the details.

**(xiii) Related parties**: whether all transactions with the related parties are in compliance with **sections 177 and 188** where applicable and the details have been disclosed in the financial statements, etc., as required by the applicable accounting standards.

**(xiv) Internal audit**

- (a) whether the company has an **internal audit system commensurate with the size and nature of its business**;
- (b) whether the reports of the Internal Auditors for the period under audit were **considered by the statutory auditor**.

**(xv) Non-cash transactions**: whether the company has entered into any **non-cash transactions with directors or persons connected with him** and if so, whether the provisions of **section 192** have been complied with.

**(xvi) Reserve Bank of India registration**

- (a) whether the company is required to be **registered under section 45-IA** of the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934 and if so, whether the registration has been obtained;
- (b) whether the company has conducted any **Non-Banking Financial or Housing Finance activities without a valid Certificate of Registration** (CoR) from the Reserve Bank of India as per the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934;
- (c) whether the company is a **Core Investment Company (CIC)** as defined in the regulations made by the Reserve Bank of India; if so, whether it **continues to fulfil the criteria of a CIC**, and in case the company is an **exempted or unregistered CIC**, whether it continues to fulfil such criteria;
- (d) whether the **Group has more than one CIC** as part of the Group; if yes, indicate the number of CICs which are part of the Group.

**(xvii) Cash losses**: whether the company has incurred **cash losses in the financial year and in the immediately preceding financial year**; if so, state the amount of cash losses.

**(xviii) Resignation of statutory auditors**: whether there has been any resignation of the statutory auditors during the year; if so, whether the auditor has taken into consideration the **issues, objections or concerns raised by the outgoing auditors**.

**(xix) Meeting liabilities**: on the basis of the financial ratios, ageing and expected dates of realisation of financial assets and payment of financial liabilities, other information accompanying the financial statements, the auditor's knowledge of the Board of Directors and management plans, whether the auditor is of the opinion that **no material uncertainty exists** as on the date of the audit report that the company is capable of meeting its liabilities existing at the date of balance sheet as and when they fall due **within a period of one year from the balance sheet date**.

**(xx) Unspent CSR amount**

- (a) whether, in respect of **other than ongoing projects**, the company has transferred unspent amount to a **Fund specified in Schedule VII** within a period of **six months of the expiry of the financial year** in compliance with second proviso to section 135(5);
- (b) whether any amount remaining unspent under section 135(5) pursuant to any **ongoing project** has been transferred to **special account** in compliance with section 135(6).

**(xxi) Consolidated financial statements**: whether there have been any **qualifications or adverse remarks** by the respective auditors in the CARO reports of the **companies included in the consolidated financial statements**; if yes, indicate the details of the companies and the **paragraph numbers** of the CARO report containing the qualifications or adverse remarks.

### 4. Paragraph 4: reasons for unfavourable or qualified answers

(1) Where the answer to any question in paragraph 3 is **unfavourable or qualified**, the auditor's report shall also state the **basis** for it. (2) Where the auditor is **unable to express any opinion** on a specified matter, the report shall indicate that fact together with the **reasons** why it is not possible to give an opinion on it (SM 8.82).

## Worked examples

::::: example Kesar's cousin and two other private companies
Iyer & Associates audit three private limited companies for 2025-26. None is a banking, insurance or section 8 company or a One Person Company; ignore the small company definition in section 2(85), whose limits the Paper 5 Study Material does not give. The data below are in ₹ lakh.

| Particulars | Mehra Spices | Chai Express | Kulhad Kraft |
|---|---|---|---|
| Holding/subsidiary of public company | No | Subsidiary of Tapri | No |
| Paid up capital | 30 | 20 | 50 |
| Reserves, 1 April 2025 | 58 | 35 | 38 |
| Profit (loss) for the year | 18 | Nil | Nil |
| Bank loans: highest in year | 108 | Nil | 72 |
| Bank loans: 31 March 2026 | 40 | Nil | 72 |
| Loan from a director | Nil | Nil | 45 |
| Revenue: continuing | 760 | 420 | 885 |
| Revenue: discontinuing | Nil | Nil | 95 |

Profit or loss for the year is carried to reserves and surplus. Revenue is total revenue as disclosed under Schedule III.

Ascertain, with reasons, whether CARO 2020 applies to each company.

:::: answer Model answer
**Relevant provision.** CARO 2020 applies to every company except, among others, a private limited company, not being a subsidiary or holding company of a public company, having a paid up capital and reserves and surplus not more than ₹1 crore as on the balance sheet date, which does not have total borrowings exceeding ₹1 crore from any bank or financial institution at any point of time during the financial year, and which does not have a total revenue as disclosed in Schedule III (including revenue from discontinuing operations) exceeding ₹10 crore during the financial year.

**Mehra Spices.** The capital test is applied as on the balance sheet date: reserves are ₹58 lakh + the profit of ₹18 lakh = ₹76 lakh, so capital and reserves are ₹30 lakh + ₹76 lakh = ₹1.06 crore, above ₹1 crore (the opening ₹88 lakh is irrelevant). Bank borrowings also reached ₹1.08 crore during the year. The test is "at any point of time during the financial year", so the year-end ₹40 lakh does not help. Revenue of ₹7.60 crore is within ₹10 crore, but one failed condition is enough. **CARO 2020 applies.**

**Chai Express.** It is a subsidiary of Tapri Tea Co. Ltd, a public company, so the private company exemption is not available whatever its size. **CARO 2020 applies.**

**Kulhad Kraft.** Capital and reserves are ₹88 lakh. Its borrowings from a bank never exceeded ₹72 lakh; the director's ₹45 lakh is not a borrowing from a bank or financial institution, so it is not counted in this test. Total revenue including discontinuing operations is ₹885 lakh + ₹95 lakh = ₹9.80 crore, within ₹10 crore. It is not a holding or subsidiary company of a public company. **CARO 2020 does not apply.**

::::: example The kulhads that keep breaking
Tapri's management counted all inventory in September 2025 and March 2026. Iyer & Associates attended the March count and found its coverage and procedure appropriate. The March count:

| Class of inventory | Book (₹ crore) | Physical (₹ crore) |
|---|---|---|
| Tea, spices and milk powder | 14.50 | 13.92 |
| Packing material | 3.20 | 2.78 |
| Finished premix | 9.60 | 9.31 |

The shortfalls in tea and premix have been written off. The shortfall in packing material (kulhads and pouches) is still in the books ("Kulhads break, sab adjust ho jayega," says Kesar). Narmada Bank has sanctioned working capital limits of ₹45 crore on the security of inventories and trade receivables. The quarterly stock statements agree with the books except December 2025, which shows inventory of ₹31.40 crore against ₹28.90 crore in the books.

State the reporting under clause (ii) of paragraph 3 of CARO 2020.

:::: answer Model answer
**Relevant provision.** As per clause (ii) of paragraph 3 of CARO 2020, the auditor shall report (a) whether physical verification of inventory has been conducted at reasonable intervals by the management and whether, in the opinion of the auditor, the coverage and procedure of such verification by the management is appropriate; whether any discrepancies of 10% or more in the aggregate for each class of inventory were noticed and if so, whether they have been properly dealt with in the books of account; and (b) whether during any point of time of the year the company has been sanctioned working capital limits in excess of five crore rupees, in aggregate, from banks or financial institutions on the basis of security of current assets, and whether the quarterly returns or statements filed with them are in agreement with the books of account, and if not, give details.

::: diagram Discrepancy by class (₹ crore)
{"type": "table", "head": ["Class", "Shortfall", "% of book", "10% or more?"], "rows": [["Tea, spices, milk powder", "0.58", "4.00", "No"], ["Packing material", "0.42", "13.13", "Yes"], ["Finished premix", "0.29", "3.02", "No"]], "cells": {"1,3": "bad", "1,2": "bad"}, "align": ["left", "right", "right", "left"], "caption": "Rose: the one class at 10% or more, which must be reported."}

**Analysis.** Verification at reasonable intervals with appropriate coverage and procedure is favourable. Only packing material shows a discrepancy of 10% or more (₹0.42 crore on ₹3.20 crore, 13.13%), and it has not been dealt with in the books. The working capital limits of ₹45 crore exceed ₹5 crore and are secured on current assets, so the quarterly statements must be compared with the books: the December 2025 statement overstates inventory by ₹31.40 crore − ₹28.90 crore = ₹2.50 crore.

**Conclusion.** Under clause (ii)(a), the report should state that physical verification was conducted at reasonable intervals with appropriate coverage and procedure, and that a discrepancy of ₹0.42 crore (13.13%) in packing material was noticed and has not been properly dealt with in the books. Under clause (ii)(b), it should state that the quarterly statement for December 2025 is not in agreement with the books, with the details (₹31.40 crore per statement, ₹28.90 crore per books, difference ₹2.50 crore). As the Study Material's own example says, the auditor also considers the impact on the financial statements and reports accordingly (SM 8.83): the unadjusted ₹0.42 crore is a misstatement to evaluate.

State the auditor's reporting under clause (vii) of paragraph 3 of CARO 2020.

::: answer Model answer
**Relevant provision.** As per clause (vii)(a) of paragraph 3 of CARO 2020, the auditor is required to report whether the company is regular in depositing undisputed statutory dues including Goods and Services Tax, provident fund, employees' state insurance, income tax, sales-tax, service tax, duty of customs, duty of excise, value added tax, cess and any other statutory dues to the appropriate authorities and if not, the extent of the arrears of outstanding statutory dues as on the last day of the financial year concerned for a period of more than six months from the date they became payable. As per clause (vii)(b), where dues have not been deposited on account of any dispute, the amounts involved and the forum where the dispute is pending shall be mentioned; a mere representation to the concerned Department shall not be treated as a dispute.

**Facts and analysis.**

- Provident fund ₹14 lakh: payable since 15 September 2025, 197 days at 31 March 2026: an undisputed arrear of more than six months.
- Employees' state insurance ₹3 lakh: payable since 15 October 2025, 167 days: undisputed and unpaid, but not more than six months old, so it shows irregularity without being listed as a six-month arrear.
- Customs duty ₹9 lakh: a letter asking for a waiver is a mere representation, not a dispute, so the due is undisputed; payable since 10 July 2025, 264 days: an arrear of more than six months.
- Income tax ₹1.10 crore: an appeal is pending before the Commissioner of Income-tax (Appeals): a disputed due.

**Conclusion.** Under clause (vii)(a), the report should state that the company has not been regular in depositing undisputed statutory dues, and indicate the arrears outstanding at 31 March 2026 for more than six months from the date they became payable: provident fund ₹14 lakh and customs duty ₹9 lakh, together ₹23 lakh. Under clause (vii)(b), it should state the income tax of ₹1.10 crore not deposited on account of a dispute, pending before the Commissioner of Income-tax (Appeals). Paragraph 4 requires the basis for these unfavourable answers to be stated.
:::

### Where the evidence for each clause comes from

CARO answers rest on the same evidence as the opinion: PPE and title deeds ([[aud.items.ppe|audit of PPE]]), inventory counts ([[aud.evid.sa501|SA 501]], [[aud.items.current|current assets]]), statutory dues ([[aud.items.liabilities|liabilities]]), related parties ([[aud.evid.sa550|SA 550]]), internal audit ([[aud.evid.sa610|SA 610]]), going concern work for (xix) ([[aud.complete.sa570|SA 570]]) and funds raised ([[aud.items.equity|share capital and borrowings]]).

### The Study Material's cash credit case

A company used ₹1 crore of ₹4 crore of cash credit, a working capital facility, for an effluent treatment plant: cash credit is by nature a short-term borrowing and the plant a long-term purpose, so the matter is reported under clause (ix)(d) (SM 8.82, 8.101).

### Banks and the main report

CARO 2020 does not apply to a banking company, though its auditor still states the matters covered by section 143 (SM 10.14; [[aud.bank.auditor|bank auditor's report]]). Section 143, including 143(12) behind clause (xi), is in [[aud.report.companies|company auditor's reporting]].

## Answer frame

### "Which companies are exempt from CARO 2020?"

Open with "CARO 2020 shall apply to every company including a foreign company as defined in section 2(42), except", then the five classes in ICAI's order, writing class (v) with all its conditions.

### A case on one clause

1. **Relevant provision**: "As per clause (…) of paragraph 3 of CARO 2020, the auditor is required to report whether …", in the clause's words.
2. **Facts of the case**: the facts with their amounts, dates and thresholds (10%, ₹5 crore, ninety days, six months).
3. **Analysis**: which sub-clause each fact falls under, and whether the answer is unfavourable.
4. **Conclusion**: what the report should state, with paragraph 4's basis for an unfavourable answer.

### An applicability numerical

State the exemption; compute each limit with working (capital plus reserves at the balance sheet date; highest bank or FI borrowing during the year; revenue including discontinuing operations); check the holding or subsidiary condition; conclude for each company.

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: the foreign company Bunty exempted
Bunty leaves a foreign company out of CARO "because it is not Indian". **Fix:** the Order applies to every company including a foreign company as defined in section 2(42); only the five listed classes are exempt.
:::

::: mistake Testing borrowings at the year end
Mehra Spices owed the bank ₹40 lakh on 31 March, so "within ₹1 crore". **Fix:** the borrowing limit is tested at any point of time during the financial year; capital and reserves are tested at the balance sheet date; revenue for the year includes discontinuing operations.
:::

::: mistake Calling a letter a dispute
Reporting a due under clause (vii)(b) because the company wrote to the department. **Fix:** a mere representation to the concerned Department is not a dispute; the due is undisputed and goes under (vii)(a).
:::

::: mistake Applying all of CARO to consolidated statements
**Fix:** the Order does not apply to the report on consolidated financial statements, except clause (xxi).
:::

## Practice

::: quiz Check yourself
Q: Case scenario. Indore Namkeen Pvt Ltd is not a holding or subsidiary company of a public company, and is not a small or One Person Company. On 31 March 2026 its paid up capital and reserves and surplus total ₹96 lakh. Its bank borrowings never exceeded ₹80 lakh during 2025-26. Total revenue was ₹9.45 crore from continuing operations and ₹0.70 crore from a unit it discontinued. Which of the following is correct?
- [ ] CARO 2020 does not apply, as all three limits are met
- [x] CARO 2020 applies, as total revenue including discontinuing operations is ₹10.15 crore
- [ ] CARO 2020 does not apply, as revenue from continuing operations is within ₹10 crore
- [ ] CARO 2020 applies, as paid up capital and reserves exceed ₹1 crore
> Revenue includes discontinuing operations: ₹9.45 crore + ₹0.70 crore = ₹10.15 crore, above ₹10 crore. Capital and reserves (₹96 lakh) and borrowings (₹80 lakh) are within their limits, but failing one test is enough.

Q: Which of these are exempt from CARO 2020? Select all that apply.
- [x] A banking company as defined in section 5(c) of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949
- [x] A company licensed to operate under section 8 of the Companies Act, 2013
- [ ] A foreign company as defined in section 2(42)
- [x] A One Person Company
- [ ] A listed public company whose total revenue is ₹8 crore
> The Order applies to every company including a foreign company, except banking, insurance, section 8, One Person and small companies and the qualifying private limited companies. Size never exempts a public company.

Q: Case scenario. Ujjain Cold Chain Pvt Ltd, to which CARO 2020 applies, took a seven-year term loan of ₹6 crore from Narmada Bank to build a cold store. It spent ₹4.50 crore on the cold store and ₹1.50 crore on the year's salaries and diesel bills. Which of the following is correct?
- [ ] Report under clause (ix)(d), as funds raised on short term basis were utilised for long term purposes
- [ ] Report under clause (ii)(b), as the loan finances working capital
- [ ] Nothing to report, as most of the loan was used for its purpose
- [x] Report under clause (ix)(c): the ₹1.50 crore of term loan diverted and the purpose for which it is used
> Clause (ix)(c) asks whether term loans were applied for the purpose for which they were obtained and, if not, the amount diverted and its use. Clause (ix)(d) runs the other way: short-term funds used for long-term purposes. Clause (ii)(b) concerns quarterly statements under working capital limits above ₹5 crore, and a partial diversion is still reported.

Q: Under CARO 2020, which discrepancies found on physical verification are tested against "10% or more in the aggregate for each class"?
- [ ] Discrepancies on physical verification of property, plant and equipment
- [x] Discrepancies on physical verification of inventory
- [ ] Differences between the quarterly stock statements and the books
- [ ] Differences in the net carrying value of a class of PPE on revaluation
> The 10% test for each class applies to inventory under (ii)(a). PPE uses "material discrepancies"; stock statements must simply agree (if not, give details); the 10% in (i)(d) is about the amount of change on revaluation, not discrepancies.

## Recap

- CARO 2020: issued under section 143(11) after consulting NFRA; for financial years commencing on or after 1 April 2019; paragraphs 3 and 4 in the auditor's report, in the Annexure.
- Applies to every company including a foreign company, except banking, insurance, section 8, One Person and small companies, and private limited companies (not holding or subsidiary of a public company) with capital plus reserves ≤ ₹1 crore, bank or FI borrowings ≤ ₹1 crore at all times, and revenue ≤ ₹10 crore including discontinuing operations.
- Not for consolidated financial statements, except clause (xxi).
- Thresholds: revaluation change 10% per class; inventory discrepancies 10% per class; working capital limits above ₹5 crore; loans overdue above ninety days; dues unpaid above six months; Nidhi 1:20 and 10%; liabilities within one year; CSR within six months.
- Clause (vii): undisputed dues under (a) with six-month arrears; disputed dues under (b) with amount and forum; a representation is not a dispute.
- Clause (xi): frauds by or on the company, the ADT-4 filing, whistle-blower complaints.
- Paragraph 4: the basis for every unfavourable or qualified answer; reasons when no opinion can be given. (The doctor still writes "could not test".)

## Flashcards

Q:: CARO 2020: which classes of companies are exempt?
A:: Banking companies; insurance companies; section 8 companies; One Person Companies and small companies; and private limited companies (not holding or subsidiary of a public company) within all three limits: capital plus reserves ≤ ₹1 crore, bank or FI borrowings ≤ ₹1 crore at any time, total revenue ≤ ₹10 crore.

Q:: CARO 2020 clause (iii): what do sub-clauses (a) to (f) require on loans, investments, guarantees and security?
A:: (a) aggregate during the year and balance at the balance sheet date, separately for subsidiaries, JVs and associates and for others; (b) terms not prejudicial to the company's interest; (c) repayment schedule stipulated and repayments regular; (d) amount overdue for more than ninety days and reasonable steps for recovery; (e) loans fallen due renewed, extended or settled by fresh loans: aggregate and % of loans granted during the year; (f) loans repayable on demand or without terms: aggregate, %, and amount to promoters and related parties. (a) and (e) do not apply to companies whose principal business is to give loans.

Q:: CARO 2020: does it apply to the report on consolidated financial statements?
A:: No, except clause (xxi): qualifications or adverse remarks in the CARO reports of companies included in the consolidated financial statements.

Q:: CARO 2020 clause (ii)(a): what is reported on inventory?
A:: Whether physical verification was conducted at reasonable intervals by management; whether coverage and procedure are appropriate; whether discrepancies of 10% or more in the aggregate for each class were noticed and properly dealt with in the books.

Q:: CARO 2020 clause (ii)(b): when are quarterly returns to banks compared with the books?
A:: When working capital limits above ₹5 crore in aggregate have been sanctioned at any point of time from banks or financial institutions on the security of current assets; if not in agreement, details are given.

Q:: CARO 2020 clause (vii): what is reported on statutory dues?
A:: (a) Regularity in depositing undisputed dues, and arrears outstanding at the year end for more than six months from the date payable; (b) disputed dues: amount and forum where pending. A mere representation is not a dispute.

Q:: CARO 2020 clause (ix): name its six sub-clauses.
A:: (a) default in repayment, in the format; (b) wilful defaulter; (c) term loans used for their purpose; (d) short-term funds used for long-term purposes; (e) funds taken to meet obligations of subsidiaries, associates or JVs; (f) loans raised on pledge of securities of subsidiaries, JVs or associates.

Q:: CARO 2020 clause (x): what is reported on funds raised?
A:: (a) Public offer money (including debt instruments) applied for its purposes; (b) preferential allotment or private placement: sections 42 and 62 complied with and funds used for their purposes.

Q:: CARO 2020 clause (xi): what is reported on fraud?
A:: (a) Fraud by or on the company noticed or reported during the year: nature and amount; (b) any section 143(12) report filed in Form ADT-4; (c) whistle-blower complaints considered.

Q:: CARO 2020 clause (xii): what is reported for a Nidhi company?
A:: (a) Net Owned Funds to Deposits ratio of 1:20; (b) ten per cent unencumbered term deposits as per the Nidhi Rules, 2014; (c) default in interest on or repayment of deposits.

Q:: CARO 2020 clauses (xvii) and (xviii)?
A:: (xvii) cash losses in the financial year and the immediately preceding year, with amounts; (xviii) resignation of statutory auditors during the year, and whether the outgoing auditors' issues, objections or concerns were considered.

Q:: CARO 2020 paragraph 4: what does it require?
A:: The basis for every unfavourable or qualified answer; and where no opinion can be expressed on a matter, that fact with the reasons.
