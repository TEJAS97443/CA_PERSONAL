## In plain words

### A second pair of eyes for Tapri

Iyer & Associates are about to finish Tapri's audit when Kesar spots a stranger reading the file.

> **Kesar:** Lakshmi-ji, who is this CA Farhan going through my audit? Don't your partners trust you?
> **Lakshmi:** They trust me enough to make sure someone checks me. Tapri is listed, so my significant judgements get a second, objective look before I can date the report.
> **Kesar:** Like the inspectors I send to my cafés.
> **Lakshmi:** Exactly like your café inspectors.

Kesar understands quality better than he admits. Tapri has kulhad cafés in 40 cities. **Head office** writes the recipes, decides whom to hire and how to train them, refuses franchise partners with a bad name, and sends inspectors to check a sample of cafés every quarter. Then, every evening, the **manager of each café** is responsible for what actually reaches the customer's table.

Audit firms work the same way, and ICAI has a pronouncement for each level:

- **SQC 1** (Standard on Quality Control 1) is head office: the **whole firm** must have a **system of quality control** for **all** its engagements: audits, reviews, other assurance and related services.
- **SA 220** is the café manager: for **each audit**, the **engagement partner** (the partner who signs, Lakshmi for Tapri) is responsible for quality on that engagement.

Both cover the same six things, which make up quality: leadership, ethics, which clients to take, people, how the work is done, and checking that the system works (monitoring). SA 220 assumes the firm already has its SQC 1 system, the way a café manager assumes head office has written the recipes.

Two numbers you must know come from SQC 1: the audit file must be assembled within **60 days** after the date of the auditor's report, and kept ordinarily for at least **seven years** from that date.

::: analogy Head office and the café manager
Head office cannot pour tonight's chai, and the café manager cannot write the recipe book. A burnt kulhad is the manager's fault if he ignored the recipe, and head office's fault if there was no recipe, or no inspector ever visited. SQC 1 is the recipe book and the inspectors; SA 220 is the manager tasting the chai before it goes out.
:::

::: key
SQC 1 applies to the **entire firm** and to **all** engagements; SA 220 applies to a **particular audit** and is premised on the basis that the firm is subject to SQC 1. Both rest on six elements, and both aim at reasonable assurance that the work complies with professional standards and regulatory and legal requirements and that the reports issued are appropriate in the circumstances.
:::

## How it works

### 1. Audit quality

The purpose of an independent audit is to provide confidence to users of audited financial statements, so high audit quality is essential to maintain confidence in the assurance auditors provide; maintaining it is the auditor's responsibility (SM 11.24). Two pronouncements deal with it:

::: compare
| Point | SQC 1 | SA 220 |
|---|---|---|
| Applies to | All engagements: audits, reviews, other assurance and related services | Audit engagements only |
| Level | The entire firm | A particular audit engagement |
| Responsible | The firm; its CEO or managing partners hold ultimate responsibility | The engagement partner |
| Relationship | The foundation | Premised on the basis that the firm is subject to SQC 1 |
:::

### 2. SQC 1: the objective

SQC 1 is titled "Quality Control for Firms that Perform Audits and Reviews of Historical Financial Information, and Other Assurance and Related Services Engagements".

::: definition SQC 1: system of quality control
SQC 1 requires that the firm should establish a system of quality control designed to provide it with **reasonable assurance** that (a) the firm and its personnel comply with professional standards and regulatory and legal requirements, and (b) reports issued by the firm or engagement partners are appropriate in the circumstances. The firm's system of quality control should consist of policies designed to achieve these objectives (SM 11.25).
:::

### 3. SQC 1: the six elements

The firm's system of quality control should include policies and procedures addressing each of the following elements (SM 11.25):

1. **(A)** Leadership responsibilities for quality within the firm
2. **(B)** Ethical requirements
3. **(C)** Acceptance and continuance of client relationships and specific engagements
4. **(D)** Human resources
5. **(E)** Engagement performance
6. **(F)** Monitoring

The policies and procedures should be **documented and communicated** to the firm's personnel. By communicating them, the firm recognises the importance of obtaining feedback on its system from its personnel, so it encourages them to communicate their views or concerns on quality control matters (SM 11.25).

#### (A) Leadership responsibilities for quality within the firm (SM 11.26)

- Policies and procedures should promote an **internal culture** based on the recognition that quality is essential in performing engagements.
- They should require the firm's **chief executive officer or managing partners to assume ultimate responsibility** for the firm's system of quality control. The example set by the firm's leadership encourages a culture that recognises high quality work.
- Persons assigned operational responsibility for the system by the CEO or managing partners should have **sufficient and appropriate experience, ability and the necessary authority** to assume that responsibility.

#### (B) Ethical requirements (SM 11.26–11.27)

- Policies should give the firm reasonable assurance that the firm and its personnel comply with the relevant ethical requirements in the **Code of Ethics issued by ICAI**: integrity, objectivity, professional competence and due care, confidentiality and professional behaviour ([[aud.ethics.principles|the fundamental principles]]).
- Observance of **independence** in all engagements is the basic requirement. Policies should give reasonable assurance that the firm, its personnel and, where required, experts contracted by the firm and network firm personnel maintain independence where the Code requires. They should enable the firm to: (a) communicate its independence requirements to its personnel; and (b) identify and evaluate circumstances and relationships that create threats to independence, and take appropriate action to eliminate them or reduce them to an acceptable level by applying safeguards or, if appropriate, withdraw from the engagement ([[aud.ethics.independence|threats and safeguards]]).
- There should be a **mechanism** by which engagement partners provide the firm with relevant information about client engagements, and personnel promptly notify the firm of circumstances and relationships that create a threat to independence. **All breaches of independence should be promptly notified** to the firm for appropriate action.
- **At least annually**, the firm should obtain **written confirmation** of compliance with its independence policies and procedures from all firm personnel required to be independent under the Code.

#### (C) Acceptance and continuance of client relationships and specific engagements (SM 11.27–11.28)

Before accepting an engagement, the firm should acquire vital information about the client, to help it decide on:

With regard to the **integrity of a client**, the matters the firm considers include, for example (SM 11.28):

1. The identity and business reputation of the client's principal owners, key management, related parties and those charged with its governance.
2. The nature of the client's operations, including its business practices.
3. Information concerning the attitude of the client's principal owners, key management and those charged with governance towards matters such as aggressive interpretation of accounting standards and the internal control environment.
4. Whether the client is aggressively concerned with maintaining the firm's fees as low as possible.
5. Indications of an inappropriate limitation in the scope of work.
6. Indications that the client might be involved in money laundering or other criminal activities.
7. The reasons for the proposed appointment of the firm and non-reappointment of the previous firm.

Any **conflict of interest** between the firm and the client should be properly resolved before accepting. Where the firm obtains information that would have caused it to decline an engagement had it been obtainable earlier, its continuance policies should include consideration of (a) the professional and legal responsibilities that apply, including any requirement to report to the person or persons who made the appointment or, in some cases, to regulatory authorities; and (b) the possibility of withdrawing from the engagement, or from both the engagement and the client relationship (SM 11.28).

#### (D) Human resources (SM 11.29)

Policies should give reasonable assurance that the firm has **sufficient personnel with the capabilities, competence, and commitment to ethical principles** necessary to perform its engagements in accordance with professional standards and regulatory and legal requirements, and to enable the firm or engagement partners to issue appropriate reports. They address recruitment, compensation, training, career development, performance evaluation and so on, with emphasis on **continuing professional development**.

#### (E) Engagement performance (SM 11.29–11.30)

- **Consistency** in the quality of engagement performance is achieved through briefing engagement teams on their objectives, processes for complying with engagement standards, supervision and training, methods of reviewing the work performed, and appropriate documentation.
- **Consultation** should take place on difficult or contentious matters: discussion, at the appropriate professional level, with individuals within or outside the firm who have specialised expertise. A firm without appropriate internal resources may use advisory services of other firms or of professional and regulatory bodies.
- **Engagement quality control review (EQCR).** Significant judgements made in an engagement should be reviewed by an engagement quality control reviewer, for taking an objective view, before the report is issued. The extent of the review depends on the complexity of the engagement and the risk that the report might not be appropriate. ==The review does not reduce the responsibilities of the engagement partner.== EQCR is **mandatory for all audits of financial statements of listed entities**; for other engagements, the firm should devise criteria to determine which require it.
- **Differences of opinion** within the team, with those consulted, or between the engagement partner and the reviewer: the report should be issued only after they are resolved. If the reviewer's recommendations are not accepted by the engagement partner and the matter is not resolved to the reviewer's satisfaction, it is resolved through the firm's established procedures, such as consulting another practitioner or firm, or a professional or regulatory body.
- **Assembly of files.** Engagement teams should complete the assembly of final engagement files on a timely basis after the reports are finalised: for audit engagements, in **not more than 60 days after the date of the auditor's report**; for others, within limits appropriate to the engagement.
- **Custody and ownership.** Policies should maintain the confidentiality, safe custody, integrity, accessibility and retrievability of engagement documentation. Unless otherwise specified by law or regulation, **engagement documentation is the property of the firm**. The firm may, at its discretion, make portions of or extracts from it available to clients, provided this does not undermine the validity of the work performed or, for assurance engagements, the independence of the firm or its personnel.
- **Retention.** Documentation is kept long enough for monitoring procedures to evaluate compliance with the system, or longer if law or regulation requires. For audit engagements the retention period is ordinarily **no shorter than seven years from the date of the auditor's report or, if later, the date of the group auditor's report**.

#### (F) Monitoring (SM 11.30)

The firm should ensure that its quality control policies and procedures are **relevant, adequate, operating effectively and complied with in practice**. This includes an ongoing consideration and evaluation of the system, including a **periodic inspection of a selection of completed engagements**.

### 4. SA 220: quality control for an audit

Based on the firm's system, the engagement team applies the quality control policies to its audit, and the **engagement partner** is responsible for the quality control procedures on that engagement. SA 220 is premised on the basis that the firm is subject to SQC 1: within that system, engagement teams implement the procedures applicable to the audit and provide the firm with the information it needs for the part of the system relating to independence (SM 11.31).

::: definition SA 220: objective of the auditor
To implement quality control procedures at the engagement level that provide the auditor with reasonable assurance that (a) the audit complies with professional standards and regulatory and legal requirements; and (b) the auditor's report issued is appropriate in the circumstances (SM 11.31).
:::

SA 220 is modelled on SQC 1 and sets the engagement partner's responsibilities for six matters (SM 11.31):

| Element | SQC 1 (the firm) | SA 220 (engagement partner) |
|---|---|---|
| (A) | Leadership responsibilities for quality within the firm | Leadership responsibilities for quality on audits |
| (B) | Ethical requirements | Relevant ethical requirements |
| (C) | Acceptance and continuance of client relationships and specific engagements | Acceptance and continuance of client relationships and audit engagements |
| (D) | Human resources | Assignment of engagement teams |
| (E) | Engagement performance | Engagement performance |
| (F) | Monitoring | Monitoring |

#### (A) Leadership responsibilities for quality on audits (SM 11.31–11.32)

The engagement partner takes responsibility for the **overall quality on each audit engagement**. His actions and appropriate messages to the other members of the team emphasise:

- (a) the importance to audit quality of: (i) performing work that complies with professional standards and regulatory and legal requirements; (ii) complying with the firm's quality control policies and procedures as applicable; (iii) issuing auditor's reports that are appropriate in the circumstances; and (iv) the engagement team's ability to **raise concerns without fear of reprisals**; and
- (b) the fact that quality is essential in performing audit engagements.

#### (B) Relevant ethical requirements (SM 11.32)

The engagement partner's responsibilities:

- **Identifying** a threat to independence regarding the audit engagement that safeguards may not be able to eliminate or reduce to an acceptable level.
- **Reporting** to the relevant persons within the firm to determine appropriate action, which may include eliminating the activity or interest that creates the threat, or withdrawing from the audit engagement, where withdrawal is legally permitted.

#### (C) Acceptance and continuance of client relationships and audit engagements (SM 11.32–11.33)

On the lines of SQC 1: information such as the integrity of principal owners, the competence of the engagement team and the necessary capabilities including time and resources, compliance with relevant ethical requirements, and significant matters that arose in the current or previous audit and their implications help the engagement partner determine whether the conclusions on acceptance and continuance are appropriate.

#### (D) Assignment of engagement teams (SM 11.33)

The engagement partner should be satisfied that the engagement team and any **auditor's experts who are not part of the team**, collectively, have the appropriate competence and capabilities to perform the engagement in accordance with professional standards and regulatory and legal requirements.

#### (E) Engagement performance (SM 11.33)

- The engagement partner is responsible for the **direction, supervision and performance** of the audit in accordance with professional standards and regulatory and legal requirements, and for the auditor's report being appropriate in the circumstances.
- **Reviewing** the audit documentation before the report is issued is his responsibility; he ensures that sufficient appropriate audit evidence has been obtained to support the conclusions reached and the report.
- He is responsible for the team undertaking appropriate **consultation** on difficult or contentious matters, within the team and with others at the appropriate level within or outside the firm.
- For audits of **listed entities**, and other audits for which the firm has determined that an EQCR is required, the engagement partner shall: (a) determine that an engagement quality control reviewer has been appointed; (b) discuss significant matters arising during the audit, including those identified during the EQCR, with the reviewer; and (c) **not date the auditor's report until the completion of the engagement quality control review**.
- If differences of opinion arise within the team, with those consulted or between the engagement partner and the reviewer, the team shall follow the firm's policies and procedures for dealing with and resolving them.

#### (F) Monitoring (SM 11.34)

An effective system includes a monitoring process giving the firm reasonable assurance that its policies and procedures are relevant, adequate and operating effectively. The engagement partner shall consider the **results of the firm's monitoring process**, as evidenced in the latest information circulated by the firm and, if applicable, other network firms, and whether deficiencies noted in that information may affect the audit engagement.

**What the engagement partner documents** (SM 11.34):

1. Issues identified with respect to compliance with relevant ethical requirements and how they were resolved.
2. Conclusions on compliance with independence requirements that apply to the audit engagement, and any relevant discussions with the firm that support these conclusions.
3. Conclusions reached regarding the acceptance and continuance of client relationships and audit engagements.
4. The nature and scope of, and conclusions resulting from, consultations undertaken during the audit.

### Two clocks that start on the date of the auditor's report

*Reproduce this from memory: both limits run from the date of the auditor's report, not from the year-end or the end of fieldwork.*

| Rule (SQC 1) | Limit | Report dated 30 May 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Assembly of the final audit file | Not more than 60 days after the report date | By 29 July 2026 |

## Worked examples

:::: example The quality partner's inspection notes
As part of monitoring, the quality partner of Iyer & Associates inspects a selection of completed engagements and the firm's policies. Her notes:

(a) Kulhad Crafts Ltd (unlisted): the auditor's report is dated 30 May 2026 and the final engagement file was assembled on 10 July 2026.

(b) The firm's policy manual says: "Audit files may be destroyed five years after the end of the financial year audited."

(c) The firm's policy requires an engagement quality control review only where the audit fee exceeds ₹50 lakh. Masala Mills Ltd, listed on BSE, pays ₹18 lakh, so its audit had no review.

(d) The managing partner has handed "all quality matters" to CA Simran Gill, qualified six months ago, without authority to change any procedure, and tells the partners that quality is "now Simran's responsibility, not mine".

(e) Ujjain Agro Foods Pvt Ltd was accepted as a new client on a partner's friend's recommendation. The file holds no information about the client's owners or management, the team's competence and time, or ethical requirements.

Comment on each observation with reference to SQC 1.

::: answer Model answer
**(a) Assembly of the file: complies.** SQC 1 requires engagement teams to complete the assembly of final engagement files on a timely basis after the reports are finalised; for audit engagements, in not more than 60 days after the date of the auditor's report. The file was assembled 41 days after the report dated 30 May 2026, within the limit of 29 July 2026.

**(b) Retention policy: does not comply.** For audit engagements the retention period is ordinarily no shorter than seven years from the date of the auditor's report or, if later, the date of the group auditor's report. The policy is wrong twice: five years instead of seven, and counted from the end of the financial year instead of from the date of the auditor's report. It should be revised.

**(c) No review of Masala Mills: does not comply.** Engagement quality control review is mandatory for all audits of financial statements of listed entities; only for other engagements may the firm devise criteria, such as a fee level, to decide when a review is needed. Masala Mills is listed, so its audit required a review of significant judgements before the report was issued, whatever the fee. The firm's criteria should be restricted to unlisted engagements.

**(d) Leadership: does not comply.** SQC 1 requires the firm's chief executive officer or managing partners to assume ultimate responsibility for the system of quality control. Persons assigned operational responsibility should have sufficient and appropriate experience, ability and the necessary authority. The managing partner cannot transfer his ultimate responsibility, and CA Simran lacks both the experience and the authority the role needs.

**(e) Acceptance of a new client: does not comply.** Before accepting an engagement the firm should acquire vital information to decide on the integrity of the client, its competence (including capabilities, time and resources) to perform the engagement, and compliance with ethical requirements. On integrity it considers matters such as the identity and business reputation of the principal owners, key management and those charged with governance, the nature of operations, attitudes to aggressive interpretation of accounting standards, and the reasons for the appointment and the non-reappointment of the previous firm. A recommendation is not information. The firm should obtain the information now, consider whether to continue, and document how any issues are resolved.
:::

:::: example Lakshmi's engagement file
Ms. Lakshmi Iyer is the engagement partner for the 2025-26 audit of Tapri Tea Co. Ltd. A review of her file shows:

(a) The cold-chain division now bills customers through an IoT-based system that records deliveries and temperatures automatically. Lakshmi assigned two article assistants to this area. The firm's IT audit specialist was not involved.

(b) Bunty disagreed with the audit senior over the provision for obsolete premix. The senior told him: "Articles do not raise concerns. Drop it, or your appraisal will show it." The disagreement was never escalated.

(c) The firm's latest monitoring circular reports deficiencies in the inventory sampling template used across the firm. Lakshmi used the template unchanged, saying the deficiencies were "in other partners' files".

(d) When Bunty's sister became Tapri's Head of Treasury, Lakshmi took Bunty off the team. Nothing about the issue or her conclusion on independence is recorded.

Examine, with reference to SA 220, whether Lakshmi has discharged her responsibilities as engagement partner, and what she should do.

::: answer Model answer
**Relevant provision.** As per SA 220, the engagement partner is responsible for quality control procedures on the audit, with the objective of reasonable assurance that the audit complies with professional standards and regulatory and legal requirements and that the auditor's report is appropriate in the circumstances.

**(a) Assignment of the team: not discharged.** The engagement partner should be satisfied that the engagement team and any auditor's experts who are not part of the team collectively have the appropriate competence and capabilities to perform the engagement in accordance with professional standards and regulatory and legal requirements. Two article assistants without IT audit skills cannot collectively provide that competence for an automated billing system. Lakshmi should involve the firm's IT audit specialist and assign team members with the necessary capabilities.

**(b) Leadership and differences of opinion: not discharged.** The engagement partner's actions and messages must emphasise the engagement team's ability to raise concerns without fear of reprisals. If differences of opinion arise within the team, the team shall follow the firm's policies and procedures for dealing with and resolving them. The senior's threat contradicts this. Lakshmi should make clear that concerns may be raised without reprisal, hear Bunty's point, and resolve the difference under the firm's policies, consulting where the matter is difficult or contentious, before the report is issued.

**(c) Monitoring: not discharged.** The engagement partner shall consider the results of the firm's monitoring process, as evidenced in the latest information circulated by the firm, and whether deficiencies noted in it may affect the audit engagement. A deficiency in a firm-wide template affects every audit that uses it. Lakshmi should evaluate its effect on Tapri's inventory testing and correct the sample or perform further procedures.

**(d) Ethical requirements and documentation: partly discharged.** The threat (a close relative of a team member in a senior position at the client) was identified, and a safeguard was applied by removing Bunty from the team; had a safeguard not been enough, the engagement partner would report to the relevant persons within the firm to determine appropriate action. But the engagement partner should document the issues identified with respect to compliance with relevant ethical requirements and how they were resolved, and the conclusions on compliance with independence requirements, with any relevant discussions with the firm. Lakshmi should record the threat, the safeguard applied and her conclusion on independence.

**Conclusion.** Lakshmi has not fully discharged her responsibilities under SA 220. She should take the steps above, and review the documentation to ensure that sufficient appropriate audit evidence supports the conclusions before the auditor's report is issued.
:::

## Answer frame

### A case on SQC 1 or SA 220

1. **Provision**: "As per SQC 1 …" or "As per SA 220 …", only the element the facts touch, in its words.
2. **Facts**: "In the given case, …" with the dates, the client's status (listed or not) and who did what.
3. **Analysis**: compliant or not, and why (the 41 days, the listed status, the missing authority).
4. **Conclusion and action**: "Hence, the firm has not complied …; it should …".

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: the firm-wide SA 220
Bunty writes "SA 220 applies to the whole firm and SQC 1 to each audit". **Fix:** the reverse. SQC 1 applies to the entire firm and all engagements; SA 220 to a particular audit, and it is premised on SQC 1.
:::

::: mistake The fee-sized review
"EQCR only for big clients." **Fix:** EQCR is mandatory for all audits of listed entities, whatever the fee; the firm devises criteria only for other engagements.
:::

::: mistake Clocks started at the year-end
**Fix:** assembly within 60 days after the date of the auditor's report; retention ordinarily not shorter than seven years from the date of the auditor's report (or the group auditor's report, if later).
:::

::: mistake The reviewer as the new signatory
"Once the EQC reviewer clears the file, responsibility passes to the reviewer." **Fix:** the review does not reduce the responsibilities of the engagement partner; he still may not date the report until the review is complete.
:::

- [ ] The report may be dated now, because the review only has to be finished before the file is assembled
- [x] The review is required under the firm's criteria, and the report shall not be dated until it is completed
- [ ] The report may be dated now if the reviewer signs a note accepting responsibility for the report
> A review is mandatory for audits of listed entities and is also required wherever the firm's own criteria say so. For both, SA 220 says the engagement partner shall not date the auditor's report until the review is complete, and the review does not reduce the engagement partner's responsibilities.

Q: An auditor's report is dated 12 June 2026. Under SQC 1, by when should the assembly of the final audit file ordinarily be completed?
- [ ] 31 May 2026, 60 days after the year-end
- [x] 11 August 2026
- [ ] 12 June 2026, on the date of the report
- [ ] 12 June 2033
> Not more than 60 days after the date of the auditor's report: 12 June plus 60 days is 11 August 2026. Seven years (12 June 2033) is the minimum retention period, a different rule.

Q: Which of the following is NOT one of the six elements of a firm's system of quality control under SQC 1?
- [x] Assignment of engagement teams
- [ ] Ethical requirements
- [ ] Monitoring
- [ ] Human resources
> SQC 1's elements are leadership responsibilities, ethical requirements, acceptance and continuance, human resources, engagement performance and monitoring. "Assignment of engagement teams" is SA 220's counterpart of human resources.

Q: Under SQC 1, which matters does a firm consider regarding the integrity of a prospective client? Select all that apply.
- [x] Whether the client is aggressively concerned with maintaining the firm's fees as low as possible
- [ ] The client's expected growth in profit next year
- [x] Indications of an inappropriate limitation in the scope of work
- [x] The reasons for the proposed appointment of the firm and non-reappointment of the previous firm
> These three are in SQC 1's list, with the reputation of owners and management, the nature of operations, attitudes to aggressive accounting and internal control, and indications of money laundering or other criminal activities. Profit prospects are not an integrity matter.

Q: Under SA 220, the engagement partner's actions and messages to the team emphasise all of the following EXCEPT:
- [ ] The team's ability to raise concerns without fear of reprisals
- [ ] Issuing auditor's reports that are appropriate in the circumstances
- [ ] Complying with the firm's quality control policies and procedures as applicable
- [x] Completing the audit within the hours budgeted for the fee
> The four matters are compliance with professional standards and legal requirements, the firm's quality control policies, appropriate reports, and raising concerns without fear of reprisals, plus the fact that quality is essential. A fee budget is not among them.

Q: Unless law or regulation specifies otherwise, engagement documentation belongs to:
- [ ] The client, which paid for the engagement
- [x] The firm, which may give extracts to the client at its discretion
- [ ] The engagement partner personally
- [ ] The Institute of Chartered Accountants of India
> SQC 1: engagement documentation is the property of the firm; it may make portions or extracts available to clients if that does not undermine the validity of the work or, for assurance engagements, the independence of the firm or its personnel.

## Recap

- SQC 1: the entire firm, all engagements; SA 220: a particular audit, the engagement partner, premised on SQC 1.
- SQC 1 objective: reasonable assurance that the firm and its personnel comply with professional standards and regulatory and legal requirements, and that reports are appropriate.
- Six elements: leadership, ethical requirements, acceptance and continuance, human resources (SA 220: assignment of engagement teams), engagement performance, monitoring.
- Leadership: CEO or managing partners hold ultimate responsibility; operational people need experience, ability and authority. Independence: communicate, identify and evaluate threats, notify breaches, written confirmation at least annually.
- Acceptance: integrity of the client, competence (capabilities, time, resources), ethical requirements; seven integrity matters, including money laundering indications and why the previous firm left.
- EQCR: mandatory for audits of listed entities; does not reduce the engagement partner's responsibilities; the report is not dated until it is complete.
- Files assembled within 60 days after the report date; retained at least seven years from it (or the group auditor's report, if later); the property of the firm.
- SA 220 engagement partner: overall quality, raising concerns without reprisals, competent team with experts, direction, supervision and review, monitoring results, four documentation matters. (Kesar now calls his café inspectors "EQC reviewers".)

## Flashcards

Q:: SQC 1 versus SA 220: scope and level of each?
A:: SQC 1 applies to all engagements (audits, reviews, other assurance and related services) and to the entire firm; SA 220 applies only to audits, at the level of a particular engagement.

Q:: SQC 1: what should the firm's system of quality control provide?
A:: Reasonable assurance that the firm and its personnel comply with professional standards and regulatory and legal requirements, and that reports issued by the firm or engagement partners are appropriate in the circumstances.

Q:: List the six elements of a system of quality control under SQC 1.
A:: Leadership responsibilities for quality within the firm; ethical requirements; acceptance and continuance of client relationships and specific engagements; human resources; engagement performance; monitoring.

Q:: SQC 1: who assumes ultimate responsibility for the firm's system of quality control?
A:: The firm's chief executive officer or managing partners.

Q:: SQC 1: how often must the firm obtain written confirmation of compliance with independence policies, and from whom?
A:: At least annually, from all firm personnel required to be independent under the Code of Ethics.

Q:: SQC 1: for which engagements is engagement quality control review mandatory?
A:: All audits of financial statements of listed entities; for other engagements the firm devises criteria.

Q:: SQC 1: time limit for assembling the final file of an audit engagement?
A:: Not more than 60 days after the date of the auditor's report.

Q:: SQC 1: retention period for audit documentation?
A:: Ordinarily no shorter than seven years from the date of the auditor's report or, if later, the date of the group auditor's report.

Q:: SA 220: objective of the auditor?
A:: To implement quality control procedures at the engagement level giving reasonable assurance that the audit complies with professional standards and regulatory and legal requirements, and that the auditor's report issued is appropriate in the circumstances.

Q:: SA 220: three duties of the engagement partner where an EQCR is required?
A:: Determine that a reviewer has been appointed; discuss significant matters, including those identified in the review, with the reviewer; not date the auditor's report until the review is complete.

Q:: SA 220: the four matters the engagement partner documents?
A:: Ethical compliance issues and their resolution; conclusions on independence with supporting discussions; conclusions on acceptance and continuance; the nature, scope and conclusions of consultations.

Q:: SA 220: what do the engagement partner's leadership actions and messages emphasise?
A:: The importance to quality of complying with professional standards and legal requirements, complying with the firm's quality control policies, issuing appropriate reports, and the team's ability to raise concerns without fear of reprisals; and that quality is essential in audits.
