## In plain words

### Whose tick is it anyway?

Iyer & Associates have taken on two new audits for 2025-26. **Tapri Cold Chain Pvt Ltd**, Tapri's refrigerated-delivery company, was audited last year by another firm, Joshi Kale & Co. **Mehra Masala Traders**, the spice firm run by Kesar's cousin Pinky, has never been audited at all: its bank now wants audited accounts.

> **Bunty:** Ma'am, the opening balances are just last year's closing balances. Joshi Kale & Co. already audited them. Tick, done.
> **Lakshmi:** Their tick, Bunty. Our report. If last year's stock was wrong, whose evidence do we show?
> **Bunty:** But last year is over.
> **Lakshmi:** Last year's closing stock is this year's cost of sales. It is very much this year.

Both jobs are **initial audit engagements**: the first audit of an entity by this auditor, either because nobody audited the previous year or because a different firm did. The **opening balances** are the amounts the year starts with: last year's closing figures, together with the commitments and contingencies that existed on day one.

Lakshmi's point is that opening balances do not stay in the past. Opening inventory becomes part of this year's cost of goods sold; opening receivables are collected, or written off, this year; the accounting policies used to build those balances must be the policies used this year too. A wrong opening figure quietly distorts this year's profit, and the new auditor signs the report on this year.

So the new auditor collects evidence that the opening balances do not contain misstatements that materially affect this year, and that the accounting policies behind them have been applied consistently (or that any change is properly accounted for and disclosed). The predecessor's audited accounts help; this year's own audit work helps; where neither is enough, the auditor performs specific procedures. If the evidence cannot be had, or shows a problem nobody fixes, the opinion is modified.

::: analogy The meter reading on moving day
When you move into a rented flat, you note the electricity meter on day one. The previous tenant's last bill is a good start (the predecessor's audited accounts), and the units used in your first month confirm it (this year's audit procedures). If nobody read the meter when the last tenant left, you read it now and work backwards from the units used since (the stock roll-back). Skip all three, and the last tenant's summer of air-conditioning lands on your bill.
:::

::: key
Opening balances are not last year's problem: they flow into this year's profit. A predecessor's audit helps the new auditor get evidence about them; it never replaces getting it.
:::

## How it works

SA 510, "Initial Audit Engagements – Opening Balances", deals with the auditor's responsibilities relating to opening balances when conducting an initial audit engagement. Opening balances include, besides the amounts in the financial statements, matters requiring disclosure that existed at the beginning of the period, such as contingencies and commitments. When the financial statements include comparative information, [[aud.report.sa710|SA 710]] also applies (SM 4.87).

### 1. Three definitions

::: definition Initial audit engagement
An engagement in which either (i) the financial statements for the prior period were not audited; or (ii) the financial statements for the prior period were audited by a predecessor auditor (SM 4.87).
:::

::: definition Opening balances
Those account balances that exist at the beginning of the period. Opening balances are based upon the closing balances of the prior period and reflect the effects of transactions and events of prior periods and accounting policies applied in the prior period. They also include matters requiring disclosure that existed at the beginning of the period, such as contingencies and commitments (SM 4.87).
:::

::: definition Predecessor auditor
The auditor from a different audit firm, who audited the financial statements of an entity in the prior period and who has been replaced by the current auditor (SM 4.87).
:::

"From a different audit firm" matters: if the same firm audited last year, this is not an initial audit engagement.

### 2. The objective

In conducting an initial audit engagement, the objective of the auditor with respect to opening balances is to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence about whether (SM 4.88):

- (a) opening balances contain misstatements that ==materially affect the current period's financial statements==; and
- (b) appropriate accounting policies reflected in the opening balances have been consistently applied in the current period's financial statements, or changes thereto are properly accounted for and adequately presented and disclosed in accordance with the applicable financial reporting framework.

### 3. What the auditor does

The auditor shall read the most recent financial statements, if any, and the predecessor auditor's report thereon, if any, for information relevant to opening balances, including disclosures (the Study Material's answer to Test Your Knowledge Q8, SM 4.131; ICAI's suggested answers open with it too). The auditor shall then obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence about whether the opening balances contain misstatements that materially affect the current period's financial statements by (SM 4.88):

- (a) determining whether the prior period's closing balances have been correctly brought forward to the current period or, when appropriate, any adjustments have been disclosed as prior period items in the current year's Statement of Profit and Loss;
- (b) determining whether the opening balances reflect the application of appropriate accounting policies; and
- (c) performing one or more of the following:
  - (i) where the prior year financial statements were audited, perusing the copies of the audited financial statements including the other relevant documents relating to the prior period financial statements;
  - (ii) evaluating whether audit procedures performed in the current period provide evidence relevant to the opening balances; or
  - (iii) performing specific audit procedures to obtain evidence regarding the opening balances.

If the auditor obtains audit evidence that the opening balances contain misstatements that could materially affect the current period's financial statements, the auditor shall perform such additional audit procedures as are appropriate to determine the effect on the current period's financial statements. If the auditor concludes that such misstatements exist in the current period's financial statements, the auditor shall communicate them with the appropriate level of management and those charged with governance (SM 4.89).

### 4. What decides how much work

The nature and extent of audit procedures necessary to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence regarding opening balances depend on such matters as (SM 4.89):

- The accounting policies followed by the entity.
- The nature of the account balances, classes of transactions and disclosures and the risks of material misstatement in the current period's financial statements.
- The significance of the opening balances relative to the current period's financial statements.
- Whether the prior period's financial statements were audited and, if so, whether the predecessor auditor's opinion was modified.

### 5. Procedures, balance by balance

**Predecessor auditor.** If the prior period's financial statements were audited by a predecessor auditor, the auditor may be able to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence regarding the opening balances by perusing the copies of the audited financial statements including other relevant documents such as supporting schedules. Ordinarily, the current auditor can place reliance on the closing balances contained in the financial statements for the preceding period, except when during the performance of audit procedures for the current period the possibility of misstatements in opening balances is indicated (SM 4.89). By type of balance (SM 4.90):

| Balance | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Receivables, payables | Collection or payment this year |
| Inventories | Extra procedures: this year's closing count says little |
| PPE, investments, long-term debt | Underlying records; third-party confirmation for debt and investments |

- **Current assets and liabilities.** Some evidence comes from the current period's audit procedures: the collection (payment) of opening accounts receivable (accounts payable) during the current period gives some evidence of their existence, rights and obligations, completeness and valuation at the beginning of the period.
- **Inventories.** The current period's procedures on the closing inventory provide little evidence about inventory on hand at the beginning of the period, so one or more of these may be needed: observing a current physical inventory count and reconciling it to the opening inventory quantities; performing audit procedures on the valuation of the opening inventory items; performing audit procedures on gross profit and cut-off.
- **Non-current assets and liabilities** (property, plant and equipment, investments, long-term debt): some evidence by examining the accounting records and other information underlying the opening balances; in certain cases by confirmation with third parties (long-term debt and investments); in other cases, additional procedures.

### 6. Consistency of accounting policies

The auditor shall obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence about whether the accounting policies reflected in the opening balances have been consistently applied in the current period's financial statements, and whether changes in them have been properly accounted for and adequately presented and disclosed (SM 4.91).

### 7. Reporting

SA 510 sends three problems to [[aud.report.sa705|SA 705]] (SM 4.91):

1. Unable to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence regarding the opening balances: **qualified opinion or disclaimer of opinion**, as appropriate, in accordance with SA 705.
2. The opening balances contain a misstatement that materially affects the current period's financial statements, and its effect is not properly accounted for or not adequately presented or disclosed: **qualified opinion or adverse opinion**, as appropriate.
3. The current period's accounting policies are not consistently applied in relation to opening balances, or a change in accounting policies is not properly accounted for or not adequately presented or disclosed: **qualified opinion or adverse opinion**, as appropriate.

## Formats and formulas

### Why an opening figure is a this-year figure

::: formula Cost of goods sold
$$\text{Cost of goods sold} = \qquad \text{Opening inventory} + \text{Purchases} - \text{Closing inventory}$$

Every rupee of overstatement in opening inventory is a rupee of overstated cost of goods sold, and so a rupee of understated profit, this year.
:::

Tapri Cold Chain's 2025-26 figures (₹ crore) show it. Suppose ₹0.90 crore of the ₹15 crore opening stock of frozen snacks had in fact spoiled before 31 March 2025:

| Particulars | As recorded | If opening stock was right |
|---|---:|---:|
| Opening inventory | 15.00 | 14.10 |
| Add: purchases | 117.00 | 117.00 |
| Less: closing inventory | 33.00 | 33.00 |
| **Cost of goods sold** | **99.00** | **98.10** |

This year's cost of goods sold is overstated, and its profit understated, by ₹0.90 crore, though the error was made last year.

## Worked examples

:::: example Tapri Cold Chain: the predecessor's tick
Iyer & Associates have been appointed statutory auditors of Tapri Cold Chain Pvt Ltd for 2025-26. The 2024-25 financial statements were audited by Joshi Kale & Co., whose report was qualified because trade receivables of ₹3.20 crore could not be confirmed. Opening balances include inventory of frozen snacks of ₹15 crore and trade receivables of ₹20 crore. Bunty proposes no procedures on opening balances: "Joshi Kale & Co. signed these numbers last year. Why audit the same rupee twice?"

(i) Is this an initial audit engagement, and is Bunty's approach correct?

(ii) State the audit procedures Iyer & Associates should perform on opening balances.

(iii) Which matters in this case will influence the nature and extent of those procedures?

::: answer Model answer
**(i) Relevant provision.** As per SA 510, "Initial Audit Engagements – Opening Balances", an initial audit engagement is an engagement in which either (i) the financial statements for the prior period were not audited; or (ii) the financial statements for the prior period were audited by a predecessor auditor. In such an engagement the auditor shall obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence about whether the opening balances contain misstatements that materially affect the current period's financial statements.

**Facts and analysis.** The 2024-25 financial statements were audited by Joshi Kale & Co., a different firm. The engagement is therefore an initial audit engagement. The predecessor's audit does not remove the requirement: its audited financial statements are one source of evidence about the opening balances, not a substitute for the procedures SA 510 requires.

**Conclusion.** Bunty's approach is **not correct**.

**(ii) Procedures.** The auditor shall read the most recent financial statements and the predecessor auditor's report thereon for information relevant to opening balances, including disclosures, and shall obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence by:

1. Determining whether the prior period's closing balances have been correctly brought forward to the current period or, when appropriate, any adjustments have been disclosed as prior period items in the current year's Statement of Profit and Loss;
2. Determining whether the opening balances reflect the application of appropriate accounting policies; and
3. Performing one or more of the following: (i) perusing the copies of the audited financial statements, including the other relevant documents relating to the prior period financial statements; (ii) evaluating whether audit procedures performed in the current period provide evidence relevant to the opening balances; or (iii) performing specific audit procedures to obtain evidence regarding the opening balances.

For the receivables, collections during 2025-26 give some evidence of their existence, rights and obligations, completeness and valuation at the beginning of the year. For the inventory, additional procedures may be needed: observing a current physical count and reconciling it to the opening quantities, testing the valuation of opening items, and procedures on gross profit and cut-off.

**(iii) Matters influencing the nature and extent.** The nature and extent depend on the accounting policies followed; the nature of the balances and the risks of material misstatement in the current period's financial statements; the significance of the opening balances relative to the current period's financial statements; and whether the prior period was audited and, if so, whether the predecessor's opinion was modified. Here the opening inventory and receivables are significant, frozen stock can spoil, and the predecessor's opinion was **qualified on receivables**: the procedures on opening receivables, in particular, must be more extensive and focused.
:::

:::: example Pinky's turmeric that was never there
Mehra Masala Traders, a partnership firm, appoints Iyer & Associates for its first audit, for 2025-26; its 2024-25 financial statements were not audited, and nobody counted the stock on 31 March 2025. Iyer & Associates attended the count on 31 March 2026. Turmeric is the main item:

| Turmeric | Quantity (kg) |
|---|---:|
| Counted on 31 March 2026 | 18,400 |
| Sold during 2025-26 | 2,26,000 |
| Purchased during 2025-26 | 2,30,500 |
| Opening stock per the firm's records | 16,200 |

Opening turmeric is valued at ₹180 per kg, which agrees with March 2025 purchase invoices; the other opening items reconcile. The draft Statement of Profit and Loss shows sales ₹4,20,00,000, purchases ₹3,40,00,000, opening inventory ₹46,00,000, closing inventory ₹52,00,000 and other expenses ₹58,00,000. Materiality is ₹1,40,000.

Required:

(i) State the procedures that give evidence about the opening inventory here.

(ii) Compute any difference in the opening turmeric and its effect on the 2025-26 Statement of Profit and Loss.

(iii) Pinky refuses to show the difference as a prior period item: "The net profit does not change, na?" Advise Iyer & Associates on their report.

::: answer Model answer
**(i) Procedures.** As per SA 510, in the case of inventories the current period's audit procedures on the closing inventory provide little audit evidence regarding inventory on hand at the beginning of the period, so additional procedures may be necessary: observing a current physical inventory count and reconciling it to the opening inventory quantities; performing audit procedures on the valuation of the opening inventory items; and performing audit procedures on gross profit and cut-off. Since the prior period was not audited, there is no predecessor's work to peruse: the evidence must come from this year's procedures and specific procedures on the opening balances.

**(ii) Working note: rolling the count back to 1 April 2025**

| Turmeric | kg |
|---|---:|
| Counted on 31 March 2026 | 18,400 |
| Add: sold during the year | 2,26,000 |
| Less: purchased during the year | 2,30,500 |
| **Opening stock supported by the roll-back** | **13,900** |
| Opening stock per records | 16,200 |
| **Difference** | **2,300** |

Value of the difference: 2,300 kg × ₹180 = **₹4,14,000**, against materiality of ₹1,40,000: material.

| Statement of Profit and Loss, 2025-26 (₹) | As drafted | Corrected |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sales | 4,20,00,000 | 4,20,00,000 |
| Cost of goods sold | 3,34,00,000 | 3,29,86,000 |
| Gross profit | 86,00,000 | 90,14,000 |
| Other expenses | 58,00,000 | 58,00,000 |
| Profit before prior period item | 28,00,000 | 32,14,000 |
| Prior period item: opening stock overstated | – | (4,14,000) |
| **Net profit** | **28,00,000** | **28,00,000** |

Cost of goods sold as drafted = 46,00,000 + 3,40,00,000 − 52,00,000. Corrected opening inventory = 46,00,000 − 4,14,000 = 41,86,000. The opening stock overstatement belongs to 2024-25 but has passed through this year's cost of goods sold, understating this year's gross profit (20.48% of sales instead of 21.46%) by ₹4,14,000.

**(iii) Relevant provision.** As per SA 510, the auditor determines whether, when appropriate, adjustments to the prior period's closing balances have been disclosed as prior period items in the current year's Statement of Profit and Loss. If the opening balances contain a misstatement that materially affects the current period's financial statements, and its effect is not properly accounted for or not adequately presented or disclosed, the auditor shall express a qualified opinion or an adverse opinion, as appropriate, in accordance with SA 705.

**Analysis.** Net profit is unchanged, but this year's results are misstated by ₹4,14,000, nearly three times materiality, and the prior period item is not disclosed. The effect is confined to cost of goods sold and gross profit: material, not pervasive.

**Conclusion.** If the firm does not present the difference as a prior period item, Iyer & Associates should express a **qualified opinion**, describing and quantifying the matter in the Basis for Qualified Opinion section. Since the prior period was not audited, the report should also state in an Other Matter paragraph that the corresponding figures are unaudited (SA 710); that statement does not relieve the auditor of the evidence on opening balances obtained above.
:::

## Under the hood

### SA 510 meets SA 710 and SA 300

- **Unaudited prior period.** When the prior period financial statements were not audited, the auditor shall state in an Other Matter paragraph that the corresponding figures are unaudited; such a statement does not relieve the auditor of the requirement to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence that the opening balances do not contain misstatements that materially affect the current period's financial statements (SM 8.60).
- **Predecessor auditor.** If the prior period was audited by a predecessor auditor and the auditor is permitted by law or regulation to refer to the predecessor's report on the corresponding figures and decides to do so, the Other Matter paragraph states that the prior period was audited by the predecessor auditor, the type of opinion expressed (with reasons, if modified), and the date of that report (SM 8.60–8.61).
- **Before accepting.** In case of initial engagements, communication with the predecessor auditor should be made where there has been a change of auditors, as part of the preliminary engagement activities of [[aud.plan.sa300|SA 300]] (SM 2.8).

### Two corners that catch people

- **Disclosures are opening balances too.** A guarantee given or a lawsuit pending on 1 April is part of the opening balances; the auditor checks it is carried into this year's notes.
- **"Ordinarily rely" is not "never check".** The Study Material allows the current auditor ordinarily to rely on the preceding period's closing balances, except when this year's procedures indicate a possible misstatement in them (SM 4.89). Reliance is reached by performing procedure (c)(i), perusing the audited statements and their schedules; ICAI's suggested answers treat "no procedures at all because the predecessor audited them" as incorrect (MTP May 2026 Series II Q3(a); MTP September 2025 Series II Q1(c)).

## Answer frame

### A case question: provision, facts, analysis, conclusion

1. **Relevant provision**: "As per SA 510, an initial audit engagement is…", both limbs, then the requirement the case needs (objective, the (a)–(c) procedure, the factors, or the reporting pair).
2. **Facts of the case**: who audited last year, or that nobody did; which balances are significant; any modified predecessor opinion.
3. **Analysis**: is it an initial engagement? Which procedure gives evidence for each balance? Is there missing evidence, a misstatement, or a policy problem?
4. **Conclusion**: "Hence, the approach of … is not correct", or the opinion: qualified or disclaimer; qualified or adverse.

### "Distinguish: prior period not audited vs audited by a predecessor"

| Point | Prior period not audited | Predecessor audited it |
|---|---|---|
| Initial audit engagement? | Yes | Yes |
| Procedure (c)(i) available? | No | Yes: peruse the audited statements and schedules |
| Evidence mainly from | This year's procedures and specific procedures | Predecessor's statements, then this year's procedures |
| Other Matter paragraph (SA 710, corresponding figures) | States that the corresponding figures are unaudited | Only if law permits a reference to the predecessor's report and the auditor decides to make it |

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: Bunty's borrowed tick
"Audited by the predecessor, so no work." **Fix:** an engagement after a predecessor is an initial audit engagement by definition. Perusing the predecessor's audited statements is one way to get evidence, inside procedure (c); it is not an exemption from (a), (b) and (c).
:::

::: mistake The closing count that "proves" opening stock
Using the 31 March 2026 count as evidence of 1 April 2025 stock. **Fix:** this year's closing inventory procedures give little evidence of opening inventory; reconcile the current count back to opening quantities, test opening valuation, and test gross profit and cut-off.
:::

::: mistake Mixing up the reporting pairs
Writing "adverse" for missing evidence, or "disclaimer" for a known misstatement. **Fix:** missing evidence: qualified or disclaimer. Misstatement, or inconsistent policies: qualified or adverse.
:::

::: mistake The new partner who thought the audit was "initial"
A new engagement partner at Iyer & Associates takes over a client the firm audited last year and calls it an initial audit engagement. **Fix:** a predecessor auditor is one "from a different audit firm". If the same firm audited the prior period, there is no predecessor auditor and no initial audit engagement; it is initial only if the prior period was unaudited or audited by another firm.
:::

## Recap

- Initial audit engagement: the prior period was not audited, or was audited by a predecessor auditor (from a different audit firm).
- Opening balances: balances at the start of the period, built from last year's closing balances and policies, including contingencies and commitments existing then.
- Objective: evidence whether opening balances contain misstatements that materially affect this year, and whether policies are consistently applied or changes properly accounted for and disclosed.
- Procedure: read the latest statements and predecessor's report; then (a) brought forward correctly, (b) appropriate policies, (c) one or more of: peruse audited statements, use this year's procedures, perform specific procedures.
- Nature and extent depend on: policies; nature of balances and risks; significance of opening balances; whether audited and whether the predecessor's opinion was modified.
- Receivables and payables: this year's collections and payments. Inventory: current count rolled back, valuation, gross profit and cut-off. Non-current items: underlying records; confirmations for long-term debt and investments.
- Reporting: no evidence: qualified or disclaimer; misstatement not properly dealt with, or policies inconsistent: qualified or adverse.
- Misstatements found: additional procedures, then communicate to management and those charged with governance. (Read the meter on moving day.)

## Flashcards

Q:: SA 510: what is an initial audit engagement?
A:: An engagement in which either (i) the financial statements for the prior period were not audited; or (ii) they were audited by a predecessor auditor.

Q:: SA 510: define opening balances.
A:: Account balances that exist at the beginning of the period, based on the prior period's closing balances and reflecting prior transactions, events and accounting policies; they include matters requiring disclosure at the start, such as contingencies and commitments.

Q:: SA 510: who is a predecessor auditor?
A:: The auditor from a different audit firm who audited the prior period's financial statements and has been replaced by the current auditor.

Q:: SA 510: what is the objective of the auditor regarding opening balances?
A:: To obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence about whether (a) opening balances contain misstatements that materially affect the current period's financial statements; and (b) appropriate accounting policies reflected in them are consistently applied, or changes are properly accounted for and adequately presented and disclosed.

Q:: SA 510: the three things the auditor does to obtain evidence about opening balances?
A:: (a) Determine whether prior closing balances are correctly brought forward (or adjustments disclosed as prior period items); (b) determine whether opening balances reflect appropriate accounting policies; (c) one or more of: peruse the audited prior statements, evaluate this year's procedures, perform specific procedures.

Q:: SA 510: the four matters on which the nature and extent of procedures depend?
A:: The entity's accounting policies; the nature of balances and transactions and the risks of material misstatement this year; the significance of opening balances relative to this year; whether the prior period was audited and, if so, whether the predecessor's opinion was modified.

Q:: SA 510: additional procedures for opening inventory?
A:: Observe a current physical count and reconcile it to opening quantities; test the valuation of opening inventory items; perform procedures on gross profit and cut-off.

Q:: SA 510: how is evidence obtained for opening long-term debt and investments?
A:: By examining the accounting records and other information underlying the opening balances, and in certain cases by confirmation with third parties.

Q:: SA 510: the opinion if sufficient appropriate evidence on opening balances cannot be obtained?
A:: A qualified opinion or a disclaimer of opinion, as appropriate, in accordance with SA 705.

Q:: SA 510: the opinion if a material opening misstatement, or a change in accounting policies, is not properly accounted for or disclosed?
A:: A qualified opinion or an adverse opinion, as appropriate, in accordance with SA 705.

Q:: SA 510: what must the auditor do on concluding that opening misstatements exist in this year's financial statements?
A:: Communicate the misstatements with the appropriate level of management and those charged with governance.
