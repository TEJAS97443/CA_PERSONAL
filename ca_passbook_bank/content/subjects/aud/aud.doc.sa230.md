## In plain words

### "I checked it, ma'am. I just did not write it down."

Lakshmi is reviewing the Tapri file before signing. The working paper for the cold-chain receivables says only "Checked. OK. – B".

> **Lakshmi:** Checked what, Bunty? Which balances, against what, and what did you find?
> **Bunty:** Ma'am, I checked the big ones against the confirmations. All fine. I remember.
> **Lakshmi:** Your memory will not be in the file when the regulator opens it in two years. As far as the file is concerned, you did not check anything.

**Audit documentation** (also called **working papers**) is the auditor's written record of three things: the audit procedures performed, the relevant audit evidence obtained, and the conclusions reached. It must be complete enough that an experienced auditor who has never seen this audit could pick up the file and understand what was done, what was found, and why the auditor concluded what he did, including who did each piece of work and who reviewed it.

It is written as the audit goes, not afterwards. Once the report is signed, the team has a short time (ordinarily not more than 60 days) to put the final file in order, which is tidying, not new auditing. Then the file is locked: nothing is deleted for at least seven years. And the file belongs to the auditor, not to the client.

::: analogy The hospital case sheet
A patient's case sheet lets the doctor on the next shift, who has never met the patient, see which tests were done, what they showed, what was concluded, and which doctor did what, without phoning anyone. Notes scribbled after discharge are less reliable than notes made at the bedside. The hospital keeps the case sheet; the patient may be given copies of parts of it.
:::

::: key
Audit documentation is the record of audit procedures performed, relevant audit evidence obtained and conclusions reached. It must let an experienced auditor with no previous connection with the audit understand the work, the evidence and the significant judgements; it is prepared on a timely basis, assembled into the final file ordinarily within 60 days of the report, kept for at least seven years, and is the property of the auditor.
:::

## How it works

### 1. Scope, definition and objective

SA 230, "Audit Documentation", deals with the auditor's responsibility to prepare audit documentation for an audit of financial statements; it is adapted as necessary when applied to audits of other historical financial information. The specific documentation requirements of other SAs do not limit the application of this SA, and laws or regulations may establish additional requirements (SM 6.3).

::: definition Audit documentation
The record of audit procedures performed, relevant audit evidence obtained, and conclusions the auditor reached (terms such as "working papers" or "work papers" are also sometimes used) (SM 6.4).
:::

The objective of the auditor is to prepare documentation that provides (SM 6.4):

- (a) ==a sufficient and appropriate record of the basis for the auditor's report==; and
- (b) evidence that the audit was planned and performed in accordance with SAs and applicable legal and regulatory requirements.

**Nature.** Audit documentation provides (a) evidence of the auditor's basis for a conclusion about the achievement of the overall objectives of the auditor, and (b) evidence that the audit was planned and performed in accordance with SAs and applicable legal and regulatory requirements (SM 6.4–6.5).

### 2. Six purposes

Audit documentation serves the following purposes (SM 6.5):

1. Assisting the engagement team to plan and perform the audit.
2. Assisting members of the engagement team to direct and supervise the audit work, and to discharge their review responsibilities.
3. Enabling the engagement team to be accountable for its work.
4. Retaining a record of matters of continuing significance to future audits.
5. Enabling the conduct of quality control reviews and inspections in accordance with SQC 1.
6. Enabling the conduct of external inspections in accordance with applicable legal, regulatory or other requirements.

### 3. Form, content and extent: the experienced auditor test

The auditor shall prepare audit documentation that is sufficient to enable **an experienced auditor, having no previous connection with the audit**, to understand (SM 6.5–6.6):

- (a) the nature, timing and extent of the audit procedures performed;
- (b) the results of the audit procedures performed and the audit evidence obtained; and
- (c) significant matters arising during the audit, the conclusions reached thereon, and significant professional judgements made in reaching those conclusions.

In documenting the nature, timing and extent of audit procedures performed, the auditor shall record (SM 6.6):

- (a) the identifying characteristics of the specific items or matters tested;
- (b) who performed the audit work and the date such work was completed; and
- (c) who reviewed the audit work performed and the date and extent of such review.

The auditor shall also document **discussions of significant matters** with management, those charged with governance and others, including the nature of the significant matters discussed and when and with whom the discussions took place. If the auditor identified information that is **inconsistent with the auditor's final conclusion** regarding a significant matter, the auditor shall document how the auditor addressed the inconsistency (SM 6.6).

The form, content and extent of audit documentation depend on factors such as (SM 6.6):

1. The size and complexity of the entity.
2. The nature of the audit procedures to be performed.
3. The identified risks of material misstatement.
4. The significance of the audit evidence obtained.
5. The nature and extent of exceptions identified.
6. The need to document a conclusion or the basis for a conclusion not readily determinable from the documentation of the work performed or audit evidence obtained.
7. The audit methodology and tools used.

### 4. What goes in, and what can stay out

Audit documentation may be recorded **on paper or on electronic or other media** (SM 6.6).

The auditor may include copies of the entity's records (for example, significant and specific contracts and agreements) as part of audit documentation, but ==audit documentation is not a substitute for the entity's accounting records== (SM 6.7).

### 5. Timely preparation

The auditor shall prepare audit documentation **on a timely basis**. Preparing sufficient and appropriate documentation on a timely basis helps to enhance the quality of the audit and facilitates the effective review and evaluation of the audit evidence obtained and conclusions reached before the auditor's report is finalised. Documentation prepared after the audit work has been performed is likely to be less accurate than documentation prepared at the time such work is performed (SM 6.8).

### 6. The audit file and its final assembly

::: definition Audit file
One or more folders or other storage media, in physical or electronic form, containing the records that comprise the audit documentation for a specific engagement (SM 6.8).
:::

The auditor shall assemble the audit documentation in an audit file and complete the administrative process of assembling the final audit file on a timely basis after the date of the auditor's report (SM 6.9):

- SQC 1 requires firms to establish policies and procedures for the timely completion of the assembly of audit files.
- An appropriate time limit for completing the assembly is ordinarily **not more than 60 days after the date of the auditor's report**. Completing the assembly after that date is an **administrative process** that does not involve the performance of new audit procedures or the drawing of new conclusions.
- Changes may be made to the documentation during the final assembly process **if they are administrative in nature**, for example: deleting or discarding superseded documentation; sorting, collating and cross-referencing working papers; signing off on completion checklists relating to the file assembly process; documenting audit evidence that the auditor has obtained, discussed and agreed with the relevant members of the engagement team **before the date of the auditor's report**.
- After the assembly of the final audit file has been completed, the auditor shall **not delete or discard** audit documentation of any nature before the end of its retention period.
- SQC 1 requires firms to establish policies and procedures for the retention of engagement documentation. The retention period for audit engagements ordinarily is **no shorter than seven years** from the date of the auditor's report or, if later, the date of the group auditor's report.

### 7. Significant matters and significant professional judgements

Judging the significance of a matter requires an objective analysis of the facts and circumstances. Examples of **significant matters** include (SM 6.10):

- Matters that give rise to significant risks.
- Results of audit procedures indicating (a) that the financial statements could be materially misstated, or (b) a need to revise the auditor's previous assessment of the risks of material misstatement and the auditor's responses to those risks.
- Circumstances that cause the auditor significant difficulty in applying necessary audit procedures.
- Findings that could result in a modification to the audit opinion or the inclusion of an Emphasis of Matter paragraph in the auditor's report.

An important factor in determining the form, content and extent of documentation of significant matters is **the extent of professional judgement exercised** in performing the work and evaluating the results. Documentation of significant professional judgements serves to explain the auditor's conclusions and to reinforce the quality of the judgement; such matters are of particular interest to those reviewing the documentation, including those carrying out subsequent audits when reviewing matters of continuing significance (for example, a retrospective review of accounting estimates) (SM 6.10).

It is appropriate to document the use of professional judgement, where the matters and judgements are significant, in circumstances such as (SM 6.10–6.11):

- the rationale for the auditor's conclusion when a requirement provides that the auditor "shall consider" certain information or factors, and that consideration is significant in the context of the engagement;
- the basis for the auditor's conclusion on the reasonableness of areas of subjective judgements (for example, the reasonableness of significant accounting estimates);
- the basis for the auditor's conclusions about the authenticity of a document when further investigation (such as using an expert or confirmation procedures) is undertaken in response to conditions that caused the auditor to believe the document may not be authentic.

### 8. The completion memorandum

The auditor may consider it helpful to prepare and retain, as part of the audit documentation, a summary (sometimes known as a **completion memorandum**) that describes the significant matters identified during the audit and how they were addressed. Such a summary may facilitate effective and efficient review and inspection of the audit documentation, particularly for large and complex audits; may assist the auditor's consideration of the significant matters; and may help the auditor consider whether there is any individual relevant SA objective that the auditor cannot achieve that would prevent the auditor from achieving the overall objectives of the auditor (SM 6.11).

### 9. Who owns the file

SQC 1 provides that, unless otherwise specified by law or regulation, **audit documentation is the property of the auditor**. The auditor may, at his discretion, make portions of, or extracts from, audit documentation available to clients, provided such disclosure does not undermine the validity of the work performed or, in the case of assurance engagements, the independence of the auditor or of his personnel (SM 6.11).

## Formats and formulas

### What every working paper must let a reader see

*Build each working paper so that these items are visible on its face (SM 6.6).*

| Item on the working paper | SA 230 requirement it meets |
|---|---|
| Items tested, by identifying characteristic | Identifying characteristics of the items or matters tested |
| Prepared by, and date completed | Who performed the work and the date it was completed |
| Reviewed by, date, extent of review | Who reviewed it, and the date and extent of review |
| Results, evidence, conclusion | Results of procedures, evidence obtained, conclusions |
| Discussions: what, when, with whom | Discussions of significant matters |

### The two numbers

::: formula The assembly deadline and the retention period
Assembly of final file complete: ordinarily not more than **60 days** after the date of the auditor's report.

Retention: ordinarily no shorter than **7 years** from the date of the auditor's report or, if later, the group auditor's report.
:::

Worked dates for a report dated 22 May 2026: assembly ordinarily by **21 July 2026** (60 days: 9 days of May, 30 of June, 21 of July); retention at least until **22 May 2033**.

## Worked examples

:::: example Assembly week at Iyer & Associates
Iyer & Associates signed the auditor's report on Tapri Tea Co. Ltd's 2025-26 financial statements on 22 May 2026. On 2 July 2026 the team assembles the final audit file and proposes to:

(a) cross-reference the receivables working papers to the lead schedule;

(b) discard three superseded drafts of the inventory note;

(c) add a memo recording a distributor's balance confirmation that was received on 19 May 2026 and discussed and agreed with Lakshmi on 20 May 2026;

(d) send a fresh confirmation request to a distributor who never replied, and file the reply with a new conclusion on that balance;

(e) sign off the file-assembly checklist.

The assembly is completed on 3 July 2026. On 15 September 2026, Kesar asks the firm to delete from the file the working paper recording a discussion about the old elaichi premix stock, "because it embarrasses us".

Advise Iyer & Associates on each proposal and on Kesar's request, with reference to SA 230.

::: answer Model answer
**Relevant provision.** As per SA 230, the auditor shall assemble the audit documentation in an audit file and complete the administrative process of assembling the final audit file on a timely basis after the date of the auditor's report, ordinarily not more than 60 days after that date. Assembly is an administrative process that does not involve the performance of new audit procedures or the drawing of new conclusions. Administrative changes may be made during assembly, such as deleting or discarding superseded documentation; sorting, collating and cross-referencing working papers; signing off on completion checklists relating to the file assembly process; and documenting audit evidence that the auditor has obtained, discussed and agreed with the relevant members of the engagement team before the date of the auditor's report. After assembly is complete, the auditor shall not delete or discard audit documentation of any nature before the end of its retention period, ordinarily no shorter than seven years from the date of the auditor's report.

**Facts and analysis.** 2 July 2026 is the 41st day after the report date of 22 May 2026, within the ordinary 60-day limit (21 July 2026).

| Proposal | Allowed? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Cross-referencing | Yes | Administrative |
| (b) Discarding superseded drafts | Yes | Administrative |
| (c) Memo on the 19 May confirmation | Yes | Evidence obtained, discussed and agreed before the report date |
| (d) New confirmation and conclusion | No | A new audit procedure and a new conclusion, not part of assembly |
| (e) Signing off the assembly checklist | Yes | Administrative |

**Kesar's request.** The assembly was completed on 3 July 2026. The documentation cannot be deleted before the end of the retention period, at least until 22 May 2033. Moreover, the documentation is the property of the auditor, not of the client.

**Conclusion.** Iyer & Associates may carry out (a), (b), (c) and (e); completing the assembly on 3 July 2026 is within the ordinary limit of 21 July 2026. They should not carry out (d) as part of the assembly, and they should decline Kesar's request.
:::

:::: example Five pages from the Tapri file
Before signing the 2025-26 report, Lakshmi reviews five items in the Tapri audit file:

(a) A balance confirmation from Malwa Distributors, received through Tapri's sales office, agrees with the book balance of ₹18.60 lakh but is on a letterhead whose address differs from the distributor's registered address. The team sent a fresh request directly to the registered address and asked a forensic expert to examine the first reply.

(b) Tests of controls over cash sales at six cafés found deviations in 11 of the 60 items tested. The team revised its assessment of the risks of material misstatement for café cash sales and extended its substantive procedures.

(c) The provision of ₹1.90 crore for expired premix stock, a significant accounting estimate, rests on management's assumption that 30% of the stock will be sold in the festival season.

(d) Tapri's legal counsel replied that an outflow on a food-safety show-cause notice is probable; Dev's note to the audit team calls it remote. The team's final conclusion follows counsel's reply.

(e) Bunty's notes from the first week, guessing that "the samosa factory must be overvalued" (later found baseless), and four photocopies of the same lease deed of the Dewas factory.

> **Bunty:** Ma'am, can I at least throw away my first-week notes? They are embarrassing.
> **Lakshmi:** Embarrassing is not the test, Bunty. SA 230 is.

Advise Lakshmi, with reference to SA 230, what the file must contain for each item, and what may be left out.

::: answer Model answer
**Relevant provision.** As per SA 230, the auditor shall prepare audit documentation sufficient to enable an experienced auditor, having no previous connection with the audit, to understand the nature, timing and extent of the procedures performed, their results and the audit evidence obtained, and significant matters arising during the audit, the conclusions reached thereon and the significant professional judgements made in reaching them. Examples of significant matters include results of audit procedures indicating a need to revise the auditor's previous assessment of the risks of material misstatement and the auditor's responses. It is appropriate to document professional judgement, where significant, including the basis for the auditor's conclusion on the reasonableness of significant accounting estimates, and the basis for the auditor's conclusions about the authenticity of a document when further investigation, such as using an expert or confirmation procedures, is undertaken. If the auditor identified information inconsistent with the final conclusion on a significant matter, the auditor shall document how the inconsistency was addressed. The auditor need not include notes that reflect incomplete or preliminary thinking, or duplicates of documents.

**Facts and analysis.**

| Item | What the file must show |
|---|---|
| (a) Doubtful confirmation | The basis for the conclusion on the reply's authenticity: the fresh request, the expert's examination, their results and the conclusion |
| (b) Control deviations | A significant matter: the deviations found, the revised risk assessment and the extended procedures with their results |
| (c) Premix provision | A significant judgement: the basis for concluding that the ₹1.90 crore estimate, and its 30% assumption, is reasonable |
| (d) Counsel and Dev | How the inconsistency between Dev's note and the final conclusion was addressed |
| (e) Notes and copies | May be left out: preliminary notes and duplicates; one copy of the lease may be kept as a significant contract |

**Conclusion.** For (a) to (d) the file must also record the identifying characteristics of the items tested, who performed the work and when, and who reviewed it, when and to what extent; any discussion of these matters with management is documented with its nature, date and participants. Bunty's notes and the three extra copies of the lease need not be kept. A completion memorandum may summarise items (a) to (d) and how they were addressed.

## Under the hood

### Where SA 230 meets other Standards

- **Other SAs add their own documentation.** SA 300 requires the overall audit strategy, the audit plan and significant changes with reasons to be documented ([[aud.plan.sa300|SA 300]]); SA 450 requires the clearly trivial threshold, all misstatements accumulated and whether corrected, and the conclusion on uncorrected misstatements (SM 7.27; [[aud.complete.sa450|SA 450]]). These do not limit SA 230 (SM 6.3).
- **Significant judgements.** Documenting significant professional judgements explains the auditor's conclusions and reinforces the quality of the judgement ([[aud.nature.sa200|SA 200]]).
- **The audit programme** is the first of ICAI's examples of audit documentation (SM 6.7; [[aud.plan.program|the audit programme]]).
- **Quality control.** Assembly deadlines, retention and ownership come from SQC 1's policies ([[aud.ethics.qc|SQC 1 and SA 220]]).

### What the Study Material's own exercises show

In its case study (SM 6.13–6.15): when work assigned in the audit programme to one member is performed by another, the programme should be updated or a note made in the working papers; materiality is documented together with the factors considered in determining it; documentation of risk assessment covers the team's discussion, the procedures and the understanding of internal control, but not a "precise calculation" of the risk; and reports showing that the stock conforms to quality-control standards under law are not an essential part of the documentation of the inventory count.

## Answer frame

### A case question on the audit file

1. **Provision**: SA 230 (and SQC 1) in its words: timely preparation; assembly as an administrative process ordinarily within 60 days of the report; administrative changes only; no deletion after assembly; retention at least seven years; property of the auditor.
2. **Facts with dates**: count the days from the date of the auditor's report.
3. **Analysis**: limb by limb, allowed or not and why.
4. **Conclusion**: "Thus, the action of CA … is not valid / is in order."

### "State the matters the documentation must enable an experienced auditor to understand"

| Must understand (three) | Must record for nature, timing and extent (three) |
|---|---|
| Nature, timing and extent of procedures performed | Identifying characteristics of items tested |
| Results of procedures and evidence obtained | Who performed the work, and the date completed |
| Significant matters, conclusions and significant judgements | Who reviewed it, and the date and extent of review |

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: the memo dated today, about last May
Writing up missing work months after the report and putting the current date on it, or filing it as if done before. **Fix:** documentation is prepared on a timely basis; assembly after the report date is administrative only and draws no new conclusions. Back-filling papers puts a question mark on the quality of the whole audit.
:::

::: mistake "The 60 days are for preparing the documentation"
**Fix:** documentation is prepared during the audit, on a timely basis. The 60 days are only for the administrative assembly of the final file after the report date (RTP May 2024 Q14 turns on this).
:::

::: mistake Keeping every draft "to be safe"
**Fix:** superseded drafts of working papers and financial statements, notes of preliminary thinking, copies corrected for typing errors and duplicates need not be included; the final versions and the significant matters must be.
:::

::: mistake "The client paid for it, so the file is the client's"
**Fix:** unless law or regulation says otherwise, audit documentation is the property of the auditor. Extracts may be given at the auditor's discretion, if that does not undermine the validity of the work or the auditor's independence.
:::

## Recap

- Audit documentation: the record of audit procedures performed, relevant audit evidence obtained, and conclusions reached ("working papers").
- Objective: a sufficient and appropriate record of the basis for the report, and evidence that the audit was planned and performed in accordance with SAs and law.
- Six purposes: plan and perform; direct, supervise and review; accountability; matters of continuing significance; SQC 1 reviews; external inspections.
- The experienced auditor test: nature, timing and extent; results and evidence; significant matters, conclusions and judgements. Record items tested, who did it and when, who reviewed it, when and how far; discussions of significant matters; how inconsistencies were addressed.
- Paper or electronic; not a substitute for the entity's records; drafts, preliminary notes, corrected copies and duplicates need not be kept.
- Prepare on a timely basis; assemble the final file ordinarily within 60 days of the report (administrative changes only); no deletion afterwards; retain at least 7 years from the report date (or the group auditor's report, if later).
- Completion memorandum: significant matters and how they were addressed. The file is the auditor's property; extracts at discretion. (The case sheet stays with the hospital.)

A:: Assisting the team to plan and perform the audit; to direct, supervise and review; accountability of the team; record of matters of continuing significance; quality control reviews under SQC 1; external inspections.

Q:: SA 230: documentation must enable an experienced auditor with no previous connection with the audit to understand what?
A:: The nature, timing and extent of procedures performed; the results and the evidence obtained; significant matters, the conclusions reached and significant professional judgements made.

Q:: SA 230: what must be recorded when documenting the nature, timing and extent of procedures?
A:: The identifying characteristics of the items tested; who performed the work and the date completed; who reviewed it and the date and extent of the review.

Q:: SA 230: on what factors do the form, content and extent of documentation depend?
A:: Size and complexity of the entity; nature of procedures; identified risks of material misstatement; significance of evidence; nature and extent of exceptions; need to document a conclusion not readily determinable; audit methodology and tools.

Q:: SA 230: what need not be included in audit documentation?
A:: Superseded drafts of working papers and financial statements; notes reflecting incomplete or preliminary thinking; previous copies corrected for typographical or other errors; duplicates of documents.

Q:: SA 230 / SQC 1: time limit for assembling the final audit file?
A:: Ordinarily not more than 60 days after the date of the auditor's report; assembly is an administrative process with no new procedures or conclusions.

Q:: SA 230: give examples of administrative changes allowed during final assembly.
A:: Deleting or discarding superseded documentation; sorting, collating and cross-referencing; signing off file-assembly checklists; documenting evidence obtained, discussed and agreed with the team before the report date.

Q:: SQC 1: retention period for audit documentation?
A:: Ordinarily no shorter than seven years from the date of the auditor's report or, if later, the date of the group auditor's report.

Q:: SA 230: what is a completion memorandum?
A:: A summary describing the significant matters identified during the audit and how they were addressed; it facilitates review and inspection, especially of large and complex audits.

Q:: Who owns audit documentation?
A:: Unless law or regulation specifies otherwise, the auditor (SQC 1); extracts may be given to clients at the auditor's discretion if validity of the work and independence are not undermined.
