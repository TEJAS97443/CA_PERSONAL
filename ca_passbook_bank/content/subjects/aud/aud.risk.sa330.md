## In plain words

### The risk map is ready. Now what?

Lakshmi's team has finished the SA 315 work on Tapri Tea Co. Ltd: a list of what could go wrong, where, and how badly.

> **Bunty:** Ma'am, so now we just test everything harder?
> **Lakshmi:** Harder where it is risky, Bunty. Lighter where it is not. And never zero.
> **Bunty:** What if their controls are excellent?
> **Lakshmi:** Then prove it, if you want to lean on them. Excellence on a slide is not audit evidence.

**SA 330** is about the **response**. If a risk spreads across the whole set of accounts (a management under pressure to show profit, a weak control environment), the auditor responds **overall**, at the level of the whole audit: for example, who is put on the team and how closely their work is directed and supervised. If a risk sits in a particular assertion (Diwali sales recorded early, slow stock valued at cost), the auditor designs **further audit procedures** for it, choosing their **nature** (which test), **timing** (when) and **extent** (how much).

Further audit procedures come in two kinds. **Tests of controls** check whether the company's controls actually worked throughout the year; they are needed when the auditor wants to rely on those controls, or when there is no other way to get enough evidence. **Substantive procedures** look for misstatements directly, either by checking details (vouching transactions, verifying balances) or by analytical comparison. Some substantive work is required for every material item, whatever the risk. The rule running through all of it: ==the higher the assessed risk, the more persuasive the evidence must be==.

::: analogy Weather forecast, then packing
A cyclone warning for the whole city changes everything: you postpone the trip and send your most experienced driver. That is an overall response. Showers forecast for one suburb at 4 p.m. need an umbrella there at 4 p.m.: the right tool (nature), at the right time (timing), in the right quantity (extent). And if you plan to rely on the hotel's generator, you check that it actually starts, not just that the brochure mentions it.
:::

::: key
SA 330: respond to financial statement level risks with overall responses, and to assertion level risks with further audit procedures whose nature, timing and extent are based on the assessed risk. Test controls when relying on them or when substantive procedures alone cannot give sufficient appropriate evidence; perform substantive procedures for every material class of transactions, account balance and disclosure regardless; and obtain more persuasive evidence the higher the risk.
:::

## How it works

### 1. What SA 330 deals with, and its objective

SA 330, "The Auditor's Responses to Assessed Risks", deals with the auditor's responsibility to design and implement responses to the risks of material misstatement identified and assessed in accordance with [[aud.risk.sa315|SA 315]] in a financial statement audit (SM 3.65).

::: definition Objective of the auditor (SA 330)
To obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence about the assessed risks of material misstatement, through designing and implementing appropriate responses to those risks (SM 3.66).
:::

### 2. Two levels of response

SA 330 states that (SM 3.66):

- (a) the auditor shall design and implement **overall responses** to address the assessed risks of material misstatement at the **financial statement level**;
- (b) the auditor shall design and perform **further audit procedures** whose nature, timing and extent are based on and are responsive to the assessed risks of material misstatement at the **assertion level**.

### 3. Designing further audit procedures

In designing the further audit procedures to be performed, the auditor shall (SM 3.66):

- (a) consider the reasons for the assessment given to the risk of material misstatement at the assertion level for each class of transactions, account balance and disclosure, including:
  - (i) the likelihood of material misstatement due to the particular characteristics of the class, balance or disclosure (the **inherent risk**); and
  - (ii) whether the risk assessment takes account of relevant controls (the **control risk**), thereby requiring evidence that the controls are operating effectively (that is, the auditor intends to rely on them in determining the nature, timing and extent of substantive procedures); and
- (b) **obtain more persuasive audit evidence the higher the auditor's assessment of risk**.

### 4. Tests of controls: when they are compulsory

The auditor shall design and perform tests of controls to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence as to the operating effectiveness of relevant controls **when** (SM 3.66):

- (a) the auditor's assessment of risks of material misstatement at the assertion level includes an expectation that the controls are operating effectively (that is, the auditor **intends to rely** on their operating effectiveness in determining the nature, timing and extent of substantive procedures); **or**
- (b) **substantive procedures alone cannot provide sufficient appropriate audit evidence** at the assertion level.

In designing and performing tests of controls, the auditor shall obtain **more persuasive audit evidence the greater the reliance** placed on the effectiveness of a control. A **higher level of assurance** may be sought when the approach consists primarily of tests of controls, in particular where it is not possible or practicable to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence only from substantive procedures (SM 3.67).

### 5. Nature and extent of tests of controls

In designing and performing tests of controls, the auditor shall (SM 3.67):

- (a) perform other audit procedures **in combination with inquiry** to obtain evidence about operating effectiveness, including: (i) how the controls were applied at relevant times during the period; (ii) the consistency with which they were applied; and (iii) by whom or by what means they were applied;
- (b) determine whether the controls to be tested depend upon other controls (**indirect controls**) and, if so, whether evidence about the effective operation of those indirect controls is needed.

**Inquiry alone is not sufficient** to test operating effectiveness. Inquiry combined with **inspection or reperformance** may provide more assurance than inquiry and **observation**, since an observation is pertinent only at the point in time at which it is made. The nature of the control decides the procedure: if effectiveness is evidenced by documentation, the auditor may inspect it. When more persuasive evidence is needed, the extent of testing may be increased (SM 3.67).

Matters the auditor may consider in determining the **extent** of tests of controls (SM 3.68):

1. The frequency of the performance of the control by the entity during the period.
2. The length of time during the audit period that the auditor is relying on the control.
3. The expected rate of deviation from a control.
4. The relevance and reliability of the evidence to be obtained regarding the control's operating effectiveness at the assertion level.
5. The extent to which audit evidence is obtained from tests of other controls related to the assertion.

### 6. Timing, and evidence from earlier audits

The auditor shall test controls for the particular time, or throughout the period, for which the auditor intends to rely on them. Evidence at a point in time may be enough, for example for controls over the entity's physical inventory counting at the period end; reliance over a period needs tests that show the control operated at relevant times during that period, which may include tests of the entity's monitoring of controls (SM 3.68).

To decide whether to use evidence about operating effectiveness from **previous audits**, and how long before retesting, the auditor shall consider (SM 3.68–3.69):

- (a) the effectiveness of other elements of internal control, including the control environment, the entity's monitoring of controls and its risk assessment process;
- (b) the risks arising from the characteristics of the control, including whether it is manual or automated;
- (c) the effectiveness of general IT controls;
- (d) the effectiveness of the control and its application, including deviations noted in previous audits and personnel changes that significantly affect its application;
- (e) whether the lack of a change in a particular control poses a risk due to changing circumstances; and
- (f) the risks of material misstatement and the extent of reliance on the control.

The auditor shall establish the **continuing relevance** of that evidence by obtaining evidence about whether significant changes in those controls have occurred since the previous audit (SM 3.69).

### 7. Evaluating operating effectiveness, and deviations

The auditor shall evaluate whether misstatements detected by substantive procedures indicate that controls are not operating effectively. ==The absence of misstatements detected by substantive procedures does not provide audit evidence that controls related to the assertion are effective.== A material misstatement detected by the auditor's procedures is a strong indicator of a significant deficiency in internal control (SM 3.69).

When **deviations** from controls the auditor intends to rely on are detected, the auditor shall make specific inquiries to understand them and their potential consequences, and shall determine whether (SM 3.69–3.70):

- (a) the tests of controls performed provide an appropriate basis for reliance on the controls;
- (b) additional tests of controls are necessary; or
- (c) the potential risks of misstatement need to be addressed using substantive procedures.

### 8. Substantive procedures: never skipped

**Irrespective of the assessed risks of material misstatement, the auditor shall design and perform substantive procedures for each material class of transactions, account balance, and disclosure.** This reflects that (i) the auditor's assessment of risk is judgmental and may not identify all risks of material misstatement; and (ii) there are inherent limitations to internal control, including management override (SM 3.70).

::: definition Substantive procedures
Audit procedures designed to detect material misstatements at the assertion level. They comprise (i) tests of details (of classes of transactions, account balances and disclosures) and (ii) substantive analytical procedures (SM 3.70).
:::

**Tests of details** are tests of transactions (**vouching**) and tests of balances (**verification**). A purchase may be vouched with the purchase invoice, goods received note and inward gate entry register, establishing the authenticity of recorded transactions. Verifying a fixed asset establishes its existence at the balance sheet date, for example by reviewing the entity's plan for physical verification and obtaining evidence that management performed it (SM 3.70).

**Substantive analytical procedures** are analytical procedures (evaluations of financial information through analysis of plausible relationships among financial and non-financial data, with investigation of inconsistent fluctuations) used as substantive procedures. Widely recognised ratios (such as profit margins for types of retail entities) can support the reasonableness of recorded amounts, and a prediction of total rental income from rates, units and vacancy rates can be persuasive enough to remove the need for tests of details. They are generally more applicable to **large volumes of transactions that tend to be predictable over time** (SM 3.71). How to perform them is in [[aud.evid.sa520|SA 520: analytical procedures]].

Depending on the circumstances, the auditor may determine that (SM 3.71):

- performing **only substantive analytical procedures** will be sufficient to reduce audit risk to an acceptably low level (for example, where the risk assessment is supported by evidence from tests of controls);
- **only tests of details** are appropriate; or
- **a combination** of the two is most responsive to the assessed risks.

Because the risk assessment takes account of internal control, the extent of substantive procedures may need to be **increased when the results of tests of controls are unsatisfactory**. The extent of tests of details is ordinarily thought of as the sample size, though other selective means of testing may be more effective (SM 3.71).

## Worked examples

:::: example Tapri Express: 31 lakh orders and no paper
Tapri's delivery app processed about 31 lakh orders in 2025-26, worth ₹84 crore. Orders, prices, cashbacks and payment-gateway settlements are captured and calculated by the app with no paper documents; the app also reconciles gateway receipts with orders every night. Iyer & Associates assess the risk of material misstatement for the occurrence and accuracy of app revenue as high, and conclude that substantive procedures alone cannot provide sufficient appropriate audit evidence.

Required:

(i) Must the auditor test the app's controls? Give reasons.

(ii) What level of evidence and assurance should the auditor seek from those tests?

(iii) Bunty proposes to skip substantive procedures on app revenue "since the controls will be tested". Comment.

::: answer Model answer
**(i)** As per SA 330, the auditor shall design and perform tests of controls to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence as to the operating effectiveness of relevant controls when (a) the assessment of risks at the assertion level includes an expectation that controls are operating effectively, that is, the auditor intends to rely on them in determining the nature, timing and extent of substantive procedures; or (b) substantive procedures alone cannot provide sufficient appropriate audit evidence at the assertion level. In the given case, the high volume and automated, paperless processing mean that substantive procedures alone cannot provide sufficient appropriate evidence. Tests of controls over the app (such as the automated price and cashback calculations and the nightly gateway reconciliation) are therefore **required**.

**(ii)** In designing and performing tests of controls, the auditor shall obtain more persuasive audit evidence the greater the reliance placed on the effectiveness of a control. Since the approach consists primarily of tests of controls, because sufficient appropriate evidence cannot be obtained only from substantive procedures, **a higher level of assurance** about operating effectiveness may be sought, and is appropriate here. The tests must cover how, how consistently and by whom or by what means the controls were applied throughout the period, and inquiry alone will not do.

**(iii)** Not correct. Irrespective of the assessed risks, the auditor shall design and perform substantive procedures for each material class of transactions, account balance and disclosure, because the risk assessment is judgmental and internal control has inherent limitations, including management override. App revenue of ₹84 crore is material, so substantive procedures (for example, substantive analytical procedures on order volumes and average values, and tests of details of gateway settlements) must still be performed.
:::

:::: example Seven missing signatures
Tapri's policy requires the purchase manager's approval on every purchase order. Iyer & Associates intend to rely on this control. Of 60 purchase orders tested across the year, 7 have no approval, all from August and September, when the purchase manager had resigned and the post was vacant. Separately, substantive tests of 40 purchase invoices found no misstatement. Bunty concludes: "No misstatements, so the approval control is effective. We can rely on it." Comment and advise the auditor.

::: answer Model answer
**Relevant provision.** As per SA 330, when deviations from controls upon which the auditor intends to rely are detected, the auditor shall make specific inquiries to understand these matters and their potential consequences, and shall determine whether (a) the tests of controls performed provide an appropriate basis for reliance; (b) additional tests of controls are necessary; or (c) the potential risks of misstatement need to be addressed using substantive procedures. Further, the absence of misstatements detected by substantive procedures does not provide audit evidence that controls related to the assertion being tested are effective.

**Facts of the case.** 7 of 60 orders (11.67%) lack approval; all fall in the two months when the post was vacant.

**Analysis.** Deviations may be caused by changes in key personnel; when they are detected, the auditor makes specific inquiries, particularly about the timing of staff changes in key internal control functions, and ensures that the tests of controls appropriately cover that period (SM 3.53). Bunty's conclusion is not correct: clean substantive results do not prove that the control worked.

**Conclusion.** The auditor should inquire into the vacancy and how purchases were authorised in August and September; decide whether the control can still be relied on for the other ten months, whether additional tests of controls are needed, and, for the two months at least, address the risk with substantive procedures (for example, larger samples of purchases from that period vouched to goods received notes and invoices). Where the deviations show that the assessed level of control risk needs revision, the nature, timing and extent of planned substantive procedures are modified (SM 3.53).
:::

:::: example Kiosk rent by prediction
Tapri lets 48 kiosks at metro stations to franchisees at ₹30,000 a month each. Leasing records show average occupancy of 90% for the year. Recorded rental income is ₹1,63,80,000. Lakshmi considers a difference of up to ₹4,00,000 acceptable without investigation.

Required:

(i) Name the procedure and compute the expected rental income.

(ii) What should Lakshmi do next?

::: answer Show the working
**(i)** This is a **substantive analytical procedure**: the auditor predicts the recorded amount from a plausible relationship between financial and non-financial data (rates, number of units, occupancy), as in the Study Material's rental income example.

| Working | Amount (₹) |
|---|---|
| Rent per kiosk per year (30,000 × 12) | 3,60,000 |
| Full occupancy (48 kiosks × 3,60,000) | 1,72,80,000 |
| **Expected rent at 90% occupancy (1,72,80,000 × 90%)** | **1,55,52,000** |
| Recorded rental income | 1,63,80,000 |
| **Difference (recorded − expected)** | **8,28,000** |

**(ii)** The difference of ₹8,28,000 (5.3% of the expectation) exceeds the ₹4,00,000 Lakshmi can accept, so it must be investigated: inquire of management and obtain appropriate evidence for its explanation (for example, rent escalations or income from extra kiosks), and perform other audit procedures where the explanation is inadequate, such as tests of details of rent agreements and receipts (SM 4.114). Until the difference is explained by appropriate evidence, the expectation does not corroborate the recorded amount.
:::

## Under the hood

### Overall responses

The Study Material requires overall responses for financial statement level risks but gives no list of them (SM 3.66). The nearest it comes is in planning under [[aud.plan.sa300|SA 300]]: planning assists in selecting engagement team members with appropriate levels of capabilities and competence to respond to anticipated risks, and facilitates their direction and supervision and the review of their work (SM 2.4). In MTP September 2026 Series II, decisions of this kind (deploying IT specialists, assigning experienced personnel to revenue and consolidation) are treated as part of the overall audit strategy. In an answer, keep to the Standard's words: "overall responses to address the assessed risks of material misstatement at the financial statement level".

### The persuasiveness rule, in analytical procedures

Because SA 330 requires more persuasive evidence the higher the assessed risk, the difference the auditor accepts without investigation in a substantive analytical procedure falls as the risk rises (SM 4.114). And when significant risks are identified, evidence obtained solely from substantive analytical procedures is unlikely to be sufficient (SM 4.107).

### Has enough evidence been obtained?

Concluding whether sufficient appropriate audit evidence has been obtained to reduce audit risk to an acceptably low level is a matter of professional judgment, required by SA 330 (SM 4.12); see [[aud.evid.sa500|SA 500]].

### Where testing techniques live

Inspection, inquiry and observation, and reperformance as tests of controls (the September 2024 exam asked them in Q2(a)) are in [[aud.risk.testing|testing internal control]]; sample sizes for tests of controls and tests of details are in [[aud.evid.sa530|SA 530]].

::: trick Rely or can't-do-without
Tests of controls are compulsory in exactly two cases: you **rely** on the control, or you **can't do without** it (substantive procedures alone cannot give sufficient appropriate evidence). Substantive procedures are compulsory in one case: **always**, for every material class, balance and disclosure. Condition: "compulsory" here means required by SA 330; the auditor may still choose to test other controls.
:::

## Answer frame

### "When shall the auditor perform tests of controls?" in a case

1. **Provision**: the two circumstances (reliance intended; substantive procedures alone insufficient), and "more persuasive evidence the greater the reliance".
2. **Facts**: why the case falls in one of them (high volume, automation, no documents; or planned reliance).
3. **Analysis**: what the tests must cover (how, how consistently, by whom or by what means; throughout the period of reliance; inquiry not alone), and the higher assurance where the approach is mainly tests of controls.
4. **Conclusion**: tests of controls required, and substantive procedures still required for each material item.

### "Distinguish tests of controls from substantive procedures"

| Point | Tests of controls | Substantive procedures |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Evaluate the operating effectiveness of controls in preventing, or detecting and correcting, material misstatements | Detect material misstatements at the assertion level |
| When required | When relying on controls, or when substantive procedures alone cannot give sufficient appropriate evidence | Always, for each material class of transactions, account balance and disclosure |
| Forms | Inquiry combined with inspection, observation or reperformance | Tests of details (vouching, verification) and substantive analytical procedures |
| Effect of results | Deviations may change reliance and the substantive plan | Misstatements found may indicate that controls are not effective |

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: "the controls are strong, so no substantive procedures"
**Fix:** substantive procedures are required for each material class of transactions, account balance and disclosure irrespective of the assessed risk. Strong controls may reduce their extent, never to nothing.
:::

::: mistake No misstatements found, so the controls work
**Fix:** the absence of misstatements detected by substantive procedures is not evidence that controls are effective (SM 3.69). Test the controls.
:::

::: mistake Testing a control by asking about it
**Fix:** inquiry alone is not sufficient to test operating effectiveness; combine it with inspection or reperformance, which may give more assurance than inquiry and observation, since an observation is pertinent only at the point in time at which it is made (SM 3.67).
:::

::: mistake Writing "tests of controls are always compulsory"
**Fix:** they are required in two circumstances only. Outside them the auditor may choose not to test controls, but then the risk assessment cannot be reduced for the effective operation of controls (SM 4.58).
:::

## Recap

- Objective: sufficient appropriate audit evidence about the assessed risks, through appropriate responses.
- Financial statement level risks: overall responses. Assertion level risks: further audit procedures whose nature, timing and extent respond to the risk.
- Design: consider why the risk was assessed (inherent risk; control risk), and get more persuasive evidence the higher the risk.
- Tests of controls required when relying on controls, or when substantive procedures alone cannot give sufficient appropriate evidence; more persuasive evidence the greater the reliance; inquiry alone never enough. (Check that the generator starts before you rely on it.)
- Extent of tests of controls: frequency, period of reliance, expected deviation, relevance and reliability, evidence from other controls.
- Deviations: specific inquiries, then decide reliance, more tests of controls, or substantive procedures. Clean substantive results do not prove controls work.
- Substantive procedures for every material class, balance and disclosure, always: tests of details (vouching, verification) and substantive analytical procedures, alone or combined.

A:: Frequency of performance of the control; length of time of reliance; expected rate of deviation; relevance and reliability of the evidence; extent of evidence from tests of other related controls.
