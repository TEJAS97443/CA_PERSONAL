## In plain words

### The cash that was waiting in an envelope

On 31 March Bunty is sent to count the cash at Tapri's Indore café.

> **Bunty:** Ma'am, the café cash tallied to the rupee. The cashier even had it ready in an envelope.
> **Lakshmi:** Ready? Who told him we were coming?
> **Bunty:** I did. I phoned to make sure he would be there.
> **Lakshmi:** Then you counted a performance, Bunty. Cash is counted by surprise, and every till at the same time.

The kiln in Dewas will still be there next week. Tapri's **current assets** will not: ₹38 crore of chai premix, kulhads and frozen samosas is sold and replaced every few weeks, ₹64 crore of receivables from supermarkets and airlines is collected and re-billed, cash moves through 40 cafés and 22 bank accounts, and loans and advances (to franchisees, staff, the GST department) come and go. Assets that move are tested at a **point in time**, and wherever possible with **evidence from outsiders**: count it, confirm it, reconcile it.

::: analogy Checking your roommate's money
Your roommate says he has ₹2,000 in his wallet, ₹5,000 owed by three friends, a cupboard of snacks and ₹3,000 lent to his cousin. You count the wallet now, before he can borrow from the snack money (surprise, simultaneous count); you message the three friends yourself, not through him (direct confirmation); you open the cupboard and check the expiry dates (count, and value at no more than they will fetch); and you ask to see the cousin's IOU (the loan agreement and acknowledgement).
:::

::: key
Current assets are proved at the balance sheet date: **receivables and loans** by direct confirmation and a recoverability review, **cash** by a surprise count of all balances together and by bank reconciliations and bank confirmations, **inventories** by attending the count and valuing at the lower of cost and net realisable value, and each is **disclosed** as Schedule III requires.
:::

### 1. Trade receivables: "they owe us, really?"

Receivables (debtors) arise when an invoice is raised and unpaid; amounts not recoverable are written off as bad and charged to profit and loss (SM 5.37). The auditor first **tests the controls over sales**, considering these points (SM 5.37–5.38):

1. Only bona fide sales lead to trade receivables.
2. All such sales are made to approved customers.
3. All such sales are properly recorded in the books of accounts.
4. Once recorded, debtors can be settled only by receipt of cash or on the authority of a responsible official.
5. Segregation of duties at every point in the sales transaction (accounting for debtors, collecting the payments, sending reminders and so on).
6. Debtors are collected on time.
7. In case debtors are not collected in time, reminders are sent and legal action is taken if required.
8. Balances are regularly reviewed.
9. A proper system of follow-up exists and, if necessary, adequate provision for bad debts is made by preparing an adequate ageing schedule of the debtors.

**Existence** (SM 5.38–5.41):

- check whether controls ensure that invoices cannot be recorded more than once and that receivable balances are automatically recorded in the general ledger from the original invoice;
- ask for a period-end accounts receivable **ageing report** and trace the balance as per the report to the general ledger;
- check whether realisation is recorded invoice-wise; if not, check that money received from debtors is adjusted chronologically, invoice-wise and on a FIFO basis (the previous bill adjusted first); if realisation is made on account, verify whether the company has obtained confirmations from the debtors;
- if any large balance is due for a long time, ask for the reasons and justification;
- **direct confirmation**: contact customers directly and ask them to confirm the unpaid receivables at the period end; necessarily for all significant balances, while some random customers with smaller outstanding invoices are also selected;
- confirmations are sought with the entity's consent; if management asks the auditor not to confirm certain balances, consider whether there are valid grounds and, where appropriate, reconsider the nature, timing and extent of procedures and the planned reliance on management's representations;
- the date: (a) the balance sheet date, or (b) any other selected date reasonably close to it, decided by the auditor in consultation with the company; the form: (a) **with the balance** as per the company, the customer responding whether or not it agrees, or (b) **without any balance**, the customer responding with the balance as per its records; ==the use of the form without any balance is preferable==;
- the method of selecting the debtors to be circularised is not revealed to the company until the trial balance of the debtors' ledger is handed over; the selected list is given to the entity to prepare properly addressed request letters; the auditor maintains strict control over their correctness and proper despatch, and **confirmations and undelivered letters are returned to the auditor, not the client**;
- discrepancies revealed by the confirmations or by additional tests may bear on accounts outside the original sample: the company is asked to investigate and reconcile them;
- **no reply**: alternative procedures, such as agreeing the balance to cash received subsequently, or preparing a detailed analysis of the balance, ensuring it consists of identifiable transactions and confirming that those revenue transactions actually occurred;
- review related party receivables for collectability, proper authorisation and whether their value was reasonable and at arm's length; check that receivables for other than sales or services are not included in the list;
- analytical procedures: review a trend line of sales and receivables, or compare the two over time, for unusual trends, and measure the average collection period; enquire of management into changes in trends and document the answers in the working papers.

**Completeness and cut-off** (SM 5.41–5.43):

- **cut-off**: for invoices of the last few days (the last 5 days of the year) included in debtors, the goods were dispatched and are not lying with the company; on a test check basis, all goods dispatched before the year end are invoiced and included, and none dispatched after it;
- test invoices from the ageing report for correct amounts, customers and dates;
- match invoice dates to the shipping or dispatch log, including invoices issued after the period;
- examine **bill and hold** sales for whether a sale took place;
- review the receiving log for inordinately large customer returns after the period end (goods pushed out to inflate profit);
- review discounts and incentives against policy and industry trends;
- review credit memos during and after the period for authorisation and period, and the reason for any deduction against a bill.

**Valuation** (SM 5.43–5.44):

- review the process for the allowance for doubtful accounts: consistent with last year, appropriate to the business;
- obtain the ageing report (debit and credit balances) and the list of debtors under litigation, compared with the previous year;
- identify doubtful debtors not provided for and discuss why with management, testing further where disputes exist;
- check that provisions are at appropriate rates, given recoverability;
- prepare a schedule of movements in the provision and write-offs; compare bad debt expense to sales with prior years;
- check that write-offs are approved by an appropriate authority (the Board of Directors in a company).

**Disclosure** (SM 5.44): check that foreign currency trade receivables are restated properly as per AS 11; that related party transactions regarding receivables are properly disclosed as per AS 18 or Ind AS 24; ensure that transactions with parties covered under section 189 (Register of Contracts or Arrangements in which Directors are interested) of the Companies Act, 2013 are reported properly in CARO 2020; and the Schedule III disclosures in Formats below. The mechanics of confirmations are in [[aud.evid.sa505|SA 505]].

::: warning
The section 189 point is given as SM 5.44 states it. CARO 2020's own clause (iii), as the Study Material reproduces it (SM 8.76–8.77), covers loans, advances in the nature of loans, guarantees, security and investments given to companies, firms, LLPs or any other parties, and does not mention section 189. Write the point as SM 5.44 gives it in a receivables answer. Verify against the Study Material and Statutory Update for your attempt.
:::

### 2. Cash and cash equivalents: count it before it walks

Cash in hand, stamps, balances in current and margin money accounts, cash credit accounts (debit balance), fixed deposits and cheques in hand are the most liquid assets: readily convertible into cash, with insignificant value risk, and prone to misappropriation, so ==utmost professional skepticism== is needed (SM 5.45).

**Existence and completeness** (SM 5.46–5.49):

::: steps
1. **Surprise check.** Count cash on the last day of the year and again some time after the close, without notice to the entity or its staff: otherwise there is no certainty that the cash produced was in fact held by the custodian.
2. **All balances together.** Cashier, petty cashier, branch cashier and imprest balances with employees are counted simultaneously, as far as practicable, so that a shortage in one is not made good from another.
3. **Cashier present and signing.** The cashier attends the count and signs the statement of the balance with its denominations; if absent, he may hold the auditor responsible for any shortage.
4. **Rough cash book.** Test its entries, or the separate record of daily balances, against the Cash Book.
5. **Slips, chits and IOUs** for temporary advances to employees counted as cash: approved by an authorised official and recorded in the appropriate accounts.
6. **Cash sensitivity analysis.** Summarise receipts and payments month by month, look for variations, and ask management to explain them.
7. **Bank reconciliation statements (BRS)** for all bank accounts at the period end: understand the process and periodicity, and ensure each BRS is signed by authorised personnel so that responsibility can be assigned.
8. **Bank confirmations.** Confirm directly with banks and financial institutions all period-end balances: current, deposit, EEFC, cash credit and restrictive use accounts (dividend, escrow); the company reconciles discrepancies and seeks written explanations from the banks.
:::

**Verifying the BRS** (SM 5.47–5.48):

- tally the balance as per the bank book to the bank confirmation or statement;
- **cheques issued but not presented**: check material items to the bank book and to the next period's bank statements; a cheque that has become **stale (3 months or more since issue) should not appear in the BRS but be taken back to liabilities**;
- **cheques deposited but not credited**: check bank-acknowledged deposit slips and the next period's statements; for cheques not cleared beyond a reasonable time, seek explanations and, if unsatisfactory, verify that revenue recognition for those parties was in order and as per the revenue recognition policy;
- **amounts debited or credited by the bank but not accounted for**: if material, management should record them; if it does not, the auditor shall consider qualifying the report.

The auditor should emphasise confirmation of **100% of bank balances**. In remote cases of no reply: agree the balance to the bank statement received by the company or to an online login in the auditor's presence, or send a team member to the branch with the entity's personnel (SM 5.49). **Valuation**: bank accounts in foreign currency are restated at closing exchange rates (SM 5.49).

### 3. Inventories: count, cut off, cost or NRV

Inventories are assets (a) held for sale in the ordinary course of business, (b) in the process of production for such sale, or (c) materials or supplies to be consumed in production or in rendering services. Under AS 2 they are valued at the **lower of cost and net realisable value**, on a basis applied consistently, with any change in policy disclosed (SM 5.50).

**Existence** (SM 5.50–5.52): review the entity's plan for the inventory count; ensure that consigned goods have been segregated; **participate in the count** with management. The auditor's test counts should include:

- observing that employees are adhering to the agreed plan;
- assuring that there is appropriate supervision of the count procedure;
- assuring that all items are properly **tagged**;
- observing that proper amounts are shown on tags;
- determining that tags and summary sheets are controlled and reconciled;
- reconciling test counts with tags and summary sheets, with discrepancies, if any, summarised and agreed with client personnel;
- staying alert at all times, and specifically cautious about empty boxes and obsolete items;
- performing **cut-off** testing by documenting the last 5–10 receiving reports and shipping documents as of the period end;
- ensuring the exclusion of third-party stock and of damaged or obsolete stock;
- ensuring the accounting of all stock sheets;
- investigating any significant differences between the physical stock take and the stock records, and asking the entity's personnel to sign all stock count sheets and agree the variances observed, to avoid any conflicts.

When the entity uses a **periodic** system, the count is undertaken at the end of the period; with a **perpetual** system and proper, adequate records, inventory may be counted at interim dates. Confirm or investigate any inventory of the entity lying with a third party (specifically relevant where the entity gets job work done). The count itself is the subject of [[aud.evid.sa501|SA 501]].

**Completeness**: only the inventories held by the entity are recorded, none belonging to third parties, but including the entity's inventories lying with a third party (SM 5.52–5.53):

- perform analytical procedures (comparison with industry averages, budgets, prior years, trend analysis): compute the **inventory turnover ratio** (COGS / average inventory); perform vertical analysis (inventory / total assets); compare budgetary expectations with actuals;
- examine non-financial information about inventory, such as weights and other measurements;
- perform purchase and sales cut-off tests: trace shipping documents (bills of lading and receiving reports, warehouse records and inventory records) to the accounting records immediately before and after the year end;
- for tagged inventory, perform tests for omitted transactions and tests for invalid transactions;
- verify the clerical and arithmetical accuracy of inventory listings;
- reconcile physical inventory amounts with perpetual records;
- reconcile physical counts with general ledger control totals;
- reconcile inventories that belong to the client but are held with third parties, like transporters, warehouses and port authorities;
- check that goods received on a consignment basis have been properly segregated from other items of inventory.

**Rights and obligations**: the entity has valid legal ownership rights over the inventories it records (SM 5.53):

- vouch recorded purchases to the underlying documentation (purchase requisition, purchase order, receiving report, vendor invoice and cancelled cheque or payment file);
- evaluate the consigned goods;
- examine client correspondence, sales and receivables records, purchase documents;
- determine the existence of **collateral agreements**;
- review consignment agreements;
- review material purchase commitment agreements;
- examine invoices for evidence of ownership: they shall be **in the name of the client**;
- obtain confirmation for significant items of inventory;
- for inventory held by a third party, insist on obtaining a **declaration from the third party on its business letterhead**, signed by its authorised personnel, confirming that the items belong to the entity and are held by the third party on behalf of and for the benefit of the entity.

**Valuation** (SM 5.54–5.56):

- management may value inventory on a first-in first-out (FIFO) or weighted average basis, depending on how the business operates: consider the reasonableness of the method adopted;
- **raw materials and consumables**: ascertain what elements of cost are included (carriage inward, non-refundable duties); if standard costs are used, enquire into the basis of the standards, how they are compared with actual costs and how variances are analysed and treated; test-check cost prices with purchase invoices received in the month(s) before the count; follow up the valuation of all damaged or obsolete inventories noted at the count, to establish a realistic net realisable value;
- **work in progress**: ascertain how the stages of production or value additions are measured and the basis of any estimates; ascertain what elements of cost are included and, if overheads are included, the basis, compared with the entity's costing and financial data; ensure that material costs exclude any abnormal wastage factors;
- **finished goods and goods for resale**: enquire what costs are included and how they were established, and ensure that the overheads included are based on normal costs and appear reasonable; ensure that inventories are valued at net realisable value if they are likely to fetch less than cost and, for any such item, that the related work in progress and raw materials have also been written down;
- **obsolete, damaged and slow-moving items**: ascertain their possible realisable value; ask the client for the inventory ageing split and follow up items noted as damaged or obsolete at the count; compare recorded costs with replacement costs; examine vendor price lists to see whether recorded cost is less than current prices; calculate the inventory turnover ratio (obsolete inventory may be revealed if it is significantly lower); in manufacturing, test overhead allocation rates and ensure that only direct labour, direct material and overhead are included; verify the correct application of the lower of cost and net realisable value.

### 4. Loans and advances and other current assets

Loans and advances include those to related parties, security deposits, capital advances, and amounts recoverable in cash or in kind or for value to be received (rates, taxes and insurance paid in advance). Other current assets are mainly balances with statutory or government authorities (SM 5.80).

- **Existence** (SM 5.80–5.81): direct confirmation procedures similar to those for receivables, the only difference being that, in addition to the principal, interest receivable, if any, under the agreed terms may also be included in the balance to be confirmed.
- **Completeness** (SM 5.81–5.82):
  - obtain a list of all advances and other current assets and compare them with the balances in the ledger;
  - verify loan agreements and the parties' acknowledgements of outstanding loans; a material loan or advance is granted only if authorised by the **Memorandum and Articles of Association** in the case of a company, and the loans must be within the competence of the persons who advanced them (directors in a company, partners in a firm, trustees in a trust);
  - inspect the **minutes of the board** to confirm that all material loans and advances were approved;
  - verify that the loan has been acknowledged by the party, inspect any security deposited against due repayment, and ascertain whether loans are being recovered regularly as per the agreed instalments;
  - review related party loans and advances for proper authorisation and whether their value was reasonable and at arm's length;
  - for balances with statutory authorities like **GST input credit**, prepare a reasonability by applying the applicable rate to the purchases and expenses, ask for the reasons for any variance with the asset recorded, and verify that the amount in the books tallies with the claim in the GST returns filed.
- **Valuation** (SM 5.82–5.83):
  - assess the allowance for doubtful accounts, reviewing the process for consistency with last year's method and its fitness for the business;
  - obtain the ageing report of loans and advances and the list of those under litigation, compared with the previous year;
  - identify those that appear doubtful and discuss with management why they are not provided for;
  - assess write-offs, preparing a schedule of movements on the provision accounts and the amounts written off;
  - check that write-offs or other reductions in recoverable balances are approved by the authorised and appropriate senior authority;
  - check that foreign currency loans, advances and other current assets are restated properly as per AS 11.

## Formats and formulas

::: formula Inventory turnover ratio
$$\text{Inventory turnover ratio} = \qquad \frac{\text{Cost of goods sold}}{\text{Average inventory}}$$
A significantly lower ratio than before may reveal obsolete inventory (SM 5.52, 5.55).
:::

:::: example The BRS with a ghost cheque
Tapri's HDFC Bank current account at Indore shows ₹18,46,300 in the bank book and ₹17,59,800 in the bank statement at 31 March 2026. Accounts' BRS lists:

- cheques issued but not presented: ₹3,20,000 to a milk supplier, dated 26 March 2026 (cleared on 4 April 2026); ₹2,40,000 to a transporter, dated 10 December 2025, not yet presented;
- cheques deposited but not credited: ₹5,60,000 deposited on 30 March 2026 (credited on 2 April 2026); ₹1,10,000 from a franchisee, deposited on 18 February 2026, still not credited;
- bank charges of ₹18,500 and interest of ₹42,000 shown in the statement but not in the books.

Required:

(i) State what the auditor should verify for each reconciling item.

(ii) Compute the bank book balance after the adjustments the auditor should require, and show that it reconciles with the bank statement.

::: answer Show the working
**(i) Verification**

1. Tally the bank book balance to the bank statement or confirmation, and confirm the balance directly with HDFC Bank.
2. **₹3,20,000**: trace to the bank book and to the April statement; cleared on 4 April, so a genuine reconciling item.
3. **₹2,40,000**: more than 3 months have lapsed since 10 December 2025, so the cheque is **stale**. It should not appear in the BRS: the amount is taken back to liabilities (the transporter is still owed).
4. **₹5,60,000**: check the deposit slip acknowledged by the bank and the credit on 2 April.
5. **₹1,10,000**: uncleared for six weeks, beyond a reasonable time. Seek management's explanation; if it is unsatisfactory, verify that revenue recognition for the franchisee was in order and as per Tapri's revenue recognition policy.
6. **Charges and interest**: management should record them; if material and not recorded, the auditor should consider qualifying the report.

**(ii) Adjusted bank book and revised BRS**

| Particulars | Amount (₹) |
|---|---:|
| Balance as per bank book | 18,46,300 |
| Add: stale cheque taken back to liabilities | 2,40,000 |
| Add: interest credited by the bank | 42,000 |
| Less: bank charges | (18,500) |
| **Adjusted balance as per bank book** | **21,09,800** |
| Add: cheque issued but not presented | 3,20,000 |
| Less: cheques deposited but not credited (₹5,60,000 + ₹1,10,000) | (6,70,000) |
| **Balance as per bank statement** | **17,59,800** |

The ₹1,10,000 stays a reconciling item only until the explanation is obtained; if the cheque was dishonoured, the receivable is reinstated and the bank book reduced.

:::: example Kesar's cousin lends to the family
Mehra Masala Pvt Ltd, run by Kesar's cousin, has these loans in the nature of loans outstanding at 31 March 2026 (₹ lakh):

| Borrower | Terms | Amount |
|---|---|---:|
| Neha Mehra, promoter | Repayable on demand | 18 |
| Arjun Mehra, director | 8 quarterly instalments | 12 |
| Arjun Mehra jointly with his wife | No terms or period | 9 |
| Rakesh Jain, CFO (a KMP) | No terms or period | 6 |
| Mehra Agro LLP, a related party | Repayable on demand | 25 |
| Unrelated distributors | Fixed repayment terms | 80 |

Kesar's cousin argues that the joint loan "is the wife's, really", and that the director's instalment loan is "family money, so obviously it goes in".

Advise the auditor on the Additional Regulatory Information required by Schedule III.

::: answer Model answer
**Relevant provision.** Schedule III requires disclosure where loans or advances in the nature of loans are granted to promoters, directors, KMPs and related parties (as defined under the Companies Act, 2013), either severally or jointly with any other person, that are (a) repayable on demand or (b) without specifying any terms or period of repayment: for each type of borrower, the amount outstanding and its percentage to the total loans and advances in the nature of loans.

**Facts and analysis.** Total loans in the nature of loans: ₹18 + ₹12 + ₹9 + ₹6 + ₹25 + ₹80 = ₹150 lakh.

1. Neha Mehra's and Mehra Agro LLP's loans are repayable on demand, and Rakesh Jain's has no terms: all three are reported.
2. The joint loan is reported under directors: the requirement covers loans to a director "jointly with any other person", and it has no terms.
3. Arjun Mehra's own loan has a repayment schedule, so it is not reported, however "family" the money; nor are the distributors' loans. Both stay in the total.

| Type of borrower | Outstanding (₹ lakh) | % of total loans |
|---|---:|---:|
| Promoters | 18 | 12.00 |
| Directors | 9 | 6.00 |
| KMPs | 6 | 4.00 |
| Related parties | 25 | 16.67 |

**Conclusion.** The auditor should ensure that this table appears in the notes. Percentages: 18/150, 9/150, 6/150 and 25/150 × 100. The auditor should also verify that each loan was authorised by the Memorandum and Articles and approved by the board, is acknowledged by the borrower, and is confirmed directly with the interest receivable.

::: trick "Count, Confirm, Cut off, Cost"
Every current asset question is answered by some of these four: **count** (cash by surprise and simultaneously; inventory at the count), **confirm** (debtors, banks, loans, third-party stock), **cut off** (last days' invoices and dispatches; receiving and shipping documents), **cost** (lower of cost and NRV; allowance for doubtful debts). Condition: it organises the procedures; the answer must still use the Study Material's list for the assertion asked.
:::

## Answer frame

### "State the audit procedures to verify [assertion] of [current asset]"

1. One line on the assertion ("all inventories owned by the entity, including those lying with third parties, and none belonging to others, are recorded").
2. The Study Material's procedures for that caption and assertion, in its order and words, as points.
3. One line applying them to the case (the job worker gets a declaration on its letterhead; the stale cheque goes back to liabilities).
4. A conclusion on the evidence, and the reporting consequence if it cannot be obtained.

### Three answers to "How will you verify cash balances at three locations?"

::: cols
**Loses marks**

"Count the cash and tally it with the cash book."

No surprise, no simultaneous count, no signature, no IOUs.
---
**Passes**

"Count cash by surprise at all locations together; get the cashier's signature; check IOUs are authorised."

Three points; misses rough cash book, sensitivity analysis and the second count after the year end.
---
**Full marks**

All the steps in the Study Material's order, each applied to the head office, branch, petty cash and imprest balances, ending with what the auditor does about a shortage or an unapproved IOU.
:::

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: the cash count Bunty announced
Phoning ahead turns a count into a rehearsal. **Fix:** count cash by surprise, on the last day and again after the close without notice, and count every balance (cashier, petty cash, branch, imprest) simultaneously.
:::

::: mistake Leaving a stale cheque in the BRS
A cheque issued 3 months or more ago, left as "issued but not presented", keeps the bank balance understated and the creditor shown as paid. **Fix:** remove it from the BRS and take it back to liabilities.
:::

::: mistake Confirmations through the client
Letting replies come back to the client's office, or using only the "agree / disagree" form. **Fix:** the auditor controls despatch, replies and undelivered letters return to the auditor, and the form without the balance is preferable.
:::

::: mistake Writing down finished goods but not their raw materials
**Fix:** when finished goods are valued at net realisable value below cost, verify that the related work in progress and raw materials have also been written down.
:::

## Practice

::: quiz Check yourself
Q: Case scenario. Rewa Rotis Ltd holds cash at its factory office and in the till of its city outlet. The auditor counts the factory cash at 6 pm on 31 March, then phones the outlet manager to "keep the till ready" and counts it at noon on 1 April. The outlet manager signs the count sheet; the factory cashier, who had left for the day, does not. Which of the following is correct?
- [ ] The procedure is adequate, because both balances were counted within 24 hours of the year end
- [ ] The only flaw is that the outlet till should have been counted before the factory cash
- [x] The counts were neither by surprise nor simultaneous, so a shortage in one balance could be made good from the other; and the absent factory cashier may later hold the auditor responsible for any shortage
- [ ] Cash counts are unnecessary when the cash book has been checked with the rough cash book
> The Study Material requires surprise checks without notice, simultaneous counting of all balances as far as practicable, and the cashier's presence and signature. The rough cash book test is an extra procedure, not a substitute.

Q: Kulhad Crafts Ltd's cost of goods sold for the year is ₹96 crore; inventory was ₹14 crore at the start and ₹18 crore at the end of the year. What is the inventory turnover ratio (times)?
= 6
> Average inventory = (₹14 + ₹18) / 2 = ₹16 crore; ₹96 / ₹16 = 6 times. If the ratio was 8 last year, the auditor follows up for obsolete or slow-moving items.

Q: For confirming trade receivables, which form of request does the Study Material prefer?
- [ ] The form showing the balance as per the company, asking the debtor to agree or disagree
- [x] The form without any balance, asking the debtor to state the balance as per its records
- [ ] A request sent by the company's sales team to its own contacts
- [ ] A written representation from management in place of confirmations
> The form without the balance makes the debtor look up its own records; the auditor controls despatch and receipt in either case.

Q: A cheque issued to a supplier on 12 November 2025 has not been presented by 31 March 2026. How should it be treated?
- [ ] Shown in the BRS as a cheque issued but not presented
- [x] Removed from the BRS and taken back to liabilities
- [ ] Written off to the Statement of Profit and Loss
- [ ] Shown under cash and cash equivalents as cheques on hand
> More than 3 months have passed since issue, so it is stale: it does not appear in the BRS and the amount owed returns to liabilities.

Q: Bhopal Biscuits Ltd's inventory includes goods lying with a job worker. Which procedures help verify completeness and rights over these goods? Select all that apply.
- [x] Obtain a declaration on the job worker's letterhead, signed by its authorised personnel, that the goods belong to the company and are held on its behalf
- [x] Reconcile the company's inventories held with third parties
- [ ] Exclude the goods from inventory because they are not on the company's premises
- [x] Confirm or investigate the inventory lying with the third party
> Goods with a job worker belong to the entity and must be included; the declaration, the reconciliation and the confirmation are all Study Material procedures.

## Recap

- Receivables: confirm all significant balances directly, the form without balance preferred, replies to the auditor; no reply means subsequent receipts or a transaction analysis.
- Cash: count by surprise on the last day and again after the close, all balances simultaneously, cashier signing; verify every BRS; confirm 100% of bank balances.
- Inventories: attend the count, test cut-off, confirm third-party stock by a signed declaration, value at the lower of cost and NRV.
- Receivables also: test sales controls; cut-off on the last 5 days; allowance reviewed with the ageing and litigation lists; write-offs approved by the board.
- Ageing schedule: undisputed or disputed, good or doubtful, from the due date (else the transaction date); unbilled dues separately.
- Stale cheque (3 months or more) leaves the BRS and goes back to liabilities; unrecorded bank items adjusted or the report qualified.
- Loans and advances: confirmations with interest, agreements, MOA/AOA authority, board minutes; GST credit by reasonability and returns. Loans on demand or without terms to promoters, directors, KMPs and related parties: amount and percentage to total loans. (Never phone the cashier first.)

## Flashcards

Q:: Trade receivables: name four points considered when testing controls over sales.
A:: Only bona fide sales lead to receivables; sales only to approved customers; all sales properly recorded; once recorded, settled only by cash or on a responsible official's authority (also: segregation of duties, timely collection, reminders and legal action, regular review, follow-up and provision from an ageing schedule).

Q:: Which form of confirmation request for trade receivables is preferable?
A:: The form without any balance, in which the debtor states the balance as per its records.

Q:: What alternative procedures apply when a debtor does not reply to a confirmation?
A:: Agreeing the balance to cash received subsequently; preparing a detailed analysis of the balance into identifiable transactions and confirming that those sales occurred.

Q:: Why must cash be counted by surprise and all balances simultaneously?
A:: Without surprise there is no certainty that the cash produced was held by the custodian; simultaneous counting stops a shortage in one balance being made good by transfer from another.

Q:: BRS: how is a cheque issued 3 months or more ago and not presented treated?
A:: It is stale: it should not appear in the BRS and should be taken back to liabilities.

Q:: BRS: what if bank charges or credits not accounted for are material?
A:: Management should record the adjustments; if it does not, the auditor shall consider qualifying the report.

Q:: Schedule III: the four rows of the trade receivables ageing schedule?
A:: Undisputed, considered good; undisputed, considered doubtful; disputed, considered good; disputed, considered doubtful (columns: < 6 months, 6 months–1 year, 1–2, 2–3, > 3 years, total).

Q:: Schedule III: how are cash and cash equivalents classified?
A:: Balances with banks; cheques, drafts on hand; cash on hand; others (specify nature); with earmarked balances, margin money, repatriation restrictions and deposits over 12 months' maturity shown separately.

Q:: How does the auditor verify ownership of inventory held by a third party?
A:: By insisting on a declaration on the third party's letterhead, signed by its authorised personnel, that the items belong to the entity and are held on its behalf and for its benefit.

Q:: What is the inventory turnover ratio, and what does a low one suggest?
A:: Cost of goods sold / average inventory; a significantly lower ratio may reveal obsolete inventory.

Q:: Loans and advances: what must the auditor confirm about a material loan's authority?
A:: That it is authorised by the Memorandum and Articles of Association, within the competence of those who advanced it (directors in a company), and approved by the board of directors.

Q:: Schedule III: which loans to promoters, directors, KMPs and related parties need the additional disclosure?
A:: Loans or advances in the nature of loans that are repayable on demand or without any terms or period of repayment: amount outstanding and percentage to total loans and advances in the nature of loans.
