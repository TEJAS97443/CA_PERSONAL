## In plain words

### Bunty's week of four buildings

Iyer & Associates do not audit only Tapri Tea. This week Bunty's diary holds four other audits in Indore: Shri Ahilya Vidya Mandir, a school run by a trust; Narmada Seva Hospital, a charitable hospital; the Malwa Gymkhana Club; and Rajwada Residency, the 64-room hotel where Kesar puts up every visiting distributor.

> **Bunty:** Ma'am, a school, a hospital, a club and a hotel. Four different audits?
> **Lakshmi:** One audit, four rulebooks. Find out who should have paid, check who did pay, and ask who allowed the difference.
> **Kesar:** *(on speaker)* Lakshmi-ji, the hotel gives my distributors a small discount. Sab adjust ho jayega.
> **Lakshmi:** Then somebody at the hotel had the power to give it, Kesar-ji. Bunty will find the signature.

All four live on **many small receipts from many people**, so the same three leaks appear in each building:

- **Money that should have been charged and was not**: a student on the class roll with no fee demand, a patient discharged without a bill, a member's drinks never debited to his account, a guest's extra night never charged.
- **Concessions given by someone without the power to give them**: a free studentship, a waived fine, a room let below the standard rate.
- **Things that walk away**: food and liquor in a hotel, medicines and linen in a hospital, crockery and sports goods in a club.

Their rules come from a **constitution** (a trust deed, an Act of the Legislature for a university, bye-laws, the Managing Committee's resolutions), so the auditor reads it before the ledger. Much of their money is **restricted** (a donation for a ward, an endowment for a prize, a grant), so the auditor checks it was used for that purpose, and reports what is wrong to the Managing Committee or the trustees.

::: analogy The tuition teacher's two notebooks
A home-tuition teacher keeps two notebooks: an attendance diary (who came this month) and a fee notebook (who paid). At month end she compares them. A child who came but did not pay is a leak. A child let off half the fee is fine only if the parent's request was approved by her, not by the child's elder brother who collects the money. And the box of new pencils she bought must still be in the cupboard. Every special step in this lesson is one of those three checks, done with a bigger notebook.
:::

::: key
In educational institutions, hospitals, clubs and hotels the auditor first reads the constitution and the minutes, then matches the record of who should pay (class register, patients' attendance record, register of members, guest register) with the record of who paid, checks every concession and every large expense against the authority allowed to sanction it, and controls inventories that are easy to carry off.
:::

### 2. Educational institutions (school, college or university)

The special steps are the following (SM 9.43–9.46):

**(A) General**

1. Examine the **Trust Deed or Regulations** in the case of a school or college and note all the provisions affecting accounts. In the case of a **university**, refer to the **Act of Legislature** and the Regulations framed under it.
2. Read the **minutes** of the meetings of the Managing Committee or Governing Body, noting resolutions affecting accounts, to see that they have been complied with, specially the decisions on the operation of bank accounts and the sanctioning of expenditure.

**(B) Fee from Students**

1. Check the names entered in the **Students Fee Register** for each month or term with the respective **Class Registers** (the names of students on rolls) and test the amount of fees charged; verify that a system of internal check ensures that **demands against the students are properly raised**.
2. Check fees received by comparing the **counterfoils of receipts** with entries in the **Cash Book**, and trace the collections in the Fee Register, to confirm that the revenue from this source has been duly accounted for.
3. Total up the columns of the Fees Register for each month or term to see that **fees paid in advance** have been carried forward and that **irrecoverable arrears** have been written off under the sanction of an appropriate authority.
4. Check **admission fees** with admission slips signed by the head of the institution and confirm that the amount has been credited to a **Capital fund**, unless the Managing Committee has taken a decision to the contrary.
5. See that **free studentship and concessions** have been granted by a person authorised to do so, having regard to the Rules prepared by the Managing Committee.
6. Confirm that **fines** for late payment or absence, etc. have been either collected or remitted under proper authority.
7. Confirm that **hostel dues** were recovered before students' accounts were closed and their deposits of **caution money** refunded.

**(C) Other Receipts / Grants and Donations**

1. Verify **rental income** from landed property with the rent rolls, etc.
2. Vouch income from **endowments and legacies**, and interest and dividends from investments; inspect the securities for the investments held.
3. Verify any **Government or local authority grant** with the memo of grant. If any expense has been disallowed for purposes of the grant, ascertain the reasons.

**(D) Expenditure**

1. Verify that the **Provident Fund** money of the staff has been invested in appropriate securities.
2. Vouch **donations** with the list published with the annual report; if some were meant for a specific purpose, see that the money was used for it.
3. Vouch all **capital expenditure** in the usual way and verify it with the sanction of the Committee in the minute book.
4. Vouch all **establishment expenses** and enquire into any unduly heavy expenditure under any head. If an annual **budget** was prepared, see that any excess over the budget under any head was sanctioned by the Managing Committee; if not, bring it to the Committee's notice in the report.
5. See that **increases in the salaries** of the staff have been sanctioned and minuted by the Committee.

**(E) Assets and Liabilities**

1. Report any old heavy **arrears** of fees, dormitory rents, etc. to the Managing Committee.
2. Confirm that **caution money** and other deposits paid by students on admission are shown as a **liability** in the balance sheet, not transferred to revenue, unless they are not refundable.
3. See that the investments representing **endowment funds for prizes** are kept separate and that any income in excess of the prizes has been accumulated and invested along with the corpus.
4. Ascertain that the system of inspection on receipt and issue of provisions, foodstuffs, clothing and other equipment is efficient, and that all bills are authorised and passed before payment.
5. Verify the **inventories** of furniture, stationery, clothing, provisions and equipment by reference to the Inventory Register or the previous year's inventories, and test check the values applied.

**(F) Compliances**

1. Confirm that the **refund of taxes deducted** from income from investments (interest on securities, etc.) has been claimed and recovered, since the institutions are generally exempt from income-tax.
2. Finally, verify the annual statements of account and see that **separate statements of account** have been prepared for the Poor Boys Fund, Games Fund, Hostel and Provident Fund of staff, etc.

### 3. Hospitals

The special steps are (SM 9.46–9.48):

1. **Register of Patients**: vouch the Register of patients with copies of the bills issued to them. Verify bills for a selected period with the patients' attendance record to see that they have been correctly prepared, and that bills have been issued to all patients from whom an amount was recoverable under the rules of the hospital.
2. **Collection of Cash**: check cash collections in the Cash Book with the receipts, counterfoils and other evidence, for example copies of patients' bills, counterfoils of dividend and interest warrants, copies of rent bills.
3. **Income from Investments, Rent etc.**: see, with reference to the Property and Investment Register, that all income due by way of rent, dividends and interest on securities has been collected.
4. **Legacies and Donations**: ascertain that legacies and donations received for a **specific purpose** have been applied in the manner agreed upon.
5. **Reconciliation of Subscriptions**: trace all collections of subscriptions and donations from the Cash Book to the registers; reconcile the total subscriptions due (as shown by the Subscription Register) with the amount collected and that still outstanding.
6. **Authorisation and Sanctions**: vouch all purchases and expenses; verify that capital expenditure was incurred only with the **prior sanction of the Trustees or the Managing Committee**, and that appointments and increments to staff were authorised.
7. **Grants and TDS**: verify that grants from Government or a local authority have been accounted for, and that refund of taxes deducted at source has been claimed.
8. **Budgets**: compare the totals of items of income and expenditure with the budgeted amounts and **report significant variations** to the Trustees or the Managing Committee.
9. **Internal Check**: examine the internal check over the receipt and issue of stores, medicines, linen, apparatus, clothing, instruments, etc., so that purchases are recorded in the Inventory Register and issues are made only against proper authorisation.
10. **Depreciation**: see that depreciation has been written off against all the assets at the appropriate rates.
11. **Registers**: inspect the bonds, share scrips and title deeds of properties and compare them with the Property and Investment Registers.
12. **Inventories**: obtain inventories, especially of stocks and stores, at the end of the year; check a percentage of the items physically and compare their total values with the ledger balances.
13. **Management Representation and Certificate**: obtain a proper management representation and certificate on the various aspects covered during the audit (see [[aud.complete.sa580|written representations]]).

### 4. Clubs

The special steps are (SM 9.48–9.49):

1. **Entrance Fee**: vouch receipts of entrance fees with members' applications, the counterfoils issued to them and the minutes of the Managing Committee.
2. **Subscriptions**: vouch members' subscriptions with the counterfoils of receipts; trace receipts for a selected period to the **Register of Members**; reconcile the total subscriptions due with the amount collected and that outstanding.
3. **Arrears of Subscriptions**: ensure that arrears of the previous year have been correctly brought over, and that arrears for the year under audit and **subscriptions received in advance** have been correctly adjusted.
4. **Arithmetical accuracy**: check the totals of the columns of the Register of Members and tally them across.
5. **Irrecoverable Member Dues**: see from the Register of Members which members' dues are in arrear and whether steps have been taken to recover them; the amount considered irrecoverable should be **mentioned in the Audit Report**.
6. **Pricing**: verify the internal check over members being charged the price of foodstuffs and drinks provided to them and their guests, and the fees for special services such as billiards and tennis.
7. **Member Accounts**: trace debits for a selected period from the subsidiary registers of supplies and services to confirm that **every member's account has been debited** with the amounts recoverable from him.
8. **Purchases**: vouch purchases of sports items, furniture, crockery, etc. and trace them into the inventory registers.
9. **Margins earned**: vouch purchases of foodstuffs, cigars, wines, etc. and test their sale price to confirm that the **normal rates of gross profit** have been earned; verify the year-end inventory of unsold provisions and stores physically and check its valuation.
10. **Inventories**: check the inventory of furniture, sports material and other assets physically with the inventory registers or the year-end inventories.
11. **Investments**: inspect the share scrips and bonds, check their current values for disclosure in the final accounts, and see that the arrangements for their safe custody are satisfactory.
12. **Management Powers**: examine the **financial powers of the secretary** and, if they have been exceeded, report the specific case for confirmation by the Managing Committee.

### 5. Hotels

The special considerations in a hotel audit are (SM 9.56–9.58):

**(1) Internal Controls.** ==Pilfering is one of the greatest problems in any hotel==, and it is management's responsibility to introduce controls that minimise the leakage. Evidence of their success is the preparation of regular, perhaps weekly, **trading accounts for each sales point** and a detailed scrutiny of the resulting profit percentages, with any deviation from the anticipated pattern investigated. The auditor should obtain these trading accounts, examine them and obtain explanations for any apparent deviations. The auditor should verify a few restaurant bills with the **K.O.T.s (Kitchen Order Tickets)** or basic record, to see that the revenue-cycle controls are in order, and should be satisfied that all **taxes collected from occupants** on food and occupation have been paid over to the proper authorities. If internal control is weak or breaks down, a very serious problem exists: because many of the clients' records are transient, the auditor must rely to a very large extent on the **gross margin** shown by the accounts. The scope of the audit tests must then be increased and, if a material margin discrepancy remains unexplained, the auditor will have to **consider qualifying** the report (see [[aud.report.sa705|SA 705]]).

**(2) Room Sales and Hall Bookings.** The charge for rooms is normally posted to guest bills by the receptionist or front office, or in large hotels by the night auditor, from the **guest register**; tests should ensure that the correct number of guests is charged for the correct period. Any difference between the rates charged and the **standard room rate** should be investigated to see that it was properly authorised. In many hotels the **housekeeper prepares a daily report** of rooms occupied the previous night and the number of beds in each; it tends not to be kept permanently, so the auditor should ensure that enough reports are available to test with the guest register and the individual guest bills. The auditor should ensure that a proper valuation of **occupancy-in-progress** at the balance sheet date is made and included in the accounts, and that proper records are kept of the booking of halls and premises for special parties, recovered on the basis of the tariff.

**(3) Inventories.** Hotel inventories, particularly food and beverages, are both readily portable and saleable, so all movements and transfers must be properly documented to control each store and sales point; the auditor tests that this documentation is accurately processed. Large stores should be kept locked, the key held by the departmental manager and released only to trusted personnel; unauthorised persons should not enter stores except under constant supervision, and every movement of goods in or out should be checked. Many hotels use **specialised professional valuers** to count and value inventories continuously through the year, and their valuation becomes the balance sheet figure. Although the valuers are independent of the client, the auditor must be satisfied that the amounts are reasonable: the auditor should consider **attending the physical inventory taking** and carrying out some pricing and calculation tests, whose extent can be limited because the figures were prepared independently of the hotel.

**(4) Fixed Assets.** Accounting policies differ between hotels. Many account for **quasi-fixed assets such as silver and cutlery on an inventory basis**, which can cause confusion with similar items accounted for as assets; detailed definitions of inventory items should exist, and the auditor tests that they have been followed. **Repairs and minor renovation and redecoration** are revenue expenditure; **major alterations and additions** to the building and facilities are capitalised.

**(5) Casual Labour.** The hotel trade runs to a very large extent on casual labour, whose wage records are frequently inadequate. The auditor should ensure that defalcation does not take place on this account by suggesting proper controls to management.

**(6) Travel Agents and Shops.** For guests coming through travel agents or booking agencies, bills are usually made on the agency: the auditor should ensure that the money is recovered from them as per the terms of credit allowed, and check any **commission** paid to them with the agreement.

### Testing gross margin (hotels, clubs)

::: formula Expected sales at the normal rate of gross profit
$$\text{Expected sales} = \frac{\text{Cost of goods consumed}}{1 - \text{normal GP ratio}}$$

Cost of goods consumed = opening stock + purchases − closing stock of the sales point; normal GP ratio = the gross profit ÷ sales the entity normally earns at that point. Shortfall = expected sales − recorded sales.
:::

### Occupancy-in-progress (hotels)

::: formula Revenue earned but not yet billed at the balance sheet date
$$\text{Occupancy-in-progress} = \qquad \sum \text{rooms} \times \text{unbilled nights} \times \text{tariff}$$

Unbilled nights = nights stayed up to the balance sheet date and not yet billed; guests billed weekly or at checkout leave such nights at the year end. It is included in the accounts on the accrual basis (SM 9.57, 9.90).
:::

## Worked examples

:::: example Five decisions at Shri Ahilya Vidya Mandir
Iyer & Associates audit Shri Ahilya Vidya Mandir, a school run by a public trust in Indore, for 2025-26. Bunty's notes:

(i) The Managing Committee resolved on 12 April 2025 that admission fees of the year (₹18,60,000) should be credited to revenue to meet a deficit; the accountant did so.

(ii) Caution money of ₹6,40,000 deposited by students who left in 2023 and 2024, and never claimed, was transferred to income. The school's rules say caution money is refundable on leaving.

(iii) The corpus of the Kamla Joshi Memorial Prize Fund, ₹10,00,000, earned interest of ₹70,000; prizes cost ₹25,000 and the remaining ₹45,000 was spent on annual-day decorations.

(iv) Caution money was refunded to 14 hostel students on leaving, although their hostel dues of ₹1,12,000 were outstanding.

(v) Teachers' salaries were increased from July 2025 on a note approved by the Principal.

Comment on each as the auditor.

::: answer Model answer
**(i) Admission fees.** As per the special steps for educational institutions, the auditor checks admission fees with admission slips signed by the head of the institution and confirms that the amount has been credited to a Capital fund, unless the Managing Committee has taken a decision to the contrary. The Committee has so decided, so crediting ₹18,60,000 to revenue is in order; the auditor verifies the resolution in the minute book and tests the admission slips.

**(ii) Caution money.** Caution money and other deposits paid by students on admission must be shown as a liability in the balance sheet and not transferred to revenue, unless they are not refundable. Under the school's rules they are refundable, so ₹6,40,000 has been wrongly taken to income: income is overstated and liabilities understated. The auditor should ask for the transfer to be reversed and, if it is not, report the matter.

**(iii) Prize endowment.** The investments representing endowment funds for prizes must be kept separate, and any income in excess of the prizes must be accumulated and invested along with the corpus. The excess of ₹45,000 should have been added to the fund and invested; its use for decorations is a misapplication of a restricted fund, which the auditor should report to the Managing Committee and in the report.

**(iv) Hostel dues.** The auditor must confirm that hostel dues were recovered before students' accounts were closed and their caution money refunded. Refunds made while ₹1,12,000 of hostel dues were outstanding show a failure of control; the auditor should examine whether the dues are recoverable, see that they are not written off without the sanction of an appropriate authority, and report the weakness.

**(v) Salary increases.** Increases in the salaries of staff must be sanctioned and minuted by the Committee. A note approved by the Principal alone is not that sanction; the auditor should ask for the Committee's ratification and, failing it, bring the matter to the Committee's notice in the report.
:::

::::: example The Malwa Gymkhana Club's ₹1,20,000 question
The Malwa Gymkhana Club, Indore, charges an annual subscription of ₹12,000. For 2025-26 its Register of Members shows 820 members on the roll for the whole year, 40 new members admitted on 1 October 2025 (who pay half the year's subscription), and 30 life members, who pay none. The Cash Book shows subscription receipts of ₹1,05,30,000, of which ₹3,60,000 are arrears of 2024-25 (included in last year's income and receivables) and ₹4,80,000 are subscriptions for 2026-27 received in advance. The club has credited the whole ₹1,05,30,000 to subscription income. The Register of Members shows ₹2,70,000 outstanding for 2025-26.

The club's rules let the secretary sanction expenditure up to ₹1,00,000 on any one item. During the year he sanctioned a new billiards table for ₹3,40,000.

Required:

(a) Reconcile the subscriptions for 2025-26 and comment on the difference.

(b) Comment on the subscription income credited.

(c) State what the auditor should do about the billiards table.

:::: answer Model answer
**(a) Reconciliation of subscriptions, 2025-26**

| Particulars | Amount (₹) |
|---|---:|
| Members on roll for the year: 820 × ₹12,000 | 98,40,000 |
| New members from 1 October 2025: 40 × ₹6,000 | 2,40,000 |
| Life members: 30 × nil | 0 |
| **Subscriptions due for 2025-26** | **1,00,80,000** |
| Cash received ₹1,05,30,000 less arrears ₹3,60,000 less advance ₹4,80,000 | (96,90,000) |
| **Outstanding per the books** | **3,90,000** |
| Outstanding per the Register of Members | 2,70,000 |
| **Difference to be explained** | **1,20,000** |

₹1,20,000 equals ten members' annual subscriptions. As per the special steps for clubs, the auditor must reconcile the total subscriptions due with the amount collected and that outstanding, and check the totals of the Register of Members. The difference means either ten members' dues were collected but not accounted for, or they were removed from the Register without authority, or the Register is wrong. The auditor should identify the ten members, vouch their receipts with the counterfoils, trace them to the Register, enquire whether any waiver was sanctioned, and report what remains unexplained.

**(b) Subscription income.** Arrears of the previous year must be correctly brought over, and arrears of the year under audit and subscriptions received in advance correctly adjusted. Income for 2025-26 is the ₹1,00,80,000 due. Crediting the cash received overstates income by ₹4,50,000: the advance of ₹4,80,000 belongs to 2026-27, the arrears of ₹3,60,000 were last year's income, and the ₹3,90,000 still outstanding is this year's (4,80,000 + 3,60,000 − 3,90,000 = 4,50,000). The advance should be shown as a liability and the outstanding amount as a receivable, after considering whether any of it is irrecoverable; the amount considered irrecoverable should be mentioned in the audit report.

**(c) Billiards table.** The auditor should examine the financial powers of the secretary and, since they have been exceeded, report the specific case for confirmation by the Managing Committee. The auditor should also vouch the purchase and trace the table into the inventory register.

::::: example The bar that lost its margin at Rajwada Residency
Rajwada Residency Pvt Ltd runs a 64-room hotel in Indore. Its overall materiality for the 2025-26 audit is ₹6,00,000. Bunty extracts the following:

**Relevant provision.** In a hotel the auditor should obtain the regular trading accounts for each sales point, examine them and obtain explanations for any apparent deviations in the profit percentages. Where internal control is weak, the auditor must rely to a very large extent on the gross margin shown by the accounts; the scope of the audit tests is increased and, if a material margin discrepancy remains unexplained, the auditor will have to consider qualifying the report. The auditor should also ensure that a proper valuation of occupancy-in-progress at the balance sheet date is made and included in the accounts.

**Facts of the case.** The fourth-quarter gross profit is 56.81% against a normal 65%; after the approved scheme, ₹7,87,143 of expected sales is unexplained, above materiality of ₹6,00,000. Room revenue of ₹4,52,400 earned up to 31 March 2026 is not recorded.

**Analysis.** The deviation points to pilfering, unrecorded sales or unauthorised free drinks. The auditor should extend the tests: bar bills with K.O.T.s or the basic record, the bar store's movement records and key custody, pricing, and the year-end stock-taking. The unrecorded occupancy breaks the accrual basis: revenue and receivables are understated by ₹4,52,400, not material alone but to be corrected and aggregated with other misstatements.

**Conclusion.** Iyer & Associates should ask management to include occupancy-in-progress of ₹4,52,400 in the 2025-26 accounts. If, after the extended tests, a material margin discrepancy (here ₹7,87,143) still cannot be explained, they should consider qualifying the report under SA 705, after communicating the matter to those charged with governance.

## Under the hood

### Hospitals and charitable institutions overlap

Many hospitals are run by charitable trusts, so their audit also uses the points for charitable institutions, trusts and societies (constitution, receipt books, donations, the trust's purposes): see [[aud.entities.npo|not-for-profit organisations, trusts and societies]]. ICAI's questions about "a hospital run by a charitable trust" are still answered with the hospital list first.

### The hotel's valuers, and the cutlery problem

Independent valuers' figures do not replace the auditor's own satisfaction: attending the physical inventory taking ([[aud.evid.sa501|SA 501]]) and some pricing and calculation tests remain, only smaller (SM 9.57). Silver and cutlery on an inventory basis are a classification risk: the same spoon can be an asset in one hotel and inventory in another, so the test is whether this hotel's written definitions were followed (SM 9.58).

## Answer frame

### "State the special steps in the audit of a hospital (club, school, hotel)"

1. One opening line: "The special steps involved in the audit of a … are the following:".
2. The Study Material's points **under its headings, in its order** (for a school, group them under (A) General to (F) Compliances).
3. For the marks available, give about one point per mark, each with its heading and the record it is checked with.

### A case on one of these entities

1. **Relevant provision**: the special step that fits each fact, in the Study Material's words.
2. **Facts of the case**: the facts and figures that decide it.
3. **Analysis**: is the treatment in line with the step, the constitution or the Managing Committee's decision? Quantify the effect if the question gives numbers.
4. **Conclusion**: what the auditor should do (verify, ask for correction, report to the Managing Committee, mention in the audit report, consider qualifying).

### Three answers to "verify fees from students" (5 marks)

**Loses marks**

"Check the fee register and the cash book and see that all fees are received."

One vague point; no class registers, no concessions, no admission fees, no caution money.
---
**Passes**

Class Registers vs Fee Register; counterfoils vs Cash Book; concessions authorised.

Three correct points, but four of ICAI's seven are missing.

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: the caution money that became income
Bunty sees years of unclaimed caution money and calls it "obviously income". **Fix:** caution money and deposits paid on admission are a liability, not revenue, **unless they are not refundable** (SM 9.45). Check the rules before you transfer a rupee.
:::

::: mistake Admission fees always go to Capital fund
Half of it is right. **Fix:** the auditor confirms the credit to a Capital fund **unless the Managing Committee has taken a decision to the contrary** (SM 9.44). A case that mentions a Committee resolution is testing exactly that exception.
:::

::: mistake Trusting the margin explanation
Writing "management explained the fall in gross profit, so no action" in a hotel case. **Fix:** where controls are weak the gross margin is the auditor's main evidence; increase the scope of tests and, if a material discrepancy stays unexplained, consider qualifying (SM 9.56).
:::

::: mistake Mixing up the lists
Writing "free studentship" in a club answer or "secretary's powers" in a hospital answer. **Fix:** learn each list with its own headings: the club has the secretary's powers and members' accounts; the hospital has the patients' register, budgets and the management certificate; the school has fees, caution money and separate funds; the hotel has K.O.T.s, room sales and occupancy-in-progress.
:::

## Practice

::: quiz Check yourself
Q: Case scenario. Iyer & Associates audit Narmada Seva Hospital, run by a trust. The trustees approved a pharmacy budget of ₹40 lakh for 2025-26; actual pharmacy purchases were ₹52.8 lakh. The excess was approved by the medical superintendent, and the purchases are properly vouched. What does the Study Material require the auditor to do about the budget?
- [ ] Nothing, because the purchases are vouched and budgets are only internal documents
- [ ] Qualify the opinion, because expenditure exceeded the budget
- [x] Compare income and expenditure with the budget and report significant variations, such as this 32% excess, to the Trustees or the Managing Committee
- [ ] Report the variation to the Registrar of Companies
> The hospital list's "Budgets" step: report significant variations to the Trustees or the Managing Committee. A vouched over-budget expense is not a misstatement, so it is no ground for qualification by itself.

Q: Ujjain Public School's Class Registers show 1,240 students on rolls for July 2025, of whom 60 hold authorised free studentships and 35 hold authorised half-fee concessions. The monthly fee is ₹2,400. The Students Fee Register shows a demand of ₹27,42,000 for July. By how many rupees is the demand short of what should have been raised?
= 48000
> Demand due = (1,240 − 60) × 2,400 − 35 × 1,200 = 28,32,000 − 42,000 = ₹27,90,000. Short by 27,90,000 − 27,42,000 = ₹48,000, which is twenty students' full fee: the auditor checks the names in the Fee Register with the Class Registers to find them.

Q: Rajwada Residency's housekeeper prepares a daily report of rooms occupied the previous night but throws it away after a week. What should the auditor do?
- [ ] Ignore it, since the guest register is enough evidence
- [x] Ensure that a sufficient number of reports are available to test with the guest register and with individual guest bills
- [ ] Ask the housekeeper for a written representation instead
- [ ] Treat room revenue as unverifiable and disclaim an opinion
> The report tends not to be kept permanently, so the auditor ensures enough reports are available to test with the guest register and the guest bills. A disclaimer is far beyond what one missing record calls for.

Q: Which of the following are special steps in the audit of a club? Select all that apply.
- [x] Examine the financial powers of the secretary and report any excess for confirmation by the Managing Committee
- [x] Trace debits from subsidiary registers to confirm that every member's account has been debited with the amounts recoverable
- [ ] See that free studentships have been granted by an authorised person
- [x] Mention the amount of members' dues considered irrecoverable in the audit report
> Free studentship is in the school's list; the other three are the club's "Management Powers", "Member Accounts" and "Irrecoverable Member Dues".

Q: During the year the hotel replaced curtains and repainted 20 rooms (₹9 lakh) and added a new banquet floor (₹2.1 crore). How should these be treated?
- [ ] Both capitalised, since both improve the hotel
- [ ] Both charged to revenue
- [x] The repainting and curtains as revenue expenditure; the banquet floor capitalised
- [ ] The repainting capitalised; the banquet floor deferred
> Costs of repairs and minor renovation and redecoration are revenue expenditure; costs of major alterations and additions to the hotel building and facilities are capitalised (SM 9.58).

## Recap

- Read the constitution first: the Trust Deed or Regulations (an Act of Legislature for a university), bye-laws and the Managing Committee's minutes decide what is allowed.
- School: Fee Register vs Class Registers, counterfoils vs Cash Book, concessions and fines under authority, admission fees to Capital fund unless the Committee decides otherwise.
- School: caution money is a liability unless not refundable; prize endowment income above the prizes is added to the corpus; claim tax refunds; separate statements for each fund.
- Hospital: patients' register vs bills and attendance record, specific-purpose donations applied as agreed, capital expenditure only with prior sanction of the Trustees, budget variations reported, management certificate.
- Club: reconcile subscriptions due, collected and outstanding; adjust arrears and advances; every member's account debited; secretary's excess powers reported; irrecoverable dues mentioned in the report.
- Hotel: pilfering is the big risk; weekly trading accounts per sales point; K.O.T.s; room rates vs standard rate; housekeeper's reports kept; occupancy-in-progress accrued.
- Hotel: an unexplained material margin discrepancy means wider tests and possibly a qualified report. (The bar never drinks its own margin, but somebody does.)

## Flashcards

Q:: Audit of an educational institution: what does the auditor check the Students Fee Register with?
A:: The respective Class Registers showing the names of students on rolls, testing the amount of fees charged and the internal check that demands against students are properly raised.

Q:: Audit of an educational institution: where should admission fees be credited?
A:: To a Capital fund, unless the Managing Committee has taken a decision to the contrary (checked with admission slips signed by the head of the institution).

Q:: Audit of an educational institution: how should caution money be shown?
A:: As a liability in the balance sheet, not transferred to revenue, unless it is not refundable.

Q:: Audit of an educational institution: what must be seen about endowment funds for prizes?
A:: The investments are kept separate, and any income in excess of the prizes is accumulated and invested along with the corpus.

Q:: Audit of an educational institution: which constitution does the auditor read for a university?
A:: The Act of Legislature and the Regulations framed under it (for a school or college, the Trust Deed or Regulations).

Q:: Audit of a hospital: what is checked with the Register of Patients?
A:: Copies of bills issued; bills for a selected period with the patients' attendance record; and that bills were issued to all patients from whom an amount was recoverable under the hospital's rules.

Q:: Audit of a hospital: who must sanction capital expenditure, and when?
A:: The Trustees or the Managing Committee, in advance (prior sanction).

Q:: Audit of a hospital: what is done with budgets?
A:: Compare the totals of income and expenditure with the budgeted amounts and report significant variations to the Trustees or the Managing Committee.

Q:: Audit of a club: what must be done if the secretary exceeded his financial powers?
A:: Report the specific case for confirmation by the Managing Committee.

Q:: Audit of a club: how are margins earned tested?
A:: Vouch purchases of foodstuffs, cigars, wines, etc. and test their sale price to confirm that the normal rates of gross profit were earned; verify the year-end inventory of unsold provisions physically and check its valuation.

Q:: Audit of a hotel: what must the auditor do if a material margin discrepancy remains unexplained?
A:: Having increased the scope of the audit tests, consider qualifying the audit report.

Q:: Audit of a hotel: what is occupancy-in-progress, and what must the auditor ensure?
A:: Room revenue earned up to the balance sheet date but not yet billed; the auditor ensures it is properly valued and included in the accounts.
