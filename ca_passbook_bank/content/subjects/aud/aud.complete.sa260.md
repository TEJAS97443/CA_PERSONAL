## In plain words

### Kesar wants to be the post office

Iyer & Associates are planning the 2025-26 audit of Tapri Tea Co. Ltd. Tapri is listed, so its board has an **audit committee**, chaired by an independent director, Mrs. Rukmini Nair.

> **Kesar:** Lakshmi-ji, whatever you want to tell the board, tell me. I will pass it on.
> **Lakshmi:** Some things I must tell the audit committee myself, Kesar-ji. A few of them are about management.
> **Bunty:** *(whispering)* Ma'am, for the control weaknesses, can I just write "controls are weak"?
> **Lakshmi:** One line tells them nothing, Bunty. What is weak, what could go wrong, and why they are hearing it from us.

**Those charged with governance** are the people who oversee where the company is going and whether it is accountable, including how its accounts are prepared: at Tapri, the board and its audit committee. Management runs the company; they watch over it.

**SA 260** makes the auditor talk with them, both ways, all through the audit. At the start the auditor explains what an audit does and does not do, and how and when it will be done, including the biggest risks. During and at the end, the auditor reports what it found that matters to them: doubtful accounting choices, difficulties, disputes, what may change the report. For a listed company, the auditor also confirms its independence and lists its fees. And the committee tells the auditor what it knows.

**SA 265** deals with one kind of finding: weaknesses in **internal control** (the entity's own checks). The serious ones, **significant deficiencies**, go in writing, on time, to those charged with governance and to management, saying what is wrong, what it could lead to, and that the audit was not designed to give an opinion on internal control.

::: analogy The structural engineer and the housing society
A housing society hires an engineer to certify its building. He tells the society's committee, not just the builder, what he will check and when, and asks them about the leaks they have noticed. At the end he tells them what he found. For a cracked beam he does not say "building weak": he writes what is cracked, what could happen, and that he inspected to certify the building, not to vouch for every pipe. The builder gets a copy too, unless the builder is the problem.
:::

::: key
SA 260: two-way communication with those charged with governance, throughout the audit, on the auditor's responsibilities, the planned scope and timing, significant findings and, for listed entities, independence; in writing where oral would not be adequate, and always documented. SA 265: significant deficiencies in internal control go in writing, on a timely basis, to those charged with governance and to management, with a description, their potential effects and their context.
:::

## How it works

### Part A: SA 260, communication with those charged with governance

#### Why two-way communication matters

An effective two-way communication is important in assisting (SM 7.39–7.40):

- (a) the auditor and those charged with governance in understanding matters related to the audit in context, and in developing a constructive working relationship, while maintaining the auditor's independence and objectivity;
- (b) the auditor in obtaining from those charged with governance information relevant to the audit (for example, understanding the entity and its environment, identifying sources of audit evidence, and information about specific transactions or events); and
- (c) those charged with governance in fulfilling their responsibility to oversee the financial reporting process, thereby reducing the risks of material misstatement.

#### Who they are

::: definition Those charged with governance
The person(s) or organization(s) (e.g., a corporate trustee) with responsibility for overseeing the strategic direction of the entity and obligations related to the accountability of the entity. This includes overseeing the financial reporting process. For some entities, those charged with governance may include management personnel, for example, executive members of a governance board of a private or public sector entity, or an owner-manager (SM 7.40).
:::

Governance structures vary with culture, law, size and ownership (SM 7.40–7.41):

- some entities have a supervisory board separate from the executive board; in others, one board does both;
- sometimes those charged with governance hold positions integral to the entity's legal structure, such as company directors;
- some or all of them may be involved in managing the entity, or they may be different persons from management;
- in most entities governance is the collective responsibility of a governing body: a board of directors, supervisory board, partners, proprietors, committee of management, trustees or equivalent; in some smaller entities one person, such as an owner-manager or a sole trustee;
- where the structure is not clearly defined (some family-owned entities and not-for-profit organisations), the auditor may need to discuss and agree with the engaging party the persons with whom to communicate. The understanding of governance obtained under [[aud.risk.sa315|SA 315]] is relevant, and the appropriate persons may vary with the matter.

#### Scope and objectives

SA 260 deals with the auditor's responsibility to communicate with those charged with governance in an audit of financial statements. The objectives of the auditor are (SM 7.41):

- (a) to communicate clearly the responsibilities of the auditor in relation to the financial statement audit, and an overview of the planned scope and timing of the audit;
- (b) to obtain from those charged with governance information relevant to the audit;
- (c) to provide them with timely observations arising from the audit that are significant and relevant to their responsibility to oversee the financial reporting process; and
- (d) to promote effective two-way communication.

The auditor shall **determine the appropriate person(s)** within the entity's governance structure with whom to communicate (SM 7.42).

#### What must be communicated

**(a) The auditor's responsibilities**, including that (SM 7.42):

- the auditor is responsible for forming and expressing an opinion on the financial statements prepared by management with the oversight of those charged with governance; and
- the audit does not relieve management or those charged with governance of their responsibilities.

**(b) Planned scope and timing**: an overview, which includes communicating the **significant risks** identified by the auditor (SM 7.43).

**(c) Significant findings from the audit** (SM 7.43):

1. The auditor's views about **significant qualitative aspects** of the entity's accounting practices, including accounting policies, accounting estimates and financial statement disclosures; when applicable, explaining why a significant accounting practice that is acceptable under the framework is ==not considered most appropriate== to the entity's particular circumstances.
2. **Significant difficulties**, if any, encountered during the audit.
3. Unless all of those charged with governance are involved in managing the entity: (i) significant matters arising during the audit that were discussed, or subject to correspondence, with management; and (ii) the **written representations** the auditor is requesting.
4. Circumstances that affect the **form and content of the auditor's report**, if any.
5. Any other significant matters arising during the audit that, in the auditor's professional judgment, are relevant to the oversight of the financial reporting process.

**(d) Independence, for listed entities.** The auditor shall communicate (SM 7.43–7.44):

- (a) a statement that the engagement team and others in the firm as appropriate, the firm and, when applicable, network firms have complied with relevant ethical requirements regarding independence; and
- (b)(i) all relationships and other matters between the firm, network firms and the entity that, in the auditor's professional judgment, may reasonably be thought to bear on independence, including **total fees** charged during the period for audit and non-audit services provided by the firm and network firms to the entity and components controlled by it, allocated to categories that help those charged with governance assess the effect of the services on independence; and (ii) the related **safeguards** applied to eliminate identified threats or reduce them to an acceptable level.

#### How: the communication process

- The auditor shall communicate the **form, timing and expected general content** of communications (SM 7.44).
- Significant findings go **in writing** if, in the auditor's professional judgment, oral communication would not be adequate; written communications need not include all matters that arose during the audit (SM 7.44).
- Independence, for listed entities, is communicated in writing (SM 7.44).
- Communication shall be on a **timely** basis (SM 7.44).
- **Adequacy.** The auditor shall evaluate whether the two-way communication has been adequate for the purpose of the audit. If not, the auditor shall evaluate the effect on the assessment of the risks of material misstatement and on the ability to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence, and take appropriate action (SM 7.44).
- **Documentation.** Matters communicated orally are included in the audit documentation, with **when and to whom** they were communicated; for written communications, a copy is retained (SM 7.45).

### Part B: SA 265, communicating deficiencies in internal control

#### Why, and what SA 265 covers

Communicating significant deficiencies in writing to those charged with governance reflects the importance of these matters and assists them in fulfilling their oversight responsibilities (SM 7.45). SA 265 deals with the auditor's responsibility to communicate appropriately to those charged with governance and management the deficiencies in internal control the auditor has identified in an audit. The auditor obtains an understanding of internal control relevant to the audit to design appropriate procedures, **not to express an opinion on its effectiveness**, and may identify deficiencies at any stage of the audit (SM 7.46; see [[aud.risk.ic|internal control]] and [[aud.risk.testing|testing it]]).

**Objective.** To communicate appropriately to those charged with governance and management deficiencies in internal control that the auditor has identified during the audit and that, in the auditor's professional judgment, are of sufficient importance to merit their respective attentions (SM 7.46).

#### Two definitions

::: definition Deficiency in internal control
This exists when (i) a control is designed, implemented or operated in such a way that it is unable to prevent, or detect and correct, misstatements in the financial statements on a timely basis; or (ii) a control necessary to prevent, or detect and correct, misstatements in the financial statements on a timely basis is missing (SM 7.46).
:::

::: definition Significant deficiency in internal control
A deficiency or combination of deficiencies in internal control that, in the auditor's professional judgment, is of sufficient importance to merit the attention of those charged with governance (SM 7.46–7.47).
:::

Significance depends not only on whether a misstatement has actually occurred, but also on the **likelihood** that one could occur and its potential **magnitude**. So ==significant deficiencies may exist even though the auditor has not identified misstatements== during the audit (SM 7.47).

#### Matters the auditor may consider in judging significance (SM 7.47–7.48)

- The likelihood of the deficiencies leading to material misstatements in the financial statements in the future.
- The susceptibility to loss or fraud of the related asset or liability.
- The subjectivity and complexity of determining estimated amounts, such as fair value accounting estimates.
- The financial statement amounts exposed to the deficiencies.
- The volume of activity that has occurred or could occur in the account balance or class of transactions exposed to the deficiency or deficiencies.
- The importance of the controls to the financial reporting process, for example:
  - General monitoring controls (such as oversight of management).
  - Controls over the prevention and detection of fraud.
  - Controls over the selection and application of significant accounting policies.
  - Controls over significant transactions with related parties.
  - Controls over significant transactions outside the entity's normal course of business.
  - Controls over the period-end financial reporting process (such as controls over non-recurring journal entries).
- The cause and frequency of the exceptions detected as a result of the deficiencies in the controls.
- The interaction of the deficiency with other deficiencies in internal control.

#### Indicators of significant deficiencies (SM 7.48)

- Evidence of ineffective aspects of the control environment, such as:
  - Indications that significant transactions in which management is financially interested are not being appropriately scrutinised by those charged with governance.
  - Identification of management fraud, whether or not material, that was not prevented by the entity's internal control.
  - Management's failure to implement appropriate remedial action on significant deficiencies previously communicated.
- Absence of a risk assessment process within the entity where such a process would ordinarily be expected to have been established.
- Evidence of an ineffective entity risk assessment process, such as management's failure to identify a risk of material misstatement that the auditor would expect the entity's risk assessment process to have identified.
- Evidence of an ineffective response to identified significant risks (e.g., absence of controls over such a risk).
- Misstatements detected by the auditor's procedures that were not prevented, or detected and corrected, by the entity's internal control.
- Disclosure of a material misstatement due to error or fraud as prior period items in the current year's Statement of Profit and Loss.
- Evidence of management's inability to oversee the preparation of the financial statements.

#### Determining and communicating

The auditor shall determine, on the basis of the audit work performed, whether one or more deficiencies have been identified and, if so, whether, individually or in combination, they constitute significant deficiencies (SM 7.48). Then (SM 7.49):

- **To those charged with governance**: significant deficiencies, **in writing, on a timely basis**.
- **To management** at an appropriate level of responsibility, on a timely basis: (a) **in writing**, the significant deficiencies communicated or to be communicated to those charged with governance, unless it would be inappropriate to communicate directly to management in the circumstances; and (b) other deficiencies identified that have not been communicated to management by other parties and that, in the auditor's judgment, are of sufficient importance to merit management's attention.

## Formats and formulas

### What the written communication of significant deficiencies must contain (SM 7.49)

*Reproduce the two contents and the three explanations, in this order.*

The auditor shall include:

- (a) a **description** of the deficiencies and an explanation of their **potential effects**; and
- (b) **sufficient information** to enable those charged with governance and management to understand the context of the communication.

In particular, the auditor shall explain that:

- (i) the **purpose** of the audit was for the auditor to express an opinion on the financial statements;
- (ii) the audit included consideration of internal control relevant to the preparation of the financial statements in order to design audit procedures that are appropriate in the circumstances, but **not for the purpose of expressing an opinion on the effectiveness of internal control**; and
- (iii) the matters being reported are **limited** to those deficiencies that the auditor has identified during the audit and has concluded are of sufficient importance to merit being reported to those charged with governance.

## Worked examples

:::: example Who hears what at Tapri
In the 2025-26 audit of Tapri Tea Co. Ltd (listed):

- The cold-chain division delayed giving its records to the audit team by five weeks, despite repeated requests.
- Tapri depreciates its delivery scooters on the straight-line method, acceptable under the framework, though the scooters lose most of their usefulness in their first two years.
- Lakshmi and Dev Malhotra, the CFO, exchanged emails over the provision for a food-safety case; Lakshmi is requesting written representations on it.
- Besides the audit fee of ₹1.20 crore, Iyer & Associates charged Tapri ₹0.25 crore for the tax audit and ₹0.08 crore for certificates to lenders.

Kesar, the managing director, wants everything told to him orally "to save the committee's time".

Required: Advise Lakshmi under SA 260 on (i) with whom to communicate; (ii) the significant findings to communicate; (iii) the independence communication; and (iv) the form and documentation.

::: answer Model answer
**(i) With whom.** As per SA 260, the auditor shall determine the appropriate person(s) within the entity's governance structure with whom to communicate. Tapri's board, through its audit committee, oversees the financial reporting process; Kesar is part of management. Not all of those charged with governance manage Tapri, so Lakshmi should communicate with the audit committee, not through Kesar alone.

**(ii) Significant findings.** SA 260 requires communication of the auditor's views about significant qualitative aspects of accounting practices, explaining, when applicable, why a significant practice acceptable under the framework is not considered most appropriate in the entity's circumstances; significant difficulties encountered; and, unless all of those charged with governance are involved in managing the entity, significant matters discussed or corresponded on with management and the written representations requested; circumstances affecting the form and content of the report; and other significant matters relevant to oversight. Here:

1. **Qualitative aspects**: the straight-line method for scooters, with Lakshmi's explanation of why it is not the most appropriate given how quickly they lose usefulness.
2. **Significant difficulties**: the five-week delay in access to the cold-chain records.
3. **Matters discussed with management**: the email correspondence with Dev on the food-safety provision; and the **written representations** she is requesting.
4. Any circumstance that will affect the form and content of her report, if one arises.

**(iii) Independence.** Tapri is listed, so Lakshmi shall communicate, in writing, a statement that the engagement team, others in the firm as appropriate and the firm have complied with relevant ethical requirements regarding independence; all relationships and other matters that may reasonably be thought to bear on independence, including the total fees for the period allocated to categories (audit ₹1.20 crore, tax audit ₹0.25 crore, certification ₹0.08 crore: ₹1.53 crore in all); and the related safeguards applied.

**(iv) Form and documentation.** Lakshmi shall communicate the form, timing and expected general content of her communications and communicate on a timely basis. Kesar's preference does not decide the form: significant findings go in writing where, in her judgment, oral communication would not be adequate, and the independence matters go in writing. She shall document matters communicated orally, with when and to whom, and keep copies of written communications. She shall also evaluate whether the two-way communication has been adequate for the purpose of the audit.
:::

:::: example Bunty's one-line letter
During the 2025-26 audit, Bunty finds:

(a) The 340 manual journal entries posted in March 2026, totalling ₹48.60 crore, were prepared and approved by the same senior accountant; the system allows posting with no second approval. Bunty tested 40 of them and found no misstatement.

(b) The head office petty cash of ₹20,000 is counted only by the cashier who holds it.

(c) Kesar approves his own travel claims (₹18 lakh in the year); the audit committee does not review them.

Bunty's draft to the audit committee reads: "Journal entries are not reviewed. Petty cash is not checked. MD approves own claims."

Required: Comment on Bunty's draft and advise Lakshmi under SA 265.

::: answer Model answer
**Relevant provision.** As per SA 265, a deficiency in internal control exists when a control is designed, implemented or operated in such a way that it cannot prevent, or detect and correct, misstatements on a timely basis, or when a necessary control is missing. A significant deficiency is a deficiency, or combination of deficiencies, that in the auditor's professional judgment is of sufficient importance to merit the attention of those charged with governance; its significance depends on the likelihood and potential magnitude of misstatement, so it may exist even though no misstatement has been identified. Significant deficiencies shall be communicated in writing, on a timely basis, to those charged with governance and, in writing, to management unless inappropriate in the circumstances; other deficiencies meriting management's attention, not communicated by others, go to management. The written communication shall describe the deficiencies, explain their potential effects, give sufficient information for context, and explain the purpose and limits of the audit.

**Analysis.**

- **(a) Journal entries.** A necessary control, independent review of period-end manual entries, is missing: a deficiency. Controls over the period-end financial reporting process, such as controls over non-recurring journal entries, are among the matters the Study Material lists as important to financial reporting; the amount exposed (₹48.60 crore) is large and the control also guards against fraud. Bunty's clean sample does not make it insignificant. In Lakshmi's judgment it is a **significant deficiency**.
- **(b) Petty cash.** The control is designed so that it cannot detect a misstatement by the person it should check: a deficiency. With ₹20,000 exposed, it is not of sufficient importance to merit the committee's attention, but it merits management's: communicate it to management on a timely basis.
- **(c) Kesar's claims.** Significant transactions in which management is financially interested, not appropriately scrutinised by those charged with governance, are an indicator of a significant deficiency in the control environment: a **significant deficiency**. Lakshmi should consider whether communicating directly to management would be inappropriate in the circumstances; it goes in writing to the audit committee in any case.

**Bunty's draft.** It is not proper. A one-line statement neither describes the deficiencies nor explains their potential effects, and it mixes in a deficiency meant for management. The letter to the audit committee should, for (a) and (c), describe each deficiency, explain its potential effect (for example, that unreviewed period-end entries could misstate profit by fraud or error without detection), give the context, and explain that the purpose of the audit was to express an opinion on the financial statements, that internal control was considered to design audit procedures and not to express an opinion on its effectiveness, and that the matters reported are limited to the deficiencies identified and judged important enough to report. Highlighting the importance of each control, it may suggest that responsibility be fixed for adhering to it.
:::

## Under the hood

### Where other Standards send the auditor to those charged with governance

| Standard | What is communicated |
|---|---|
| SA 450 | Uncorrected misstatements and their possible effect on the opinion; prior-period effects; a request for correction (SM 7.26; [[aud.complete.sa450|SA 450]]) |
| SA 505 | Management's unreasonable refusal to allow a confirmation request, or failed alternative procedures (SM 4.82, 4.84) |
| SA 705 | A management-imposed limitation, unless all of them manage the entity (SM 8.40); the circumstances and wording of an expected modification (SM 8.47) |
| SA 701 | Key audit matters are selected from the matters communicated with those charged with governance (SM 8.55; [[aud.report.sa701|SA 701]]) |
| SA 700 | The report states that the auditor communicates with them on scope, timing and significant findings, including significant deficiencies, and, for listed entities, on independence (SM 8.19–8.20) |
| IT environment | Report deficiencies to management (internal controls memo or management letter); significant deficiencies in writing to those charged with governance (SM 3.65) |

### Adequacy is a finding too

If the two-way communication has not been adequate, the problem is not etiquette: the auditor evaluates its effect on the assessed risks of material misstatement and on the ability to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence, and takes appropriate action (SM 7.44). A committee that never answers is itself audit information.

- **Multiple choice.** Significant deficiencies go in writing to those charged with governance, not an oral briefing: MTP January 2026 Series II MCQ 15. Content of the communication, without an opinion on internal control: RTP May 2024 MCQ 2.4.

::: exam How the marks are earned
The one-liner questions are answered in ICAI's suggested answers in one pattern: the communication must describe the deficiency, explain its potential effects and give enough information for context; the one-liner does neither, so it "is not proper"; then the potential effect for the facts (misstatement of inventories, of related party transactions), ending with the suggestion that responsibility be fixed for adhering to the control.
:::

::: trick RSF + I, and the three Ps
SA 260's matters: **R**esponsibilities, **S**cope and timing (with significant risks), **F**indings, plus **I**ndependence for listed entities. SA 265's three explanations: **P**urpose (an opinion on the financial statements), **P**artial look (internal control considered to design procedures, not to opine on it), **P**icked items (only the deficiencies identified and judged worth reporting). Condition: the three Ps belong to the written communication of significant deficiencies.
:::

## Answer frame

### A one-liner question ("Is the communication proper?")

1. **Provision**: SA 265's definition of significant deficiency, and the required content: description, potential effects, sufficient information for context (and the three explanations).
2. **Facts**: quote the one-liner.
3. **Analysis**: it states the deficiency but explains no effect and gives no context.
4. **Conclusion**: "not proper"; then write the missing parts for the facts, and suggest fixing responsibility for the control.

### "What significant findings should be communicated?"

The five points in the Study Material's order: qualitative aspects (with "acceptable but not most appropriate"); significant difficulties; unless all of them manage the entity, matters discussed with management and written representations requested; circumstances affecting the report; other significant matters relevant to oversight. Apply each to the facts in a line.

### "Explain the independence communication for a listed entity"

The statement of compliance (engagement team, firm, network firms); all relationships and other matters bearing on independence, with total fees for audit and non-audit services by category; the safeguards applied. In writing.

## Common mistakes

::: mistake Hall of Shame: "controls are weak"
Reporting a significant deficiency in one line. **Fix:** describe it, explain its potential effects, give enough information for context, and explain the purpose and limits of the audit.
:::

::: mistake "No misstatement found, so not significant"
**Fix:** significance depends on the likelihood and potential magnitude of misstatement, not only on whether one occurred: a significant deficiency may exist although the auditor found no misstatement.
:::

::: mistake Telling only the managing director
Treating management as the only audience. **Fix:** SA 260 asks the auditor to determine the appropriate persons within the governance structure; matters such as those discussed with management and the written representations requested go to those charged with governance unless all of them manage the entity, and significant deficiencies go to them in writing.
:::

::: mistake An opinion on internal control by accident
Writing "internal control is effective except for…" in the letter. **Fix:** the communication must say the audit considered internal control to design procedures, **not** to express an opinion on its effectiveness.
:::

## Practice

::: quiz Check yourself
Q: Case scenario. Last year the auditor of Malabar Spices Ltd reported in writing to its audit committee a significant deficiency: the same clerk records supplier invoices and releases payments. This year nothing has changed; the finance head says "we are working on it". Extended testing of payments found no misstatement. Which of the following is correct under SA 265?
- [ ] Nothing need be communicated this year: the committee already knows, and no misstatement was found
- [ ] An oral reminder to the finance head is enough, since the matter was reported in writing last year
- [x] Management's failure to implement appropriate remedial action on a significant deficiency previously communicated is itself an indicator of a significant deficiency; it is communicated in writing to those charged with governance on a timely basis
- [ ] The deficiency ceases to be significant once management acknowledges it
> Significance rests on the likelihood and potential magnitude of misstatement, not on whether one was found; the unremedied deficiency, identified again in this audit, goes in writing to those charged with governance, and in writing to management unless that would be inappropriate.

Q: Case scenario. Rana Fabrics Pvt Ltd has two directors, the brothers who own it and run it day to day; there is no other governance body. Which of the following significant findings is the auditor NOT required to communicate to those charged with governance under SA 260?
- [ ] Significant difficulties encountered during the audit
- [ ] The auditor's views about significant qualitative aspects of the entity's accounting practices
- [x] The written representations the auditor is requesting
- [ ] Circumstances that affect the form and content of the auditor's report
> Significant matters discussed with management, and the written representations requested, are communicated "unless all of those charged with governance are involved in managing the entity". Here both directors manage the company, so they already know. The other three findings are communicated in every case.

Q: In the audit of a listed company, which must the auditor's independence communication include? Select all that apply.
- [x] A statement that the engagement team, the firm and network firms have complied with relevant ethical requirements regarding independence
- [x] Total fees for audit and non-audit services, allocated to appropriate categories
- [x] The safeguards applied to eliminate threats or reduce them to an acceptable level
- [ ] An opinion on the effectiveness of the company's internal control
> The first three are SA 260's listed-entity requirements; an opinion on internal control is what SA 265 says the audit does not give.

Q: Case scenario. CA Joshi, auditor of Konkan Ports Ltd (listed), emails her significant findings to the audit committee. She tells the committee chair over the phone about a significant difficulty in getting records, and about her firm's independence and fees. Which of the following does SA 260 require? Select all that apply.
- [x] Retain a copy of the email as part of the audit documentation
- [x] Document the matters told over the phone, with when and to whom they were communicated
- [x] Communicate the independence matters in writing
- [ ] Have the chair sign a note of every oral communication
> Written communication: a copy is retained. Oral communication: documented with when and to whom. For a listed entity, independence is communicated in writing, so a phone call does not discharge it. SA 260 asks for no signature from the chair.

Q: Case scenario. Shanti Trust, a family-run charitable trust, has no defined governance structure. With whom should its auditor communicate under SA 260?
- [ ] Only the accountant who keeps the books
- [x] The persons the auditor discusses and agrees with the engaging party, using the understanding of governance obtained under SA 315
- [ ] Nobody, as SA 260 applies only to companies
- [ ] The donors, since they provide the funds

## Recap

- Those charged with governance: the persons overseeing the entity's strategic direction and accountability, including the financial reporting process; they may include management (an owner-manager).
- SA 260 objectives: communicate responsibilities and the planned scope and timing; obtain relevant information; give timely significant observations; promote two-way communication.
- Communicate: responsibilities; scope and timing (with significant risks); significant findings (five points); independence and fees for listed entities.
- Process: form, timing and content agreed; in writing where oral is not adequate (independence always, for listed entities); timely; adequacy evaluated; oral matters documented with when and to whom.
- Deficiency: a control unable to prevent, or detect and correct, misstatements on time, or a necessary control missing. Significant: important enough to merit the attention of those charged with governance.
- Significance rests on likelihood and magnitude: it can exist with no misstatement found.
- Significant deficiencies: in writing, timely, to those charged with governance and (unless inappropriate) management; other deficiencies worth it, to management.
- Letter content: description and potential effects; context; the three Ps. (The engineer writes "cracked beam", never "building weak".)

## Flashcards

Q:: SA 260: who are "those charged with governance"?
A:: The person(s) or organization(s) (e.g., a corporate trustee) with responsibility for overseeing the strategic direction of the entity and obligations related to its accountability, including overseeing the financial reporting process.

Q:: SA 260: in what three ways is effective two-way communication important?
A:: It assists (a) both in understanding audit matters in context and building a constructive working relationship; (b) the auditor in obtaining relevant information; (c) those charged with governance in overseeing financial reporting, reducing risks of material misstatement.

Q:: SA 260: what are the auditor's four objectives?
A:: Communicate the auditor's responsibilities and an overview of planned scope and timing; obtain relevant information; provide timely significant observations; promote effective two-way communication.

Q:: SA 260: what does the auditor communicate about its responsibilities?
A:: That the auditor forms and expresses an opinion on financial statements prepared by management with the oversight of those charged with governance, and that the audit does not relieve management or those charged with governance of their responsibilities.

Q:: SA 260: the five significant findings?
A:: Qualitative aspects of accounting practices; significant difficulties; (unless all manage the entity) matters discussed with management and written representations requested; circumstances affecting the report's form and content; other significant matters relevant to oversight.

Q:: SA 260: what independence matters are communicated for listed entities?
A:: A statement of compliance with independence requirements; all relationships and other matters bearing on independence, including total audit and non-audit fees by category; the related safeguards.

Q:: SA 260: how are orally communicated matters documented?
A:: In the audit documentation, with when and to whom they were communicated; for written communications, a copy is retained.

Q:: SA 265: what is a deficiency in internal control?
A:: A control designed, implemented or operated so that it cannot prevent, or detect and correct, misstatements on a timely basis; or a necessary control that is missing.

Q:: SA 265: what is a significant deficiency in internal control?
A:: A deficiency or combination of deficiencies that, in the auditor's professional judgment, is of sufficient importance to merit the attention of those charged with governance.

Q:: SA 265: can a significant deficiency exist when no misstatement was found?
A:: Yes. Significance depends on the likelihood that a misstatement could occur and its potential magnitude, not only on whether one occurred.

Q:: SA 265: to whom and how are significant deficiencies communicated?
A:: In writing, on a timely basis, to those charged with governance; and in writing to management at an appropriate level, unless inappropriate in the circumstances.

Q:: SA 265: what must the written communication of significant deficiencies contain?
A:: A description and the potential effects; sufficient information for context; and explanations that the audit's purpose was an opinion on the financial statements, that internal control was considered to design procedures and not to opine on its effectiveness, and that matters reported are limited to those identified and judged important enough.
